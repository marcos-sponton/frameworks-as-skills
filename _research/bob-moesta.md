## 2. THE DEMAND-SIDE METHOD (structural detail)

### 2a. Core axiom
> "People don't buy products; they hire them to make progress in a particular circumstance."

**Progress** is Moesta's unit of measure — deliberately chosen over "need", "pain", "job", "outcome". A skill built around Moesta must default to "progress" as the operative word.

### 2b. Supply-side vs. demand-side (the *load-bearing* distinction)

| | Supply-side (Christensen strategic / Kalbach frame / Ulwick ODI) | Demand-side (Moesta) |
|---|---|---|
| Reference point | The product/technology/company | The customer's life circumstance |
| Starting question | "Who would buy this? What job could this do?" | "What progress is this person struggling to make? What made today the day?" |
| Research | Job maps, outcome statements, quantitative unmet-need scoring (Ulwick) | Timeline reconstruction via Switch Interview of *recent buyers* |
| Deliverable | Job Map / Job Statement / Outcome list | Forces diagram + Timeline + language quotes |
| Best used for | Strategy, market sizing, feature prioritization | Understanding causation of a switch, sales/marketing, killing features |
| Vocabulary | "Job executor", "desired outcomes", "job steps" | "Struggling moment", "the four forces", "vector of progress", "context creates value" |

Moesta himself worked with Christensen for 27 years (met quarterly). His framing: Christensen gave the theory; Moesta built the *method*. Ulwick built a *different* method (ODI) with a much more structured, quantitative bent. Kalbach synthesizes — he explicitly bridges Moesta's motivational lens with Ulwick's task lens (his "Frame → Discovery" phases in the JTBD Playbook borrow from both).

**Critical skill guardrail:** never write "JTBD says X" without disambiguating which lineage. If the artefact is a Forces diagram or Switch Interview timeline → Moesta. If it's a job map with outcome statements → Ulwick. If it's a Hypothesis Canvas / Frame → Kalbach.

### 2c. The Switch Interview — actual method

**Purpose:** Reconstruct the causal chain of a specific past purchase to reveal the forces at work.

**Recruiting rules:**
- Interview people who *already made progress* — recent buyers or churned customers, NOT prospects or leads.
- 10–12 interviews per round. Prefer two rounds of 12 over one of 24.
- **Intentional segment coverage** (distinct market segments), not random sampling.
- Patterns emerge by interview 6–7; by 10–12 you see 3–5 distinct buying patterns covering ~90% of the market.

**Duration:** 30–60 min minimum. "If you do it in 10 minutes, you're at the pablum level." Equal debrief time afterward with cross-functional team.

**Structural sequence** (composited from Moesta's own descriptions):
1. **Setting the stage** (~2m): "I don't have a long list of questions. I just want to hear your story."
2. **The world before the purchase** (~2m): role, context, what "normal" looked like.
3. **First Thought** (~3m): "What was happening the day you decided to look?" — the trigger.
4. **The struggle** (~4m): "What was frustrating about how you were handling this before?"
5. **Passive → Active looking** (~5m): "What did you Google?" — alternatives considered/rejected.
6. **Deciding** (~5m): "What made you decide this was the one?" — tipping point.
7. **First use / early wins** (~5m): onboarding, moment of validation.
8. **Ongoing use** (~7m): how usage evolved.
9. **Current pain** (~9m): workarounds — signals of next switch.
10. **Map the Four Forces** (post-interview, whiteboard).

**Interview micromoves:**
- No discussion guide. Ask *around* pushes/pulls/anxieties/habits.
- Never "why?" repeated — use "tell me more" and "give me an example."
- **Play back incorrectly on purpose** to trigger corrections — getting a "no" produces richer detail than a "yes."
- **Columbo move**: ask the real question just as you're about to close the notebook.
- Match the interviewee's tone/energy.
- The interviewer is an "empty vessel."

### 2d. The Four Forces of Progress

The equation: **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit → Switch**.

- **Push of the Situation** — friction, frustration, broken moment with the *current* solution. Not "meh" — status quo has to become unacceptable.
- **Pull of the New Solution** — the *vision of progress* the customer imagines. Not features — the improved life. Effective marketing visualizes this.
- **Anxiety of the New** — fear of the unknown, learning curve, image risk, data risk. "The most underestimated force in business." Mitigated by guarantees, social proof, onboarding — not features.
- **Habit of the Present** — comfortable inertia. "Why great products lose to mediocre incumbents."

**Key operational insight:** organizations obsess over increasing Pull (features, marketing) and forget the *asymmetric leverage* on the other two: reducing Anxiety and Habit is often cheaper and faster to move the equation. Adding features frequently *increases* Anxiety instead of Pull.

**Terminology precision:**
- Push and Habit are **forces on the current situation** (rearward-facing).
- Pull and Anxiety are **forces on the new solution** (forward-facing).
- Push + Pull = forces *for* progress. Anxiety + Habit = forces *against* progress.

### 2e. The Buying Timeline — six stages

Universal across purchase types (gum, software, church, mattress, career). Contra a funnel (one-directional), a timeline permits backward movement when context changes.

1. **First Thought** — "what we're doing isn't good enough." Triggered by: questions without answers, new metrics that reframe, relevant stories, obvious-but-overlooked truths.
2. **Passive Looking** — learning the domain, no urgency. Sales role: teach language, share progress stories. **Mistake**: firehose specifications.
3. **Active Looking** — wishing for features, exploring, no price connection yet. "Playground mentality." Sales role: expand possibilities, don't close.
4. **Deciding** — trade-offs. **Present exactly three options.** Never one, rarely two, never more than three. Customers eliminate one first, then compare the remaining two against the eliminated one. Design one "albatross" (obvious eliminate). Help them *articulate why they chose* — for internal justification.
5. **First Use** — must feel progress fast. Early wins.
6. **Ongoing Use / Habit Building** — new struggles restart the timeline.

**Canonical case (AutoBooks, fintech):** built three demos — one per stage (Passive/Active/Deciding). Sales cycle cut ~50%, conversion doubled. The lesson: "same action (demo request) means completely different things at different stages."

### 2f. Struggling Moments — the seed
- "The struggling moment is the seed for all innovation."
- "If your customer's not struggling, they can't see you."
- "Demand is only generated by a customer's struggling moment."
- "Eliminating the struggle is not progress. Customers *overcoming* the struggle is progress." ← This is a very Moesta distinction and worth carrying into any skill.

### 2g. Progress vs. Job — the semantic distinction
Moesta uses **Progress** as the operative noun; **Job** is the shorthand. The "Job to Be Done" *is* the progress the person is trying to make. Language slippage happens constantly in the JTBD world where "job" gets collapsed into "task" or "outcome" — Moesta resists this. A well-written Job-to-be-Done is a *progress statement in context*, not a task.

### 2h. Job Moves (2024) — what it adds
Applies the demand-side lens to career decisions. Key additions:

**The Four Quests** (the "why" of leaving):
1. **Get Out** — escape burnout/depletion
2. **Take the Next Step** — growth, advancement, skills
3. **Regain Control** — autonomy over time and responsibilities
4. **Realign** — refocus on core competencies after overextension

**The 9 Steps** (approximate synthesis — the book title's 9 steps aren't perfectly enumerated in public sources, but the components are):
1. Audit your past job moves — pattern-recognize the quest
2. Identify current energy drivers vs. drains
3. Reconstruct why you took each past job (Switch Interview on yourself)
4. Define what "progress" means for you (not features — experiences)
5. Build a "story spine" — 30-second career narrative
6. Prototype 3–5 potential futures
7. Draft your "cheat sheet" of how you do your best work
8. Learn-before-switching (interview people already in the target role)
9. Network / social approach — not online applications

**Key concepts specific to the book:**
- **Jobcation** — a low-demand recovery job to reset while exploring the next real move.
- **Features vs. Experiences** — salary/title/company brand are features (static, over-indexed on). Day-to-day activities and interactions are experiences (dynamic, actually determine satisfaction). Aim for 40–50% of time on energizing work.
- **Energy audit** — daily/weekly tracking of what energizes vs. depletes.
- **Career Story Template**: "Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that... Until finally... Ever since that day..."
- Interviews with 1000+ professionals underpin the framework.

### 2i. Demand-Side Sales — the sales adaptation
Same theory, sales lens. Key adds beyond the six-stage timeline:
- **Salesperson = concierge / coach / mentor.** Not persuader.
- "Help people to buy, without selling."
- **"Three sources of buyer energy"** — struggle, curiosity, and vision.
- **Anti-free-trial stance** — free prevents commitment/effort needed for real evaluation.
- **Marketing + Sales + CS = one lead** — not sequential handoff.
- **Sales training is technique; Moesta insists on theory.** "Schools don't teach techniques; they teach theory."

## 3. VOICE & TONE (for skill's writing style)

### 3a. Register
**Blue-collar-scientist.** Moesta is dyslexic (mentioned often — origin story for observational-first method). Detroit design-firm vibe. Direct, story-heavy, uses everyday objects (mattresses, milkshakes, Snickers). Rejects academic hedging. Comfortable with profanity-adjacent phrasing ("bitchin' ain't switchin'", "surface crap").

### 3b. Recurring rhetorical moves
- **Aphorism-then-story.** Delivers a short principle, then a case that grounds it.
- **"Context makes the irrational rational."** — his signature reframe move whenever someone dismisses customer behavior as stupid.
- **"It's not random. It's causal."** — reasserts causation theory against probability-thinking.
- **Ranting against a lie.** "'Build it and they will come' is just a lie." Frequently opens by naming and killing a received idea.
- **Domino metaphor** for causation chains.
- **First-person confession.** "I would go to research and they'd take six months..." — earned authority from having been the practitioner.

### 3c. Signature vocabulary (skill glossary)
- **Progress** (unit of measure — NOT need, pain, outcome)
- **Hire / fire** (verbs — "you don't buy, you hire")
- **The Forces** (never "the four forces" mid-flow — just "the Forces")
- **Struggling moment** (seed of demand)
- **Vector of progress** (direction the customer is trying to move)
- **Energy for switching** (the currency being spent)
- **Push / Pull / Anxiety / Habit** (canonical order — never rearrange)
- **First Thought / Passive Looking / Active Looking / Deciding / First Use / Ongoing Use** (six stages, exact capitalization matters in his slides)
- **The pablum layer** (vague surface responses — "good", "easy")
- **Context creates value, contrast creates meaning** (twin aphorisms — often deployed together)
- **The mustard, not the sandwich** ("your product is the mustard" — you're an ingredient, not the whole)
- **The wall** (invisible barrier between supply-side and demand-side thinking)
- **Bitchin' ain't switchin'** (complaints don't predict behavior)
- **Jobcation** (Job Moves specific)
- **The messy middle** (Re-Wired Group positioning)

### 3d. How Moesta distinguishes demand-side from supply-side (his own framing)
He tends to be diplomatic about Christensen ("we worked together 27 years, met quarterly") but sharp about downstream misapplications. His typical framing:
- "Christensen gave us the theory. My job was to make it operational."
- "Supply-side is 'we built this, who wants it?' Demand-side is 'what progress are they trying to make, and what do they hire to do it?'"
- Does not name-attack Ulwick or Kalbach in public — but distinguishes: "I don't do outcome statements. I don't do job maps. I do Switch Interviews and I map the Forces."

### 3e. How he critiques bad JTBD applications
Explicit and repeated targets:
1. **JTBD as demographic segmentation** — "millennials hire coffee to..." (wrong: context ≠ persona)
2. **JTBD as feature list** — treating outcomes as features to build
3. **Surveys of hypothetical customers** — "never ask people what they wish existed"
4. **Feature-first product roadmaps disguised in JTBD language**
5. **Job Statements written without a Switch Interview** — armchair jobs
6. **Building for multiple contradictory jobs simultaneously** — the "InVideo trap" (serving experts + beginners at once)

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

### 4a. Spotting a real Job vs. a fake one
- Real jobs are *causal, contextual, timestamped*. "On Tuesday morning at 7:15, driving to work..." (milkshake)
- Fake jobs are *general, aspirational, feature-flavored*. "Users want to be productive."
- Real jobs surface a **struggling moment** with specificity — a broken thing, a workaround, a frustration that occurred in a specific moment.
- Real jobs have all three dimensions active: functional (task), emotional (feeling), social (perception).
- If you can't map the Four Forces onto the story with real quotes for each force → it's not a real Job, it's a Job-shaped assertion.

### 4b. Switch Interview anti-patterns
- Interviewing *prospects* instead of recent buyers (top mistake).
- Using a discussion guide.
- Asking "why?" repeatedly (drives to pablum / rationalization).
- Accepting the first answer.
- Not doing the debrief (equal-time synthesis afterwards).
- Assuming interviews of size 24 are better than two rounds of 12 (they're not — you lose iteration).
- Failing to segment recruitment intentionally.
- Talking >20% of the time as interviewer.
- Not asking about **what they gave up** (Habit force) and **what they worried about** (Anxiety force).

### 4c. Common misapplications of JTBD (targets to guard against in the skill)
- **Persona substitution** — reframing personas as "jobs".
- **Survey-driven JTBD** — quantitative unmet-need scoring is Ulwick's ODI, not Moesta.
- **Feature-driven JTBD** — writing "as a user I want X so that Y" and calling it a Job.
- **Job statements without interviews** — desk-generated Jobs.
- **Collapsing Job = Task** — misses emotional/social dimensions and the progress vector.
- **Treating the Four Forces as a checklist** — Moesta treats them as forces-in-tension, not four buckets to fill.
- **Ignoring the *timeline*** — jumping to Forces without reconstructing the sequence loses causation.
- **Skipping the debrief** — the interview is 50% of the work; synthesis is the other 50%.

### 4d. The Moesta vs. Christensen confusion (what makes each valid)
- **Christensen's variant** = *strategic-mindset* JTBD. Best for: leadership, positioning, "what business are we really in", disruption analysis. Language is high-level, philosophical.
- **Moesta's variant** = *causal-research-method* JTBD. Best for: product decisions, sales/marketing execution, killing features, understanding *why* someone switched. Language is operational.
- Both agree on the core axiom. They diverge on what you *do* about it. A skill claiming "Moesta demand-side" must deliver the *method* (Switch Interview + Forces + Timeline), not the strategic mindset.

### 4e. Kalbach vs. Moesta — for the Kalbach conversation
Kalbach's JTBD Playbook is a synthesis — his "Frame → Discovery → Design" phases integrate Ulwick's ODI (job map, outcome statements) with Moesta's motivational lens. Kalbach's *Frame* phase is the closest analog to a Moesta Timeline recon, but Kalbach then hands off to outcome statements (Ulwick), which Moesta does not use. Kalbach and Moesta are *complementary, not competing* — a company can do Kalbach Frame + Moesta Switch Interview + Kalbach Discovery.

For the JTBD Assistant product (which does *both* schools — Kalbach + Switch/Moesta): honoring the *distinct outputs* is what preserves fidelity. Moesta's output is a **Progress Making Forces Diagram + Timeline + verbatim quotes**. Kalbach's output is a **Hypothesis Canvas + Job Statement**.

### 4f. Gotchas specific to writing skill prompts / agent flows in Moesta's voice
- Never write "the JTBD framework" — Moesta refuses this framing. Say "Jobs to be Done" or "the demand-side lens."
- Never write "user needs" — say "the progress they're trying to make."
- Never write "the persona" — say "the person in the struggling moment."
- Never open a Switch Interview by asking about the product. Open with life-context.
- Never use "why did you buy X?" — always "walk me through the day you decided to look."
- The Forces are always four, in that order, always in tension.
- Case-sensitive: "Push", "Pull", "Anxiety", "Habit" — Moesta capitalizes.

## 5. REAL CASES he uses (verified from sources)

**Classic canon:**
- **Milkshake** (McDonald's, w/ Christensen) — hired for a long boring morning commute, one hand on the wheel. Competitors: bananas, bagels, boredom. Second job: parents buying for kids as reward, hired to be a "good parent" — different job entirely. The classic that shows same product, different Jobs.
- **Mattress** (documented Switch Interview at jobstobedone.org) — customer who hated old mattress, moved houses, dragged old one along, spouse dynamics, physical pain ("the spring dug into my back"). Reveals Habit force (bringing the old one), Anxiety, and the emotional weight of a purchase people underestimate.
- **Snickers vs. Milky Way** — same category, different Job. Snickers: quick fuel between meetings, no mess, no chocolate on fingers. Milky Way: dessert/reward. Same ingredients, different progress.
- **Dining room table** — homebuilder for downsized empty-nesters. Buyers *said* they didn't want a dining room. Builder included table space anyway → sales +22%. Emotional/social job of "hosting the family for holidays" invisible in stated preferences.
- **Basecamp calendar** — customers demanded "calendar feature." Interviews revealed they only needed to see available time slots. Solution: dot view (6-week build) instead of full calendar (9-month build).
- **Casper mattress** — real competitor was **ZzzQuil** (sleep aid), not other mattresses. Ad: "How many bottles of ZzzQuil before you realize you need a new mattress?" → sales +37%.

**Newer cases:**
- **AutoBooks (fintech for SMB banking)** — three-demo restructure by buying-timeline stage. Cycle -50%, conversion 2x, valuation up.
- **Facebook Marketplace** — no existing customers → interviewed users of Etsy/Craigslist/eBay (products they'd fire if Marketplace succeeded). Method for launching without a user base.
- **SNHU (Southern New Hampshire University)** — Choosing College research, revealed adult-learner jobs (career pivot, kid respect, personal proof) distinct from traditional-student jobs.
- **Hudl** (client of Re-Wired) — sports video analytics.
- **InVideo (anti-example)** — 3-year trap serving experts + beginners with one product.

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

### 6a. Christensen JTBD (supply-side/strategic)
Same origin. Christensen owned theory + strategic implications (disruption, competing against luck). Moesta owned method. Collaboration 27 years, quarterly meetings. Moesta was Christensen's field practitioner — the Switch Interview *is* the operationalization Christensen never wrote.

### 6b. Ulwick Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI)
Sibling but methodologically distinct. Ulwick: quantitative — surveys, unmet-need scoring, job maps, "desired outcome statements" (110-word structured sentences). Moesta: qualitative — Switch Interviews, Forces diagrams, timelines, verbatim customer language. Both call themselves JTBD. Both trace to Christensen. **Different deliverables, different tools, different bets.**

### 6c. Kalbach JTBD Playbook
Synthesis. Kalbach's "Frame → Discovery → Design" bridges Ulwick and Moesta. His Frame phase asks "who is the job performer, what is the focus job" (Ulwick-flavored). His Discovery phase includes Switch-Interview-style motivational research (Moesta-flavored). His deliverable is a **Hypothesis Canvas** (his own artifact). Kalbach explicitly positions JTBD as a *mindset* with a *toolkit* — practitioners pick tools. He and Moesta are compatible; a JTBD Assistant product can honor both by keeping *outputs* distinct.

### 6d. Teresa Torres — Continuous Discovery Habits
Different lineage (Product Discovery school — Cagan, Marty). Overlap: interview cadence, opportunity solution trees, evidence-driven. **Key contrast:** Torres frames the unit as "opportunities" (unmet needs / pain points), Moesta frames it as "progress + forces." Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree is closer to a strategic PM artifact; Moesta's Forces diagram is closer to a causal-research artifact. Torres does not use Push/Pull/Anxiety/Habit as her frame. They *coexist* — a team can run Torres-style weekly interviews as the operating cadence and Moesta-style Switch Interviews as periodic deep-dives.
