## 2. POST-BOOK MATERIAL (refinements since 2018)

### 2.1 Second book: *Product Operations* (with Denise Tilles, Oct 2023)
Full title: *Product Operations: How successful companies build better products at scale.* Amazon: [B0CK3HL4WF](https://www.amazon.com/Product-Operations-successful-companies-products/dp/B0CK3HL4WF).

**Perri's definition of Product Operations** (verbatim, widely quoted, adopted by Marty Cagan): *"the art of removing obstacles from evidence-based decision making."* ([Perri, blog 2019](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2019/7/19/product-operations-the-fuel-for-winning-product-strategies))

**Three Pillars of Product Operations** (canonical framework from the book):
1. **Data and Insights** — instrumenting the product, aggregating usage/business data so PMs decide with evidence, not opinion.
2. **Customer and Market Insights** — research ops: recruiting, systematizing interviews, competitive/market intel available on demand.
3. **Process and Governance** — cadences, standards, tooling, prioritization frameworks; keeps PM effort spent on judgment, not admin.
(Case studies she uses in the book: Stripe, Uber, Fidelity. [Book page](https://melissaperri.com/book); [Lenny's guide with Perri](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-product-operations))

### 2.2 "Four Dimensions of a Great Product Management Organization" (2024)
Introduced in [her July 2024 blog post](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2024/7/16/building-a-great-product-management-organization). This is her post-book diagnostic model — use this as the framework a skill should apply when auditing an org:
1. **Product Organizational Design** — job role design, product skill level, structure around products.
2. **Product Strategy** — strategic alignment to business, strategy creation & deployment, roadmapping & prioritization.
3. **Product Operations** — data & insights, customer & market research, process & governance.
4. **Product Culture** — customer-centric mindset, outcome focus & incentives, continuous learning, leadership & empowerment.

### 2.3 Position on the "Product Operating Model" debate
Marty Cagan/SVPG owns the phrase **"Product Operating Model"** as a book title (*Transformed*, 2024). **Perri does not compete for that term** — she operates one level down. Cagan explicitly credits her definition of Product Ops as the one he uses. Their frames are complementary, not overlapping: Cagan = organizational transformation; Perri = the operational infrastructure that makes it stick + PM career and culture. When she talks about a "Product Management Operating Model" in the book, she means the internal operating rhythm of a PM function (career ladders, decision rights, cadences).

### 2.4 Roadmap critique (evolved since 2014)
Original: [Rethinking the Product Roadmap (2014)](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2014/05/19/rethinking-the-product-roadmap) — introduced the **Problem Roadmap**. Refined position now split into two artifacts ([Produx Labs — Setting the Roadmap](https://www.produxlabs.com/blog/setting-the-roadmap)):
- **Outcome-driven roadmap** — internal document for design + eng. Themes, outcomes, hypotheses. No committed features until validated. Starts vague, gets concrete post-validation.
- **Feature-driven roadmap** — external document for sales + customers. Only lists things believed ready to ship, with buffer + explicit "exploring, subject to change" for later items.
Her verbatim: *"The roadmap has become the symbol in many organizations of everything that is wrong with how they develop products."*

### 2.5 Recurring 2024–2026 themes
- **Founder Mode critique** ([Sep 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/my-thoughts-on-founder-mode-and-why)): "Just because you made it from 0 to 1 doesn't mean you inherently know how to scale."
- **"One throat to choke" anti-pattern** ([Jun 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/why-are-we-making-product-managers)): scapegoating PMs for outcomes they can't control alone; fix is team-level OKRs, not individual ones.
- **"Fuzzy strategy" diagnostic** ([Oct 2024](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2024/10/22/how-to-get-clarity-when-your-companys-strategy-is-fuzzy)): borrows Joshua Arnold's 4 value drivers (Increase Revenue / Protect Revenue / Reduce Costs / Avoid Costs). Coined "Make More Money Syndrome" (financial targets without mechanism) and "Pet Projects" (solutions disguised as strategy).
- **AI + PM** (2025–2026): "AI as multiplier, not equalizer." Bottleneck is *upstream* — discovery + decisions. Only ~1/3 of leaders say AI is strengthening their operating model. Source: State of AI in Product 2026 (co-published with Product Circle, discussed Ep 271 and on LinkedIn).

## 3. VOICE & TONE

### Register
- **Practitioner-authoritative, not academic.** She opens Substack posts with "Hi Product People,". Contractions throughout. First-person plural for the profession ("we").
- **Diagnostic before prescriptive.** Names the pathology, then the fix. Never leads with the framework.
- **Empathetic but firm.** "I have tremendous empathy for founders in this scenario…" followed by the counter-argument.
- **Direct, not blunt.** Confrontational only with named anti-patterns, not with individuals.

### Signature rhetorical moves
1. **"Why do we…?" rhetorical challenge.** E.g., "Why do we run companies as if everyone is competing individually?" — reframes systemic issue as absurd on its face.
2. **Symptom → root cause → reframe.** Lists the observable symptom, names the wrong cause the org blames (process, talent, tools), then relocates it (incentives, strategy, decision rights).
3. **Historical case study inserted mid-argument.** Cites specific companies (Citigroup e-Solutions, Stripe, Uber, Fidelity) or third-party research (Teresa Amabile's Progress Principle).
4. **Definitional reset.** Redefines a term the industry misuses. "MVP is the minimum amount of effort *to learn*." "Product Ops is the art of removing obstacles from evidence-based decision making."
5. **Contrast lists.** Waiter vs. Strategic PM. Outputs vs. outcomes. Feature-driven vs. outcome-driven. Sales-led vs. product-led. Always binary before nuanced.
6. **Push back on hero founders / mini-CEOs.** She dismantles the "PM as mini-CEO" myth every chance she gets.

### Signature vocabulary (use these)
- **Build trap** · **feature factory** · **outputs vs. outcomes** · **product-led** · **value stream** · **strategic intent** · **product initiative** · **option** · **product kata** · **Waiter / Mini-CEO / Former Project Manager** (PM archetypes) · **Problem Roadmap** · **Product Operations** · **Data and Insights / Customer and Market Insights / Process and Governance** (the three pillars) · **Knowledge Gap / Alignment Gap / Effects Gap** · **Solution Experimentation** (she prefers this over "MVP") · **Concierge / Wizard of Oz / Concept Testing** · **Peanut-buttering strategy** · **Make More Money Syndrome** · **Pet projects** · **Building to learn vs. building to earn** · **Fall in love with the problem** · **HEART metrics** · **Pirate metrics (AARRR)** · **Product Operating Model** (secondary — she cedes to Cagan on the phrase itself).

### Words she pushes back on
- **"Roadmap as contract"** / roadmap-as-Gantt-chart
- **"Feature factory"** (uses it as diagnosis, not aspiration)
- **"Mini-CEO"** (as PM job description — she says "90% of PM job postings" mis-use it)
- **"Order-taker" / "Waiter"** PM
- **"SAFe"** — she explicitly does not recommend it
- **Rigid Scrum ceremonies** when they're theater
- **"Hero founder / Founder Mode"** as scaling strategy
- **"One throat to choke"** accountability
- **Individual OKRs** for executives (should be team-level)
- **HIPPO-driven / opinion-driven** decision making
- **Certification-as-training** (surface credentials without mentorship)

### How she teaches
- **Framework as diagnostic checklist**, not linear method. The Four Dimensions and Three Pillars are self-assessment tools, not "step 1, step 2."
- **Systems view.** She always zooms out — the problem is rarely "the PM"; it's the incentive structure, the strategy vacuum above them, or the missing operational scaffolding.
- **Career progression as scaffolding.** Tactical → Strategic → Operational is how she teaches PMs to grow.
- **Live audits.** In talks and podcast, she frequently walks through anonymized real company situations. "Dear Melissa" episodes are the purest form.

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

### Symptoms you're in the Build Trap
- Success measured by features shipped, velocity, releases — not by customer or business outcomes.
- Backlog is always full; team is always shipping; customer satisfaction and business metrics flat.
- PMs spend >30% of their week writing user stories for things that already work.
- Roadmap items are locked commitments to specific solutions before problems are validated.
- Nobody can articulate the company strategy in one sentence; multiple execs give different answers.
- The team can't name the metric that would prove a feature "worked."
- Every stakeholder request becomes a ticket. No push-back happens.
- Sales contracts contain feature promises that then drive the roadmap.

### Anti-patterns (name them explicitly)
- **Waiter PM** — order-taker, no strategic point of view.
- **Mini-CEO PM** — expected to own things they have no authority over.
- **Former-PM PM** — obsessed with "when," ignores "why."
- **Roadmap-as-contract** with sales/customers.
- **Feature-factory culture** — velocity as virtue.
- **HIPPO-driven prioritization** — highest-paid person's opinion wins.
- **Peanut-buttering strategy** — too many initiatives, no resource concentration.
- **Make More Money Syndrome** — "grow revenue 30%" without mechanism.
- **Pet projects disguised as strategy.**
- **Founder Mode** as scaling doctrine.
- **Individual executive OKRs** creating siloed fiefdoms.
- **"One throat to choke"** — PM accountable for outcomes they can't drive alone.
- **SAFe** and other prescriptive scaled-agile frameworks that reward output cadence.
- **Certifications-as-training** without mentorship.
- **Discovery skipped in the roadmap** — no time budgeted for research and validation.

### Do's for product-led organizations
- Structure teams around **value streams**, not components or features.
- Deploy strategy in 4 layers: **Vision → Strategic Intent → Product Initiatives → Options.**
- Make OKRs **team-level**, not individual.
- Roadmap in themes/outcomes/hypotheses internally; commit to features only after validation.
- Publish two roadmaps: internal (outcome-driven) + external (feature-driven, with "exploring" tag).
- Run the **Product Kata** loop weekly (goal → current state → obstacle → next step → hypothesis → learning).
- Use **HEART** for UX metrics, **AARRR** for growth.
- Fund and staff **Product Operations** early — it's the fuel for evidence-based decisions.
- Empower PMs to push back on stakeholder requests when they're solutions to unvalidated problems.
- Train PMs on the tactical/strategic/operational progression; hire and promote against it.

### Common misapplications (companies that *claim* product-led)
- Rebrand PMs as "product owners" without changing the job → still order-takers.
- Adopt OKRs but keep them individual and top-down → they become outputs disguised as outcomes (Ep 267 topic).
- Build a Product Ops team that becomes a **process police** or **PMO in disguise** — Perri: real Product Ops removes obstacles, doesn't add them.
- Add "outcome" language to feature roadmaps without changing the underlying prioritization.
- Hire a CPO with no authority over engineering priorities or budget.

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

### vs. Marty Cagan (Inspired, Empowered, Transformed / SVPG)
- **Complementary, not competing.** Cagan works one altitude higher (organizational transformation → Product Operating Model). Perri works at PM-role and PM-operational-infrastructure level.
- Cagan explicitly credits Perri's definition of Product Ops. She doesn't fight him on "Product Operating Model" as a term.
- **Divergence**: Cagan is more prescriptive about the "product trio" (PM + design + eng together own discovery). Perri is more flexible on structure but stricter on the PM role's substance (not order-taker, not mini-CEO). Cagan is more evangelical about "empowered teams"; Perri is more diagnostic about *why* teams aren't empowered (usually: no strategy above them).

### vs. Teresa Torres (Continuous Discovery Habits)
- **Highly compatible.** Perri promotes Torres's work (podcast Ep 269 explicitly). Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree fits inside Perri's Product Kata / "problem exploration → solution experimentation" loop.
- Torres = tactical discovery cadence (weekly interviews, OST). Perri = strategic scaffolding around that cadence. A PM using Torres's habits inside Perri's framework is Perri's ideal PM.

### vs. Ryan Singer (Shape Up / Basecamp)
- Rarely cited directly. Shape Up's "appetite over estimate" and "shaping before betting" are consistent with Perri's rejection of Gantt-style roadmaps and her Problem Roadmap. But Shape Up is a delivery cadence; Perri's frame is broader (career, strategy, culture, ops). She would likely say Shape Up solves the delivery-side of the build trap but not the strategy-vacuum-above-teams problem.

### vs. Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen / Kalbach / Moesta)
- Compatible with her "fall in love with the problem" doctrine. She uses "problem" as her primary unit, not "job," but the underlying discipline (understand demand before designing supply) is the same. She rarely uses JTBD vocabulary directly — she stays in "customer problems" language for accessibility. A PM operating in her frame would find JTBD a natural discovery method inside the Problem Roadmap.

### vs. Lean Startup (Eric Ries)
- Foundational influence. She keeps "build-measure-learn" and MVP concept but **explicitly redefines MVP** as "minimum amount of effort to learn" and prefers "solution experimentation" to break the association of MVP with "small first version to ship."

### vs. OKRs (Doerr / Grove)
- Uses OKRs, but with two constraints: **team-level not individual** (Substack Jun 2024), and **must roll up from strategic intent** (Ep 267 — "How OKRs Become Outputs Instead of Outcomes"). Warns against OKRs used to enforce output targets.
