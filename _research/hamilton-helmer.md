**Three-stage business phase model (post-2016 refinement)** — every Power lives in a specific stage:
- **Origination** (pre-takeoff): Counter-Positioning + Cornered Resource
- **Takeoff** (explosive growth): Scale Economies, Network Economies, Switching Costs
- **Stability** (post-explosive, <30–40% market growth): Process Power + Branding

Barriers per stage: Collateral Damage / Fiat (Origination) → Share Gain Cost-Benefit (Takeoff) → Hysteresis (Stability).

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

**The mandatory two-part test:** every Power must simultaneously exhibit a **Benefit** (materially improved cash flow relative to competitors) AND a **Barrier** (something that prevents competitors from arbitraging the benefit away). No Barrier → not Power, just a temporary advantage.

**The 3 S's — the go/no-go screen for any claimed Power:**
- **Superior** — does it improve free cash flow vs. competitors?
- **Significant** — is the improvement material?
- **Sustainable** — is it largely immune to competitive arbitrage?

**Identifying which of the 7 a company has — barrier-first diagnosis:**
- Barrier is **collateral damage** to an incumbent → likely Counter-Positioning.
- Barrier is **prohibitive share-gain cost** for a smaller rival → Scale Economies.
- Barrier is **network tipping point** → Network Economies.
- Barrier is **customer's cost to leave** → Switching Costs.
- Barrier is **hysteresis** (long time to build, path-dependent) → Branding OR Process Power (Branding if it's about willingness-to-pay; Process Power if it's about cost/quality of production).
- Barrier is **fiat / legal / physical exclusivity** → Cornered Resource.

**Common misapplications (Helmer's explicit "gotchas"):**
- **Operational excellence ≠ Process Power.** Excellence without hysteresis is table stakes; competitors mimic it.
- **Network effects ≠ Network Economies.** Effects are near-universal in any market; Economies require the value increase to be *material* AND create a sharp, winner-take-most tipping.
- **Brand recognition ≠ Brand Power.** Power means willingness-to-pay a premium for an identical offering. Being known ≠ commanding a premium.
- **Data scale is not automatically Power.** Common founder claim; usually fails the Barrier test.
- **Speed / execution is not Power.** "It may boost Power once you have it, but if Power does not yet exist, you can't rely on it."
- **Counter-Positioning is *partial* Power** — protects only against incumbents constrained by their existing business; does NOT protect against fresh entrants copying the new model.
- **Switching Costs only accrue on the *next* purchase** from existing customers — no benefit with new customers or non-repeat purchases.
- **Cornered Resource: leadership teams almost never qualify** (they fail the sufficiency and transferability tests). Five screens: idiosyncratic, non-arbitraged, transferable, ongoing, sufficient.

**Statics vs. Dynamics — the single most misunderstood distinction:**
- **Statics** — "how *having* Power creates value." Identify it in a mature company (retrospection); easy.
- **Dynamics** — "how *getting* Power happens." Requires invention; not planable in advance. "Strategy cannot be designed in advance, but must be crafted over time, with breakthrough opportunities recognized and seized."
- **Consequence:** frameworks that only describe Statics (Porter) are diagnostic-only. 7 Powers is dual: taxonomy for Statics, staged theory of invention for Dynamics.

**Sequencing / evolution (post-2016 refinement):**
- **Origination:** only Counter-Positioning + Cornered Resource are available. This is the window where invention matters most.
- **Takeoff:** Scale Economies, Network Economies, Switching Costs can be locked in — but only if you scale faster than rivals.
- **Stability:** Branding + Process Power emerge from *sustained* action over years.
- **Implication:** if you missed the window for a stage, that Power is off the table for you. Read: don't try to build Network Economies after the takeoff has ended in someone else's favor.

**Combining Powers:** Helmer treats them as additive/compounding, not mutually exclusive. Netflix classic sequence: Counter-Positioning (2011, streaming/originals) → Scale Economies (content spend across ballooning subscribers) → Branding. Nvidia example applied by others: Cornered Resource (CUDA IP, ecosystem) + Switching Costs + Scale Economies.

**The 5 stages of incumbent response to Counter-Positioning:** Denial → Ridicule → Fear → Anger → Capitulation. Predictable, economically-rational; not a failure of vision.

**"Value Moment":** the point at which uncertainty about market size and Power radically diminishes — a step-change in cash-flow transparency. This is what Strategy Capital's investing method hunts for.

**Do:** think about Power *before* PMF, not after.
**Do:** test each claimed Power against each competitor individually (direct, functional, potential) — one arbitrageur destroys differential margins.
**Don't:** confuse benefit with Power ("benefits are common, and often bear little positive impact on company value, as they are generally subject to full arbitrage").
**Don't:** substitute planning for invention. "Planning rarely creates Power."
**Don't:** retrofit — "lemons into lemonade is rare."

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

**vs. Porter's Five Forces:**
- **Porter asks: "Which industry?"** (industry attractiveness).
- **Helmer asks: "Which company within an industry, and why does it persistently earn above-average returns?"**
- Porter is diagnostic of industry structure. Helmer is diagnostic + prescriptive of firm-level advantage.
- Porter's five forces influence *whether* Power is available in an industry; 7 Powers identify *which* Power a specific firm can hold.
- Helmer's Barrier concept is more explicit and testable than Porter's "barriers to entry" — it's tied to a specific Power type with a specific mechanism (hysteresis, fiat, collateral damage, tipping, cost-to-switch, share-gain cost).

**vs. Rumelt's diagnosis / kernel of strategy:**
- Rumelt: diagnosis-first, kernel = diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent action, plus "the crux."
- Helmer: taxonomic-first — Power exists in exactly seven shapes.
- Rumelt is more useful when the problem is *what is wrong / what is the pivotal challenge*. Helmer is more useful when the problem is *what durable advantage does this business have or could plausibly build*.
- **They are complementary, not competing.** Rumelt tells you the crux; Helmer tells you which Power you're either defending or building toward.

**vs. Martin's Playing to Win cascade (5 questions):**
- Martin: winning aspiration → where to play → how to win → capabilities → management systems.
- Helmer: the "how to win" question, unpacked into a specific mechanistic taxonomy.
- Martin's cascade is a strategy-*making* process. Helmer is a strategy-*validation* framework. Where Martin says "how to win," Helmer forces the answer to name one of seven Powers and prove Benefit + Barrier + 3 S's.

**vs. Buffett's "economic moats":**
- Helmer explicitly maps to Buffett: "economic castles [Benefit] protected by unreachable moats [Barrier]." He treats 7 Powers as the operational taxonomy Buffett's metaphor never gave.

**vs. Christensen's disruption theory:**
- Counter-Positioning is Helmer's more precise reformulation of the "why don't incumbents respond?" question. Rather than psychology / innovator's dilemma alone, Helmer isolates *economically rational* self-preservation (collateral damage on existing P&L) as the primary Barrier, with cognitive bias as secondary.
