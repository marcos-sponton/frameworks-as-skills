## Framework: the Four Fits

Four interdependent fits any $100M+ venture-backed company must land — and keep landing — together. "Any break in the chain kills the business."

1. **(Market) Product Fit** — the classic Ellis / Andreessen fit: build something a meaningful segment desperately wants. Balfour insists on getting market/problem BEFORE solution: *"You are thinking about the solution before properly understanding the problem and audience that has that problem."*

2. **Product Channel Fit** — the product is designed for how customers actually discover it. Signature line: *"Products are built to fit with channels. Channels do not mold to products."* You don't bolt a channel onto a finished product; product shape (freemium, viral loops, content-heavy, sales-ready) has to match the channel's mechanics.

3. **Channel Model Fit** — the economics of the business model work with the chosen channel. Framed as an ARPU spectrum with five zones (see below). The middle is the danger zone.

4. **Model Market Fit** — the way you sell + charge matches how the market wants to buy AND the math clears $100M: `ARPU × Total Customers In Market × % you can capture ≥ $100M`. Five archetypes (Elephants → Flies).

**System property:** *"Each of these fits influence each other, so you can't think about them in isolation... When [a fit breaks], you can't simply change one element, you have to revisit and potentially change them all."*

**Failure mode Balfour names:** *"Tugboats"* — companies with PMF alone, grinding for growth. *"Smooth Sailers"* — all four fits aligned, growth feels effortless despite imperfect execution.

**Chegg case (from AI-era essay):** Chegg valued at $1.2B January 2024 → $150M October 2024 after ChatGPT ate the homework-help value proposition. All four fits can be lost overnight in the AI era, not gradually.

## Framework: Growth Loops (vs. funnels)

Balfour's second signature contribution (co-developed with Casey Winters, Kevin Kwok, Andrew Chen; Reforge blog 2018).

**Definition:** a self-reinforcing system where **the output of one cycle becomes the input of the next**. Compounding, closed. Funnels are open — input at top, output at bottom, no feedback into next cycle. *"Loops compound. Funnels decay."*

**Types of loops:**
- **Viral loops** — users invite users (WhatsApp, Facebook)
- **Content loops** — content creates users who create content (Pinterest UGC, Yelp reviews, Quora)
- **Paid loops** — revenue funds paid acquisition of more users (DTC subscription, DraftKings)
- **Sales loops** — closed customers surface referrals / expansion / ICP data for outbound
- **UGC / SEO loops** — user content indexed by Google drives new users (Yelp, Reddit, Pinterest)

**Loop design pattern:** `input → action → output that feeds back to input`. If you can't diagram the arrow returning to the top, you have a funnel wearing loop language.

**Why teams get it wrong:** they dress a funnel up with loop vocabulary but the output never actually becomes input. Balfour's test: draw the diagram — arrows must close.

## Channel-Model Fit — the five ARPU zones

From the ARPU-CAC Danger Zone essay.

| Zone | ARPU | Channels that fit | Example companies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Very low | ~$0 (ad-supported) | Virality, UGC SEO | Facebook, WhatsApp, Yelp |
| 2. Low-mid | $10s–low $100s | Paid marketing | Dollar Shave Club, DraftKings |
| 3. **DANGER ZONE** | mid ($100–$1k) | No clean fit — patchwork | many failed SaaS |
| 4. Mid-high (B2B) | $1k–$10k+ | Content, inbound/inside sales, partnerships | HubSpot, Zendesk |
| 5. Very high | $100k+ | Enterprise / outbound sales | Palantir, Veeva |

**Danger zone diagnosis:** ARPU high enough that low-CAC viral/UGC channels have too much friction, but low enough that paid or sales channels can't earn their CAC. Companies in this zone need to move up or down (raise ARPU or drop it).

## Model-Market Fit — five archetypes

`ARPU × Total Customers In Market × % capture ≥ $100M`

- **Elephants** — 1,000 customers × $100k+ (enterprise SaaS, Palantir)
- **Moose** — 10,000 × $10k+ (mid-market SaaS, HubSpot mid-tier)
- **Rabbits** — 100,000 × $1k (SMB SaaS)
- **Mice** — 1M × $100 (prosumer, Netflix-adjacent)
- **Flies** — 10M × $10 (ad-supported consumer, Facebook)

**10% SaaS heuristic:** great SaaS companies capture more than 10% of their target market over time. Use 10% as sanity check on the capture rate.

## Growth Machine — the operating layer

Five principles for growth teams (from `brianbalfour.com/growth-machine` + `growth-culture`):
1. **Maximize Learning** — learning is the competitive advantage
2. **Seek Impact** — measure growth output distinctly, not vanity metrics ("Wheel of Meaningless Growth")
3. **Balance Art + Science** — creative + quantitative in the same team
4. **Embrace Change** — org must be nimble
5. **Growth Is Never Done** — kill the "we're done optimizing" mentality

Process: **Zoom Out (goals, opportunities, problem framing) → Zoom In (brainstorm, prioritize, test, learn, systemize)**. Quarterly / monthly / weekly cadence.

## AI-era updates (2024–2025)

From *Four Fits for the AI Era* essay:
- **Speed of change collapsed.** The original framework held 10 years; AI now forces re-checks monthly, not yearly.
- **PMF can die overnight.** Chegg lost 87% of value in 9 months when ChatGPT ate its value prop. Old world: PMF eroded gradually.
- **Product-Channel Fit disrupted by ChatGPT-as-discovery.** SEO / paid ads face structural threat as users start research inside LLMs.
- **Channel-Model Fit stressed by AI unit economics.** Freemium models break when AI features carry per-inference cost. Balfour: *"Freemium products will have difficulty introducing meaningful AI features"* without exploding infrastructure expense.
- **Model-Market Fit shifts.** New markets emerge (no-code AI), others contract or vanish (homework help, boilerplate legal, level-1 support).

Balfour did NOT introduce new fits — he reframed the four.

## Universal Growth Loop (meta)

Different essay, same word: Balfour's *Universal Growth Loop* describes the company-level flywheel of `product growth → attracts capital and talent → resources solve new problems → drives next cycle of growth`. Distinct from product-level growth loops. Failure mode: `growth stalls → resources misallocated to non-growth → compounding destruction`.

## Framework relationships

- **vs. Sean Ellis growth hacking** — built on Ellis's PMF definition; explicitly moves past "growth hacking" as a bag of tricks. Balfour: growth is a *system* between acquisition, retention, monetization.
- **vs. AARRR / Pirate Metrics (McClure)** — critiques funnel-only thinking. AARRR is a funnel; Balfour argues loops replace funnels for compounding businesses.
- **vs. North Star Framework (Amplitude / John Cutler)** — compatible. Balfour has a whole essay *Don't Let Your North Star Metric Deceive You* warning that a wrong North Star breaks the growth model.
- **vs. Casey Winters** — peer, co-author of Growth Loops post.
- **vs. Playing to Win (Martin)** — Four Fits sits one layer *down* from PtW. Martin decides where to play + how to win; Balfour tells you whether that combo can produce $100M with real unit economics. Complementary.
- **vs. ODI (Ulwick), JTBD (Christensen/Moesta/Kalbach)** — sit inside Product-Market Fit. Balfour uses them as inputs; Four Fits tests the chain end-to-end.
- **vs. Amazon Flywheel / Jim Collins Flywheel** — same family of "compounding loop" thinking, less methodological.

## Heuristics / gotchas Balfour is explicit about

- **Don't decouple Product from Channel.** Products designed without channel mechanics in mind can't retrofit them later.
- **Diagnose which fit is broken.** Growth stalling ≠ generic growth problem. Almost always one specific fit has broken. Name the fit before naming the fix.
- **The Danger Zone is real.** ARPU $100-$1k SaaS is where most companies die. Force yourself up-market or down-market.
- **Don't copy playbooks from companies with different Fits.** Airbnb's playbook works for Airbnb. If your ARPU/TAM/channel shape differs, the playbook is noise.
- **A funnel dressed as a loop is still a funnel.** Test: does output feed back to input?
- **Hire growth team AFTER PMF.** Growth team pre-PMF wastes calories.
- **"Growth" is not "marketing".** It's a cross-functional practice with product embedded.
- **North Star metric can lie.** If your NSM optimizes only one leg of AAR (acquisition, activation, retention), you'll misallocate.
