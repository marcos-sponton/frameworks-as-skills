## 2. THE CYNEFIN FRAMEWORK — DETAILED

### Origin & naming history
- **1999**: Snowden begins developing at IBM Global Services for intellectual capital management
- **2002**: IBM Cynefin Centre for Organizational Complexity founded (Snowden director)
- **2003**: Kurtz & Snowden, "The new dynamics of strategy," *IBM Systems Journal* — domains called **Known / Knowable / Complex / Chaotic**
- **2004**: Cynefin Centre spun out from IBM → Cognitive Edge
- **2007**: Snowden & Boone, "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making," *HBR* — domains become **Simple / Complicated / Complex / Chaotic** + central **Disorder**. Wins Academy of Management outstanding practitioner award
- **2014**: "Simple" → **"Obvious"**
- **2015-onward**: "Obvious" → **"Clear"**; "Disorder" → **"Confusion"**
- **2017**: Liminality introduced (transitional zones between domains)
- **2019-2020**: **Aporetic Turn** — Confused domain split into **Aporetic** (authentic disorder, "we know what we don't know") and **Confused** (inauthentic disorder, unaware confusion)

Source: [Wikipedia Cynefin framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynefin_framework), [Cynefin.io wiki](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains)

### The 5 domains (current form)

| Domain | Constraint | Cause-Effect | Decision Model | Practice | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear** (ex-Obvious, ex-Simple) | Rigid/fixed | Self-evident | Sense–Categorize–Respond | **Best practice** (only legitimate use) | Hand luggage rules |
| **Complicated** | Governing | Discoverable via analysis | Sense–Analyze–Respond | Good practice | Bridge engineering |
| **Complex** | Enabling | Coherent only in hindsight | **Probe–Sense–Respond** | Exaptive (radical repurposing) | Children's party, raising a kid |
| **Chaotic** | Absent | None | Act–Sense–Respond | Novel practice | Crisis, carnival |
| **Confusion** (central) | Absent | Unknown | — | Aporetic if aware | Not knowing which domain you're in |

Source: [Cynefin.io Domains wiki](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains)

### The Cliff (Clear ↔ Chaotic boundary)
Direct quote from Cynefin.io: *"The boundary between Clear and Chaotic is a catastrophic fold, or cliff, a collapse where the liminality in Clear is not visible and it is all too easy to walk blindly off the cliff through excessive confidence in the applicability of rigid constraints."* Over-reliance on best practice creates brittle systems that collapse into chaos when context shifts.
Source: [cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains)

### Liminal zones (2017 addition)
Green transitional bands between adjacent domains — Complex↔Complicated (the "CO-CO" band where Agile/Scrum operate — making the complex complicated) and Complex↔Chaotic. Not full domains; transitional spaces where a system has begun to shift but not stabilized.

### The Aporetic turn (2019-2020)
Confusion split into two:
- **Aporetic** (authentic): "at a loss" — you know you don't know; **suspension of action is legitimate**. Deliberate creation of paradox to enable different thinking.
- **Confused** (inauthentic): you don't realize you don't know. Often the result of biases and entrained patterns. Dangerous.

**Exits from aporetic (5 moves per cynefin.io):**
1. → Complex: run multiple competing hypotheses
2. → Complicated: bring in experts, commission research
3. → Complex-Chaotic liminal: MassSense with cognitively diverse groups
4. → Complicated via different expertise: introduce competing expert paradigms
5. → Clear (high risk — usually creates confusion; go via Complicated instead)

Source: [cynefin.io/wiki/Aporetic_Turn](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Aporetic_Turn)

### Constraints — Snowden's central refinement
Three main types (in Cynefin), six subtypes (in Estuarine):
- **Rigid/Fixed** — walls, fences. Predictable, enforceable, brittle. Governs Clear domain.
- **Governing** — rules/policies. Robust: survive by getting stronger, fail catastrophically. Governs Complicated.
- **Enabling** — bound the system while allowing emergence. Endoskeleton metaphor: allows variation around a coherent centre. Governs Complex.

Snowden's endoskeleton/exoskeleton metaphor: *"The external constraint of an insect's skeleton bounds its nature [governing], while the endoskeleton of a mammal allows for significant variation around a coherence centre [enabling]."*
Source: [cynefin.io Constraints](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Constraints)

### Anticipatory triggers
Not the same as prediction. AI/pattern-detection triggers a human to heightened alert when statistical conditions suggest something *may* happen. Developed for DARPA counterterrorism; now used in elder-care abuse detection. Distinguishes **Gaussian** (bell-curve, ordered, predictable) vs **Pareto** (power-law, complex, unpredictable). You can't predict Pareto events but you can prime humans to spot them.
Source: [Jim Rutt Show EP184 transcript](https://jimruttshow.blubrry.net/the-jim-rutt-show-transcripts/transcript-of-ep184-dave-snowden-on-managing-complexity-in-times-of-crisis/)

### The critique of "best practices"
Best practice is legitimate **only in Clear**. Applied to Complicated it becomes brittle; applied to Complex it destroys emergence. *"Failure repeats but success rarely does"* — because success is context-dependent and initial conditions never repeat. Case studies teach retrospective coherence, not causal knowledge. *"Especially when things become highly uncertain, case studies are the last thing you need to rely on."*

## 3. POST-2007-HBR EVOLUTION

**SenseMaker** (Snowden's software, originated ~2000s). Distributed ethnography: participants tell micro-narratives, then self-signify them along multi-dimensional frameworks the researcher designs. Combines qualitative depth with quantitative pattern-detection. Works with the Complex domain's need to detect emergent narrative patterns without imposing categories.
Source: [Sitra on SenseMaker](https://www.sitra.fi/en/articles/sensemaker-tool-decision-making-new-kind-world/)

**Estuarine Framework / Estuarine Mapping** (formalized 2023-2024, refined at St David's Day 2024). Snowden's "third major framework" after Cynefin and SenseMaker. Called by one academic *"the complexity equivalent of Porter's Five Forces."*

- **Metaphor**: estuary where river meets sea — always in movement, tides come and go, never stable
- **Axes**: Energy cost of change (Y) × Time to change (X)
- **Actants** (three types): Constraints, Constructors (produce replicable outcomes), Actors (act with intention)
- **6 constraint sub-types**: Rigid/Fixed, Elastic/Flexible, Tethers, Permeable, Phase Shift, Dark constraints
- **Two critical borders**:
  - **Counterfactual border** (top-right): what's practically unchangeable
  - **Volatile border** (bottom-left, formerly "vulnerable"; renamed 2024): where change is too easy, warning of volatility
- **The operational space is between the two borders**
- **7-step process**: pre-process → identify actants → map on grid → draw counterfactual → identify volatile → design vector/signal/communication actions → set direction & portfolio
- **Guiding line**: *"Find out where you are and what is possible before you leap into the whole vision and goals thing."*

Snowden: *"[Cynefin] is a decision support framework that recognises complexity theory; Estuarine is a full-on complexity framework."*
Source: [thecynefin.co estuarine-mapping](https://thecynefin.co/estuarine-mapping/), [cynefin.io wiki Estuarine](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Estuarine_framework)

**EU Field Guide** — practical field guide developed with the European Commission JRC for managing complex crises, source of the constraint mapping methodology that became Estuarine.

## 5. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

### How to identify which domain you're in
1. **Start in Confusion** — assume you don't know which domain until you've contextualized. Premature categorization is the #1 error.
2. **Look at cause-effect visibility**:
   - Self-evident → Clear
   - Discoverable with expertise → Complicated
   - Only visible in hindsight → Complex
   - Not visible at all → Chaotic
3. **Look at constraints in play** — what type of constraint is actually operating? Rigid → Clear/Complicated; Enabling → Complex; Absent → Chaotic
4. **Test with a small probe** — if the intervention produces predictable outcomes, you're in the ordered domains; if it produces surprises, Complex
5. **Watch for aporia** — genuine paradox is a signal you're in the aporetic liminal, not that you should force a decision

### Anti-patterns (Snowden's list of ways people get Cynefin wrong)

- **Categorization mistake**: Using Cynefin as a 2×2 to sort projects. It's a sense-making framework, not a categorization tool. You contextualize into it, not sort into it.
- **Complicated methods in Complex domain**: Root-cause analysis, expert consultation, roadmap. Wrong tool.
- **Best-practice extraction from a Complex success**: The success is context-bound; extraction destroys it.
- **Treating "Disorder/Confusion" as a rejection**: It's a legitimate starting point.
- **Skipping the aporetic**: Rushing to a domain when suspension is the right move.
- **The single pilot**: In Complex, you need multiple parallel probes, not one big bet.
- **Fail-safe design in Complex**: You can't build for no failure; build for cheap failure.
- **Mistaking Clear for Complicated**: If you're using experts to solve something obvious, waste. If you're using best practice on something that needs experts, brittle.
- **Walking off the cliff**: Over-relying on rigid constraints in Clear → catastrophic collapse into Chaotic

### Do's in the Complex domain
- **Multiple parallel safe-to-fail probes** — not sequential, not one-at-a-time
- **Design probes for observability**, not measurement — you want to see amplify/dampen patterns
- **Amplify what works, dampen what doesn't** — no "success/failure" verdict; ongoing modulation
- **Coherent heterogeneity** — diverse enough to generate variation, coherent enough to converge
- **Cognitive diversity in decisions** — different disciplines, not just different demographics
- **Distributed (not delegated) decision-making** — push decisions to where the information is
- **Micro-narratives via SenseMaker** — patterns from what people actually experience, not what they say they experience
- **Constant iteration between Complex and Complicated** — stabilize what's stable; leave the rest fluid

### Cynefin dynamics — how situations move
- **Stable pattern**: Constant iteration Complex ↔ Complicated, with material moving to Clear when stable and low-risk
- **The fall into Chaotic**: Usually via the cliff from Clear — rigid constraints break catastrophically. Rare that Complex flows into Chaotic; more common that Clear collapses.
- **Exit from Chaos**: Deliberate move via the aporetic liminal — not straight back to order. Chaos → temporary constraint imposition → aporetic → complex/complicated.
- **Phase shifts require energy** — like latent heat. Domain transitions aren't smooth; they're transitions of state.

## 7. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

### Wardley Mapping (peer, compatible)
Snowden and Simon Wardley did a **joint 2-day masterclass in December 2018** exploring synergies. Both are respected peers. Consensus:
- **Cynefin** = decoding problem domains, deciding approach
- **Wardley** = mapping components along evolution axis (genesis → custom → product → commodity), understanding value chains
- **Complementary, not substitutable.** Simon Wardley explicitly: *"You can't simply transport Cynefin terms onto the evolutionary axis of a map."* Don't map "complex" to "custom."
- Wardley Maps most useful when problem is Complicated or Complex
- Sources: [Snowden on the joint masterclass](https://thecynefin.co/library/cynefin-and-wardley-maps-recordings-from-a-masterclass-exploration/), [Wardley on the combination](https://swardley.medium.com/wardley-maps-and-cynefin-d0c7a2b960e8)

### Agile / SAFe (hostile)
Original Agile (2001 Manifesto) had Complex-domain awareness. SAFe drags it into Complicated with false certainty. See §4 for direct quotes.

### Design Thinking (dismissive)
Once had substance, now commoditized brand-methodology. Applies Complicated (categorization, personas, templates) to Complex problems.

### Systems Thinking (rejected as flawed)
YouTube: "Systems Thinking is Flawed and Transitionary" — argues systems thinking is too engineering-mechanical, treats organizations as machines, ignores complex adaptive dynamics.

### Lean Startup (partial critique)
MVP as one-pilot approach is a Complex-domain error (needs multiple parallel probes, not sequential single bets). "Build-Measure-Learn" is right in principle but degraded in practice into a template.

### Learning Organization (Senge) — rejected
Imposes homogeneity of values/goals/objectives — destroys the coherent heterogeneity resilience requires.

### Nonaka SECI / tacit-explicit conversion — rejected
Codification loses context; knowledge shouldn't always be codified. Snowden's 3rd-gen KM explicitly counters SECI.

### Six Sigma / BPR / Appreciative Inquiry / Myers-Briggs — dismissed as fads
*"lack underlying theory, serving consultant interests rather than organizational needs."*
