## 2. THE METHOD IN DETAIL

### 2.1 The 5-Part Strategic Narrative (2016 formulation, canonical)

Source of record: [The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0) (Sep 2016).

1. **Name a Big, Relevant Change in the World.** Don't open with your company. Open with an undeniable shift creating stakes and urgency for the buyer. *"What attracts human attention is change"* — Raskin cites Robert McKee.
2. **Show There Will Be Winners and Losers.** Adapting yields survival/upside; ignoring yields extinction. Combats loss aversion. (Zuora: Fortune 500 "mass extinction" among non-adapters.)
3. **Tease the Promised Land.** A concrete, aspirational, difficult-to-reach future state — described *before* introducing product. The Promised Land is defined by criteria the buyer wants to hit, not by what your product does.
4. **Introduce Features as "Magic Gifts" for Overcoming Obstacles.** Only after the Promised Land is on the table. Features are the tools the buyer needs to make the crossing.
5. **Present Evidence You Can Make the Story Real.** Customer stories showing prospects like the buyer already reaching the Promised Land.

### 2.2 The 2023 refinement (Lenny's Podcast formulation)

Source: [Lenny's Podcast, May 2023](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-power-of-strategic-narrative) (timestamps 19:27–29:18).

Same 5-part structure, re-labeled with more strategic language:
1. **Name the Old Game** (19:27)
2. **Name the Stakes** (20:59)
3. **Name the Objective** (23:29) — the buyer's mission, not your company's
4. **Name the Obstacles** (25:17)
5. **Overcome the Obstacles** (26:35) — where product enters

Relabeling matters: it foregrounds the fact that the framework is about the buyer's world (game/stakes/objective/obstacles) and the product only enters at step 5.

### 2.3 The Zuora canonical example

- Old game: buying software as a one-time purchase; product-centric business.
- Change: *"subscription economy"* — buyers now prefer recurring relationships.
- Winners/losers: Fortune 500 extinction chart vs. subscription-native winners (Netflix, Salesforce) + rejuvenated incumbents.
- Promised Land: *"Turn customers into subscribers"* — spelled out with concrete criteria (recurring-revenue infrastructure, customer records built around subscribers, etc.).
- Magic gifts: features like customer record model, order-to-revenue, subscription analytics — reframed as capabilities you need to win the new game.
- Evidence: customer proof of orgs that made the transition.
- Multi-channel: Tien Tzuo told the same story on TV (Mad Money w/ Cramer), in the deck, on the website, and eventually as a book (*Subscribed*). Referenced in [This Is How a CEO Tells a Great Story](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/this-is-how-a-ceo-tells-a-great-story-5d158620cec9).

### 2.4 Narrative vs. positioning vs. messaging (Raskin's operating distinctions)

- **Positioning** = internal/invisible research artifact that guides the narrative. Definitional baggage; not what he sells.
- **Messaging** = downstream copy assets (taglines, benefit statements, landing pages).
- **Strategic narrative** = the single top-level story that is the strategy. It is CEO-owned, appears as the sales deck's spine, and *"reveals internal misalignment — when teams can't agree on the story, they lack agreement on strategy itself"* (paraphrased from Lenny episode + [foundercoho interview](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys)).
- His stronger claim: *"this story really is the strategy"* — the narrative is not a wrapper; it exposes whether strategy exists.

### 2.5 How the narrative shows up across the org

Source: [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb) + Lenny episode.

- **Sales deck** — the *core artifact*. Raskin's "true north." Anchors everything.
- **Website & content** — same change/stakes/promised-land story, in landing-page voice.
- **CEO all-hands / external talks** — CEO models the narrative in every appearance (Tzuo's TV hits are the canonical example).
- **Investor deck** — same 5 acts; product introduced late.
- **Recruiting** — Promised Land is the org's North Star for hires.
- **Product** — the roadmap gets rationalized as "capabilities the buyer needs to win the new game."
- **Sales enablement** — sales reps use the "change" slide as a discovery-opener: *"How is this shift playing out for you?"* — generates "gold" customer language (SpotMe example).

### 2.6 Post-2016 refinements (in Raskin's own timeline)

- **"Old Game / New Game" > "Old Way / New Way"** ([LinkedIn Nov 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_sometimes-people-boil-down-my-strategic-narrative-activity-7131327379045576704-YwbE)): *"'Old way/new way' encourages you to talk primarily about *product*. 'Old game/new game'…encourages you to build the narrative around a shift taking place in the *buyer's head*."*
- **"Name the Enemy" clarified** ([LinkedIn Oct 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_one-part-of-my-strategic-narrative-framework-activity-7126247639502782464-LtXQ)): *"your buyer's enemy is not competitors. It's not even a problem the buyer faces, at least in the traditional sense. Rather…your buyer's enemy is an *old mindset* that has become a road to ruin."*
- **"Goodbye Product. Hello Movement."** (2023 essay + Pavilion talk): *"'Goodbye product' does not mean your product is unimportant. It does not mean you don't talk about features, speeds and feeds. It means don't start there."*
- **"Arrogant Doctor" → "Humble Awakener"** (via [foundercoho interview](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys)): Raskin's anti-persona is the arrogant doctor ("you have a problem, I have the cure, here's why mine is better"); his prescribed persona is the *humble awakener* — someone who names the change that already happened in the buyer's world.
- **Setup is not for education, it's for emotional engagement** ([Is the Greatest Sales Deck Great for Savvy Prospects?](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/is-the-greatest-sales-deck-great-for-savvy-prospects-d5de48fb2f94)): *"your primary goal in setting context is not educating prospects, but engaging them emotionally."*

## 3. VOICE & TONE

### 3.1 Register

- Warm, first-person, story-obsessed. Every essay opens with a scene — a CEO, a deck moment, a call.
- Clear, teacher-y prose. Short paragraphs. Bolded pivots. Uses italics for emphasis (*old mindset*, *game*, *don't start there*).
- Rarely academic. Cites McKee (screenwriting), Ben Horowitz, Marc Benioff — practitioners, not theorists.
- Deferential to the CEO he's writing about; the essay is usually *their* win, structurally.
- On LinkedIn: same voice, tighter. Often begins with a client anecdote → misconception → clarification → generalized principle.

### 3.2 Recurring rhetorical moves

- **Case-first exposition.** He almost never opens with a definition. He opens with a company that reframed, tell what they did, then extract the principle.
- **The reveal template.** "X had been pitching Y. Then they made this move. Here's what happened."
- **Named enemies.** He gives concepts proper nouns: the *Arrogant Doctor*, the *Humble Awakener*, the *Chest Beater*, the *Old Game*, the *Promised Land*, the *Magic Gifts*, the *Change in the World*.
- **Anti-definition.** He often defines by negation: "*Strategic narrative is not about 'using storytelling'…*" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_strategic-narrative-is-not-about-using-storytelling-activity-7044706892291469313-gYt6)).
- **Reframe as headline.** His post titles are the reframe: "Goodbye Product. Hello Movement." / "Goodbye Category. Hello Movement." / "To Create a New Category, Name the New Game."

### 3.3 Signature vocabulary (mandatory glossary for the skill)

- **Strategic Narrative** — the top-level story.
- **The Change in the World / The Shift** — step 1.
- **Winners and Losers** — step 2 pattern.
- **The Promised Land** — step 3 target state; buyer-defined, not product-defined.
- **Magic Gifts** — features reframed as tools for crossing.
- **Old Game / New Game** — preferred phrasing for the shift (not old way / new way).
- **Name the Enemy** — but the enemy = old mindset, not competitors.
- **Movement** — not category, not product. The organizing frame.
- **The Arrogant Doctor** — the anti-pattern persona.
- **The Humble Awakener** — the prescribed persona.
- **The Chest Beater** — self-promoting CEO pitch style he rejects.
- **Life or death stakes** — the emotional register the stakes should hit.
- **Disputable purpose** — the test that separates a movement from a slogan (from the Gong "Goodbye opinions, hello reality" post cluster).
- **Air cover** — org-wide reinforcement of the narrative so sales isn't alone.

### 3.4 Words / framings he pushes back on

- *"We help X do Y"* — features-first, no shift.
- *"We are the leader in [category]"* — chest-beater, no stakes.
- *"Our value prop is…"* — treats value prop as narrative; they're not the same thing.
- *"Old way / new way"* — encourages product-comparison thinking.
- Positioning the competitor as the enemy — the enemy is the old mindset the competitor represents.
- Long messaging documents — his line: *"nobody uses them."*
- "Storytelling" as decoration on claims of superiority — the point isn't to make your claim sound nicer, it's to change what the buyer thinks the story is about.

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO'S / DON'TS / GOTCHAS

### 4.1 Signs your company doesn't have a strategic narrative

Composite from Lenny episode + [foundercoho](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys) + [nektar summary](https://nektar.ai/the-strategic-narrative-playbook-lessons-on-how-stories-align-companies-and-win-markets/):

- Sales, marketing, product each describe the company differently.
- The pitch opens with company/logos/investors, not with a change in the buyer's world.
- Prospects say "cool tool" but don't feel urgency.
- Reps rely on their own custom decks.
- The CEO can't tell the story in <2 minutes.
- Every new hire re-writes the pitch.
- Long messaging doc exists; nobody uses it.
- The word "problem" appears more than the word "world."

### 4.2 Anti-patterns in construction

- Leading with logos, investors, or "we are the leader in…"
- Treating "old way / new way" as the frame → product comparison, not mindset shift.
- Naming a competitor as the enemy → guarantees you look petty and same-shaped.
- Confusing setup with education (dumping industry stats) instead of emotional stakes.
- Making the Promised Land = your product's feature set. It must be defined by the buyer's world, not yours.
- Introducing features before the Promised Land.
- Delegating narrative to marketing or an agency. *"The CEO has to be the decider… this story really is the strategy."*
- Handing sales a new deck without involving sales leadership during creation → adoption fails (Zaius/Angoff example: rating went from 2 to 4.5 only after inclusion).
- "Bolting" story onto an existing feature pitch. The whole deck has to be one story.
- Being complete instead of provocative. *"The most powerful narratives are always 'wrong,' in that they've sacrificed completeness for brevity."* ([dylan.tweney interview](https://dylan.tweney.com/the-art-of-strategic-narrative-a-conversation-with-andy-raskin-2/))

### 4.3 Common misapplications

- Thinking messaging = narrative (writing better copy without changing the frame).
- Thinking positioning = narrative (research artifact instead of story).
- Thinking storytelling = narrative (adding anecdotes to a claim of superiority).
- Copying Zuora's deck literally, slide-for-slide, instead of using it as a template for buyer-language discovery. *"Harvest language about what's at stake directly from customers."*
- Naming a fake change nobody feels — the change must be one the buyer already senses in their bones.

### 4.4 Tests for whether the narrative is working

- Prospects respond with "*How quickly can we get started?*" (from [How Great Sales Pitches Drive Urgency](https://medium.com/the-mission/how-great-sales-pitches-drive-urgency-e35aa1069de3)).
- The rep says the "change" slide and the prospect voluntarily opens up about their world (SpotMe pattern).
- Champions can pitch internally — the deck gives them a story they can retell to their CEO.
- Sales, product, CS all describe the company the same way in <30 seconds.
- Investors, hires, and customers all recognize the same "movement."
- Frame is *disputable* — a reasonable person could disagree with it. If nobody would disagree, it's a slogan.
- Narrative refreshes every 18–36 months → shows it's responsive to the market, not stale.

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

**vs. April Dunford (Obviously Awesome / Sales Pitch)**
- Dunford: *positioning* is the input — pick the market you win in, define competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value.
- Raskin: *narrative* is the output — the top-level story the org tells; positioning is upstream and invisible.
- They're compatible. Dunford's sales-pitch structure (insight → alternatives → perfect world → introducing us → proof) is structurally near-identical to Raskin's 5-part. Dunford is more analytical; Raskin is more emotional/movement-oriented.

**vs. JTBD (Christensen / Ulwick / Moesta)**
- JTBD provides the raw material for step 1 (change in the world) and step 3 (Promised Land) — the Job the customer is trying to get done *is* the Promised Land, translated. Switch interviews (Moesta) surface the forces of change that the "old game" narrative externalizes.
- Raskin doesn't cite JTBD often, but the framework consumes JTBD outputs cleanly.

**vs. Donald Miller StoryBrand**
- Miller: hero (customer) has a problem, meets a guide (you), gets a plan, is called to action, avoids failure, achieves success. Explicit Hero's Journey mapping.
- Raskin: rejects making the *product* the hero, agrees the buyer is the hero. But Raskin's "enemy" is a mindset shift, not a personal pain point, which makes his frame more strategic and less consumer-marketing. StoryBrand is tactical copy; Raskin is org-level narrative.
- Overlap: both use guide/hero/promised-land beats. Divergence: Raskin's "change in the world" is macro; Miller's problem is micro.

**vs. Hero's Journey (Campbell) / Save the Cat (Snyder)**
- Raskin explicitly parallels his framework to Hero's Journey (Lenny 26:57) but flattens it into 5 pragmatic acts. The Promised Land = Campbell's "reward"; Magic Gifts = the mentor's gift/talisman.

**vs. Marty Cagan (product narrative / vision)**
- Cagan's product vision doc = a written narrative of the future the product enables, 3–10 years out.
- Same DNA as Raskin's Promised Land, but Cagan is product-org-facing (aligns PMs, engineers), Raskin is GTM-facing (aligns sales, marketing, investors). A team using both would have Cagan's vision *inside* Raskin's narrative.

**vs. Richard Rumelt (Good Strategy / Bad Strategy, The Crux)**
- Rumelt's kernel: diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action.
- Raskin's narrative *externalizes* Rumelt's diagnosis: "Name the change in the world" = the diagnosis, phrased as a story the buyer can hear. The Promised Land ≈ the guiding policy's ambition. The Magic Gifts ≈ coherent actions the buyer takes.
- Rumelt is diagnosis-first; Raskin is diagnosis-first-narrated. They stack: use Rumelt to find the real crux, use Raskin to render it as a movement.

**vs. Roger Martin (Playing to Win cascade)**
- Martin's "how to win" ≈ Raskin's Promised Land + Magic Gifts.
- Martin's cascade is internal strategy; Raskin's narrative is the external face of that strategy. When the internal cascade is clear, the external narrative is easy. Raskin's diagnostic use — "we can't tell one story" — is exactly Martin's failure mode: the cascade isn't coherent.

**vs. Simon Sinek (Start With Why)**
- Sinek: golden circle (why → how → what), origin/purpose focused.
- Raskin: explicitly buyer-world focused. Sinek's "why" is company-internal; Raskin's "change" is buyer-external. Raskin would call Sinek's Why-first pitch a version of the *chest beater* if it centers on the company.

**vs. Ben Horowitz "The company story is the company strategy"**
- This is the axiom Raskin puts on his homepage. His entire practice is the operationalization of that Horowitz line.
