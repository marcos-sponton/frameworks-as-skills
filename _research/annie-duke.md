## 2. THE THREE BOOKS AS ONE SYSTEM

### 2a. Thinking in Bets (2018) — the mental model
**Thesis**: decisions are bets on uncertain futures under incomplete information. Two forces determine outcomes: **decision quality + luck**. The failure mode is **"resulting"** — collapsing the two.

Key moves:
- Reframe every choice as a bet ("Wanna bet?" as a mental prompt to force calibration).
- **Belief formation problem**: humans hear → believe → rarely vet. *"We form beliefs without vetting most of them, and maintain them even after receiving clear, corrective information."* [Duke, Thinking in Bets, cited in https://calvinrosser.com/notes/thinking-in-bets-annie-duke/]
- **Motivated reasoning + numeracy inversion**: *"It turns out the better you are with numbers, the better you are at spinning those numbers to conform to your beliefs."* [Same source]
- **Long-game framing**: *"Life, like poker, is one long game, and there are going to be a lot of losses, even after making the best possible bets."* [Same source]
- **Truthseeking pod**: form a small group (poker "buddies") that will punish sloppy thinking, not sloppy outcomes.
- **Mental time travel** as debias — 10-10-10 (how will I feel in 10 min / 10 mo / 10 yr) — introduced here, expanded in How to Decide.

### 2b. How to Decide (2020) — the toolkit
**Thesis**: decision quality is teachable via explicit process. The 6-step framework:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the reasonable set of possible outcomes |
| 2 | Identify your preferences over outcomes (payoffs) |
| 3 | Estimate likelihood of each |
| 4 | Assess relative likelihood of liked vs disliked outcomes for a given option |
| 5 | Repeat 1–4 for other options |
| 6 | Compare options |

[Source: https://wisdomfromexperts.com/to-make-a-good-decision-you-must-use-a-decision-tree-with-probabilities/ summarizing How to Decide]

Speed-triage tools (decision hygiene):
- **Happiness test**: *"Will my happiness, a week from now, depend on this decision?"* If no → decide fast. [Summary source: https://andivablog.wordpress.com/2021/04/13/book-review-how-to-decide-by-annie-duke/]
- **10-10-10 rule**: 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years.
- **Only-option test**: if it were the only option, would you be happy? If yes to multiple options → flip a coin.
- **Two-way door test**: Bezos-style reversibility check.
- **"When a decision is really hard, it means it's easy… you should just be flipping a coin."** [Behavioral Scientist Q&A https://behavioralscientist.org/practical-tools-for-better-decisions-a-qa-with-annie-duke-on-how-to-decide/]
- **Resulting** (defined here in book): *"Resulting is when we use the quality of an outcome to judge the quality of a decision."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/how-to-decide-annie-duke]
- **"You can't know if you made a good decision until you know the things you couldn't know at the time you made it."** [Same]

### 2c. Quit (2022) — the psychology of stopping
**Thesis**: grit is oversold; quitting on time is a decision skill. The tools:
- **Kill criteria = state + date**: pre-commit to *"if I'm not in [state] by [date], I quit."* [Duke, Quit, cited in https://wisdomfromexperts.com/kill-criteria/]
- **Monkeys and pedestals** (borrowed from Astro Teller, X): tackle the *monkey* (the hard, unknowable problem) first. Pedestal-building is fake progress. Duke: *"There is no point building the pedestal if you can't train the monkey. In other words, you ought to tackle the hardest part of the problem first."* [https://behavioralscientist.org/annie-duke-quit-mental-models-to-help-you-cut-your-losses/]
- **The core insight**: *"Quitting on time usually feels like quitting too early."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/quit-annie-duke]
- **Sunk cost as accumulation**: *"The sunk cost effect turns your life into a Katamari: the more you roll forward, the more debris you accumulate, and the harder it is to stop."* [Same]
- **Identity is the hardest quit**: *"When it comes to quitting, the hardest thing to quit is who you are."* [Same]
- **Ownership goes mental**: *"We own what we've bought and what we've thought."* [Same]
- **Asymmetric error**: *"When we're in the gains, we tend to quit too early. When we're in the losses, we tend to stick too long."* [Same]
- **Grit reframe**: *"Success does not lie in sticking to things; it lies in picking the right thing to stick to and quitting the rest."* [Same]
- **Waste is prospective, not retrospective**: *"Waste is a forward-looking problem: the real waste is spending more time and money on something that's no longer worth it."* [Same]

### 2d. How the three cohere
- Thinking in Bets = **the frame** (decisions are bets under uncertainty; separate DQ from OQ).
- How to Decide = **the toolkit** (structured 6-step, hygiene tests, decision journal).
- Quit = **the counter-force** (once you're committed, kill criteria + monkeys-first + quitting coach protect against sunk cost, endowment, identity, status quo).
- **One integrated system**: name what you're betting → structure the decision → track it → set kill criteria in advance → revisit dispassionately.

## 3. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

**Resulting** (anti-pattern #1). Judging decision quality by outcome. Poster child: Pete Carroll Super Bowl XLIX pass call from the 1-yard line — outcome disastrous, decision defensible (interception rate ~1–2% in that spot). Duke: *"just because the result was a bad outcome did not make the decision itself bad."* [https://thepastorsworkshop.com/sermon-illustrations/annie-duke-pete-carrolls-call-and-the-error-of-id_2178]

**Calibration**. Never state beliefs at 100%. Force a percentage ("I'm 62% sure"). *"Certainty is an illusion. You're always deciding under conditions of uncertainty."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/how-to-decide-annie-duke] Practice: give upper/lower bounds; most people are overconfident on width.

**Premortem** (borrowed from Gary Klein). *"A pre-mortem is imagining that you've failed and asking why."* [Same] Duke's twist: pair the premortem with **precommitment** — the signal you'd see and the action you'd take when you see it.

**Kill criteria — state + date**. Format: *"If I am (or am not) in a particular state at a particular date, then I have to quit."* Canonical example: Everest turnaround time (1–2 pm), Rob Hall's fatal 1996 climb where he violated his own kill criterion. [https://behavioralscientist.org/annie-duke-quit-mental-models-to-help-you-cut-your-losses/]

**Backcasting**. Imagine a *successful* future state, then trace back the steps that got you there. Pair with premortem for symmetric planning. Backcasting motivates the goal; premortem stress-tests the path.

**Decision journal**. Write down: what you decided, why, what alternatives you considered, your confidence, what you'd need to see to change your mind. Purpose: fight hindsight bias. *"Hindsight bias is the great enemy of learning from experience."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/how-to-decide-annie-duke]

**Decision hygiene (group)**. Order matters:
1. **Discover** — collect opinions independently *in writing* before any discussion.
2. **Discuss** — surface disagreements; use "nevertheless" to hold both.
3. **Decide** — re-collect independently after discussion.
This is the **nominal group technique** + First Round Capital's investment protocol. [https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/making-better-decisions-annie-duke]

**Feedback loop shortening**. *"Long feedback loops are a choice."* Use correlated intermediate signals (e.g., time-to-Series-A in VC) instead of waiting for the final result. [Lenny's Podcast, same source]

**Quitting coach**. Kahneman's line, which Duke uses constantly: *"What everybody needs is a friend who really loves them but doesn't care much about hurt feelings in the moment."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/quit-annie-duke] Founder-world equivalent: Ron Conway.

**Cognitive biases she hammers**:
- Sunk cost, loss aversion (*"Losses hurt about twice as much as equivalent gains feel good."*), endowment effect, status quo bias, identity threat, confirmation bias, motivated reasoning, hindsight bias.

**Gotchas**:
- **Pedestal-building** looks like progress but delays the "no." Always attack the monkey first.
- **Goal-induced blindness** (Siobhan O'Keeffe London Marathon, broken fibula, mile 6, kept running): once a goal is set, people barrel through changed conditions.
- **"I'm not ready to decide yet"** = *"For now, I choose the status quo."* [Duke, Quit, cited in https://grahammann.net/book-notes/quit-annie-duke]

## 5. HOW SHE DISAGREES

**With the "trust your gut" narrative**: not by dismissing intuition, but by insisting intuition must be **disciplined**. Structured judgment doesn't replace intuition — it *trains* it. She'll grant Nadal's forehand runs on System 1; the trap is treating strategic business decisions like Nadal's forehand.

**With Kahneman (whom she reveres)**: she's the **translator**, not the challenger. She accepts System 1/2 wholesale, then extends: "System 2 isn't always superior — the deer-in-headlights driver overthinks and dies." She reframes probability estimation for lay users ("how promising do you think it is?" instead of point probabilities). [https://www.annieduke.com/deciding-fast-and-slow-with-daniel-kahneman/]

**With "grit" (Duckworth)**: not a rejection. *"Quitting and grit are two sides of the same decision."* [https://grahammann.net/book-notes/quit-annie-duke] Grit is good *conditional on the goal being worth it*. Unconditional grit produces Siobhan O'Keeffe running on a broken leg.

**With business media's post-hoc narratives**: she attacks "resulting" journalism (Pete Carroll, failed startups, etc.). She'll defend a decision that lost and criticize one that won when the process was sloppy.

**Style of disagreement**: rarely combative. She uses "I'd push back on that" or "Let's slow down there." She frequently gives the opposing view its strongest form before dismantling it. She says "I don't understand" instead of "you're wrong" — she recommends this move explicitly. [Lenny's Podcast]

**Stewart Butterfield / Slack (born from killing Glitch)**. Poster child for *successful quitting*. Quit Glitch — a $17M multiplayer game he'd built for years — to pivot on the internal chat tool. [https://www.readaccelerated.com/p/quit-a-framework-for-giving-up]

## Skill-authoring notes

- **Voice priors**: default to poker-anecdote-first, tool-second. Warm and funny; never professorial. Short sentences. She says "look" and "here's the thing" often. She contracts freely.
- **Do**: separate decision quality from outcome quality every time; force calibration percentages; write kill criteria as state+date; ask "will this matter in a week/month/year?"; recommend independent-before-discussion for group calls.
- **Don't**: say "trust your gut" without qualifying; celebrate outcomes without inspecting process; use "we couldn't have known"; treat sunk cost as a reason to continue; use unconditional grit as advice.
- **Signature move**: end on a small, testable action ("write down your kill criterion right now — state and date"). She *always* lands the plane on a concrete next step, not a homily.
- **Confidence marker**: I'd tag most of this dossier **high** for concepts and **moderate** for specific verbatim quotes — quotes came through summaries/notes rather than the primary books. Before shipping a skill that puts quotes in her mouth, verify at least a sample against print editions.
