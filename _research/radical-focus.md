## Framework fundamentals — how Wodtke's OKRs are distinct

Not Doerr's OKRs, not Grove's original Intel MBOs, though she traces lineage. Wodtke's specific bets:

### One Objective at a time — the "radical" in Radical Focus

Her central claim: focus itself is the competitive advantage, not the framework. From ["One Objective to Rule Them All"](https://cwodtke.medium.com/one-objective-to-rule-them-all-1058e973bfc5):

- Multi-OKR math: 5 company OKRs × 4 KRs each = 20 data points. Add 3 team OKRs per person (12) + individual sets = 32+ goals per person. "Every single person in the company knows what the company OKRs are in their heart. Which means they have to remember them."
- **Signature phrase:** *"Do less, better."*
- **Whiskey funnel analogy:** *"You don't pour whiskey through a leaky funnel into your flask and hope to get drunk."* — retention must precede growth.
- **Frodo/Samwise analogy:** *"We can't all be Frodo; some of us are Samwise."* — support roles are essential; not every team leads every objective.
- Sequence, don't parallel — retention → conversion → acquisition, not all at once.

### Team-level OKRs, not individual

- Cascading pathology: *"Cascading is you take your boss's Key Result and make it your Objective. I really hate this one."*
- Individual OKRs defeat the collaborative purpose and turn OKRs into performance reviews.
- From ["What I've Learned from 15 Years of Doing OKRs"](https://eleganthack.com/what-ive-learned-from-15-years-of-doing-okrs/): shift from cascading to **alignment** — set one high-level company/BU OKR, empower teams to write their own.
- *"The best OKRs don't come from the top — they're co-created."*

### The weekly cadence — Mondays and Fridays

The single most distinctive operational device in Radical Focus. Distinguishes goal-*achievers* from goal-*setters*.

**Monday Commitments** (the "foursquare" or four-quadrant format):
1. **Intention for the Week** — the 3-4 most important things this team will get done toward the Objective.
2. **Forecast for the Month** — what's coming up so teammates can help.
3. **Status toward OKRs** — confidence level (1-10) on each KR, with discussion of what's changed.
4. **Health Metrics** — the 2 things being protected while pushing.

**Friday Celebrations / Wins:**
- Bragging session across departments.
- Demo what you can, learn what others are doing, celebrate wins.
- *"The Friday meeting is for fun and celebration; tough discussions belong Monday."*

### Health Metrics — the anti-gaming guard

From ["What Sam Altman Just Taught Us About OKRs"](https://eleganthack.com/what-sam-altman-just-taught-us-about-okrs-without-meaning-to/) (Dec 2, 2025) — one of her clearest recent framings:

- OKRs are **pushed**. Health Metrics are **protected**.
- Health Metrics track survival: revenue, satisfaction, code stability, team wellbeing.
- Green / yellow / red status. Red triggers **code red** — pause OKR work, fix the health metric first.
- OpenAI case (2025): paused advertising / shopping agents / health assistant OKRs when model capability + reliability (the health metric) turned red.
- *"OKRs are stretch goals. But not everything in your business needs to be pushed. Some things need to be protected. Knowing the difference might save your company."*

### The 70% rule / 50-50 confidence

From ["The Art of the OKR"](https://eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr/):
- Set KRs with a **confidence level of 5 out of 10** at the start of the quarter — roughly 50/50 shot.
- *"If you are getting a funny little feeling in the pit of your stomach saying 'It would take a miracle to hit all three of these…' then you are probably setting them correctly."*
- Land at ~70% by end of quarter = right level of stretch. Landing at 100% = too easy. Landing at 20% = too hard.

### The OKR structure

**Objective** (the pull, the story):
- Qualitative and **inspirational** — "gets people jumping out of bed."
- Time-bound (a quarter usually).
- Actionable independently by the team.
- Use the team's own language ("pwn it," "kill it").
- Good: *"Pwn the direct-to-business coffee retail market in the south bay."*
- Good: *"Launch an Awesome MVP."*
- Bad: *"Sales up 30%"* (that's a KR, not an Objective).

**Key Results** (how we know we did it):
- Quantitative, hard-edged, ~3 per Objective.
- Balance competing dimensions (growth vs. quality, revenue vs. performance) — anti-gaming.
- Example for "Launch an Awesome MVP":
  - 40% of users return 2× within one week
  - Recommendation score of 8
  - 15% conversion

### OKRs vs KPIs — dashboard vs GPS

From ["KPIs Are Your Dashboard. OKRs Are Your GPS to Somewhere New"](https://eleganthack.com/kpis-are-your-dashboard-okrs-are-your-gps-to-somewhere-new/) (Nov 14, 2025):

- **KPIs = dashboard.** Steady-state, ongoing metrics. "How are we doing at the thing we do?"
- **OKRs = GPS.** Transformation. "We are here. We need to be there."
- Vague objectives like *"be a high-performing team"* are baseline states, not destinations — belong in health metrics, NOT OKRs.
- Test question for a candidate OKR: **"What change do you want to see this quarter?"** — not what to maintain perpetually.

### The Team Health Monitor

From *The Team That Managed Itself* (2019, with Martin Eriksson):
- Diagnostic instrument for team wellbeing that runs parallel to OKR progress tracking.
- Complements the health-metric guard: if the *team* is unhealthy (burnout, low trust, unclear roles), OKR performance is a leading lie.

### Refinements from "15 Years of OKRs" (May 2025)

- **Alignment > cascading.** Explicit reversal from the book. "Cascading creates bottlenecks."
- **Learning > scoring.** "Instead of getting stuck on a 0.7 or 0.8 score, we focus on what worked, what didn't, and what we'll do differently."
- **Not everyone needs OKRs.** Especially service teams or teams without autonomy. She used to say "everyone." Now she doesn't.
- **Cadence is critical.** *"Weekly check-ins, Friday celebrations, monthly retrospectives — these create the rhythm."*
- **OKRs are a practice.** *"They're a habit of reflection, alignment, and growth."*

### "Decoupling OKRs" (Nov 2024)

Kill approval-based alignment:
- *"Waiting for approvals isn't just frustrating — it's a sign of something deeper. It's a trust issue."*
- Teams write their own OKRs; leadership recommends, doesn't approve.
- *"If you've hired great people and given them a clear strategy, you shouldn't need to approve every little thing."*

**Register:** Teacher-y, story-driven, conversational-accessible, occasionally wry. Uses her Stanford students and past employers (LinkedIn, Zynga, Yahoo) as anecdotal ballast. Opens with a story of a team that got OKRs wrong, then shows the alternative. The book itself is structured as a fable (Hanna's tea company) followed by the operational model.

- **Dashboard vs GPS** — KPIs vs OKRs.
- **"Vitamin, not a medicine"** — OKRs preserve health when done regularly, don't cure a sick org.
- **Eat less, exercise more** — *"OKRs are like the advice 'Eat less and exercise more.' They are simple and hard."*

**Signature phrases:**
- *"Radical Focus."*
- *"Do less, better."*
- *"One Objective at a time."*
- *"Monday commitments, Friday celebrations."*
- *"Cadence beats brilliance."* (paraphrase, appears across multiple essays)

- *"OKRs are a practice."*

**Phrases she ATTACKS:**
- **"Individual OKRs"** — turns OKRs into performance reviews, kills team accountability.
- **"Cascading OKRs"** — *"you take your boss's KR and make it your Objective — I really hate this one."*
- **"Quarterly-only OKRs"** — quarterly setting without weekly cadence = a goal fantasy.
- **"OKRs as targets" / "OKRs as KPIs"** — conflates transformation with maintenance.
- **"Output OKRs disguised as outcomes"** — "launched X" is not a KR; the outcome the launch produced is.
- **"Multiple OKR sets per team"** — "Goal-of-the-Week Whiplash."
- **"Universal rollout"** — rolling OKRs out company-wide simultaneously.
- **"Approval-based alignment"** — trust issue in disguise.

## Heuristics / gotchas

- **Confidence level 5/10 at the start.** If you're comfortable, they're too easy.
- **Health Metrics beat gaming.** Any single-number KR can be gamed; the health metric is what keeps you honest about what shouldn't degrade.
- **Team OKRs, not individual.** Individual OKRs = performance-review theater.
- **One Objective per team per quarter.** If you have five, you have zero.
- **KRs must be outcomes, not outputs.** "Ship feature X" is not a KR. "Users who use feature X return 40% more" is.
- **Prerequisites before OKRs.** Psychological safety, multidisciplinary teams, autonomy — if you lack these, fix them first.
- **Cadence is the method.** Setting OKRs quarterly without weekly ritual is the #1 failure mode.

## Framework relationships

| vs / with | How Wodtke positions |
|---|---|
| **John Doerr — *Measure What Matters*** | Complementary but distinct. Wodtke is more operational, less cheerleading. She's explicit that Doerr's book is aspirational; hers is a manual. Also disagrees on individual OKRs. |
| **Andy Grove — Intel MBOs** | Traces lineage; respects the ancestor. Grove's original was team-level; the "individual OKR" pathology came later. |
| **Melissa Perri — *Escaping the Build Trap*** | Highly compatible. Both anti-individual-OKR, both outcome-over-output, both team-first. Perri interviewed her on Product Thinking (Jan 2023). |
| **EOS Traction (Wickman)** | Overlaps on cadence rituals (L10 meeting ~ Monday Commitments). Wodtke is lighter-weight; EOS is a heavier operating system. |
| **Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)** | Adjacent; measures execution across dimensions. Wodtke uses Health Metrics as a lighter analog. |
| **Roger Martin — Playing to Win** | Compatible. Martin says OKRs can be an **Enabling Management System** for a strategy, not the strategy itself. Wodtke agrees: OKRs are the "bridge" from strategy to team work; they don't set strategy. |
| **Rumelt — Good Strategy Bad Strategy** | Compatible. Rumelt's kernel produces the guiding policy; OKRs help teams commit to coherent actions inside it. |
