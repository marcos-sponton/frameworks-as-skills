## 2. THE METHOD IN DETAIL

### 2.1 The founding claim (2016)

> *"Winning today isn't about beating the competition at the old game. It's about inventing a whole new game — defining a new market category, developing it, and dominating it over time. You can't build a legendary company without building a legendary category."*
> — *Play Bigger* (2016), opening thesis.

The economic backbone: **the Category King captures ~76% of the market cap of the category.** (From the [TTMC report](https://www.playbigger.com/time-to-market-cap-report); repeated as the anchor stat everywhere the four co-authors speak.) In technology categories one company earns the majority of the value created. Everyone else fights over the remaining ~24%.

Related refinements Category Pirates now cite:
- Apple captured ~93% of smartphone profits.
- Uber has been valued ~25x Lyft.
- Wall Street awards Category Queens and Kings with a data flywheel ~$4.82 of market cap per $1 of revenue growth — ~5x the multiple of the typical company. (Eddie Yoon research, cited in [Play Bigger's own page](https://www.categorydesignadvisors.com/play-bigger/) and Category Pirates newsletters.)

### 2.2 The Magic Triangle (the three-legged stool)

Category design is not a marketing exercise. It's three disciplines executed in synchronicity:

1. **Product Design** — build something that solves the category's problem. Product/category fit.
2. **Company Design** — business model, org, culture, hiring all aligned to that category. Company/category fit.
3. **Category Design** — deliberately design and condition the market that gives the product its meaning.

When the three reinforce each other, a flywheel kicks in. When one leg is missing (great product, no category work → obscurity; great category talk, weak product → dies to a real competitor; strong company, wrong category → stuck in the Existing Market Trap), the company doesn't become legendary.

Lochhead's phrasing on lochhead.com: **"Magic Triangle — equal focus on designing company, product, and category simultaneously."** ([source](https://lochhead.com/blog/every-marketing-term-you-need-to-know/))

### 2.3 The Category Design process (as of the 2016 book)

Rough phases as codified in *Play Bigger*:

1. **Discover the category** — start from a problem no one has framed yet, not from your product. The **founding insight** is either a *market insight* (a problem the world doesn't yet realize it has) or a *technology insight* (a capability looking for the right problem to name).
2. **Answer the ten fact-finding questions** — vision, customers, problem, use cases, product, ecosystem, competition, business model, go-to-market, organization, funding — assembled at a full-day leadership workshop.
3. **Name the category** — 2–3 words. Powerful. Clear. The words themselves do a lot of the work.
4. **Package the category in 5 documents** — Category Landscape, Category Ecosystem, FROM-to Journey (aka "Frotos"), Category Name and Description, The Case for the New Category.
5. **Develop the POV (Point of View)** — the story that frames the problem, the stakes, the future, and the outcomes.
6. **Mobilize** — codify the category in the four seminal artifacts: **Category Blueprint**, **Product Taxonomy**, **Use Cases**, **Category Ecosystem**.
7. **Execute Lightning Strikes** — concentrated, event-scale market conditioning to plant the category in the market's mind.
8. **Spin the flywheel** — POV + Lightning Strikes + ecosystem + capital + data + talent → compounding advantage.

Category Design Advisors' current 7-step productized version (per [their site](https://www.categorydesignadvisors.com/play-bigger/)):

1. Identify the Problem
2. Solution & Category Name
3. Category Blueprint & Ecosystem
4. Point of View (POV)
5. Internal Mobilization
6. Lightning Strike Execution
7. Strike Ops Flywheel

### 2.4 The Category Lifecycle — Define / Develop / Dominate

Three phases of a category's evolution:

| Phase | What happens | Who wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Define** | Thought leader emerges. The problem gets named. Multiple companies swarm. | Category Designer setting the language. |
| **Develop** | The number of players collapses. A king emerges. Ecosystem forms. | Whoever framed the problem best. |
| **Dominate** | Category King captures ~76% of category market cap. Flywheel spins. | The King (Queen). Nobody else. |

Play Bigger: *"Whoever frames the problem best has the best chance of winning the category."*

The corollary anti-move: obsessing over "first mover advantage." Play Bigger is emphatic — *"the first inventor is an innovator to be thanked. The first to define and develop a category is a category king to be followed."* First to market is not first to mind, not first to frame, not first to king status.

### 2.5 POV — the Point of View

**A POV is the story that frames the world differently.** Lochhead's definition:

> "A POV is a story that articulates a problem/opportunity for customers and a different approach to solving the problem or capitalizing on the opportunity. A POV is often a critical component of category design."
> — [lochhead.com marketing terms](https://lochhead.com/blog/every-marketing-term-you-need-to-know/)

Full POV structure (from Play Bigger):

- Frames the *problem* (not the product).
- Names the *ramifications* of not acting.
- Presents a *vision* of the future.
- Explains the *business outcomes* of embracing the vision.
- Has a beginning, middle, end — like a movie trailer.
- Told with **simple language, short sentences, provocative tone, emotional content**.
- CEO-owned. "Make it religion." (Play Bigger, on POV rollout.)

The POV separates loved brands from tolerated ones. It signals mission over mercenary. It magnetizes the ecosystem. It's what a Missionary company has and a Mercenary company doesn't.

### 2.6 Lightning Strike

Not a launch. Not a campaign. **A concentrated company event that plants the category in the market's mind**, timed to hijack attention.

Lochhead's definition:
> "A Lightning Strike is an event meant to explode onto the market, grab the attention of customers, investors, analysts, and media."
> — [lochhead.com marketing terms](https://lochhead.com/blog/every-marketing-term-you-need-to-know/)

Play Bigger execution notes:
- **Content must be big, bold, different** enough to cut through.
- **Evangelize the problem first, product second.**
- Assign specific roles: CEO, Strike Master Controller, Strike Leader, Chief Hijacker, Event Planner.
- Plan 3–6 months out. Identify what you're going to *hijack* (a conference, a moment, a narrative).
- Codify all category-design work into the strike materials.
- Execute → hijack → adapt → sell → evaluate → accelerate the signals that landed.
- Play Bigger's warning: *"If the CEO wavers or hedges or doesn't commit resources, the strike will fail."*

Canonical example: Salesforce hiring actors to stage a mock "End of Software" protest outside Siebel's user conference. Siebel started defending itself. Press ran with the story. Salesforce was legitimized as the category challenger the moment the incumbent acknowledged them.

### 2.7 Missionary vs Mercenary

Adopted from John Doerr (the four co-authors credit Doerr repeatedly). Category Kings are Missionary companies, not Mercenary.

| Missionary | Mercenary |
|---|---|
| Driven by passion / belief | Driven by paranoia / opportunism |
| Strategic thinking | Opportunistic thinking |
| Marathon | Sprint |
| Focus on customers and value | Focus on competitors and financials |
| Own a POV | Don't have a POV |
| Attract other missionaries | Attract fee-chasers |

Play Bigger: only Missionary companies build categories, because building a category takes 6–10 years of belief before the market catches up. A mercenary quits when the analyst report looks bad.

### 2.8 The Data Flywheel (Category Pirates, post-book)

The clearest post-book extension. Framing from Category Pirates newsletters:

> "The Data Flywheel is a system that accurately predicts the future so you can continue to design and dominate the category."

Category Kings/Queens with a data flywheel command ~$4.82 of market cap per $1 of revenue growth — ~5x the typical company. The flywheel means: **your category leadership generates proprietary data → data trains proprietary models → proprietary models predict the market → predictions let you design the next wave → next wave reinforces category leadership.**

The Data Flywheel is what makes a modern Category King structurally uncatchable. It didn't exist as a named concept in the 2016 book — it emerged from Category Pirates ~2022 onward. **This is the single biggest post-book addition.**

### 2.9 Languaging (Category Pirates)

Language does the category work. Naming the problem, naming the future, naming the "from" and the "to" (Frotos) is not marketing polish — it's the mechanism by which the market's mind reorganizes.

From *Snow Leopard*: **"Quick Guide to Languaging — a model for inventing new language to get ideas to 'stick' — and the unfair advantage this creates."**

Category Pirates on the AI-era version of this (from [Lochhead 2025](https://lochhead.com/fyd-out-of-the-exisitng-trap-christopher-lochhead-2025/)): **the language you use matters critically.** Calling your innovation an "assistant" or "copilot" positions it as secondary. Declaring it a new category magnetizes the future. Same product, different languaging, different market outcome.

### 2.10 The Existing Market Trap (2025, Ramadan + Lochhead)

The prequel move. Most companies don't fail because they build bad products — they fail because they get compared to something old and lose the comparison. **13 Deadly Sins** map the mechanics of getting trapped. Book premise:

> "Markets are people. Categories are beliefs. A market is just a group of people who share a problem. A category is what they believe can solve it."
> — *The Existing Market Trap*, 2025

Lochhead's blunt line: *"If your strategy involves simply bolting on an AI assistant or copilot, you're making a pussy move and you're fucked."* (Ep 415 of Follow Your Different.) The exit from the trap is category design — build belief in a new market, not incremental improvement of an old one.

### 2.11 No Ocean Strategy (Category Pirates)

The pushback on *Blue Ocean Strategy*. Category Pirates: **there is no ocean.** Blue-ocean thinking still assumes you're navigating a pre-existing space. Category design starts from "there is no space — now what?" The pirate move is to reject the premise. From the [No Ocean Strategy essay](https://www.categorypirates.news/p/no-ocean-strategy-why-sailing-for-e3f):

> "Pirates reject the premise! They don't just blindly accept the way it is."

## 3. VOICE & TONE

### 3.1 Register

**Loud. Evangelical. Provocative. Different-not-better-obsessed.** The four co-authors have distinct registers but a shared conviction:

- **Christopher Lochhead** is the volume dial at 11 — profane, punchy, will drop *"pussy move"* on a public podcast, calls things "legendary" or "different" with religious weight. Follow Your Different is deliberately provocative. He's a "no-BS conversation" host. On Substack he alternates capitalized epithets ("*Legendary*", "*Different*", "*Category King*") with hard direct claims.
- **Al Ramadan** is the executive voice — data-driven, credentialed, "excruciatingly organized." Time Magazine profiled him. Ramadan carries the research posture (TTMC, category economics).
- **Kevin Maney** is the journalist — Newsweek columnist voice, story-first, clean sentence rhythm.
- **Dave Peterson** is the operator — 20+ years running comms/CMO at Vantive, Mercury, Aggregate Knowledge, Coverity; teaches from execution, not theory.

**Composite voice for the skill:** loud, direct, allergic to "better", suspicious of positioning-as-decoration, quick to name a company as either a Category King (praise) or stuck in the Existing Market Trap (dismissal). Uses proper nouns aggressively (*Category King*, *POV*, *Lightning Strike*, *Missionary*, *Data Flywheel*, *No Ocean*, *Frotos*). Rejects premises rather than negotiating them.

### 3.2 Recurring rhetorical moves

- **"Different, not better."** The most-repeated 3-word position. *"People remember different in a way they don't remember better."* — Lochhead.
- **The reject-the-premise move.** *"Pirates reject the premise!"* — Category Pirates on Blue Ocean. Applied to product-improvement thinking, positioning-as-messaging, first-mover myth.
- **Naming with proper nouns.** Category King, POV, Lightning Strike, Missionary, Category Queen, Snow Leopard, Data Flywheel, No Ocean, Existing Market Trap, 13 Deadly Sins. Proper nouns force the market to adopt the vocabulary. This is the method being self-applied.
- **"Legendary" as adjective.** Legendary companies, legendary categories, legendary writers, legendary founders. Used for pattern-recognition praise.
- **Blunt reframes at essay-headline scale.** *"Category Design Kills the Minimum Viable Product and the Blue Ocean Strategy."* / *"Goodbye Better. Hello Different."* / *"The Existing Market Trap."* The title IS the argument.
- **Category-first, product-second exposition.** Lochhead almost never opens with a product. Opens with a problem framing, then names the category, then arrives at what the company does.

### 3.3 Signature vocabulary (mandatory glossary for the skill)

- **Category Design** — the discipline of creating and monetizing new markets.
- **Category King / Queen** — the company that dominates a category, captures ~76% of category market cap.
- **POV (Point of View)** — the story: problem framed, stakes named, future teased, outcomes explained.
- **Lightning Strike** — the concentrated market-conditioning event that plants the category.
- **Missionary / Mercenary** — the two founder archetypes (Doerr-derived); only Missionaries build categories.
- **Magic Triangle** — Product Design + Company Design + Category Design executed in synchronicity.
- **Frotos (FROM-to)** — the articulation of how a category works *from* today's state *to* the future state.
- **Data Flywheel** — the compounding data → prediction → design → dominance loop.
- **Languaging** — the deliberate invention of language to install a new category in the market's mind.
- **Snow Leopard** — the "category of one" archetype (from the Category Pirates book on writing).
- **Niche Down** — deliberately narrowing to a differentiated position where you can be #1.
- **Ecosystem** — the deliberately built network of partners, analysts, developers, media orbiting a category.
- **Existing Market Trap** — the invisible force that kills companies that improve instead of differentiating.
- **13 Deadly Sins** — the patterns of getting stuck in the Existing Market Trap.
- **No Ocean** — the anti-Blue-Ocean posture: there's no pre-existing space; reject the premise.
- **TTMC (Time to Market Cap)** — the metric behind the 76% research.
- **Chief Category Officer** — the CEO's real job in a category-design company.

### 3.4 Words / framings they push back on

- **"Better."** The word triggers a rewrite. Better = losing.
- **"Blue Ocean."** Still competing; wrong premise.
- **"Positioning."** Positioning happens inside an existing category. Category design creates the category. **Don't confuse them.** (Dunford's positioning is inside; Play Bigger's category design is outside/around.)
- **"First mover."** Wrong bar — first to mind, first to frame the problem, first to king status is what matters.
- **"Assistant" / "Copilot"** — languaging that positions your work as secondary to an incumbent (Lochhead 2025).
- **"MVP."** Category Pirates: *"Category Design Kills the Minimum Viable Product."* MVP thinking assumes the market pre-exists.
- **"We're the leader in [existing category]."** If it's an existing category, you're not the King — the King is already there and taking 76%.
- **"Category creation without a Lightning Strike."** = wishcasting.
- **"Great product, we'll figure the category out later."** = Magic Triangle broken.

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO'S / DON'TS / GOTCHAS

### 4.1 The core do's

- **Frame the problem before you name the product.** Whoever frames the problem best owns the category.
- **Sell different, not better.** If your pitch can be steel-manned as "same as X but 20% cheaper/faster/prettier", you're competing, not designing.
- **Name the category in 2–3 words.** If it takes a paragraph, you don't have the category yet.
- **CEO-owned POV.** Not delegated to marketing. Play Bigger: the CEO is Chief Category Officer.
- **Ecosystem before product feature race.** A category has partners, analysts, developers, media, missionaries. If you're the only one talking about the category, it's not a category — it's a monologue.
- **Missionary founders.** 6–10 year belief horizons. Mercenaries won't survive the category-design cycle.
- **Languaging is the mechanism.** Deliberately invent the words. If your language matches the incumbent, so does your market position.
- **Lightning Strike, not launch.** Concentrated moments beat continuous churn.
- **Data Flywheel from day one** (post-book addition). Design the loop that will make the category defensible in year 5.

### 4.2 Anti-patterns / don'ts / gotchas

- **The Better Trap.** Every version of "we're X for Y" that positions inside an incumbent's category is a Better Trap.
- **The Existing Market Trap.** Building belief in the old market instead of a new one. Getting compared to something old and losing the comparison. See the 13 Deadly Sins.
- **Bolt-on AI.** Post-2023 flavor of the Better Trap. *"Simply bolting on an AI assistant or copilot"* is the losing move (Lochhead 2025).
- **Confusing category design with positioning.** Positioning is choosing the context inside an existing category (Dunford). Category design is creating the category itself. They compose (see §6) — don't blur.
- **Confusing category design with branding, marketing, or storytelling.** Play Bigger is a strategic discipline, not a communications exercise. Doing beautiful storytelling for a product that doesn't design its category is decoration on a losing position.
- **"First mover advantage" as strategy.** Being early to a category is not being the King. Play Bigger: *"The first inventor is an innovator to be thanked."*
- **Delegating POV to the marketing team.** Kills the strike. Play Bigger: CEO wavering = strike fails.
- **Weak POV that could be signed by any competitor.** If your POV would fit on your competitor's homepage without modification, it's not a POV.
- **Multiple categories at once, from a startup.** The 6–10 year belief cost is per-category. Startups almost never survive multi-category bets. Continuous category creation is a Corporate Playbook move — for companies who already have one king category.
- **Lightning Strike without ecosystem.** A one-company event is a press release. A Lightning Strike hijacks a conference / analyst / customer / media moment.
- **Category creation for the sake of it.** *"Not for everyone."* Category design demands bold thinking, risk tolerance, focused leadership attention, long-term commitment. It's the wrong tool for defending an existing profitable business inside a mature category.

### 4.3 Diagnostic questions the framework wants you to answer

- Are we in the Existing Market Trap? (Are we being compared to something old and losing that comparison?)
- What is the problem — as we would name it, if we owned the language?
- What 2–3 words name this category?
- What is our POV in one story: problem → ramifications → future → outcomes?
- Are we the Missionary company here, or the Mercenary?
- What's the Lightning Strike we can plan in 3–6 months?
- Where's the ecosystem going to come from — analysts, partners, developers, missionaries?
- What's the Data Flywheel we're building for year 5 defensibility?
- If we were the incumbent, how would we categorize us to bury us? Are we accepting that framing?

### 4.4 Tests for whether the category work is landing

- Analysts start using your category name in their reports.
- Prospects volunteer the category to *you* on discovery calls.
- Competitors start defending themselves against your framing (the Siebel/Salesforce signal).
- Talent applies "because they believe" (Missionary flywheel).
- Investors start pattern-matching to your category, not to the old one.
- Media doesn't need your press release to write the category story.
- You get a *"You're the [Category Name] company, right?"* from someone you've never pitched.

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

**vs. Geoffrey Moore — Crossing the Chasm.** Moore came 25 years before Play Bigger. Chasm is about market-development inside an existing technology-adoption lifecycle. Play Bigger authors themselves acknowledge they were writing *"Chasm for a new era"* — the era where category *creation* is the primary source of category *leadership*. Where Moore diagnoses the chasm between early adopters and pragmatists, Play Bigger says: **don't cross the chasm — dig a new one, in a new category, on your terms.** They compose: Chasm is inside a category; Play Bigger creates the category. See [[crossing-the-chasm]] once built.

**vs. April Dunford — Obviously Awesome / Sales Pitch.** The most important adjacency and the most common confusion. **Dunford's positioning goes *inside* a category. Category design *creates* the category.** They are complementary, not competing:

- If you're inside an existing category (or an obvious one) → **Dunford's method**. Pick your competitive alternatives, define differentiated value, choose your market category, sharpen the frame of reference. Positioning is context-setting inside an accepted container.
- If the category doesn't exist yet, or the existing category is a trap → **Play Bigger**. Design the category itself. The POV, the Lightning Strike, the ecosystem, the languaging are the tools that create the container.
- **Compose:** Play Bigger creates the category. Dunford positions inside it once it's real. A Category King who won on Play Bigger still uses Dunford's method to position individual product lines inside their now-established category.
- **Dunford is skeptical of category creation as default.** She notes that ~90% of recent successful public tech companies won inside existing categories, not by creating new ones. Category design is high-variance and expensive. Not for everyone. See [[obviously-awesome]].

**vs. Andy Raskin — Strategic Narrative.** Very close adjacency in *tone*, different in *scope*. Raskin's 5-part narrative (Change in the World → Winners & Losers → Promised Land → Magic Gifts → Evidence) is a **sales-narrative artifact**; Play Bigger's POV is the **category-defining posture** the whole company organizes around. They compose beautifully — Raskin's Promised Land ≈ Play Bigger's "to" in Frotos; Raskin's Old Game/New Game ≈ Play Bigger's category reframe. Raskin narrates the change; Play Bigger *makes* the change by naming and dominating the new category. Both agree on "different not better" and "problem-first, product-last." See [[strategic-narrative]].

**vs. Hamilton Helmer — 7 Powers.** Play Bigger's Category Kingship is one of the most durable sources of 7 Powers' *Counter-Positioning* and *Cornered Resource* powers. The Data Flywheel is Helmer's *Scale Economies* + *Network Economies* combined, applied to data. Category Kingship is where several of Helmer's 7 Powers concentrate. See [[7-powers]].

**vs. W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne — Blue Ocean Strategy.** Category Pirates explicitly reject Blue Ocean. Blue Ocean assumes there's an ocean to find. No Ocean says: the pirate move is to reject the premise — there's no pre-existing space, build the space itself. Practically, Blue Ocean is a *value-innovation* method inside a redefined industry; Category Design is a *belief-building* method to install a new category in the market's mind. Compose only carefully — they carry different assumptions about what a "market" is.

**vs. Roger Martin — Playing to Win.** Complementary. Martin's cascade (Where to Play / How to Win) is answered *inside* a chosen category. Play Bigger asks: what if the category itself is the choice? Martin's cascade + Play Bigger's category design together answer *"which category should we design, and how do we win once we've designed it?"* See [[playing-to-win]].

**vs. Rita McGrath / Clayton Christensen (Innovator's Dilemma).** Play Bigger acknowledges Christensen — the Innovator's Dilemma is the mechanism by which incumbents fail to design new categories (they'd cannibalize their old category). Play Bigger prescribes: design the new category anyway; a startup can, an incumbent almost never will (their own Play Bigger insight: *"the odds of a start-up becoming a billion-dollar category king are better than the odds of a new product from a big old corporation creating a new category"*).

**vs. Al Ries & Jack Trout — Positioning / 22 Immutable Laws.** Play Bigger repeatedly cites Ries/Trout's immutable laws (Law of Leadership, Law of the Category, Law of the Mind, Law of Perception, Law of Focus, Law of the Ladder, Law of Division) as foundational. The 22 Laws of Category Design is Category Pirates' explicit successor project to Ries & Trout for the modern era.
