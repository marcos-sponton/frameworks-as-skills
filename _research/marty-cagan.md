## 2. POST-BOOK MATERIAL (2018 → 2026)

**This is the differential of the skill.** The 2008/2018 *Inspired* is the widely-summarized book. *Empowered* (2020) and especially *Transformed* (2024) are where Cagan's current thinking lives. Plus every SVPG essay since is a refinement of one of the 20 First Principles.

### 2.1 *EMPOWERED* (2020, with Chris Jones) — the product leadership book

Written because *Inspired* left the product-leadership altitude thin. Sold as the answer to "we have PMs but they still can't produce outcomes — what's wrong at the leadership level?"

Core moves:
- **Coaching is the primary responsibility of product leadership.** Managers of PMs, designers, engineers should be coaches first. Career development for the PM as core management output.
- **Staffing bar-raisers.** Hire ordinary people, develop them into extraordinary ones — but the hire has to be coachable and mission-aligned.
- **Product vision + strategy = the leadership deliverables.** Where empowered teams are given the "what problem"; leadership owns the "why we're trying to win here."
- **Evangelism as the leader's job.** The vision has to be sold internally, repeatedly.
- **Missionaries, not mercenaries.** John Doerr quote Cagan uses ~every talk: *"We need teams of missionaries, not teams of mercenaries."* Missionaries believe in the vision; mercenaries build whatever they're told. Amazon example: never outsources core empowered-team functions, because outsourcing manufactures mercenaries.

### 2.2 *TRANSFORMED* (2024) — the transformation playbook

The book Cagan spent 2020–2024 developing because *Empowered* left the "how do we get there from here" question unanswered for legacy orgs. Written with Lea Hickman, Chris Jones, Christian Idiodi, John Moore — each contributor led a real transformation at a large pre-Internet company; three first-person case studies embedded.

**The 20 First Principles of the Product Operating Model** — organized in three groups. Chapters 15–19 of the book. Source: [Product Compass in-depth reference](https://www.productcompass.pm/p/product-model-first-principles-transformed-cagan).

**Product Team principles** (4):
1. **Empowered with Problems to Solve** — teams get customer/business problems + desired outcomes, not solutions to build.
2. **Outcomes over Output** — measured by results and value created, not features shipped.
3. **Sense of Ownership** — teams own discovery AND delivery, clear purpose, autonomy.
4. **Collaboration** — cross-functional, psychological safety, disagree-and-commit.

**Product Strategy principles** (4):
5. **Focus** — "saying no to the hundred other good ideas" to concentrate resources on the few problems that matter most.
6. **Powered by Insights** — from data, customer conversations, enabling technologies, industry trends.
7. **Transparency** — reasoning, data, stakeholder impact shared openly.
8. **Placing Bets** — strategy is a portfolio of bets, some fail; multiple teams may attack the same problem in parallel.

**Product Discovery / Delivery / Culture principles** (12) — cover: continuous discovery, prototype-based validation, tackling the four risks early, small/frequent/reliable releases (CI/CD as default), instrumentation, monitoring & rollback, customer-informed decisions, principled disagreement, learning culture, career development, trust-based relationships with stakeholders, missionary culture.

### 2.3 The Product Operating Model (POM) — the 2024 umbrella term

Definition from [The Product Operating Model: An Introduction](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction/):
> "A conceptual model based on a set of first principles that leading product companies believe to be true about creating products. Its core purpose is achieving outcomes versus merely producing output."

**Three dimensions of the POM** (this is the level above the 20 principles, the framing Cagan uses in talks):
1. **How products are built** — small, frequent, reliable releases; minimum every 2 weeks, ideally CI/CD.
2. **How problems are solved** — cross-functional teams (PM + designer + engineers) receive problems and desired outcomes; produce solutions that are valuable, usable, feasible, and viable.
3. **How you decide which problems to solve** — a customer-focused **product vision** + an **insight-driven product strategy**.

The POM is what Cagan wants Perri's Product Ops to fuel; Perri explicitly cedes the top-level phrase to him (see her *Product Operations*, 2023, and cross-linked skill [[escaping-the-build-trap]]).

### 2.4 The Product Discovery / Delivery split — refined

Original *Inspired* laid out discovery and delivery as parallel tracks. Post-book Cagan hardened the distinction:

- **Product Discovery = build to learn.** Prototype-based. 10–20+ prototypes per week is normal for a strong team. Tests the four risks against real users and stakeholders before writing production code. The unit of work is the *experiment*.
- **Product Delivery = build to earn.** Commercial-quality product that can be sold, serviced, supported, and run in production. Addresses scale, performance, fault tolerance, reliability, accuracy, privacy, security, operations, provisioning, internationalization. The unit of work is the *release*.

Source for the sharpened framing: [Build to Learn vs Build to Earn](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn/) (2026-04-16). Credits Jeff Patton for the distinction. Cagan's 2026 argument: AI is collapsing the cost of *delivery*, which shifts the bottleneck to *discovery* — which is exactly what most orgs are worst at.

### 2.5 The Four Big Risks — still the canonical framework

From [The Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/) — Cagan's most-cited framework, introduced in *Inspired*, hardened in every subsequent book:

| Risk | Definition (Cagan verbatim) | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** | *"Whether customers will buy it or users will choose to use it"* | Product Manager |
| **Usability** | *"Whether users can figure out how to use it"* | Product Designer |
| **Feasibility** | *"Whether our engineers can build what we need with the time, skills and technology we have"* | Lead Engineer |
| **Business Viability** | *"Whether this solution also works for the various aspects of our business"* (legal, finance, sales, marketing, brand) | Product Manager |

Rule: attack the big risks *early*, in discovery, cheaply, with prototypes. Attacking them late (in delivery) is the source of most product failure. Value and viability are the two most under-attended by weak PMs.

### 2.6 Product Vision vs. Mission — the canonical distinction

From [Product Vision vs. Mission](https://www.svpg.com/product-vision-vs-mission/) and the [Product Vision FAQ](https://www.svpg.com/product-vision-faq/):

- **Mission statement** = "a pithy slogan…to describe your company's mission." Corporate purpose. Does NOT do the job of a product vision.
- **Product vision** = *"the future we are trying to create"* — a **persuasion tool**, meant to be *"compelling, inspiring, and empowering."* Serves as the North Star that creates common understanding across all product teams, so they can coordinate without micromanagement.

**Time horizon** (Cagan's rules):
- Software companies: **2–5 years out.**
- Device / hardware companies: **5–10 years out.**

**Format:** increasingly a **visiontype** (Cagan's coinage) — a short video prototype that dramatizes the user experience and shows the customer's life demonstrably improving. The prototype makes the vision *emotional*, not decorative.

### 2.7 Product Strategy — the four requirements

From [Product Strategy — Overview](https://www.svpg.com/product-strategy-overview/) and [Product Strategy — Focus](https://www.svpg.com/product-strategy-focus/):

1. **Focus** — the willingness to make tough choices on what's really important. Saying no to the 100 other good ideas.
2. **Insights** — generating, identifying, and leveraging insights from data, customer conversations, enabling technologies, and industry trends.
3. **Actions** — converting insights into action; assigning problems to teams with clear desired outcomes.
4. **Active management without micromanagement** — the leadership discipline.

**"Placing bets"** is the vocabulary. Multiple teams may attack the same hard problem in parallel; some bets fail. Strategy is a portfolio.

### 2.8 The Alternative to Roadmaps

Post-2018 hardened position from [The Alternative to Roadmaps](https://www.svpg.com/the-alternative-to-roadmaps/) and [Roadmap Alternative FAQ](https://www.svpg.com/roadmap-alternative-faq/). Roadmaps are *replaced*, not fixed:

- **Product Vision** — the multi-year holistic view.
- **Business objectives per team** (OKR-shaped) — the *problems to solve* + *desired outcomes*.
- **High-Integrity Commitments** — for specific cases requiring a date-based deliverable (regulatory, key partnership, major event). Handled separately, sparingly, and only after discovery has validated the solution.

The "outcome-based roadmap" that most orgs adopt post-2020 is, for Cagan, still a roadmap — just relabeled. His position: don't fix the roadmap, *replace* the artifact that necessitated it.

### 2.9 Product Management Theater (2024 coinage, still live)

From the Lenny 2024 episode [Product management theater | Marty Cagan](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/product-management-theater-marty):

The industry is full of people with the PM title who are not doing product management. They are **"Agile coaches, product owners, product operations, or business analysts"** relabeled as PMs. They administer backlogs, run ceremonies, translate stakeholder requests into tickets. They are not accountable for **value** or **viability**. Fed by ZIRP-era over-hiring; exposed now.

**Cagan's Steve Jobs–via–Andy Grove line for what a real PM looks like:** the PM is not the "CEO of the product" in authority — but *is* the person accountable for whether the product is valuable and viable. Everything else is theater.

**Related coinage: "backlog administrator"** — Cagan's dismissive term for a product owner in a delivery/scrum team who has no strategic ownership. From the "Product vs Feature Teams" essay.

### 2.10 AI + product (2025–2026) — the current theme

Two load-bearing essays:
- [Product Coaching and AI](https://www.svpg.com/product-coaching-and-ai/) (2026-02-04) — Cagan's public reversal. For 20 years SVPG's position was that great product people are developed through human coaching only. In 2026 he changed: foundation models grounded in the right operating context can serve as scalable, always-on product coaches. Explicit acknowledgement that this is a shift.
- [The AI Productivity Paradox](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/) (2026-07-23) — the productivity paradox: 89% of executives say AI has sped up work; only 6% can point to organization-wide ROI. Cagan's diagnosis: AI accelerates whatever operating model you already have. Feature teams + AI = faster feature factory. Empowered teams + AI = faster discovery + faster delivery + widening advantage. Hilary Gridley quote he uses: *"It's never been faster to build, which means it's never been easier to run 10 times faster in the wrong direction."*

This dovetails with the *Build to Learn vs Build to Earn* essay: AI collapses delivery cost, which promotes discovery to primary bottleneck.

Adjacent 2025–2026 essays:
- [The Politics of Pilot Teams](https://www.svpg.com/the-politics-of-pilot-teams/) (2025-07-14). "A pilot team is the MVP for a full transformation." Hand-pick members; problem must be "impressive but not impossible."
- [Stakeholders and the Product Model](https://www.svpg.com/stakeholders-and-the-product-model/) (2025-12-01, with Chris Jones). Fix: share business context, frame problems (not solutions), enable customer access.
- [The Product Model at Google](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-at-google/) (2025-12-19, with Elias Lieberich). "Empowered teams don't require less management; they require better management." Tech Lead Managers + Group PMs.
- [Great Products, Bad Companies](https://www.svpg.com/great-products-bad-companies/) (2026-06-30). Success attracts governance capture. Endorses Eric Ries's *Incorruptible*.

## 3. VOICE & TONE

### Register
- **Opinionated, direct, practitioner-authoritative.** Not academic. Not warm. Not hedged.
- **Confident to the point of provocation.** He will name specific companies (positive: Amazon, Google, Netflix, Apple; negative: unnamed but described types), name specific practices as anti-patterns (SAFe, scrum-as-religion, roadmaps, "product owner" as a role), name specific people approvingly (Doerr, Ries, Torres, Perri, Martin) and dismissively (unnamed "influencers" and "coaches" without operator background).
- **"Strong opinions loosely held" is his brand** — but the "loosely" applies rarely. He shifts position openly when he does (the 2026 AI-coach reversal is signature) and cites the shift explicitly.
- **Uses "we" for SVPG collectively and for the profession.** "The best product companies…", "we've seen this pattern for 20 years…"
- **Empathy is thin.** He acknowledges people are stuck in bad orgs but does not comfort them. The comfort is the diagnosis: *this is why you're stuck*.

### Signature rhetorical moves

1. **Diagnosis with a name.** Never leaves an anti-pattern nameless. "Feature team." "Product management theater." "Backlog administrator." "Mini-CEO myth" (which he attributes and pushes back on). Naming is half the fix.
2. **Contrast pairs, binary before nuanced.** Product team vs. feature team. Discovery vs. delivery. Build to learn vs. build to earn. Missionaries vs. mercenaries. Outcomes vs. outputs. Empowered vs. controlled. Almost every essay opens with a contrast.
3. **"The best product companies…" as authority move.** He grounds prescriptions in what Apple, Amazon, Google, Netflix, Adobe actually do — his firsthand consulting exposure is the credential.
4. **"This isn't complicated. It's just hard."** Repeated framing. The concepts are simple; the political will to change is what's rare.
5. **The single-sentence definitional reset.** "The product vision is the future we are trying to create." "A pilot team is the MVP for a full transformation." "We need teams of missionaries, not teams of mercenaries."
6. **Reference the roster.** Cites Doerr, Ries, Grove, Kim Scott, Ben Horowitz, Torres, Perri approvingly. Cites Martin's Playing to Win as the strategy frame that composes cleanly with the POM. Positions himself in a lineage.
7. **Explicit reversals when they happen.** Rather than pretend consistency, he flags shifts — 2026 AI-coaching essay opens with "for 20 years we've said…and we're changing our position because…"

### Words he pushes back on
- **"Product owner"** as a job title — reduces PM to scrum-role administrator. His clean version: *product manager*, accountable for value + viability.
- **"Mini-CEO"** — myth. He agrees with Perri that this framing sets PMs up to fail.
- **"Roadmap"** — replace, don't fix. Even "outcome-based roadmap" is still a roadmap.
- **"SAFe"** — explicit anti-endorsement. Scaled Agile Framework rewards output cadence, undermines empowerment.
- **"Feature factory"** — uses as diagnosis, not vision. Perri's term; Cagan compatible.
- **"Best practices"** — prefers "first principles."
- **"Execution"** — he uses the word but pushes back on execution-vs-strategy framing when it excuses executives from strategic ownership. (Overlaps with Martin's critique of the term.)
- **"Agile"** and **"Scrum"** as religions — Cagan is agnostic-to-hostile on both when they become the point rather than the vehicle.
- **Certifications** — CSPO, CSM, etc. as substitute for coaching. He does not endorse.

### How he teaches
- **Essay → book → essay loop.** SVPG tests ideas as essays; ideas that hold up become book chapters; the book creates new essay threads. The 20 First Principles started as ~40 essays.
- **Case-first, framework-second.** Nearly every essay opens with a company observation (Apple, Amazon, Google, Netflix, a client's failure) before the abstraction.
- **Coaching is the delivery mechanism.** SVPG does workshops, transformation engagements, and product coaching. The books are pre-sales for those engagements — dense, high-signal, but Cagan is explicit that the transformation happens in coaching, not from reading.
- **First-person plural** — "we" for SVPG. Rare "I" moments carry weight; they usually mark a personal reversal or a career-anecdote reference.

## 4. HEURISTICS / DO's / DON'Ts / GOTCHAS

### Symptoms you're a feature factory (Cagan's diagnostics)

- Team gets prioritized lists of features to build, not problems to solve.
- Roadmap runs 2–3 quarters, is committed externally to sales and customers, includes dates on unvalidated solutions.
- Success is measured by shipped features and velocity, not by customer or business outcomes.
- PMs spend most of their time writing user stories, running backlog grooming, running ceremonies.
- Engineers show up at sprint planning to receive tickets, not to help figure out solutions.
- Nobody on the team can articulate the desired outcome the current feature is supposed to produce.
- "Product owner" is the title; "product manager" is either absent or a program-manager relabel.
- The company adopted SAFe or a similar scaled-agile framework and calls that "product operating model."

### Cagan's named anti-patterns (name them explicitly, with attribution)

- **Feature team** — the umbrella anti-pattern. Command-and-control, mercenary mindset, output-focused. *"Product vs Feature Teams"*, SVPG.
- **Backlog administrator** — Cagan's dismissive term for a PO with no strategic ownership. *Lenny 2024 podcast; Product vs Feature Teams essay.*
- **Product management theater** — 2024 coinage. Non-PM roles relabeled as PMs; ceremony over judgment. *Lenny 2024.*
- **Mini-CEO myth** — the framing that sets PMs up for failure. Cagan compatible with Perri here.
- **Roadmap as commitment device** — even in "outcome" clothing. *The Alternative to Roadmaps.*
- **Solution imposition by stakeholders** — stakeholder brings a feature request, not a problem. *Stakeholders and the Product Model, 2025.*
- **Outsourcing empowered team functions** — manufactures mercenaries. Amazon example: they don't do it. *Empowered, 2020.*
- **Certification-as-coaching** — CSPO/CSM as substitute for real career development. *Empowered, 2020.*
- **SAFe / scaled-agile-as-POM** — rewards output cadence. *Repeatedly across essays.*
- **"Everyone is a PM"** — dilutes accountability. *Product management theater, 2024.*
- **Pilot team assembled from average performers** — dooms the pilot. Hand-pick. *The Politics of Pilot Teams, 2025.*
- **Pilot team problem chosen too safe or too risky** — sweet spot is "impressive but not impossible." *Same essay.*
- **AI-as-productivity-lever without operating model change** — accelerates the feature factory. *AI Productivity Paradox, 2026.*

### Do's for empowered product teams

- Give teams problems + desired outcomes; let them figure out solutions.
- Staff each team with a real PM + real designer + real lead engineer + 2–10 engineers (**two-pizza rule**).
- Attack the four risks **early**, in discovery, cheaply, with prototypes.
- Ship small, frequent, reliable releases — at minimum every 2 weeks, ideally CI/CD.
- Product leaders own vision + strategy + coaching; teams own solutions.
- Publish a **product vision** with a 2–5yr horizon (software) or 5–10yr (devices); make it a visiontype prototype video, not a doc.
- Set business objectives per team (OKR-shaped), not a roadmap of features.
- Use **high-integrity commitments** for the rare cases that need a date; discovery validates before the commitment ships.
- Fund a **pilot team** hand-picked as the MVP for transformation; measure it by business outcome.
- Coach constantly. Coaching is the leader's job, not an extracurricular.

### Common misapplications (orgs that *claim* Product Operating Model)

- Rebrand POs as "product managers" without changing the job or reporting line → theater.
- Adopt "outcome-based" roadmaps that are still committed feature lists → still roadmaps.
- Fund a "Product Ops" team that becomes a PMO in disguise (see Perri; Cagan agrees; adopts her definition).
- Run a "transformation" without a pilot team and expect the org to change from a memo.
- Copy the 20 First Principles as a checklist without changing the incentive/authority structure that produced the feature team in the first place.
- Adopt AI tooling and celebrate velocity increase → paradox essay warns explicitly.
- Confuse *product vision* with *mission statement* → vision fails to do its job.
- Confuse *product strategy* with *quarterly OKR-writing exercise* → strategy without insights, focus, or bets.

## 5. REAL CASES CAGAN USES PUBLICLY

Primary cases (his firsthand consulting or observation, cited across essays and books):

- **Google** — deep case in [The Product Model at Google](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-at-google/) (2025). Search → PageRank; AdWords; Waymo. Tech Lead Managers, Group PMs, empowered teams that broadcast problems and let teams volunteer. Also Ep 271-era: Gemini's 650M+ MAU.
- **Amazon** — never outsources core empowered-team functions. Working Backwards process for vision (compatible with, not identical to, Cagan's visiontype). AWS as example of a second business built inside an empowered culture. Two-pizza rule origin. Amazon appears in *Inspired* and every book since.
- **Netflix** — culture of missionary teams; freedom-and-responsibility as the enabling culture. Streaming pivot, originals — used as archetype.
- **Apple** — extraordinary designer/engineer collaboration; product culture as the enduring moat. Steve Jobs stories recycled but rarely as "do what Jobs did" — more "what Jobs did shows what leadership looks like."
- **Adobe** — cited in *Inspired* for discovery + strategy.
- **Netscape** — his own operator background; used sparingly, mostly for the Andreessen anecdote.
- **eBay** — his own operator background; used for the "we shipped fast but stopped shipping outcomes" lesson.
- **Spotify** — cited (with occasional pushback on the "Spotify model" being copied without context).
- **Facebook / Meta** — cited when it fits, more careful post-2020.

*Transformed* case studies (the three first-person transformations at large pre-Internet companies, contributed by co-authors) — these are the book's differentiator vs *Inspired* + *Empowered*. Sources: co-authors Lea Hickman, Chris Jones, Christian Idiodi, John Moore.

Historical cases from *Inspired* recur — but the recent essays lean on Google, Amazon, Netflix, Apple as the modern archetypes.

## 6. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER FRAMEWORKS

### vs. Melissa Perri — *Escaping the Build Trap* + *Product Operations*
- **Complementary. One altitude apart.** Cagan = organizational transformation / POM at the top level. Perri = operational infrastructure that makes the transformation stick + PM career development.
- Cagan **explicitly adopts Perri's definition** of Product Operations: *"the art of removing obstacles from evidence-based decision making."*
- Perri does not compete on the term *Product Operating Model* — cedes to Cagan.
- **When to reach for which:** the org needs top-level transformation → Cagan / *Transformed*. The PM function is broken / needs Product Ops scaffolding → Perri.
- See cross-linked skill [[escaping-the-build-trap]].

### vs. Teresa Torres — *Continuous Discovery Habits*
- **Highly compatible.** Cagan wrote the foreword to Torres's book.
- Torres's **Opportunity Solution Tree** and **weekly customer interview cadence** are the operational cadence for Cagan's *product discovery* principle.
- Torres coined **product trio** (PM + designer + engineer as one discovery unit); Cagan's team composition was already close but not identical.
- When the user's question is specifically about the discovery cadence — how to actually run continuous discovery — reach for Torres inside Cagan's frame.

### vs. Ryan Singer — *Shape Up* (Basecamp)
- Cagan does not frequently cite Shape Up, but the frames overlap on "shape the work before betting" and "appetite over estimate."
- Shape Up is a delivery/cycle discipline; Cagan's frame is broader (vision, strategy, discovery, delivery, culture, leadership). Composable but not equivalent.

### vs. Roger Martin — *Playing to Win*
- Cagan cites Martin approvingly for strategy-making. When Cagan says "product strategy," the underlying discipline (focus / insights / bets / active management) is Martin-compatible.
- The composition: Martin's cascade at the corporate level → Cagan's POM at the product-org level → team-level problems and outcomes flow down.
- See cross-linked skill [[playing-to-win]].

### vs. Amazon — *Working Backwards* (Bryar & Carr, 2021)
- Cagan cites Amazon frequently; the Working Backwards PR/FAQ is compatible with his visiontype approach — both start from the customer end-state and reason back.
- Divergence: Working Backwards is document-first (the PR/FAQ); Cagan is prototype-first. Both are valid; both fit the POM.

### vs. Jobs to Be Done (Christensen / Moesta / Kalbach)
- Cagan uses "customer problems" and "outcomes" as his primary units, not "jobs." But the underlying discipline — understand demand deeply before designing supply — is compatible.
- A team using JTBD inside Cagan's discovery is fine. A team using JTBD *instead of* the POM is missing the operating-model layer.

### vs. Lean Startup (Ries)
- Foundational compatible influence. Cagan retains **build-measure-learn** and MVP concept but treats "MVP" carefully — prefers *prototype* for discovery and *release* for delivery.
- Cagan endorses Ries's newer *Incorruptible* work on mission-locked governance (2026 essay [Great Products, Bad Companies](https://www.svpg.com/great-products-bad-companies/)).

### vs. SAFe / Scaled Agile Framework
- **Explicit anti-endorsement.** SAFe rewards output cadence and encodes command-and-control in scaled ceremony. Perri agrees. Cagan agrees.

### vs. Scrum
- Cagan is agnostic-to-hostile when Scrum becomes the point (ceremonies as religion) rather than a vehicle. Empowered teams sometimes use Scrum, sometimes don't — Cagan's argument is that the operating model is upstream of the process.

### vs. Hamilton Helmer — *7 Powers*
- Different altitude. Helmer = firm-level competitive advantage taxonomy (7 Powers). Cagan = product-org operating model. Composable: strong POM is *necessary but not sufficient* for building Power; Power is what the POM's compounding advantage becomes over years.
