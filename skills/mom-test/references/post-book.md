> - Published **two more books:** *The Workshop Survival Guide* (2019, with Devin Hunt) and *Write Useful Books* (2021).
> - Created a **Udemy course:** "Practical Customer Development" — 1.5 hours of video + 4 group workshops.
> - Appeared on **numerous podcasts** and given talks, refining and extending the ideas.
> - Published a **revised & expanded edition** via Simon & Schuster (2026), with beta reading in 2025.
> - Started writing about **Outcome-Oriented Communities** — his fourth book, exploring community-building through a problem-solving lens.
> - Maintained a **blog at robfitz.com** — 75+ posts on entrepreneurship, publishing, community, and idea selection.
> - Built **Help This Book** — a beta-reading tool at helpthisbook.com that operationalizes Write Useful Books.
>
> Most responses about "The Mom Test" pull from the 2013 book alone. This file captures the subsequent decade of refinement.

## 1. The Mom Test, Revised & Expanded Edition (Simon & Schuster, 2026)

A revised and expanded edition published by Simon & Schuster (no longer self-published), refreshing examples and language for today's startup, product, and creator economy while preserving the core rules. As of early 2025, the first ~30% was available for beta reading on Help This Book (helpthisbook.com).

**What's new (based on Fitzpatrick's YouTube announcement and 2026 podcast appearances):**
- Updated examples for modern contexts (remote interviews, creator economy, SaaS, AI products).
- Refreshed language — the original was written in 2013 when the startup landscape was different.
- The core three rules are unchanged. The framework's structure and principles remain the same.
- Published by a major publisher (Simon & Schuster) — a shift from the original self-published edition.

**What this means for the skill:** The 2026 edition does not change the method. It modernizes the presentation. The three rules, commitment/advancement, bad-data taxonomy, and segmentation approach are preserved.

## 2. The Workshop Survival Guide (2019, with Devin Hunt)

*The Workshop Survival Guide: How to design and teach educational workshops that work every time.* Co-authored with Devin Hunt and Adam Rosen.

**Relevance to The Mom Test:** The Workshop Survival Guide reveals Fitzpatrick's pedagogical philosophy — start with learning outcomes, not slides. Design the table of contents before writing content. Iterate on structure first, prose second. This philosophy extends to how The Mom Test is designed: the book itself is structured as a set of reusable rules, not a narrative to be read once.

**Key ideas that extend The Mom Test's worldview:**
- Workshops are products — they should be designed to solve a problem, tested with users, and iterated.
- Start with the outline, not the slides. The structure is the product; the content is the packaging.
- Educational design should respect how people actually learn, not how you want to teach.

**Source:** [Amazon: The Workshop Survival Guide](https://www.amazon.com/Workshop-Survival-Guide-educational-workshops/dp/1071344374); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52283670-the-workshop-survival-guide).

## 3. Write Useful Books (2021)

*Write Useful Books: A modern approach to designing and refining recommendable nonfiction.*

**Relevance to The Mom Test:** This book reveals how Fitzpatrick thinks about creating products that grow through word of mouth — which is exactly how The Mom Test grew. Key principles:

- **Treat nonfiction like a problem-solving product.** If it solves a sharp problem for a specific person, they'll recommend it.
- **Beta reading as customer development.** Test the book with readers before publishing, just like you'd test a product with customers before building. Fitzpatrick applies The Mom Test's own principles to book development.
- **Back-catalog strategy.** Design for longevity, not launch spikes. The Mom Test has sold 300k+ copies without an email list or fancy marketing — entirely through word of mouth over a decade.
- **Niche down.** Be the best in the world for a certain type of person at a certain moment in their journey, not for everyone.

> "If you treat non-fiction like a problem solving product, like something useful, and you really niche down about who it's for... people will talk about the problem." — Fitzpatrick, The Knowledge podcast with David Elikwu.

**Source:** [Write Useful Books — usefulbooks.com](https://www.usefulbooks.com/book); [The Knowledge podcast](https://theknowledge.io/robfitz/).

## 4. Udemy Course: Practical Customer Development (ongoing)

A 1.5-hour video course + 4 group workshops covering The Mom Test methodology.

**What the course adds beyond the book:**
- Video walkthroughs of common conversation failures.
- Workshop exercises designed to be done with the full founding team (product, sales, strategy).
- Covers tricky situations: marketplaces (where do you start?), hostile meetings, remote validation.
- 4.7/5 rating, ~5,700 students enrolled.

**Key insight from Fitzpatrick's course that extends the book:** The workshops are designed for teams, not individuals. The learning bottleneck in most startups is that one person talks to customers and then summarizes (badly) for the team. The course addresses this directly by designing exercises that the whole team does together.

**Source:** [Udemy: Practical Customer Development](https://www.udemy.com/course/practical-customer-development/).

## 5. Podcast Appearances and Interviews (2020-2026)

### Indie Hackers #154 (2020) — "The Right Way to Talk to People About Your Business"

Host: Courtland Allen. This is the richest single podcast source for extended Mom Test material.

**Key refinements:**
- **Idea selection matters enormously.** Fitzpatrick's biggest lesson from his career: "The difference is idea selection" between miserable companies and pleasant ones. Not every idea deserves the effort of customer development.
- **Passive income from books is real; from software, it's not.** Fitzpatrick's passive income comes from The Mom Test and Write Useful Books, not from software products.
- **Face-to-face is still the gold standard.** "I've never been able to learn successfully from anything other than face-to-face conversations." (This was pre-COVID; Fitzpatrick has since softened slightly on remote conversations, but still prefers in-person.)
- **Decision criteria for what to work on:** "Small team, flexible timing, customers I enjoy, products with purchase price, low support needs."

**Source:** [Indie Hackers podcast #154](https://www.indiehackers.com/podcast/154-rob-fitzpatrick-of-the-mom-test).

### Indie Hackers AMA (undated but post-2020)

**Key refinements:**
- **"Idea nihilism" is a real trap.** After his early startup failures, Fitzpatrick became too skeptical — dismissing every idea. He later recognized that many factors ARE predictable about ideas.
- **Learning vs. selling are two different modes.** For learning: avoid mentioning your idea at all. For sales: the goal is a concrete commitment or advancement. Don't mix the two.
- **The Mom Test grew entirely through word of mouth.** No email list, no fancy marketing, no ad spend. 300k+ copies sold via recommendation.

**Source:** [Indie Hackers AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/im-rob-fitzpatrick-i-wrote-the-mom-test-book-went-through-yc-s07-learned-how-to-do-sales-as-a-techie-and-live-off-full-passive-income-ama-4989966b85).

### Brian Rhea's "Bright & Early" Podcast (March 2026)

**Key refinements:**
- **The bowling-lane approach to interviews.** "Put the bumpers on to keep them out of the gutters — nudge them back on track when they wander, then dig deeper into specifics about their past." Conversations need gentle structure, not rigid scripts.
- **The graveyard of abandoned solutions.** Ask what solutions they've tried and abandoned. The reasons they quit are often more informative than what they're currently using.
- **Reduce uncertainty, don't eliminate it.** "Entrepreneurship can't be done by robots. You're building a foundation of insight for your visionary leap, not removing the leap itself." The Mom Test reduces risk; it doesn't replace founder judgment.
- **Riskiest assumption first.** Different businesses fail differently. Identify what's most likely to kill you and focus your conversations there.

**Source:** [Brian Rhea's "Bright & Early" podcast](https://brianrhea.com/podcast/the-mom-test-with-rob-fitzpatrick/).

### The Learning Leader Show #451 (undated)

Host: Ryan Hawk. Fitzpatrick discusses customer conversations and meeting leadership.

**Key refinements:**
- **Meeting leadership:** Prioritize attendee value by focusing on who participates and what they gain.
- **Learning outcomes:** Help participants gain wisdom, not just hear information.

**Source:** [The Learning Leader Show #451](https://learningleader.com/robfitzpatrick/).

### The Bootstrapped Founder (with Arvid Kahl)

**Key refinements:**
- **Originality over frequency.** Fitzpatrick prioritizes depth over publishing cadence.
- **Complexity as a moat.** The Useful Books flywheel (book + community + software + publishing) creates natural protection because "only a lunatic would do what we have done to get into such a small market."
- **AI and content creation.** Fitzpatrick expresses concern about AI-generated mediocrity commoditizing shallow work. His counter: "Tell me your experiential insights. Don't tell me what you read in someone else's book."

**Source:** [The Bootstrapped Founder — Tinkerers, Thinkers, and Teachers](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/rob-fitzpatrick-tinkerers-thinkers-and-teachers/).

### The Knowledge Podcast (with David Elikwu)

**Key refinements:**
- **"Make things that become more famous than you."** If you can only sell stuff because you're famous, you're not making good stuff. The Mom Test itself is more famous than Fitzpatrick — which he sees as the correct outcome.
- **The metal-detector approach to customer data.** "When customers tell you things, that's not the data... You still need to dig. Usually several layers underneath that comment."
- **Customer segmentation is load-bearing.** "When you look at data, you didn't have a clear sense of who you were trying to talk to or who your customer segment actually was."
- **Beta reading as customer development.** "The dream of customer development is to get to watch people doing their work with no bias... the quality of learning is so good."
- **Sunk cost as a useful illusion.** "You trick yourself in a good way with the Sunk Cost Fallacy. You've started it now so you gotta keep going." (Said with self-awareness, not as advice.)

**Source:** [The Knowledge — Make Things that Matter](https://theknowledge.io/robfitz/).
