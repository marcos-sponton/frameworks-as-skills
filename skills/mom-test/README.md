# The Mom Test

**By Rob Fitzpatrick** (2013; revised & expanded edition 2026)

A practical guide to having useful customer conversations — even when everyone is lying to you.

## What it is

The Mom Test is a set of rules for talking to customers so that you learn what they actually need, not what they think you want to hear. The name comes from the core insight: you should never ask anyone whether your business idea is good, because even your mom would lie to spare your feelings. Instead, ask about their life, their problems, and what they've already done about them.

## The three rules

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.**
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not generics about the future.**
3. **Talk less, listen more.**

## What this skill covers

- The three rules and how to apply them in real conversations.
- Bad data detection: compliments, fluff, and unanchored feature requests.
- Commitment and advancement: how to tell if a meeting produced real signal.
- Meeting framing: the five-element structure for opening a conversation.
- Segmentation: why mixed-audience conversations produce noise.
- Anti-patterns: the pitch disguised as research, "everyone says they'd buy it," and asking about the future instead of the past.

## About the author

Rob Fitzpatrick is a YCombinator alum (S07), serial entrepreneur, and author of three books: *The Mom Test*, *The Workshop Survival Guide* (2019), and *Write Useful Books* (2021). His work is taught at Harvard, MIT, and UCL, and used at Shopify, Skyscanner, and Seedcamp.
