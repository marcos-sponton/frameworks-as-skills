# Playing to Win — Applications

> When the cascade fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where Playing to Win fits well

- **Making or evaluating a market-entry decision.** The where-to-play / how-to-win pairing is exactly the shape of a market-entry question.
- **Critiquing an existing strategy document.** The 5 questions give you 5 diagnostic lenses.
- **Deciding between two or more product bets.** The cascade forces the trade-offs into the open — you can't say yes to both without honestly comparing which passes the can't/won't test.
- **Diagnosing why a strategy isn't working.** Usually one or two boxes are weak; the cascade tells you which.
- **Distributed strategy — chartering choices to sub-teams.** Martin's most developed post-book contribution.
- **Personal strategy — how you allocate your time.** Martin's most-viewed Practitioner Insights essay applies the cascade to individual knowledge-worker time.
- **Repositioning an existing product or brand.** The P&G/Olay pattern is directly applicable.
- **Deciding how to compete against a dominant incumbent.** The can't/won't test helps identify what the incumbent structurally can't or won't do.
- **Annual planning cycles that produce documents but not choices.** Playing to Win is the antidote to strategic-planning-theater.

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **Pre-product/market-fit search.** Martin himself said this on Lenny 2024: *"When you're a founder in a startup, you're making a bet... The research that we have done on the startups is that generally it doesn't work. But the ones that survive and prosper switch... there's a search like a truffle hound."* Pre-PMF is search, not choice. Use Lean Startup / continuous discovery / Rumelt's crux-finding instead. When the situation is early-stage discovery, redirect the user to those frameworks.

- **Purely operational or execution questions.** "How do we ship faster?" isn't a Playing to Win question. Martin explicitly separates strategy from ops. Suggest Theory of Constraints / Lean / SRE playbooks / EOS depending on the flavor.

- **Culture, hiring, or organizational-design questions without a competitive-choice angle.** The cascade can eventually intersect with these (via capabilities and management systems), but leading with the cascade will feel forced. Use org-design frameworks first, then bring in the cascade where competitive choice appears.

- **Crisis or turnaround situations where the diagnosis is more urgent than the choice.** If the org is on fire, do Rumelt-style diagnosis first (find the crux), then apply the cascade to structure the response. Playing to Win presupposes you know what your problem is.

- **Innovation portfolio decisions where you're picking N bets out of 100.** The cascade is meant for one integrated set of choices, not for portfolio ranking. Use ODI (Ulwick) or JTBD prioritization or Wardley Mapping instead.

- **When the user just wants a summary of Playing to Win.** Give them the book link. Don't run the cascade at them.

## Adjacent frameworks — when to reach for a different one

| If the user's situation is... | Reach for... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-PMF startup search | Lean Startup, Continuous Discovery, Rumelt crux-finding | Search, not choice |
| Diagnosis of what's broken before choosing | Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis / guiding policy / coherent action) | Rumelt front-loads diagnosis; Martin front-loads choice |
| Understanding industry structure | Porter's Five Forces | Porter's tool, complementary to WTP question |
| Mapping evolutionary landscape (tech, market) | Wardley Mapping | Positioning over time; Martin's cascade is more static snapshot |
| Portfolio prioritization | ODI (Ulwick), JTBD priority scoring | Cascade doesn't rank options; it commits to one |
| Business model design | Osterwalder Canvas | Sits alongside the cascade; useful artifact, not substitute |
| Pricing strategy | Monetizing Innovation (Ramanujam), value pricing frameworks | Cascade doesn't specify pricing mechanics |
| Positioning (marketing) | April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* | Positioning is downstream of HTW |
| Coming up with a compelling narrative for the market | Andy Raskin's *Strategic Narrative* | Narrative is downstream artifact of a chosen strategy |
| Change management once strategy is chosen | Kotter, ADKAR, EOS Traction | Cascade tells you what to change to; not how to change the org |

## How Playing to Win composes with other frameworks

Martin himself has published compatibility notes. Highlights:

- **Pairs well with Rumelt's *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* kernel.** Rumelt's diagnosis / guiding policy / coherent action maps well to Martin's problem → HTW → integrated activities. Use Rumelt to *find the crux* and Martin to *make the choice*. Warning: Martin publicly disagrees with some of Rumelt's specific cases (P&G in particular — see Lenny 2024) but respects the method.

- **Pairs well with Jobs-to-be-Done.** JTBD fits inside the WTP question (segment definition by job) and the HTW question (value proposition grounded in job progress).

- **Pairs well with Blue Ocean Strategy** but not as a substitute. Blue Ocean is a *how* — a search method for the Analogies vector. But Blue Ocean by itself lacks the choice-integrity check the cascade provides.

- **Pairs well with Business Model Generation (Osterwalder).** The Canvas spans the 5 cascade boxes. Useful as an artifact of a completed strategy, not as a substitute for the cascade.

- **Balanced Scorecard belongs in Management Systems**, not Strategy. Kaplan and Norton's tool measures execution; it doesn't choose direction.

- **Compatible with 7 Powers (Helmer).** Helmer's 7 powers (scale economies, network effects, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resources, process power) are different flavors of "can't/won't replicate." Useful vocabulary for the HTW box.

- **How Brands Grow (Byron Sharp) is downstream.** Sharp's laws are useful for measuring in-market performance and marketing execution. They aren't strategy choice — they inform once your strategy is set.

## Frameworks Martin has explicitly killed (see `heuristics.md` for details)

- SWOT — replace with problem → possibilities → WWHTBT.
- OKRs as strategy — use OKRs as an Enabling Management System, not as strategy.
- Vision/Mission/Values as strategy substitute.
- "Execution" as a category — replace with distributed strategy choices (Chartering).
- Resource-based view of the firm.
- Emergent-strategy nihilism.
- Strategic planning as substitute for strategy.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work, redirect gently but clearly.
