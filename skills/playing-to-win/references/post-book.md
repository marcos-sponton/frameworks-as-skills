# Playing to Win — Material posterior al libro

> **This is the differential of this skill.** The 2013 book *Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works* laid down the 5-question cascade. Since then, Roger Martin has published:
> - **~260 essays** in the *Playing to Win / Practitioner Insights* Medium series (five annual cycles, 2020–2026)
> - **HBR articles** including *The Big Lie of Strategic Planning* (2014) and *A Plan Is Not a Strategy* (2025)
> - **Three more books**: *Creating Great Choices* (2017), *When More Is Not Better* (2020), *A New Way to Think* (2022)
> - **Podcast appearances**: Lenny Rachitsky (2024), HBR IdeaCast, Knowledge@Wharton, and others
>
> Most Claude responses about "Playing to Win" pull from the book alone. This file captures the 12+ years of subsequent refinements — where Martin has updated his positions, developed new devices, killed adjacent frameworks, and clarified misapplications. Organized so you can pull the specific piece you need.

## Refinements and reversals

### The definition of strategy — updated in 2025

Martin explicitly walked back the definition he'd used for ~25 years.

- **Old (1995–early 2020s):** *"Choices → integrated set → positions the firm → sustainable advantage → superior financial returns."*
- **New (2025):** *"An integrated set of choices that compels a desired customer action."*

Three elements only:
1. **Choices** — pass the Opposite Choice Test.
2. **Integrated set** — five-question cascade, mutually reinforcing.
3. **Compels desired customer action** — enough volume AND enough pricing to generate returns.

**Why the change:** *"The one essential element over which the company has zero control is the customer."* Strategy's job is configuring what you control to influence the one thing you don't.

Since this is the anchor of the whole cascade, use the new definition when the user asks "what is strategy?"

**Source:** [Revisiting My Definition of Strategy](https://rogermartin.medium.com/revisiting-my-definition-of-strategy-76a838aba818)

### Where-to-Play has 5 elements, not 3

The book emphasized geography, offering, and customer segment. Martin now insists on 5, adding two that practitioners routinely skip:

1. **Geography** — global / national / regional / local
2. **Offering** — the specific product or service
3. **Customer segment** — mainstream / luxury / budget / young / avant-garde
4. **Distribution channel** — direct vs. intermediated (Dell going direct; P&G going through retail were strategic bets)
5. **Value system stage** (the hidden one) — how much of the value chain you own. Ford vertically integrated vs. Apple + Foxconn were opposite value-system choices.

**Outsource test** (for stage 5): only outsource if (a) the outside party creates more value or same at lower cost, (b) they can't hold you up, (c) you don't inadvertently create a competitor. All three must be true.

**Source:** [The Hidden Where-to-Play Element in Strategy](https://rogermartin.medium.com/the-hidden-where-to-play-element-in-strategy-7cb355c9f9b7)

### From "theory of How-to-Win" to "portrait of How-to-Win"

The book talked about developing a *theory* of how you'll win. Martin has since replaced the metaphor with **portrait**. The point of the change: a portrait shows the *connections* between elements (nodes AND edges), while a "theory" or "list" hides them.

Bad HTW = disconnected list of activities that each look sensible.
Good HTW = a portrait where links matter as much as nodes. Draw the connecting arrows. If the story doesn't hold together as narrative, the pixels are empty calories.

**Source:** [Developing a Powerful How-to-Win](https://rogermartin.medium.com/developing-a-powerful-how-to-win-b28227a184fd)

### Why HTW is uniquely hard — and what to do

The book didn't dwell on why How-to-Win is where most strategies collapse. Martin has since been explicit:

- **Upstream flaws poison HTW.** A vague WA + a WTP chosen alone leaves you needing a HTW that doesn't exist. Fix upstream first.
- **The bar is higher.** For WA and WTP you have latitude. For HTW you must be *superior to everyone else in your chosen WTP*.
- **The management delusion.** People believe "doing the same as everyone else but trying harder" produces superior results. It doesn't.
- **The system doesn't help.** Capital markets punish uniqueness. Business schools teach analysis, not creation. Consultants push cost cutting.

**Prescription:** park your WA for later refinement; don't lock WTP alone; use the three creative search vectors; expect hard iterative thinking.

**Source:** [Why the How-to-Win Strategy Choice is So Hard](https://rogermartin.medium.com/why-the-how-to-win-strategy-choice-is-so-hard-8de222d62f5c)

### Value Proposition ≠ Competitive Advantage

An untangling Martin has emphasized post-book because practitioners collapse the two.

- **Competitive Advantage** = the *method* by which you deliver value in ways competitors *can't* or *won't* replicate. INPUT.
- **Value Proposition** = the *output* the market perceives — value delivered relative to cost.

**Can't test:** competitors lack the Must-Have Capabilities (Westlaw's 100-year keyword-searchable database).
**Won't test:** competitors have capability but refuse to use it (Tesla — legacy OEMs wouldn't cannibalize combustion margins).

**Warning:** clever value propositions competitors can copy tomorrow are value props, not competitive advantages.

**Source:** [Untangling Value Proposition & Competitive Advantage](https://rogermartin.medium.com/untangling-value-proposition-competitive-advantage-c3ab3ff91151)

## New material — added after the book

### WWHTBT — "What Would Have to Be True?"

Martin invented WWHTBT in 1994 but has elevated it post-book as *the* most valuable single device in the toolkit.

**How it works:**
1. Instead of asking "what is true?", ask *"what would have to be true for this option to be the right choice?"*
2. Separates *logic* from *data*. Teams agree on logic first, then collaboratively test data. Kills adversarial data-fights.
3. Items least likely to be true = **Barriers to Choice (BTC)**. Test those first.
4. **Reverse-engineer the status quo too.** This is Martin's biggest post-book correction: without it, the status quo implicitly feels riskless. It's not — it's just differently risky.
5. Every morning ritual: *"Is our WWHTBT still true?"* If yes, work. If no, retool now.

**Source:** [What Would Have to be True?](https://rogermartin.medium.com/what-would-have-to-be-true-83dac5bd2189)

### The three creative search vectors for HTW

An operational addition Martin has developed post-book. When generating HTW options, search along all three:

**1. Analogies** — every problem has been solved somewhere else. P&G applied *chemotherapy's targeted attack* to stain bleaching. Dyson applied *sawmill cyclone tech* to vacuums. Olay borrowed *Shiseido's seven-step regimen*. Warning: avoid literality — the analogy has to be similar-but-not-exact.

**2. Tradeoffs (Integrative Thinking)** — attack either/or framing:
- **Hidden Gem** — combine opposites. Four Seasons = small-hotel intimacy + large-hotel amenities.
- **Double Down** — intensify one dimension to transcend the tradeoff.
- **Decomposition** — split into components with different solutions. P&G's Connect & Develop separated invention from commercialization.

**3. Anomalies** — hunt outliers as leading indicators. 1970s hackers foreshadowed PCs. Roddick's Body Shop customers foreshadowed values-driven consumption.

**Source:** [Generating Creative Strategy Possibilities](https://rogermartin.medium.com/generating-creative-strategy-possibilities-cb5545206e85)

### Strategy Choice Chartering — the replacement for "execution"

Martin's most systematic post-book contribution to the "how does strategy actually cascade" question. He killed the word "execution" and replaced it with **Chartering**.

**Six elements:**
1. Make only your best choices — the ones your position/experience makes you uniquely qualified for.
2. Explain the strategic choice — full *why*, not just *what*.
3. Identify necessary features of subordinates' choices — constraints their choice must satisfy to reinforce yours.
4. Offer genuine help via the Responsibility Ladder.
5. Signal willingness to revisit your own choice if theirs reveals it flawed.
6. Enable cascading — subordinates charter their own team's choices.

**The Responsibility Ladder** (lowest to highest team confidence):
- You decide entirely / they observe (apprentice)
- They structure the choice, need your guidance
- They present possibilities
- They come back with a recommendation
- They decline help and just inform you afterward

**Anti-pattern:** delegation without chartering — *"logically bankrupt, delusional, and insulting."*

**Sources:** [Strategy Choice Chartering](https://rogermartin.medium.com/a-primer-on-strategy-choice-chartering-0fae9b42422b) · [Strategy and Leadership #3](https://rogermartin.medium.com/strategy-and-leadership-3-f8a219bc403c)

### Martin's personal 4-step strategy process (2025)

How he actually does strategy with clients. Published Aug 2025.

**Step 1 — Get myself psyched up**
- Project forward the current path. Where does the org end up in 5–10 years if nothing changes?
- Remind yourself of the inherent goodness/fairness of people — customers, employees, suppliers respond fairly to performance.

**Step 2 — Focus on the problem to be solved**
- Focus like a laser on the specific problem (Monitor's problem was *obscurity*; Rotman's was *irrelevance*; Skoll's was *field undefined*).
- Specify the form of the desired outcome — the "objective function" a solution must satisfy. No strategy or WA yet — just the shape solutions must take.
- Remind yourself what you don't control.

**Step 3 — Imagine possibility portraits**
- Strategy is a heuristic, not an algorithm. It's a practice.
- Target a picture, not a list. Test with can't/won't.
- Deploy the three search vectors.

**Step 4 — Make, Enforce, Adjust**
- **Make:** *"If you think you can avoid the risk of making a choice by not explicitly making one, not choosing is a choice — and it is your strategy choice."*
- **Enforce:** every board meeting, resist off-strategy resource allocation. *"If you aren't willing to enforce your strategy, then get out of the way and give someone else that job."*
- **Adjust:** flex when WWHTBT no longer holds, with principle, not convenience. Never let dilution masquerade as adjustment.

**Warning:** *"If you stroll into Step Three without Steps One and Two, you will produce crap."*

**Sources:** [How I Do Strategy — Part One](https://rogermartin.medium.com/how-i-do-strategy-be1783fe681a) · [Part Two](https://rogermartin.medium.com/how-i-do-strategy-part-two-87b52034eddd)

### The Strategic Choice Structuring Process (SCSP) — 5th generation

For teams that want the facilitated flow:

**Problem → Possibilities → Barriers to Choice → Test Design → Assessment → Choice**

Vocabulary shifts vs. earlier versions:
- "Possibilities" not "options" — invites creativity.
- "Problems" not "issues" — names the pain honestly.
- Dedicated Test Design step — designing a fair decisive test is high-skill.
- "Assessment" not "Testing & Transformation" — avoids confusion with corporate transformation programs.

Governing principle: *"If it doesn't make sense for those you are guiding, it doesn't matter that it might be theoretically sound."*

**Source:** [The Evolution of the SCSP](https://rogermartin.medium.com/the-evolution-of-the-strategic-choice-structuring-process-653521519014)

### Fixing Strategy — the 5 conventions (Dec 2025)

Martin's current agenda for the field:

1. **Performed by general managers.** Not consultants, not strategy departments.
2. **Driven by imagination.** Business is a domain where things can be other than they are.
3. **Guided by principles, not rigid rules.** Barbossa: *"the code is more what you'd call guidelines than actual rules."* Example principle: "customers' satisfaction trumps our company's convenience."
4. **Iterative.** *"You don't bet the farm. But you aren't too terrified to bet."* Sharp built one Four Seasons. Lafley tested Olay Total Effects. Knudstorp greenlit *one* Lego Movie.
5. **Distributed.** Kill *"the fantasy of the geniuses at the top making strategy choices and then the hapless drones below executing."*

**Source:** [Fixing Strategy](https://rogermartin.medium.com/fixing-strategy-560e4c09ee0d)

### Applying Playing to Win to yourself — personal strategy

Top-viewed piece across 5 years of Practitioner Insights.

- In the knowledge economy, human capacity is fixed and non-fungible. Time allocation IS the strategic choice.
- **Busyness is a signal, not a badge.** Signal that your personal WTP/HTW is weak.
- **Reverse-engineer your calendar.** What does it say your WTP actually is?
- Delegate lower-value work to people whose MHC beats yours in that area. Use an intensive context transfer ("Vulcan Mind Meld"), then pull back to 5 days/year on that thing.
- Enabling Management Systems (e.g., a chief of staff / EA) institutionalize your allocation.

**Source:** [Being Too Busy Means Your Personal Strategy Sucks](https://rogermartin.medium.com/being-too-busy-means-your-personal-strategy-sucks-8f24b9327179)

## Adjacent frameworks Martin has commented on since 2013

Compatibility mapping he's published in various essays:

- **Rumelt "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy"** — broadly compatible. Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions) maps to problem → HTW → integrated activities. Martin publicly disagrees with some of Rumelt's specific cases (P&G in particular — see Lenny 2024 where Martin says *"What he said about Procter and Gamble had zero to do with reality, like zero"*) but respects the method.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done** — fits inside WTP (segment definition) and HTW (value proposition).
- **Blue Ocean** — a *how* for the Analogies vector; but Blue Ocean alone lacks the choice-integrity check Playing to Win provides.
- **Business Model Generation (Osterwalder)** — spans all five cascade boxes; useful as an artifact, not a substitute.
- **Balanced Scorecard** — belongs in Management Systems, not Strategy.
- **"How Brands Grow" (Byron Sharp)** — useful for measuring in-market outcome, not for choice-making.
- **7 Powers (Helmer)** — compatible with HTW as different flavors of "can't replicate."
- **The Goal (Goldratt)** — bounded compatibility.
- **Integrative Thinking / "The Opposable Mind"** — his own 2007 book; the toolkit for the Tradeoffs vector.
- **"Creating Great Choices" (2017)** — the concrete methodology for Integrative Thinking-in-strategy.
- **"A New Way to Think" (2022)** — collected essays on 14 mental models that get strategy wrong.

## Frameworks Martin has explicitly killed

- **SWOT** — *"toss it into the ashbin of strategy history."* Structural problem: you can't assess strength without a WTP/HTW context.
- **OKRs as strategy** — desire is not strategy; OKRs skip WTP/HTW.
- **"Execution" as a concept** — there are only strategy choices made at various levels; use Chartering.
- **Resource-based view of the firm** — academic; useless in practice.
- **Emergent-strategy nihilism** — VUCA is not an excuse to avoid making a choice.
- **Strategic planning as substitute for strategy** — the Big Lie.
