# Playing to Win — Prompt templates

> Copy-paste templates users can adapt. Each starts with a user situation and invokes the skill in a shape Claude can execute well.

## Start a Playing to Win session from scratch

```
I want to work through Playing to Win on {{my situation}}.

Context:
- Company / product: {{describe}}
- Stage: {{seed / growth / mature — or if pre-PMF, note that explicitly}}
- Current thinking: {{3-5 sentences of what you already believe}}
- Where I'm stuck: {{one sentence — often "how to win" or "where to play"}}

Please walk me through the 5 questions integrated (matched pair between WTP and HTW).
When my answers sound weak or generic, challenge them using Roger Martin's explicit critiques.
Push me toward the can't/won't test on HTW.
Cite sources when you introduce a specific device.
```

## Critique a strategy document I already wrote

```
Here's a strategy doc I drafted:

{{paste content or attach file}}

Please critique it against Playing to Win. Specifically:
1. Which of the 5 questions is missing or weak?
2. Which parts sound like Martin's explicit anti-patterns (SWOT-style, OKRs-as-strategy, growth-AND-profit, 17 priorities, strategic-planning-as-strategy)?
3. Where does the WTP–HTW matched pair break?
4. What can't/won't test would my HTW pass?
5. What would Martin likely push back on hardest?
6. What's my strongest point that's worth doubling down on?
```

## Help me answer a specific question in the cascade

### For Where-to-Play

```
I'm working on where-to-play for {{my situation}}. Current thinking: {{paste}}.

Push me on this:
- Am I specific enough about geography, offering, customer segment, channel, AND value-system stage?
- Am I entering because "the market is big" or because "customers are bereft"?
- Have I paired this WTP with a plausible HTW? If not, is this WTP still viable?
```

### For How-to-Win

```
I'm working on how-to-win for {{my situation}}. My WTP is {{paste}}. My current HTW is {{paste}}.

Push me on this:
- Is my HTW cost or differentiation, or am I stuck in the middle?
- What can't or won't competitors do that I can/will?
- Draw the portrait — what capabilities reinforce which others? Is this a pixel-list or a connected system?
- Deploy the three creative search vectors (analogies, tradeoffs, anomalies) — what's my strongest option in each?
```

### For Capabilities

```
Given the WTP + HTW I've chosen ({{paste}}), what capabilities must I build?

Push me on:
- Which capabilities reinforce each other? Draw the arrows, not the list.
- Which are copyable individually, and which only work as a system?
- What's my Southwest / Four Seasons / Westlaw analog — the reinforcing set that competitors can't dismantle without becoming a different company?
```

### For Management Systems

```
Given my capabilities ({{paste}}), what management systems must I put in place?

Push me on:
- What hires, structures, measures, incentives, rituals build and maintain each capability over years?
- Am I confusing management systems (e.g., OKRs, KPIs) with strategy itself?
- Is my EMS strong enough to prevent backsliding, or will the capabilities decay in one management cycle?
```

## Reverse-engineer a competitor as icebreaker

```
Help me reverse-engineer {{competitor name}}'s strategy using Playing to Win.

Follow Martin's method:
1. Start from Where-to-Play (most observable from outside): what geography, offerings, segments, channels, value-system stage?
2. Infer How-to-Win: cost or differentiation? What's their can't/won't moat?
3. Infer Winning Aspiration from choices.
4. Infer Capabilities from where they invest disproportionately.
5. Infer Management Systems from culture, hiring, policies.

Focus on differences from the industry norm, not similarities. And warn me when I'm about to fall into the convergence trap (copying what makes them them).
```

## Run WWHTBT on an option

```
I'm considering {{option}} for {{context}}.

Apply the WWHTBT method:
1. What would have to be true for this to be the right choice?
2. Which of those items am I least confident in — those are my Barriers to Choice.
3. Also do WWHTBT for the status quo (staying the current course). What's implicitly assumed there? What's least likely to still be true?
4. Which BTC should we test first, and how would we design the test?
```

## Compare Playing to Win vs. an adjacent framework

```
I'm deciding whether to use Playing to Win or {{other framework — e.g., Rumelt's kernel, Wardley Mapping, Porter's Five Forces}} for {{my situation}}.

Help me pick. If the answer is "use both, in this sequence", tell me the sequence. If the answer is "neither, use something else entirely", tell me that too.
```

## Critique my personal calendar as strategy

```
Here's what a typical week looks like for me: {{paste calendar summary}}.

Apply Martin's personal Playing to Win from "Being Too Busy Means Your Personal Strategy Sucks":
1. What does my calendar reveal about my actual WTP (where I'm actually playing)?
2. Does it match my stated aspiration?
3. What activities have MHC (must-have-capabilities) where I am distinctive vs. where someone else's would be better?
4. What could I delegate via chartering, and what EMS would prevent me from backsliding?
```

## Pivot decision

```
I'm considering pivoting {{current path}} → {{proposed pivot}} because {{reason}}.

Apply Martin's pivot criteria from "Playing to Win & Pivoting":
1. Is my WWHTBT actually proven invalid — customers demonstrably not behaving as my theory predicted?
2. Or am I pivoting because things aren't working yet (impatience)?
3. If I pivot, does the new WTP/HTW pair pass its own can't/won't test?
4. Yamamoto-at-Midway question: am I about to switch weapons mid-battle without gathering enough signal?
```
