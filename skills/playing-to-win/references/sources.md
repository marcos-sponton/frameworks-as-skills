# Playing to Win — Sources

> Every source consulted while building this skill, with links. Users should be able to verify anything the skill says.

## Primary text

- **Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works** — A.G. Lafley & Roger L. Martin. Harvard Business Review Press, 2013. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Playing-Win-Strategy-Really-Works/dp/1422187396) · [HBR Store](https://store.hbr.org/product/playing-to-win-how-strategy-really-works/10714). The canonical source. Read it.

## Other books by Roger Martin

- **The Opposable Mind** (2007) — Integrative Thinking foundations.
- **The Design of Business** (2009) — design thinking meets business.
- **Fixing the Game** (2011) — critique of shareholder capitalism.
- **Creating Great Choices** (2017, with Jennifer Riel) — Integrative Thinking applied to choice.
- **When More Is Not Better** (2020) — post-recession critique.
- **A New Way to Think** (2022) — 14 mental models that get strategy wrong.

## HBR articles

- **The Big Lie of Strategic Planning** — Martin, HBR, January 2014. [https://hbr.org/2014/01/the-big-lie-of-strategic-planning](https://hbr.org/2014/01/the-big-lie-of-strategic-planning). The canonical attack on strategic planning.
- **A Plan Is Not a Strategy** — Martin, HBR, May 2025. [https://hbr.org/2025/05/a-plan-is-not-a-strategy](https://hbr.org/2025/05/a-plan-is-not-a-strategy). Updated version of the 2014 argument.
- **A Playbook for Strategy: The Five Essential Questions at the Heart of Any Winning Strategy** — Martin, HBR, 2013. The book-companion piece.
- **Strategy vs. Execution** — Martin, HBR, 2017.
- **Don't Let Strategy Become Planning** — Martin, HBR.

## What this skill did NOT consult (gaps)

Being explicit so users can PR to close them.

- **The full text of Martin's post-2013 books** (*Creating Great Choices*, *When More Is Not Better*, *A New Way to Think*). Only summaries and cited excerpts.
- **Individual essays from 2020 that predate the Year I Review compilation.**
- **Year III Review** — search didn't return it; may not exist as a compilation, only as individual essays.
- **YouTube lectures at Rotman School and Thinkers50 events** — referenced but not transcribed.
- **A.G. Lafley's writings and interviews** as co-author. Playing to Win is a joint work; Lafley's individual perspective (interviews after his P&G tenure, his book *The Game-Changer*, etc.) would enrich the skill.
- **Recent podcast appearances beyond Lenny 2024.**
- **Community critiques and applications** — practitioners applying PtW in the wild often surface gotchas Martin doesn't.
