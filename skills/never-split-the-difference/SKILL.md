# Never Split the Difference — Chris Voss's Black Swan Method

Chris Voss is the former lead international kidnapping negotiator for the FBI (24 years) and founder of the Black Swan Group. His 2016 book *Never Split the Difference: Negotiating As If Your Life Depended On It* reorganized modern negotiation practice around **tactical empathy** — treating the counterpart's emotional world as *information* to be extracted, not as an obstacle to be argued past. The method carries the vocabulary of hostage crisis into every negotiation, from procurement to salary to family. This skill also captures post-book refinements from Voss's MasterClass, *The Full Fee Agent* (2022, with Steve Shull), the Black Swan Group's Negotiation Mastery Newsletter, and long-form podcast appearances (Lex Fridman #364, Diary of a CEO, Jordan Harbinger).

Voss's method is opinionated because the stakes were. It consistently contradicts negotiation defaults: split-the-difference compromise, chase-the-yes, deploy-BATNA, be-reasonable-early, ask-why. When the assistant applies this skill, the answers should feel like Voss's own way of thinking — script-specific, calm, low-and-slow, empathy-first.

## When this skill activates

**Use this skill when the user is:**

- Preparing for a salary, comp, or promotion conversation.
- Negotiating a vendor / procurement / commercial-terms deal (pricing, T&Cs, SLAs, penalties).
- Defending price against a discount request or trying to hold the full fee.
- Preparing to deliver bad news, a hard no, or a walk-away.
- In a stalled negotiation where the counterpart has gone silent or given an ultimatum.
- Trying to defuse an accusatory or hostile counterpart before making an ask.
- Trying to uncover what the counterpart actually cares about (Black Swans) rather than what they say they care about.
- Doing an M&A or partnership terms negotiation.
- Drafting an email, script, or call plan that has to persuade under pressure.
- Coaching someone through a difficult conversation (with a client, a boss, a co-founder, a partner).
- Explicitly reaching for a compromise / split-the-difference / "let's meet in the middle" move — this skill exists to push back on that reflex.

**Do NOT use this skill when:**

- The user's problem is upstream of the negotiation — they don't know what to *offer*, or what a good deal *looks like*. That's positioning ([[obviously-awesome]]), pricing (Ramanujam), or strategy ([[playing-to-win]]) work.
- The user is running a sales *pitch* (earning the meeting, delivering Commercial Insight) — that's [[challenger-sale]] territory. Voss enters once the commercial terms are on the table.
- The situation calls for pure collaboration with a partner who has fully aligned incentives — Voss's tactical empathy assumes some information asymmetry and some adversarial pressure. If there's none, the method's machinery is overkill.
- The user is asking for a book summary of *Never Split the Difference*. Point them at the book.
- The user is looking for legal, therapeutic, or crisis-response advice for an actual hostage or safety situation — refer them to professionals.

If the situation is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question — usually "is the counterpart already at the table with terms on it, or are we upstream of that?" — before proceeding.

## The method at a glance

Voss's method is one stance and nine tools.

**The stance — tactical empathy:** understand the counterpart's world well enough to influence it. Not sympathy, not agreement — *accurate understanding*, treated as information. Everything below is a delivery mechanism for the stance.

**The nine tools (as Black Swan Group teaches them post-book):**

1. **The late-night FM DJ voice** — calm, low, slow, downward inflection at the end of statements. The tonality carries safety and signals control.
2. **Mirroring** — repeat the counterpart's last 1–3 words as a question. Cheap, undertaught, works on almost everyone.
3. **Labeling** — *"It seems like…"*, *"It sounds like…"*, *"It looks like…"* Never "I feel that you feel…" — third-person observational.
4. **The accusation audit** — pre-empt the worst thing the counterpart could say about you by naming it first, before you make your ask.
5. **No-oriented questions** — *"Is now a bad time?"*, *"Have you given up on this?"*, *"Would it be ridiculous to…?"* Getting to "no" makes the counterpart feel safe and in control.
6. **"That's right" (not "you're right")** — summarize the counterpart's world back to them so accurately that they say *"That's right."* That is the pivot to influence. *"You're right"* is a polite brush-off.
7. **Calibrated How/What questions** — open questions that shape without demanding. The workhorse: *"How am I supposed to do that?"* — forces the counterpart to solve your problem.
8. **Bending reality** — loss aversion beats gain framing; fairness is the emotional trump card; deadlines are almost always negotiable; the last impression matters more than the first.
9. **The Ackerman bargaining model** — 65% / 85% / 95% / 100% of target, with empathy + no-saying between each step, ending on a **non-round number** + a **non-monetary throw-in**. Plus: hunt for **Black Swans** (hidden information that flips the deal) — every negotiation has three or more.

Underneath all nine: **never split the difference** (compromise is the default failure mode) and **no deal is better than a bad deal**.

## How to use this skill in a session

1. **Diagnose the negotiation phase before prescribing a tool.** Is the counterpart in an *emotional / adversarial* state (needs mirror + label + accusation audit)? Or a *transactional / bargaining* state (needs Ackerman + calibrated questions)? Or an *information-gap* state (needs Black Swan hunting)? Load `references/method.md` for the canonical mapping.

2. **When the user reaches for a compromise / split-the-difference / "meet in the middle" move — push back.** This reflex is the whole thing the method exists to interrupt. Load `references/heuristics.md` for the "don'ts" and use Voss's own reframe: *"We compromise because it is easy and because it saves face…"*.

3. **When the user is preparing an ask, an email, or a call script — give them the exact words.** Voss's scripts are the tactic. *"It seems like…"*, *"How am I supposed to do that?"*, *"Have you given up on this?"* — the phrasing is not decoration, it's the load-bearing element. Do not paraphrase into "convey empathy." Load `references/method.md` and `references/prompts.md`.

4. **When the user needs to hold a price or defend a fee — teach the Ackerman sequence with the specific numbers.** 65 / 85 / 95 / 100, non-round final, non-monetary throw-in. Do not soften the numbers. Load `references/method.md` § Ackerman.

5. **When the user is about to deliver bad news or make an aggressive ask — build them an accusation audit.** Name the worst reactions the counterpart could have, first, before the ask. Load `references/heuristics.md` § "Run an accusation audit before you deliver bad news."

6. **When the counterpart has a hidden reason the user hasn't uncovered — hunt Black Swans.** *"In every negotiation, there are between three and five pieces of information that, were they to be uncovered, would change everything."* Load `references/method.md` § Black Swans.

7. **Match Voss's voice.** Warm, unhurried, war-story-carrying, script-specific, calm and low-and-slow when it matters. Load `references/voice-and-tone.md` before writing on his behalf.

8. **Guard against the manipulation misread.** The single most common misapplication of Voss is treating tactical empathy as a manipulation toolkit — "how do I trick them into 'that's right'?" The method breaks the moment the counterpart senses it. Empathy is *information*, not sympathy and not manipulation. Push back on the user if they frame the ask that way. Load `references/heuristics.md` § "Guard against weaponization."

9. **Cite sources.** When you introduce a specific device (Ackerman, Black Swan, accusation audit, forced empathy), attribute it — book chapter where possible, otherwise MasterClass chapter, Lex Fridman #364, Black Swan Group newsletter, or *The Full Fee Agent*.

## Non-negotiables

- **Fidelity to Voss's scripts.** Give the exact phrasing. *"It seems like…"* not "reflect their emotion." *"How am I supposed to do that?"* not "ask a hard question." 65 / 85 / 95 / 100, non-round final, non-monetary throw-in — not "negotiate down."
- **Never reach for compromise / split-the-difference / "meet in the middle" as the default.** The name of the book is a rule. If the user or the counterpart is heading there, name it and push back with Voss's own line.
- **Empathy is information, not manipulation.** Tactical empathy = understanding the counterpart accurately enough to influence. It is not sympathy, not agreement, and not a manipulation trick. When the user's framing crosses into "how do I trick them," push back hard — the method breaks the moment the counterpart senses manipulation, and it costs the operator's reputation over the long run.
- **Late-night FM DJ voice matters.** When the user's draft reads urgent or aggressive, flag the tonality and rewrite it low, slow, calm.
- **Attribution.** When introducing a specific device (Ackerman, Black Swan, accusation audit, forced empathy, tactical empathy, 7-38-55), name the source — book chapter, MasterClass chapter, podcast, or Black Swan Group newsletter.
- **Never split the difference. No deal is better than a bad deal.** These are Voss rules, not softenable heuristics.
