# Continuous Discovery Habits — Applications

> When Torres's method fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where the method fits well

- **Setting up a weekly customer interview cadence** with a product team that currently does project-based discovery (or none). Torres's method is purpose-built for this — the 5 habits + the 30-minute-per-week onboarding move.
- **Building or updating an Opportunity Solution Tree** — Outcome → Opportunities → Solutions → Assumption Tests. Torres's canonical artifact.
- **Coaching interview technique** — story-based, past-behavior-only, timeline-walk, redirect-generalizations. Torres's rubric (from the Interview Coach) is directly applicable.
- **Framing a product outcome** at the trio's altitude (vs a business outcome outside their control, vs an output masquerading as an outcome). The 8 mistakes essay is the reference.
- **Mapping assumptions across all 5 categories** (desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, ethical) when the team is about to commit to building a solution.
- **Designing small assumption tests** instead of jumping to full A/B experiments — especially when the user says "we don't have time" (Torres's "next hour" rule).
- **Forming or fixing a product trio** — bringing engineer and designer into the discovery loop, killing the PM-solo pattern.
- **Diagnosing "we already do this"** — when a team claims they do continuous discovery but it's actually project-based, ad hoc, or PM-solo. The 4 diagnostic questions surface the truth.
- **Reframing "opportunities" that are features in disguise** — the "is there more than one way?" test.
- **Deciding how AI fits into a discovery cadence** — Torres's 2025–2026 material is directly applicable. AI accelerates the trio, doesn't replace customer contact.
- **Auditing whether a product organization is *actually* doing continuous discovery** or just using the vocabulary.

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **Pre-product/market-fit startups without a product yet.** Torres's method assumes there's a product with users and a defined product outcome to anchor discovery against. Solo founders and seed-stage teams in search phase need Lean Startup (Ries) or JTBD (Moesta/Kalbach) — search, not habit. Come back to Torres once there's a product outcome to move. See [[lean-startup]].

- **Top-level organizational transformation to become "product-led".** Torres cedes this altitude to Marty Cagan (*Transformed*, 2024). If the user is asking "how do we transform the entire company at the exec level?", credit Cagan and position Torres's habits as the weekly practice inside Cagan's operating model. See [[inspired]].

- **PM function health, career ladders, or the operational scaffolding around discovery** (data & insights infra, research ops, process & governance). That's Melissa Perri's altitude. See [[escaping-the-build-trap]].

- **Business strategy from scratch (where to play, how to win).** Torres assumes the strategy exists and product outcomes have been set. If the user is doing business strategy, use Playing to Win (Martin) or Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Rumelt) *first*, then bring Torres in for the discovery layer.

- **Pure quantitative research / experiment design** where the assumption has been narrowed to a hypothesis and the question is picking a statistical test or setting up an A/B split. Torres works upstream of that.

- **Deep specialist ethnographic or longitudinal studies.** Torres's method is for the trio's weekly practice; deep specialist studies are the domain of dedicated user researchers. Both, not either.

- **The user just wants a summary of *Continuous Discovery Habits*.** Give them the book link. Don't run the method at them.

- **When the user is looking for a rigid recipe.** Torres actively refuses to be dogmatized. If they want "the exact 12-step process to run every week", reframe toward habit — small, sustainable, iterated.

## How Torres composes with other frameworks

Torres has commented on adjacencies in podcasts and essays. Highlights:

### Composes well with Cagan (complementary, one altitude apart)

Cagan (*Transformed*, 2024) owns the top-level phrase **Product Operating Model** and the exec-level transformation conversation. Torres operates one altitude below — the weekly discovery practice inside Cagan's operating model.

**Torres explicitly writes about POM adoption:**
- [The Product Operating Model Explained: From Pilot Teams to Full Transformation](https://www.producttalk.org/the-product-operating-model/), Oct 2025.
- [Is Your Organization Ready to Adopt the Product Operating Model?](https://www.producttalk.org/organizational-readiness/), 2025.

**They don't compete; they compose:**
- Use Cagan to align exec team on the transformation.
- Use Torres to build the trio's weekly discovery practice inside Cagan's operating model.

### Composes well with Perri (complementary, adjacent scaffolding)

Melissa Perri explicitly endorses Torres — Product Thinking Podcast Ep 269 (May 2026): "Continuous Discovery Habits That Actually Work". Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree fits inside Perri's Product Kata / problem-exploration → solution-experimentation loop.

- **Torres** = the tactical discovery cadence — weekly interviews, OST, assumption tests, trio dynamics.
- **Perri** = the strategic scaffolding around that cadence — Strategic Intent above (so the outcome has direction), Product Ops as fuel (so the trio has data + research ops + process infra), PM career progression to sustain it.

A PM trio running Torres's habits inside Perri's frame is Perri's ideal PM organization. See [[escaping-the-build-trap]].

### Composes with Cagan + Perri as the "product-led stack"

The three compose vertically:
- **Cagan** (top) — the org architecture.
- **Perri** (middle) — the PM function health + operational scaffolding.
- **Torres** (bottom, close to the team) — the weekly discovery practice.

A user asking "we want to be product-led — where do we start?" usually needs all three in sequence:
1. Cagan — what does product-led even mean at the org level?
2. Perri — do we have the strategic clarity + operational infrastructure to support product-led work?
3. Torres — is each trio running the weekly discovery habits?

### Composes well with Singer / Shape Up (compatible on delivery side)

Shape Up's "appetite over estimate" and "shaping before betting" are consistent with Torres's small-assumption-tests-before-build. But Shape Up is a *delivery cadence*; Torres's method is *discovery cadence*.

Use both: Shape Up as the delivery mechanism, Torres as the discovery cadence that feeds the shaping.

### Composes well with JTBD (Christensen / Moesta / Kalbach)

Highly compatible in substance; Torres refuses to use JTBD vocabulary explicitly (part of her methodology-agnostic stance) but her "opportunities" (customer needs, pain points, desires) map cleanly onto JTBD's "job" primitive.

A team could run JTBD interview technique inside Torres's OST structure without contradiction. Story-based interviewing has strong overlap with JTBD's Switch Interview technique — both focus on specific past-behavior narratives.

**Torres's own position:** she doesn't write about JTBD not because she disagrees but because she wants "methodology-agnostic" language and avoids tribal wars.

### Composes with Ries / Lean Startup (foundational, redefined)

Torres considers Lean Startup one of the best trends in product-building of the last 20 years. She keeps build-measure-learn but:

- **Replaces "MVP"** with "small assumption test" or specific formats (concept test, concierge, Wizard of Oz). MVP has been degraded to "small first version to ship" — the opposite of Ries's original meaning.
- **Replaces full experiments in discovery** with small assumption tests targeting one assumption at a time.
- **Adds the story-based interview + OST layer** upstream of the experiment.

If the user invokes Lean Startup, respect the lineage but use Torres's refined vocabulary. See [[lean-startup]].

### Composes with Design Thinking (with a corrective)

Torres teaches that many design-thinking-influenced teams stop at usability testing and A/B testing without doing generative discovery first. She doesn't attack Design Thinking directly but her opportunity-first framing corrects the design-thinking default of jumping to prototyping.

**Position it as:** Design Thinking gives you a good creative process for solutions; Torres gives you the discovery discipline that makes sure the solutions are addressing real customer needs.

### Composes with OKRs (with constraints)

Compatible. Product outcomes fit as the Objective; measurable behavior change as Key Results. Torres's constraints:

- Product outcomes, not business outcomes assigned to trios.
- Behavior change, not sentiment (unless paired with behavior).
- Room for exploration — not hyper-narrow traction metrics.

### Composes with User Research as a specialty (both, not either)

Torres's post-2024 pushback: the trio does the *weekly practice*; specialist researchers do the *deeper studies* the trio can't do (ethnography, longitudinal studies, statistical rigor, quant survey design). Companies that eliminated research roles using her method as justification misread the method.

**Position it as:** trio + research, not trio *or* research. Both scale differently but neither replaces the other.

## Frameworks Torres has explicitly commented on

- **Cagan / Product Operating Model** — complementary, cedes the phrase to Cagan, positions habits as the weekly practice inside. Compose vertically.
- **Perri / Escaping the Build Trap + Product Operations** — complementary; Perri endorses Torres publicly. Perri = scaffolding, Torres = habit.
- **Lean Startup / Ries** — foundational influence; redefines MVP.
- **JTBD** — refuses to use the vocabulary but the substance is compatible.
- **Design Thinking** — corrects it toward opportunity-first (rather than prototype-first).
- **OKRs** — compatible with constraints.
- **User research as a specialty** — complementary; the trio doesn't replace researchers.
- **AI as replacement for discovery** — explicitly pushes back. AI accelerates the trio; doesn't replace customer contact.

## Frameworks Torres has explicitly pushed back on

- **"Talk to your users"** as generic advice — it loses the strict interview rules.
- **MVP as "small first version to ship"** — the degradation of Ries's original meaning.
- **PM-solo discovery** — kills the trio's shared context.
- **Project-based discovery / one-and-done research** — the pattern continuous discovery exists to replace.
- **Business outcomes assigned to product teams** — trios can only move product outcomes.
- **Feature-as-opportunity framing** — the disguise pattern.
- **Trio as replacement for user researchers** — misapplication of the method.
- **AI as replacement for customer conversations** — the pattern of "let's just ask AI what users want".
- **Being turned into a rigid recipe / dogma** — Torres explicitly refuses.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work, redirect gently but clearly — with attribution to the essay / podcast / LinkedIn post where Torres makes the case.
