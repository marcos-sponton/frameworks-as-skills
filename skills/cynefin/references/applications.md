# Cynefin — Applications

> When Cynefin fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead. Also: how Cynefin sits alongside adjacent frameworks — peer/compatible with Wardley Mapping, hostile toward SAFe / Design Thinking as commodity / Systems Thinking / Learning Organization / SECI / Six Sigma / BPR.

## Situations where Cynefin fits well

- **Decision-making under uncertainty** — you're not sure what kind of problem you have and default methods don't quite fit. Sense-making first, method second.
- **Post-mortems on strategy / launch / roadmap / pilot** that didn't behave as expected. Cynefin diagnoses whether the failure was applying the wrong domain's methods.
- **Distinguishing what needs experts from what needs experiments.** Complicated (experts) vs. Complex (parallel probes) is one of Snowden's most operationally useful distinctions.
- **Designing a pilot** — especially when someone is planning *the* pilot (singular). Cynefin redirects toward multiple parallel safe-to-fail probes if the domain is Complex.
- **Crisis response and turnaround** — you're in Chaotic and need a disciplined move to Complex/Complicated via the aporetic liminal, not straight to best practice.
- **Extracting "best practice" from a success** — Cynefin's answer is usually *don't*. If the success was Complex-domain, extraction destroys what made it work.
- **Public policy and complex social interventions** — where enabling constraints and micro-narrative dominate.
- **Organizational strategy in truly Complex domains** — where the temptation is to reach for SAFe / Scaled Agile / Design Thinking templates and Cynefin's sense-making check prevents the wrong-tool error.
- **Sensemaking on ambiguous data / signal detection** — Snowden's anticipatory-triggers work applies here (Gaussian vs. Pareto).
- **Facilitating diverse-stakeholder decisions** where cognitive diversity is essential and consensus would destroy variation.
- **Knowledge management program design** — Snowden's origin domain; Cynefin was born of KM.

## Situations where Cynefin does NOT fit well

- **Genuinely routine Clear-domain operations.** Invoice processing, hand-luggage rules, deployment scripts. Cynefin's answer is "use best practice" — invoking the framework is overkill. Just apply the procedure.

- **When a decision is genuinely simple.** If the answer is self-evident to everyone in the room, running a sense-making exercise wastes time.

- **When the user needs a competitive-choice framework, not a sensemaking framework.** Cynefin doesn't tell you which market to enter or how to position. Use **Playing to Win** (Roger Martin) or **Good Strategy / Bad Strategy** (Rumelt) for that. Cynefin can tell you *whether* the situation is one where those frameworks apply.

- **When the user wants a project categorization tool.** Cynefin is not that. Push back. Offer sense-making instead.

- **When the user wants a portfolio prioritization framework.** Use ODI, JTBD prioritization, or Wardley Mapping. Cynefin describes the domain of each item, not how to rank them.

- **When the user wants a diagrammatic summary.** Give them the [cynefin.io wiki](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains) and the [HBR paper](https://hbr.org/2007/11/a-leaders-framework-for-decision-making). Don't run the framework at them.

- **Personal-productivity or time-allocation questions** — some overlap (Complex vs. Clear tasks in a personal system) but this isn't where Cynefin is strongest. Roger Martin's personal-strategy application, David Allen's GTD, or a habit framework fits better.

## Adjacent frameworks — when to reach for which

### Wardley Mapping — **peer, compatible**

Snowden and **Simon Wardley** did a **joint 2-day masterclass in December 2018** exploring synergies. Both are respected peers. The consensus that emerged:

- **Cynefin** = decoding problem domains, deciding approach.
- **Wardley** = mapping components along evolution axis (genesis → custom → product → commodity), understanding value chains over time.
- **Complementary, not substitutable.** Use both for the same situation and you'll get different, useful lenses.
- **Wardley's warning:** *"You can't simply transport Cynefin terms onto the evolutionary axis of a map."* Don't map "complex" to "custom." They're different distinctions.
- Wardley Maps most useful when the problem is Complicated or Complex.

Sources: [Snowden on the joint masterclass](https://thecynefin.co/library/cynefin-and-wardley-maps-recordings-from-a-masterclass-exploration/), [Wardley on the combination](https://swardley.medium.com/wardley-maps-and-cynefin-d0c7a2b960e8).

### Playing to Win (Roger Martin) — **complementary, different level**

Playing to Win is a competitive-choice framework — it presupposes you have enough clarity to make an integrated set of choices about where to play and how to win. Cynefin sits *upstream*: it tells you *whether the situation is one where PtW's method applies*.

- If the situation is Clear or Complicated, PtW can be applied cleanly.
- If the situation is Complex, PtW is still applicable but the "how to win" moves need to be probes rather than commitments.
- If the situation is Chaotic, PtW is premature — impose constraint first.

### Good Strategy / Bad Strategy (Rumelt) — **complementary, diagnosis-first**

Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action) is diagnosis-first, which resonates with Cynefin's sense-making-first posture. Use Cynefin to sense-make the domain and Rumelt to find the crux and act.

### Agile (original, 2001 Manifesto) — **compatible in the Complex-Complicated liminal**

The 2001 Agile Manifesto had Complex-domain awareness. Its principles (early and continuous delivery, welcoming changing requirements, close daily collaboration) work in the CO-CO liminal band — the space where the Complex is being progressively made Complicated. Snowden's stance is *not* anti-Agile-in-general; he defends original Agile against scaled variants.

**Do:** use Agile inside the Complex-Complicated liminal, disciplined by Cynefin sense-making.
**Don't:** use Agile as a universal answer, or use it inside a scaled framework that pretends Complex problems are Complicated.

### SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) — **hostile**

Snowden's most attacked framework. See `voice-and-tone.md` for direct quotes. Position:

- SAFe drags Complex work into Complicated frames with false certainty.
- *"SAFe is to Agile as Six Sigma was to BPR."*
- *"PRINCE II camouflaged in Agile language."*
- *"An obey-making machine."*

**When user invokes SAFe as answer to a Complex product problem:** name the misapplication. Attack the framing before offering the alternative. Then reframe as "use Complex-domain methods on the Complex parts; use Complicated-domain methods (which SAFe's ceremony might supply, in a Complicated context) on the Complicated parts."

### Design Thinking (as commodity template) — **hostile**

Position:
- Started with substance in IDEO's origins.
- Commoditized into brand-methodology.
- Applies Complicated methods (categorization, personas, journey maps, templates) to Complex problems.
- *"Case studies without theory."*

**When user reaches for a Design Thinking template on a Complex problem:** name the misapplication. Offer Complex-domain methods (parallel safe-to-fail probes, micro-narrative capture, distributed cognition) instead. Don't attack the *practice* — attack the *commoditization*.

### Systems Thinking — **rejected as flawed**

Snowden's position: Systems Thinking is *"flawed and transitionary."* Too engineering-mechanical. Treats organizations as machines with leverage points. Ignores complex adaptive dynamics.

**When user reaches for Systems Thinking on organizational behavior:** offer complexity science instead. Enabling constraints, not leverage points. Distributed cognition, not systems maps. Emergent narrative, not causal loops.

### Learning Organization (Senge) — **rejected**

Position: Learning Organization imposes homogeneity of values, goals, and objectives — which destroys the coherent heterogeneity resilience requires.

**When user invokes Senge as answer to organizational change:** offer coherent heterogeneity as the alternative. Diverse enough to generate variation, coherent enough to converge.

### Nonaka SECI / tacit-explicit conversion — **rejected**

Snowden's 3rd-generation Knowledge Management work explicitly counters SECI.

Position: codification destroys context. Not all knowledge should be codified. Tacit-to-explicit conversion (the central SECI move) destroys situated meaning.

**When user reaches for SECI on knowledge management:** offer distributed cognition, micro-narrative capture, and situated practice instead.

### Six Sigma / BPR / Appreciative Inquiry / Myers-Briggs — **dismissed as fads**

Snowden's list: *"lack underlying theory, serving consultant interests rather than organizational needs."*

Six Sigma applied to Complex work is the Air Force / Six Sigma case (see `examples.md`) — implementation produced hidden costs that surveys never surfaced.

BPR (Business Process Re-engineering) applied Clear-domain methods to Complex organizational transformation.

### Lean Startup — **partial critique**

The MVP-as-one-pilot approach is a Complex-domain error — you need multiple parallel probes, not sequential single bets.

The "Build-Measure-Learn" principle is right in Complex-domain principle but often degraded in practice into a template.

**Compatible:** use Lean Startup's spirit (validated learning, hypothesis-driven) inside a Complex-domain sense-making frame that runs multiple probes in parallel rather than sequentially.

### Balanced Scorecard, OKRs, KPIs — **useful in Clear/Complicated, dangerous in Complex**

Measurement systems work when cause-effect is discoverable. In Complex, they turn into gaming. Snowden on knowledge-sharing targets: *"If you try and set targets for knowledge sharing you have failed to understand the subject."*

## How Cynefin composes with other frameworks

Snowden has published compatibility notes across his blog. Highlights:

- **Cynefin + Wardley Mapping:** compatible, complementary, mutually respected. See above.
- **Cynefin + Agile (original):** Agile works inside the CO-CO liminal band, disciplined by Cynefin sense-making.
- **Cynefin + SenseMaker:** SenseMaker is Snowden's own software for micro-narrative capture in Complex domains. Native complement.
- **Cynefin + Estuarine:** Snowden's own third framework. *"Cynefin is a decision support framework that recognises complexity theory; Estuarine is a full-on complexity framework."* Use Cynefin to sense-make; move to Estuarine when the situation calls for constraint mapping and portfolio design.
- **Cynefin + EU Field Guide:** the Field Guide operationalizes Cynefin for public-sector crisis response.
- **Cynefin + Playing to Win / Rumelt:** Cynefin diagnoses the domain; the strategy frameworks operate at the level of competitive choice within that domain.

## Positioning note — this skill's framing of the attacks

Because Snowden critiques Agile / SAFe / Design Thinking — frameworks widely used and often loved by product audiences — this skill frames Cynefin as **a diagnostic tool that helps you apply those frameworks correctly** (in-context, in-domain), not as an attack on the practices themselves.

**The voice attacks the misapplication.** Applying Complicated methods to Complex problems is the target. If a user is applying Agile inside the CO-CO liminal on a genuinely emergent product problem, they're doing exactly what Snowden defends. If they're applying SAFe to try to make Complex work feel Complicated with false certainty, they're doing what Snowden attacks.

**The distinction the skill preserves:** original practice done in-domain (good) vs. commoditized template applied out-of-domain (bad). Snowden's target is the second.
