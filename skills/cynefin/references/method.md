# Cynefin — Method

> The canonical description of Cynefin in Snowden's own terms — the five domains, the constraint typology that determines them, the cliff between Clear and Chaotic, the liminal zones (2017), and the Aporetic Turn (2019–2020). Fidelity is the point. Cynefin is a **sense-making framework**, not a categorization tool. Softening that distinction collapses the method into a 2×2 workshop artifact — which is the single most common misuse Snowden calls out.

## What Cynefin is (and is not)

**Cynefin** (Welsh, pronounced roughly *kuh-NEV-in*) means *habitat* — the multiple places we belong to in ways we're only partially aware of. Snowden chose the name deliberately because the framework is about **the situation we're contextualized in**, not about a category we can pin something into.

- **Cynefin IS** a sense-making framework for *contextualizing* a situation so you know what kind of approach fits.
- **Cynefin is NOT** a 2×2 grid for sorting projects.
- **Cynefin is NOT** a categorization tool.
- **Cynefin is NOT** a decision tree.
- **Cynefin is NOT** a maturity model.

Snowden's clearest statement of the distinction: Cynefin is a framework you **contextualize into** — you sense-make until you understand which domain you're operating in — not a framework you **sort into**.

## Origin & naming history

The naming history matters because Snowden refines vocabulary when the older words invite misuse. Users hitting the framework with older names are usually pulling old material.

- **1999** — Snowden develops the framework at IBM Global Services for intellectual capital management.
- **2002** — IBM Cynefin Centre for Organizational Complexity founded; Snowden director.
- **2003** — **Kurtz & Snowden, "The new dynamics of strategy,"** *IBM Systems Journal*. Domains: **Known / Knowable / Complex / Chaotic**.
- **2004** — Cynefin Centre spun out of IBM → Cognitive Edge.
- **2007** — **Snowden & Boone, "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making,"** *HBR*. Domains become **Simple / Complicated / Complex / Chaotic** + central **Disorder**. Wins Academy of Management outstanding practitioner-oriented publication.
- **2014** — "Simple" → **"Obvious"**.
- **2015-onward** — "Obvious" → **"Clear"**; "Disorder" → **"Confusion"**.
- **2017** — **Liminality** introduced (transitional zones between domains).
- **2019–2020** — **The Aporetic Turn**. Confusion splits into **Aporetic** (authentic — you know you don't know) and **Confused** (inauthentic — unaware confusion).

Sources: [Cynefin.io wiki](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains), [Wikipedia — Cynefin framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynefin_framework).

## The five domains

Snowden's typology is not about topics ("software", "healthcare", "policy"). It's about **the type of constraint operating** and **the relationship between cause and effect**. The same topic can sit in any domain depending on which constraint is actually in play.

### Clear (ex-Obvious, ex-Simple)

- **Constraint:** rigid / fixed. Walls, fences, procedural rules.
- **Cause-effect:** self-evident. Everyone in the situation can see what causes what.
- **Decision model:** Sense → Categorize → Respond.
- **Practice:** **Best practice.** This is the *only* domain where best practice is legitimate.
- **Example:** airline hand-luggage rules. Weight and dimension limits are rigid; the response is procedural.

**Warning:** Clear is where over-confidence in best practice creates the conditions for catastrophic collapse into Chaotic — see *The Cliff* below.

### Complicated

- **Constraint:** governing. Rules and policies that can flex to accommodate variation but structure the response.
- **Cause-effect:** discoverable. Not self-evident — an expert has to analyze — but knowable.
- **Decision model:** Sense → Analyze → Respond.
- **Practice:** good practice. Multiple valid expert answers may exist.
- **Example:** bridge engineering. There is a right answer; you need an engineer to find it; there is more than one valid design.

### Complex

- **Constraint:** enabling. Bounds the system while allowing emergence — Snowden's endoskeleton metaphor.
- **Cause-effect:** coherent only in hindsight. You cannot predict what will happen; you can explain what happened after the fact (retrospective coherence).
- **Decision model:** **Probe → Sense → Respond.** Multiple parallel safe-to-fail probes, then amplify what works and dampen what doesn't.
- **Practice:** emergent. Snowden's preferred word is **exaptive** (Stephen Jay Gould's term) — radical repurposing of what already exists.
- **Example:** raising a child. A children's party (Snowden's signature teaching case). Organizational change. Any situation where agents have agency and interactions produce surprises.

**This is where most business problems actually sit** — and where practitioners incorrectly reach for Complicated methods (root-cause analysis, expert consultation, single-pilot roadmaps). Wrong tool.

### Chaotic

- **Constraint:** absent. No stable relationships between components.
- **Cause-effect:** none discernible in the moment.
- **Decision model:** **Act → Sense → Respond.** Impose a constraint first; observe; then move toward Complex or Complicated as constraint stabilizes.
- **Practice:** novel practice.
- **Example:** crisis in progress. Carnival (canonical *intentional* Chaotic — Chaos is not always bad; sometimes you deliberately induce it to allow reconfiguration).

**Chaotic is a place to visit, not to live.** Deliberate transient Chaos can be useful; being stuck in Chaos is catastrophic.

### Confusion (central)

Not knowing which domain you're in. **Legitimate starting point** — Snowden insists you should assume Confusion first, then contextualize.

Since 2019–2020, Confusion is split into two states (see *The Aporetic Turn* below):
- **Aporetic** — you know you don't know. Legitimate.
- **Confused** — you don't realize you don't know. Dangerous.

## The Cliff — Clear ↔ Chaotic

The border between Clear and Chaotic is not a smooth transition. It is a **catastrophic fold**.

Direct from [cynefin.io](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin_Domains):

> "The boundary between Clear and Chaotic is a catastrophic fold, or cliff, a collapse where the liminality in Clear is not visible and it is all too easy to walk blindly off the cliff through excessive confidence in the applicability of rigid constraints."

**What it means in practice:** rigid constraints don't degrade gracefully. When context shifts and the rigid rule no longer fits, the system doesn't gradually become suboptimal — it collapses. Best practices applied outside Clear create brittle systems that fail catastrophically when the context underlying them changes.

**Real examples:**
- Financial models that treated 2008-style events as Gaussian rather than Pareto.
- Airline check-in procedures that collapse the moment volume exceeds design parameters.
- Governance policies that were correct until they weren't and then produced a full-system failure.

Governing constraints (Complicated) fail differently — robust, they get stronger until they break. Enabling constraints (Complex) don't fail catastrophically because they allow constant micro-adaptation.

## Liminal zones (2017 addition)

Green transitional bands between adjacent domains — situations that have started to shift out of one domain but haven't stabilized in the next.

Key liminal band: **Complex ↔ Complicated** (sometimes called the "CO-CO" band). This is where Agile/Scrum operate at their best — the disciplined practice of making the Complex progressively more Complicated as patterns emerge and can be codified.

Liminality is not a domain; it is a *transitional space*. You cannot live in liminality — you have to move. But the move itself takes energy (see *Phase shifts* below).

## The Aporetic Turn (2019–2020)

Snowden's biggest post-2007 methodological addition. Confusion was one central domain; it is now two states:

- **Aporetic** (authentic disorder) — from Greek *aporia*, "at a loss". You know you don't know. Suspension of action is legitimate. Snowden encourages *deliberate creation of paradox* to force different thinking.
- **Confused** (inauthentic disorder) — you don't realize you don't know. Result of biases and entrained patterns. Dangerous — you're operating with false confidence.

### The five exits from Aporetic

Per [cynefin.io/wiki/Aporetic_Turn](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Aporetic_Turn):

1. **→ Complex** — run multiple competing hypotheses via safe-to-fail probes.
2. **→ Complicated** — bring in experts, commission research.
3. **→ Complex-Chaotic liminal** — **MassSense** with cognitively diverse groups (SenseMaker exercise).
4. **→ Complicated via different expertise** — introduce competing expert paradigms to break single-frame lock.
5. **→ Clear** — high risk; usually creates Confusion. Go via Complicated instead.

The Aporetic Turn matters because the pre-2019 framework had no legitimate answer to *"we can't tell what's happening"* other than "figure it out." Aporetic legitimizes suspension.

## Constraints — Snowden's central refinement

The most important theoretical addition Snowden made beyond the 2007 HBR paper. **The domain you're in is determined by the type of constraint that's actually operating.** Three main types in the Cynefin framework; six subtypes in Estuarine (see `post-book.md`).

- **Rigid / Fixed** — walls, fences. Predictable, enforceable, brittle. Governs Clear.
- **Governing** — rules, policies. Robust: they survive by getting stronger, then fail catastrophically. Governs Complicated.
- **Enabling** — bounds the system while allowing emergence. **Endoskeleton metaphor.** Governs Complex.

Snowden's endoskeleton/exoskeleton framing:

> "The external constraint of an insect's skeleton bounds its nature [governing], while the endoskeleton of a mammal allows for significant variation around a coherence centre [enabling]." — [cynefin.io Constraints wiki](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Constraints)

**How to use this in practice:** don't ask "what topic is this?" — ask "what constraint is operating here?" A software project with a fixed regulatory deadline has a rigid constraint on one axis and enabling constraints on the design axis. Different axes, different domains, different methods.

## Anticipatory triggers

Distinct from prediction. AI/pattern-detection triggers a human to **heightened alert** when statistical conditions suggest something *may* happen — without predicting *what*. Developed for DARPA counterterrorism, now used in elder-care abuse detection.

Underlying distinction Snowden hammers:

- **Gaussian** distributions (bell-curve) — ordered, predictable, useful for planning and best practice.
- **Pareto** distributions (power-law) — complex, unpredictable individually, statistically inevitable in aggregate.

**You cannot predict Pareto events but you can prime humans to spot them.** 9/11 was a Pareto event. Financial black swans are Pareto. Most Complex-domain surprises are Pareto.

Source: [Jim Rutt Show EP184 transcript](https://jimruttshow.blubrry.net/the-jim-rutt-show-transcripts/transcript-of-ep184-dave-snowden-on-managing-complexity-in-times-of-crisis/).

## Cynefin dynamics — how situations move between domains

Cynefin is not a static classification. Situations move — often deliberately.

- **The stable pattern:** constant iteration Complex ↔ Complicated. Material that stabilizes and is low-risk migrates to Clear. Material that no longer applies migrates back.
- **The fall into Chaotic:** usually via the cliff from Clear — rigid constraints break catastrophically when context shifts. Rare that Complex flows directly into Chaotic. More common that Clear collapses.
- **The exit from Chaos:** deliberate. Impose a temporary constraint. Move to the aporetic liminal (not straight back to order). Then Complex or Complicated as constraint types stabilize.
- **Phase shifts require energy.** Like latent heat. Domain transitions are not smooth — they are changes of state. Underestimating the energy cost of a transition is a common failure mode in change programs.

## The critique of "best practices"

Best practice is legitimate **only in Clear**. Applied to Complicated it becomes brittle (you need *good* practice — expert-mediated). Applied to Complex it destroys emergence.

Snowden's most-repeated phrasing:

> "Failure repeats but success rarely does."

Because success is context-dependent and initial conditions never repeat. Case studies teach *retrospective coherence*, not causal knowledge:

> "Especially when things become highly uncertain, case studies are the last thing you need to rely on."

Extracting "best practice" from a Complex-domain success destroys the very thing that made it work: the specific configuration of enabling constraints, actor interactions, and micro-narratives that emerged in context.

## Sequence of application

There is no strict order — sense-making is iterative, not procedural. In practice:

1. **Start in Confusion.** Assume you don't know the domain.
2. **Identify the constraint actually operating.** Not the constraint you wish were operating. Rigid → Clear/Complicated. Enabling → Complex. Absent → Chaotic.
3. **Test with a small probe** if you're uncertain. Predictable outcome → ordered domain. Surprise → Complex.
4. **If aporia is real, stay there.** Don't force a domain to feel decisive. Deliberate suspension is legitimate.
5. **Match method to domain.** Best practice / good practice / probe-sense-respond / act-sense-respond.
6. **Iterate.** Domains change. Constraints shift. Yesterday's Complex can become today's Complicated as patterns emerge.

## What this method is NOT

Snowden is emphatic about the negative definition:

- **Not a project categorization tool.** Cynefin is not a 2×2. Sorting projects into it is the single most common misuse.
- **Not a maturity model.** Domains are not ranked. Clear is not "worse than" Complex.
- **Not sequential.** You don't go through Clear → Complicated → Complex → Chaotic as a progression.
- **Not personality-typing.** People aren't Complex-oriented or Clear-oriented; situations are.
- **Not systems thinking.** Snowden explicitly rejects systems thinking as "flawed and transitionary" — engineering-mechanical, treating organizations as machines. Cynefin is complexity science, not systems thinking.
- **Not a management fad.** Snowden has spent 20+ years attacking the methods that became fads (Six Sigma, BPR, Design Thinking as commodity, SAFe). The framework's discipline is precisely to *resist* the fad instinct.
