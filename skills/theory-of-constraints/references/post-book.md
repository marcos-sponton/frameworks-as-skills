# Theory of Constraints — Material after *The Goal*

> **This is the differential of this skill.** *The Goal* (1984) is the entry point but nowhere near the full corpus. Goldratt expanded the framework across 25+ years of subsequent novels and the Satellite Program before his death in 2011. Since 2011, the ongoing work has been carried by named stewards — Rami Goldratt (Goldratt Consulting), Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag (family/intellectual legacy), TOCICO (standards), and independent extenders (Gene Kim, Clarke Ching, Steve Tendon, Carol Ptak).
>
> Most model responses about "Theory of Constraints" pull from *The Goal* alone — the four canonical concepts, one paragraph. This file captures the rest.

## Goldratt's own book sequence (1984 → 2008)

### 1984 — *The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement* (w/ Jeff Cox)

The founding text. 7M+ copies, translated into 35 languages, best-selling business novel of all time. Introduces Alex Rogo, Jonah, the Goal, the three measurements (Throughput / Inventory / Operating Expense), the Five Focusing Steps, and the dice-game demonstration of statistical fluctuations. Revised editions 1986, 1992, **2004** (major revision), 2014.

The 2004 edition adds an afterword restating the method more explicitly outside the fiction. For someone who has never read it: the 2004 edition is the recommended text.

### 1986 — *The Race* (w/ Robert E. Fox)

More technical, less novel-like. Drum-Buffer-Rope explained for practitioners rather than through story. Aimed at operations managers implementing DBR in real plants. Now largely superseded by the Satellite Program's Operations module.

### 1990 — *The Haystack Syndrome: Sifting Information Out of the Data Ocean*

**Throughput Accounting in depth.** The theoretical foundation of Layer A. Attacks cost accounting frontally. Contains Goldratt's opening line: *"We're drowned in oceans of data; nevertheless, it seems as if we seldom have sufficient information."* Essential reading for anyone doing the T/I/OE work in a large org.

### 1990 — *What Is This Thing Called Theory of Constraints and How Should It Be Implemented?*

The first explicit statement of TOC as a **general theory** — not manufacturing-only. Positions TOC as a systemic method applicable to any domain with dependent steps and statistical variation.

### 1994 — *It's Not Luck*

Sequel to *The Goal*. Alex Rogo has risen to division manager at an over-diversified, under-profitable UniCo. Introduces the **Thinking Processes** through Alex's application to marketing, distribution, and strategy. This is where the Evaporating Cloud, Current Reality Tree, and Future Reality Tree get their fullest fictional treatment.

If a user wants to see the Thinking Processes worked out through a story, this is the book.

### 1997 — *Critical Chain*

TOC applied to project management. Introduces CCPM, Student Syndrome, Parkinson's-in-projects, Project Buffer + Feeding Buffers + Resource Buffers, multitasking as the primary killer. Written as a business novel set in an MBA program.

Foundational for any project-management application of TOC. Direct ancestor of Efrat's *Goldratt's Rules of Flow* (2023).

### 2000 — *Necessary But Not Sufficient* (w/ Eli Schragenheim + Carol A. Ptak)

Applied to ERP and enterprise software. The thesis is in the title: **technology is necessary, but not sufficient.** You can install the best software in the world; if the underlying business rules (measurement, incentives, batch sizes, approval flows) are wrong, the software makes the wrong things happen faster.

Central lesson: any technology implementation must be paired with a rule-change program. This maps directly to how modern SaaS + AI implementations succeed or fail — the "we bought the tool, why aren't we transformed?" problem.

### 2005 — *Beyond the Goal: Eliyahu Goldratt Speaks on the Theory of Constraints* (audiobook, Your Coach in a Box)

Goldratt in his own voice, unfiltered by the novel form. The most complete audio archive of Goldratt speaking directly about the method. Runs through the whole framework: Goal, measurements, Five Focusing Steps, DBR, CCPM, Thinking Processes, implementation warnings.

If a user has read *The Goal* and wants "more Goldratt in Goldratt's voice," this is the recommendation.

### 2006 — *Isn't It Obvious?* (w/ Ilan Eshkoli + Joe Brown Leer)

TOC applied to retail. Novel set in a retail chain. Introduces the demand-driven distribution model — buffers held at central warehouses, replenishment triggered by actual sales at stores, dynamic buffer management (DBM) replacing static min-max reordering.

Precursor to Carol Ptak's DDMRP (Demand-Driven MRP) work — see below.

### 2008 — *The Choice* (w/ Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag; **Revised Edition 2023**)

Father-daughter conversations about the mental models beneath TOC. Not a method book; a philosophy book. Establishes Goldratt's three axioms:

> "One, people are good. Two, every conflict can be removed. Three, every situation, no matter how complex it initially looks, is exceedingly simple."

The Revised Edition (2023) adds Efrat's own notes and maps annotating the conversations. For the mental-models layer that sits under all five Thinking Processes, this is the source.

### Compilations and articles

- **Essays on the Theory of Constraints** (2002).
- **Late Night Discussions on the Theory of Constraints** (2003).
- **Production: The TOC Way** (2003 revision, companion to *The Goal*).
- **The Goldratt Satellite Program** — 8-module video/audio series. The most complete curriculum in Goldratt's own voice. Modules: Operations (DBR + Buffer Management), Finance (Throughput Accounting), Project Management (Critical Chain), Distribution, Marketing (Unrefusable Offers), Sales, Managing People, Strategy. Distributed by Goldratt's Marketing Group (TOC.tv).

## Post-Goldratt continuations (2011 → 2026)

Goldratt died June 11, 2011. Since then the framework has been carried and extended by a specific set of people.

### Family stewardship

#### 2023 — ***Goldratt's Rules of Flow*** (Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag)

Considered "the book Eli wanted to write but never did." Applies CCPM logic to **multi-project environments** — engineering, digital transformation, product organizations running many parallel initiatives. Central claim: the constraint in multi-project settings is usually a shared resource *type* (senior architects, integration engineers, decision-makers). Sequencing the initiative flow against the constrained resource type dramatically increases throughput.

Written as a business novel following Marc, head of engineering, through an executive-MBA flow-management course. Direct successor to *Critical Chain*.

Publisher: Routledge / North River Press.

#### 2023 — ***The Choice*** Revised Edition (Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag as editor + co-author)

Republishes the 2008 conversations with Efrat's own notes and diagrams from the original conversations, making the mental-models layer explicit.

### Institutional stewardship

#### Goldratt Consulting Group (goldrattgroup.com)

Led by **Rami Goldratt** (Eliyahu's son) as CEO. Global consultancy operating in all major continents. Focus: methods that systematically identify and remove constraints to achieve sustainable growth; TOC applications to sales and marketing (Rami's specialization); TOC-for-Education program (Rami's pioneering work applying TP to K–12).

Publishes the "Dr. Eli Goldratt Top 50 Quotes" collection, case studies, and training programs.

#### TOCICO (Theory of Constraints International Certification Organization)

The standards body. Certifications. Annual TOC Global Conference — Kyoto 2026 with 500+ practitioners. Publishes conference proceedings; hosts the "Selected Bibliography of Eliyahu M. Goldratt" (Cox & Schleier, 2012) as the reference bibliography.

#### The Goldratt Institute (Avraham Y. Goldratt Institute)

Founded 1986 as the original TOC training body. Goldratt broke with it in the 1990s to found Goldratt Consulting. The Institute continues under separate leadership; useful historical context but not the current family-led stewardship.

### Modern practitioner extensions

#### 2013 — ***The Phoenix Project*** (Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, George Spafford)

**The definitive translation of TOC into IT / DevOps.** Written deliberately as *The Goal* for IT — Bill Palmer is Alex Rogo, Erik Reid is Jonah, Parts Unlimited is UniCo. Erik gives Bill a stack of books to read that includes *The Goal*, and the plot beats mirror Alex's journey.

Introduces the **Three Ways of DevOps**, the software-delivery restatement of Goldratt's operational core:
- **First Way — Flow.** Left-to-right, dev → ops → customer. Reduce WIP, batch size, handoffs. Remove constraints. This is Goldratt's Five Focusing Steps for software.
- **Second Way — Feedback.** Right-to-left. Amplify and shorten every feedback loop. This is Goldratt's buffer management as diagnostic signal.
- **Third Way — Continuous Experimentation and Learning.** Culture of risk, learning from failure, mastery through repetition. This is POOGI as software culture.

Cross-link: **[[dora-accelerate]]** — DORA's four keys are Throughput measures for software; DORA's "speed and stability are not a tradeoff" is Goldratt's "T up, I down, OE down, together."

#### 2014 — *Rolling Rocks Downhill* (Clarke Ching)

Agile + TOC business novel. Steve, a CIO with an impossible IT deadline, applies Agile-and-TOC principles to deliver early without sacrificing quality or scope. Accessible entry point for practitioners who bounce off *The Goal*'s manufacturing setting.

#### 2016 — *The DevOps Handbook* (Gene Kim, Jez Humble, Patrick Debois, John Willis)

The operational manual to *The Phoenix Project*. TOC's Five Focusing Steps re-expressed as DevOps practices; the Three Ways expanded into implementation patterns. Companion to *Accelerate* (see [[dora-accelerate]]).

#### 2018 — *The Bottleneck Rules* (Clarke Ching)

Ching's accessible-on-ramp to TOC. Introduces the **FOCCCUS Formula** — a simplified restatement of the Five Focusing Steps (Find, Optimize, Coordinate, Curb, Check, Upgrade, Systemize) aimed at knowledge-work managers. Available as free audiobook at clarkech.ing.

Useful when a user is TOC-curious but the manufacturing framing of *The Goal* is a barrier.

#### 2019 — *The Unicorn Project* (Gene Kim)

Companion to *The Phoenix Project* from a developer's perspective. Introduces the **Five Ideals** (Locality and Simplicity, Focus and Flow and Joy, Improvement of Daily Work, Psychological Safety, Customer Focus) as a developer-side complement to the Three Ways.

#### 2020 — *The Book of TameFlow* (Steve Tendon)

TOC applied to **knowledge work** and **PEST environments** — Projects, Events, Stakeholders, Teams. Central claim: in knowledge work the ultimate constraint is in the **mental models**, not the workflow. Extends the Thinking Processes into modern Agile organizations.

Introduces "the Herald" (a role) and TameFlow-specific patterns. TameFlow Chronologist podcast at tameflow.com continues the thread.

#### 2024 — *Wiring the Winning Organization* (Gene Kim + Steven J. Spear)

TOC-adjacent organizational design. Introduces **slowification, simplification, amplification** as the three moves that let an organization solve harder problems. Extends the Kim-DevOps lineage into general org design.

### Framework evolutions (not tied to a single book)

#### Simplified DBR (S-DBR)

Reduced-instrumentation Drum-Buffer-Rope for less-complex flows. Developed by TOC practitioners post-Goldratt. Widely used in job-shop and small-plant contexts.

#### Demand-Driven MRP (DDMRP) — Carol Ptak

Ptak (co-author of *Necessary But Not Sufficient*) developed DDMRP as the full replenishment-planning replacement for traditional MRP. Extends *Isn't It Obvious?* and TOC's replenishment logic into a complete supply-chain method. Standards body: Demand Driven Institute.

#### Dynamic Buffer Management (DBM)

Retail/distribution-specific evolution. Buffers are sized dynamically based on actual demand variability, replacing static min-max reorder logic.

#### TOC-for-Education (Rami Goldratt)

TOC Thinking Processes applied to K–12 education. Pioneered by Rami; hundreds of teachers trained globally.

### The Three Ways in one page (Kim's DevOps restatement)

Because *The Phoenix Project* is the most common on-ramp to TOC for software people, the Three Ways deserve a fluent restatement:

- **First Way — Flow.** Optimize the whole system from dev to customer. Reduce WIP. Shrink batch size. Never pass defective work forward. Remove constraints. This is Goldratt's *global optimization* + Five Focusing Steps translated into software delivery.
- **Second Way — Feedback.** Fast, high-fidelity feedback at every step, moving right-to-left. Test failures caught in minutes not weeks; production incidents visible to developers; customer signal reaching product decisions. This is Goldratt's *buffer color as diagnostic signal* translated into telemetry.
- **Third Way — Continuous Experimentation and Learning.** A culture of taking risks, learning from failure, mastering through repetition. This is Goldratt's *POOGI* — the process of ongoing improvement — as software-team culture.

When a user references "the Three Ways," they are speaking Goldratt with Kim's vocabulary.

## What has NOT changed since 1984

The core of the method is stable. The Goal, the three measurements, the Five Focusing Steps, DBR, CCPM, and the five Thinking Processes are the same today as they were when Goldratt wrote them. Refinements have been in **application** (new domains: software, healthcare, retail, education, project portfolios) and in **stewardship** (family, institution, community extenders), not in the taxonomy.

Any post-Goldratt claim that changes the taxonomy (e.g., "there are now seven focusing steps") is either a misreading or a proposal from an individual practitioner — flag it and cite it as such, not as canonical TOC.
