# Crossing the Chasm — Material posterior al libro

> **This is the differential of this skill.** The 1991 book *Crossing the Chasm* laid down TALC, the chasm, the D-Day analogy, whole product, and beachhead segmentation. Since then, Geoffrey Moore has published **seven more books, hundreds of LinkedIn Pulse essays and blog posts, plus podcast appearances that continue to refresh and extend the framework**. The 2014 3rd edition of *Crossing the Chasm* rewrote most cases from the 1991 original and is the operational canon today.
>
> Most model responses about "crossing the chasm" pull from a hazy memory of the 1991 book alone. This file captures the 30+ years of subsequent refinements — where Moore has extended the taxonomy (Inside the Tornado, Hierarchy of Powers, Zone to Win), added organization-scale complements (Escape Velocity, Zone to Win), added enterprise-digital lenses (Digital Systems Maturity Model), and applied the framework to AI-era categories (2024–2026 essays).

## The book sequence (in order)

### Crossing the Chasm — 1991; 2nd ed 1999; 3rd ed 2014 (canonical)

The foundational text. The 3rd edition (2014) is the operational canon:
- Rewrote most cases from the 1991 original (Documentum, Salesforce, Aruba Networks, Workday, VMware, Mozilla, Lithium replace the retired Blockbuster / generic HP / generic Oracle 1991 canon).
- Added two appendices: one linking to *Inside the Tornado*, one to a consumer-market adoption model.
- Framework itself (TALC, the chasm, whole product, D-Day, beachhead) unchanged from 1991.

Skill rule: **default to 3rd edition examples**. When a 1991-era case is still the sharpest illustration, mark it explicitly as an "original 1991 example."

Book page: [geoffreyamoore.com/book/crossing-the-chasm](https://geoffreyamoore.com/book/crossing-the-chasm/).

### Inside the Tornado — 1995

The post-chasm hypergrowth playbook. Introduces the **stage-reversal doctrine** (the Tornado playbook reverses the Bowling Alley) and the **gorilla / chimp / monkey** post-tornado market-share structure.

**Central operational quote:**
> "The winning strategy does not just change as we move from stage to stage, it actually reverses the prior strategy." — *Inside the Tornado*

**On why tornado standardization is inevitable once the herd tips:**
> "Infrastructure, to be useful, must be standard and global, so once the market moves to switch out the old for the new, it wants to complete this transition as rapidly as possible." — *Inside the Tornado*

This is Moore's sharpest single formulation of the stage-conditional discipline. If a session touches hypergrowth, standardization, or share grab, *Inside the Tornado* is the primary reference.

### The Gorilla Game — 1998

The investing implications of the tornado / gorilla dynamics. Reframes TALC for public-market equity investors: identify categories about to enter the tornado, hunt for the emerging gorilla, hold through Main Street. Not central to operator use, but relevant when the user is investor-facing or venture-decision-facing.

### Living on the Fault Line — 2000

CEO framework for tech disruption at the enterprise level. Introduced core-vs-context in an early form (later fully codified in *Dealing with Darwin*, 2005). Compresses much of the "how does a CEO of a public tech company survive category disruption?" thinking.

### Dealing with Darwin — 2005

Codifies **Core vs. Context** and the **three innovation types** (differentiation, neutralization, optimization). The rule: transfer resources *from* context *to* core continuously — including via outsourcing or automation. Innovation portfolio management as a CEO discipline.

> "Any activity that creates competitive advantage is core; everything else is context." — Moore's formulation across *Dealing with Darwin* and subsequent talks

Pair with Hierarchy of Powers (*Escape Velocity*, 2011) as an operational sibling: Powers describes *where* growth comes from; Core / Context describes *how* to allocate innovation resources given those growth sources.

### Escape Velocity — 2011

Introduces the **Hierarchy of Powers** — 5 growth-source powers, top-to-bottom: Category Power, Company Power, Market Power, Offer Power, Execution Power. Investor-lens diagnostic. Argues that most operators over-weight Offer + Execution and under-weight Category + Company.

**Central operational quote:**
> "Contemporary management protocols and practices overweight a focus on performance and underweight a focus on power. The primary purpose of this book is to rectify the balance by creating a vocabulary and a context for addressing the current and future state of power of your enterprise, and driving innovation initiatives to ensure that power is replenished instead of exhausted." — Moore, *Escape Velocity*, excerpted on [LinkedIn Pulse, Aug 22, 2014](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140822002750-649711-geoffrey-moore-escape-velocity)

*Escape Velocity* is where Moore extends beyond the *category-scale* TALC / chasm framework into *company-scale* portfolio thinking. This is the pivot from "how does a category evolve?" to "how does an incumbent company manage a portfolio of products across categories at different stages?"

### Zone to Win — 2015

The four-zone operating model for incumbents navigating disruption. Companion / operational playbook for *Escape Velocity*.

Four zones, each with distinct operating model, funding logic, and KPI structure:
1. **Performance Zone** — revenue-producing lines of business.
2. **Productivity Zone** — shared services.
3. **Incubation Zone** — venture-style trial balloons, VC-milestone-funded, exempt from Performance / Productivity metrics.
4. **Transformation Zone** — the specific bet chosen from Incubation, scaled aggressively to either (a) defend against a disruptor or (b) create a new performance line ≥10% of company revenue.

Key operational rule: **Zone to Win is an operating-model overlay, not an org chart**. Same employees serve multiple zones with different rules of engagement. Rewriting the org into four divisions matching the zones is a common misapplication.

> "Transformation is hard. You should invoke the Transformation Zone rarely." — Moore, paraphrased across the Diginomica coverage of Gainsight Pulse 2022. See [Gainsight blog: Moore's four zones for customer success](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/geoffrey-moore-applies-his-four-zones-to-customer-success/).

Book page: [geoffreyamoore.com/book/zone-to-win](https://geoffreyamoore.com/book/zone-to-win/).

### The Infinite Staircase — 2021

*The Infinite Staircase: What the Universe Tells Us About Life, Ethics, and Mortality* (BenBella Books). Moore's big-idea philosophy book. A stairstep metaphor of emergent layers of reality from Big Bang to human ethics — secular Theory-of-Everything meets ethical framework.

Not a business book — but Moore returns to it frequently in his LinkedIn essays (the ongoing "Trajectory and the Infinite Staircase" series continues into July 2026). If a user references it or Moore's philosophy work, the skill should recognize it. Not central to chasm-crossing sessions.

Book page: [geoffreyamoore.com/book/the-infinite-staircase](https://geoffreyamoore.com/book/the-infinite-staircase/).

## Ongoing frameworks introduced post-book

### Digital Systems Maturity Model — LinkedIn Pulse, May 2017

Sequential "stairway" for enterprise digital investment. Sequence matters — **no leapfrogging**.

1. **Systems of Record** (ERP, CRM)
2. **Systems of Engagement** (mobile, omni-channel)
3. **Engagement Analytics** (dashboards)
4. **Systems of Intelligence** (ML-driven)
5. **Systems of Disruption** (proprietary insight → new market entry)

Reference: [Moore, "Digital Systems Maturity Model," LinkedIn Pulse, May 22, 2017](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/digital-systems-maturity-model-geoffrey-moore).

The pragmatic use: when a CIO wants to "do AI" but is still on step 1 (Systems of Record), the sequence tells you the next honest move is step 2 (Systems of Engagement), not step 4 (Systems of Intelligence). Leapfrogging fails because each step's data / behavior is the input to the next.

### Category Maturity Life Cycle (CMLC / TMLC) — Chasm Institute canonical form

The consolidated stage taxonomy Chasm Institute teaches: Early Market → Chasm → Bowling Alley → Tornado → Main Street → End of Life. Integrates the frameworks from *Crossing the Chasm* + *Inside the Tornado* into a single six-phase model. Reference: [Chasm Institute Frameworks page](https://www.chasminstitute.com/Resources/Frameworks/tabid/309/Default.aspx).

### 9-Point Strategy Checklist — Chasm Institute

The operational template Moore's consulting practice runs on. Nine slots — Target Customer, Compelling Reason to Buy, Whole Product, Partners & Allies, Distribution, Pricing, Competition, Positioning, Next Target — applied **differently per CMLC stage**. Same nine questions, different answers depending on where you are.

### Four Gears — customer-success era

The Box-endorsed cycle model for the SaaS customer lifecycle. Used in customer-success + Zone to Win contexts. Less central than the other frameworks but relevant when the user is applying Moore's thinking to Customer Success operating models. See Gainsight Pulse 2022 coverage for how Moore maps his four zones onto customer success.

## AI-era commentary — 2024–2026 (Moore's most active current thread)

### Framing: Generative AI = "Digital Transformation, Act 2"

**Source:** [Moore on InnoLead's Innovation Answered podcast, Feb 2024](https://www.innovationleader.com/podcast/geoffrey-moore-on-generative-ai-this-is-digital-transformation-act-2/).

Moore reads generative AI as a second act of digital transformation, following the mobile / cloud / SaaS wave of Act 1. The chasm-crossing dynamics apply, but the adoption speed is different because LLM infrastructure and integration ease are radically better than the prior transformation.

### AI adoption resembles iPhone adoption, not Web adoption

**Source:** [Forbes / Randy Bean, "Crossing The AI Chasm With Geoffrey Moore," Jul 14, 2024](https://www.forbes.com/sites/randybean/2024/07/14/crossing-the-ai-chasm-with-technology-thinker-and-visionary-geoffrey-moore/).

Enterprise AI adoption pattern is "one more like the Apple iPhone, less like The World Wide Web" — faster than historical enterprise-software adoption because of LLM capex + integration ease.

### AI use cases that have crossed the chasm (Moore, mid-2024)

> "AI has already crossed the chasm in many use cases — high frequency trading, digital ad placement, financial regulatory compliance, for example." — Moore, Forbes / Randy Bean, Jul 14, 2024

**Key discrimination:** Moore distinguishes between *category*-chasm and *use-case*-chasm. AI-as-a-category has crossed in narrow, high-value use cases — but that does NOT mean the category as a whole has crossed. This distinction is central to how Moore reads the AI market in 2024–2026.

### AI use cases likely to cross next

> "The next set of landing places will be organizations under unsurmountable performance pressure — air traffic control, public safety, elementary education, home elder care." — Moore, Forbes / Randy Bean, Jul 14, 2024

The screening variable: **which organizations have unsurmountable performance pressure** — i.e., trapped value large enough to force adoption regardless of change-management friction.

### Four-stage adoption model for AI

**Source:** ["The Real Future of AI," Moore blog, Oct 2024](https://geoffreyamoore.com/business_blogs/the-real-future-of-ai/).

Adoption unfolds in four stages:
1. Automate routine bureaucracy.
2. Parallel consumer experiments.
3. Re-engineer mission-critical processes.
4. Transformative replacement.

Most enterprises are in stages 1–2 for most functions as of 2024–2026. Stage 3 is where chasm crossing happens for AI in a given function.

### Agentic AI is not yet across the chasm (Feb 2026)

**Source:** ["Can agentic AI 'cross the chasm' without falling in?" — Moore blog, Feb 6, 2026](https://geoffreyamoore.com/business_blogs/can-agentic-ai-cross-the-chasm-without-falling-in-tech-thought-leader-geoffrey-moore-assesses-progress-in-straddling-the-divide/).

Moore's most recent framework-applied primary text.

> "There are some beachhead markets, but when you 'cross the chasm' it's when one function completely re-engineers itself." — Moore, Diginomica interview, Feb 2026

> "Coding is beginning to feel a bit toward that. Customer service in call centers is a bit toward that." — Moore, Diginomica Feb 2026 (on where agentic AI is closest to crossing)

**Central diagnostic:** neither coding nor customer-service call centers *yet* possess enough **trapped value in the process to cause the complete takeover** — the threshold Moore uses for a real chasm crossing.

### Enterprise strategy essay: "The Real Future of AI" (Oct 2024)

The most comprehensive single essay on Moore's current AI-era framing. Covers the four-stage adoption model, the mission-critical re-engineering test, and the trapped-value variable. Recommended read when a session touches AI adoption strategy at the enterprise level.

## After the Chasm — Scaling Beyond the Beachhead (2024)

**Source:** ["After the Chasm—Scaling Beyond the Beachhead," Moore blog, 2024](https://geoffreyamoore.com/business_blogs/after-the-chasm-scaling-beyond-the-beachhead/).

Directly addresses a common failure mode: crossing the chasm, then trying to sell to CFOs or become a platform prematurely. Moore is explicit:

> "You are still sub-scale for a customer CFO to consider you desirable as a go-to vendor. Your only ally will be a process owner with a problem process that is not getting the IT support they need."

> "Marketing is still a territory capture game, so you want to go after targets that are big enough to matter but small enough to lead."

> "Note that we are still at the departmental level, still a point-product app, not a platform, not a suite… You want your team to be plowing in the adjacent field, not harvesting in the initial one."

**Key operational addition to the original book:** the post-chasm Bowling Alley discipline of *staying departmental*. Platform ambitions premature to a scaled installed base are a common trap.

## Customer outcomes over customer delight (2024)

**Source:** ["Delighting your customers is bunk," Moore blog, 2024](https://geoffreyamoore.com/business_blogs/delighting-your-customers-is-bunk-delivering-on-their-desired-outcomes-is-not/).

The sharpest post-book compelling-reason-to-buy refinement.

> "Delight, after all, is an evanescent experience that comes and goes pretty much as it pleases. It cannot be reliably evoked."

> "What they really want to buy is outcomes."

Substitute compelling-reason discipline (broken-process pain quantified in dollars/hours) for customer-delight metrics (NPS, CSAT). Especially useful when a user is defaulting to delight-metric thinking as a proxy for pragmatist-appropriate value.

## The framework is stable — extensions, not replacements

TALC, chasm, whole product, bowling alley, tornado, main street, gorilla/chimp/monkey — **none have been renamed or restructured since 1991–1995**. What Moore has added:

- **Category-scale extensions**: Category Maturity Life Cycle (CMLC/TMLC), 9-Point Strategy Checklist (Chasm Institute canonical forms).
- **Company-scale extensions**: *Escape Velocity* Hierarchy of Powers (2011), *Zone to Win* four zones (2015).
- **Portfolio-scale extensions**: *Dealing with Darwin* Core vs. Context + three innovation types (2005).
- **Enterprise-digital lens**: Digital Systems Maturity Model (2017).
- **Case-bank refresh**: 3rd edition (2014) of *Crossing the Chasm*.
- **Applied commentary**: 2024–2026 essays on AI adoption.

What Moore has NOT done: replaced any of the original framework. The 1991 book's core is unchanged. Present the extensions as *organization-scale complements* to the *category-scale* TALC / chasm model — not as replacements.
