# DHM Model — Evolution and Refinements

> Gibson Biddle has no book. His body of work is a 12-part Medium essay series (~2018-2020), a Teachable/Maven course, conference talks, and his "Ask Gib" Substack newsletter (2022-present). This file tracks how the DHM model and its supporting frameworks have evolved and been refined over time.

## Timeline of Biddle's output

### Phase 1: The Medium essay series (~2018-2020)

The canonical text. 12 numbered essays (plus intro) under the umbrella "How to Define Your Product Strategy," now with 30,000+ likes. This is where DHM, the Strategy/Metric/Tactic lock-up, Proxy Metrics, Working Bottom-up, Swimlanes, the Product Roadmap, GLEe, GEM, the Quarterly Strategy Meeting, and the Netflix/Chegg case studies were first published in structured form.

The essays were also cross-posted to LinkedIn (as LinkedIn Pulse articles).

Before the essay series, Biddle gave the material as conference talks (e.g., Productized 2019, Mind the Product) and workshop sessions. The essays codified what he'd been teaching live.

### Phase 2: The Teachable course (2020-present)

Biddle packaged the essay content into a self-paced Product Strategy Workshop on Teachable (gibsonbiddles.teachable.com). Price: $499-$699. First two modules free. Includes recorded talks, PDFs, essays, and pre-formatted Google Slides so participants end the course with a complete strategy presentation.

The course covers seven tools/models/frameworks — the same ones from the essay series but with exercises and templates. This format pushed Biddle to make the frameworks more actionable and exercise-oriented. The DHM Scorecard (a simple 3-column table for scoring initiatives) became more prominent as a workshop artifact.

### Phase 3: Maven cohort workshops (2022-present)

Starting ~2022, Biddle launched cohort-based workshops on Maven. Two formats:
- 2x 2.5-hour sessions (longer format, ~$500+)
- 3-hour virtual workshop (shorter format, monthly cohorts, ~$200+, 9-12am PT)

Coaches included Jackie Bavaro and Heather Samarin. The cohort format introduced peer feedback — participants applied frameworks to their own products and received feedback from peers and coaches.

**Key refinement:** The shift from teaching-at-you to teaching-with-you. Maven's live format forced Biddle to develop more interactive exercises and "What would you do?" simulations. The Netflix Quarterly Product Strategy Meeting simulation became a signature workshop moment.

NPS of 69 across workshops. Sessions described as "best-attended, highest-rated, and most-referenced" at product management conferences.

### Phase 4: The "Ask Gib" Substack (2022-present)

After two years of thru-hiking in Spain and the US, Biddle launched "Ask Gib" on Substack (askgib.substack.com), a free product newsletter with ~32,000 subscribers. Cadence: 2-3 essays per month.

**Format shift:** The Substack is Q&A-driven — readers submit questions, Biddle answers them through the lens of his frameworks. This surfaced new applications and edge cases that the original essay series didn't cover.

Key Substack posts:
- "How do all your product strategy frameworks fit together?" — a meta-essay connecting DHM, GEM, GLEe, Swimlanes, and the lock-up into a coherent system.
- "Do your product strategy frameworks work for startups?" — addressing whether DHM applies pre-scale (answer: yes, but with adaptation — startups have fewer strategies and less data, so hypotheses are broader and tests are scrappier).
- "A Brief History of Netflix Personalization (Part Two, from 2007 to 2021)" — extending the personalization story through the streaming era.

### Phase 5: Stanford and INSEAD teaching (ongoing)

Biddle teaches at Stanford and INSEAD. The academic context pushed him to connect his practitioner frameworks to broader strategic theory. No published academic papers, but the teaching has influenced how he articulates the "why" behind each framework — more emphasis on competitive strategy foundations and less on pure Netflix storytelling.

## Key refinements over time

### 1. From Netflix-only to multi-company applicability

The original essays are heavily Netflix-centric. Over time, through workshops and Substack Q&A, Biddle has applied DHM to a broader range of companies:
- **Chegg** — the full case study (Essay #12) showed DHM applied to a startup/growth-stage company.
- **Workshop participant companies** — consumer, B2B, and enterprise companies bring their own products. Biddle has refined guidance for applying DHM outside consumer subscription.
- **Substack Q&A** — "Do your product strategy frameworks work for startups?" explicitly addresses stage-specific adaptation.

### 2. The DHM Scorecard became more prominent

In early talks, DHM was a conceptual filter. In the workshop/course era, the DHM Scorecard — a simple table where you list initiatives and score each on D, H, M — became a primary teaching artifact. The scorecard makes DHM tangible and actionable for product teams.

### 3. The "how frameworks fit together" synthesis

The Substack "Ask Gib" era produced a meta-level synthesis that the original essay series lacked. The question "How do all your product strategy frameworks fit together?" forced Biddle to articulate the relationships:
- **DHM** = the strategy filter (what's strategic?)
- **Strategy/Metric/Tactic lock-up** = the execution bridge (how do we test?)
- **Proxy Metrics** = the measurement layer (how do we know?)
- **GEM** = the prioritization model (which strategic initiatives first?)
- **GLEe** = the long-term phasing (what's the multi-year arc?)
- **Swimlanes** = the org design layer (who owns what?)
- **Quarterly Meeting** = the cadence (when do we review?)

This synthesis is the most useful evolution for practitioners who know individual frameworks but struggle to connect them.

### 4. Startup adaptation guidance

The original essays assumed Netflix-scale operations (millions of users, dedicated pods, A/B testing infrastructure). Through workshop and Substack feedback, Biddle added guidance for earlier-stage companies:
- Fewer strategies (2-3 instead of 4-6)
- Proxy metrics may be more qualitative (customer interviews instead of A/B tests at scale)
- The DHM filter still applies, but "hard to copy" may be about speed and focus rather than data or scale advantages
- GLEe becomes more speculative but still useful for fundraising narratives

### 5. Interactive exercises as teaching method

The workshop era introduced exercises that the essay series didn't have:
- DHM Scorecard exercise: list your top 5 initiatives, score each on D/H/M
- Strategy/Metric/Tactic lock-up exercise: fill in the table for your product
- "What would you do?" Netflix simulations
- Bottom-up scan: bring your project list, identify themes, define strategies

These exercises make the frameworks actionable rather than theoretical.

## What has NOT changed

- **The core DHM definition** — still "delight customers in hard-to-copy, margin-enhancing ways." No modification.
- **The four types of hard-to-copy** — brand, network effects, economies of scale, unique technology. No additions or removals.
- **Strategy = hypotheses** — the framing has not shifted. Biddle still insists strategy is hypotheses, not plans.
- **Netflix as primary case** — Biddle still teaches primarily through Netflix examples. This is deliberate: first-person experience carries more weight than third-party analysis.
- **The emphasis on proxy metrics** — still central. No competing measurement philosophy introduced.
