# Four Fits — Applications

> When Four Fits fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where Four Fits fits well

- **Diagnosing why growth has stalled.** The chain gives you four candidate places to look; whichever fit is weakest is your bottleneck.
- **Evaluating a "move up-market" or "move down-market" decision.** All four fits shift together on this call, and the framework makes the shifts visible.
- **Pricing / ARPU decisions.** ARPU maps directly to Channel-Model Fit zones and Model-Market Fit archetypes.
- **Deciding whether to add a self-serve / freemium tier.** This changes Product-Channel Fit (new channels open) and Channel-Model Fit (economics change) simultaneously.
- **Choosing between two candidate markets to expand into.** Model-Market Fit threshold clears or doesn't; the rest of the chain follows.
- **Critiquing a growth plan or growth section of a board deck.** Four Fits gives four diagnostic lenses.
- **Designing a growth loop from scratch for a new product.** The loop types + closure test are directly applicable.
- **Post-Series-A companies asking "how do we scale from $10M to $100M".** This is the framework's design target.
- **AI-era stress-testing of a previously-working business.** The 2024–2025 update specifically addresses this.
- **Deciding whether the ChatGPT-as-channel opportunity applies.** The distribution-shift framing plus Product-Channel Fit gives you the shape.
- **Hiring the first growth leader / structuring the growth team.** The Growth Machine operating layer is directly applicable.

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **Pre-PMF search.** Four Fits assumes you have (or are close to) PMF and are heading toward $100M scale. Pre-PMF is a search problem, not a fit-chain problem. Use JTBD / continuous discovery / Lean Startup / Sean Ellis's PMF survey instead.

- **Lifestyle / bootstrapped businesses.** The framework is calibrated for $100M+ venture-backed scale. A $10M ARR bootstrapped SaaS can still apply the fits diagnostically, but the pressure and thresholds are different. Say so up front.

- **Pure product / UX / usability decisions.** "Should this button be blue or red" isn't a Four Fits question. Use standard product-discovery methods.

- **Culture, hiring, or organizational design without a growth-system angle.** The Growth Machine layer touches these, but leading with the fit chain when the real question is org design will feel forced.

- **Turnaround / restructuring where diagnosis is more urgent than choice.** If the org is on fire (mass churn, missed payroll, product outage), stabilize first, then apply Four Fits. Balfour's framework presupposes you have runway to think.

- **Portfolio ranking across many product bets.** Four Fits is meant for one integrated chain, not for ranking 15 products against each other. Use ODI / JTBD priority scoring / opportunity solution tree instead.

- **Deeply regulated markets where "how do we win" is dominated by compliance / relationships / lobbying, not growth mechanics.** The framework assumes some level of market efficiency where product / channel / model choices matter.

- **When the user just wants a summary of Four Fits.** Give them [the essay](https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework) link. Don't run the full diagnostic.

## How Four Fits composes with other frameworks

### Sits underneath Playing to Win (Martin)

Playing to Win asks "where will we play" and "how will we win". Four Fits asks "given that where and how, does the chain support $100M with real unit economics?". Use PtW to make the choice, Four Fits to test whether the choice can scale.

**Sequence:** run PtW cascade → generate one integrated choice → run Four Fits diagnostic on it → iterate if the fit chain doesn't hold at scale.

### Sits above Jobs-to-be-Done

JTBD defines the segment and value prop that make PMF real. Four Fits then tests whether that PMF can be scaled through the fit chain.

**Sequence:** JTBD to define the job and segment → validate PMF via Ellis survey / retention curves → run Four Fits diagnostic starting from Product-Channel Fit.

### Pairs with 7 Powers (Helmer)

Helmer's seven powers (scale economies, network effects, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resources, process power) show up throughout the fit chain:
- **Network effects** → Product-Channel Fit (viral loops)
- **Scale economies** → Channel-Model Fit (low-cost channels)
- **Switching costs** → Model-Market Fit + retention
- **Cornered resources** → Channel-Model Fit (exclusive channel access)
- **Counter-positioning** → the ARPU-Danger-Zone escape move

Use 7 Powers as vocabulary for durable advantage inside the fit chain.

### Replaces AARRR / Pirate Metrics as an acquisition framework

AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue) is a funnel. Growth Loops replace it for compounding businesses. If a user is stuck in AARRR thinking, redirect to loops.

### Composes with North Star Framework (Amplitude)

North Star gives you a single metric to align on. Four Fits gives you the system that produces the metric. Balfour has warned against North Star traps — measuring one leg while others rot. Use both, with Four Fits as the check.

### Complements Rumelt's kernel

Rumelt: diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action. Four Fits: which of the four fits is broken, and what's the coordinated move across the chain to fix it. Rumelt's crux language often names WHICH fit is broken; Four Fits gives you the mechanism-level move.

## Frameworks Balfour has explicitly critiqued or reframed

- **"Growth hacking" as a discipline** — reframed. Growth is a system, not a bag of hacks. Sean Ellis's original PMF signal survives; the "hacker" framing does not.
- **AARRR / Pirate Metrics** — critiqued as funnel-only. Loops replace AARRR for compounding businesses.
- **Single-metric North Star** — critiqued. Measure the whole system, not one leg.
- **"Copy the playbook"** — critiqued as tactics-mimicry without matching fits.
- **"Add a growth marketer"** — critiqued as marketing-sub-function framing of what should be a cross-functional discipline.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work, redirect using Balfour's specific reframe.

## The AI-era addition (2024–2026)

The most important recent shift: Four Fits now assumes AI is continuously restressing all four fits. Applications where this matters most:

- **Any business where the value prop is "we do X faster/cheaper/better than manual"** — check PMF against latest ChatGPT/Claude capability.
- **Any business with a freemium tier** — check Channel-Model Fit against per-inference AI costs.
- **Any business that depends on SEO or paid discovery** — check Product-Channel Fit against ChatGPT-as-discovery shift.
- **Any business selling into markets whose buyers are consolidating around AI-native tools** — check Model-Market Fit against emerging AI-native buyer archetypes.
