# Pattern Breakers — Material outside the 2024 book

> **This is the differential of this skill.** The 2024 book *Pattern Breakers* consolidated Maples's framework. But most of the operational devices were developed years earlier on Medium, on the *Starting Greatness* podcast, and in Floodgate essays — and Maples has continued publishing on the Pattern Breakers Substack and podcast since. Most Claude or Codex responses about "Pattern Breakers" pull from the book alone. This file captures both the pre-book material that shaped the book *and* the post-book material that extends it.

## Pre-book devices Maples was already using (and still uses)

### Backcasting — the 2019 Medium essay

Maples published [*How to build a breakthrough — the secret of Backcasting*](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06) years before the 2024 book. The essay lays down the reversal that anchors the whole framework:

- **Forecasting** projects forward from known realities, assumes the world stays mostly the same. Suitable for incremental improvement; fatal to breakthrough thinking.
- **Backcasting** stands in the future and works backward. Searches for "exponentially different and unique" outcomes.
- **Three-step process:** identify inflections → envision potential futures (plausible / possible / preposterous) → gather breakthrough insights by interviewing "seers".

The book folds backcasting in but doesn't foreground it as much as this essay does. If the user's problem is *"how do I even find a breakthrough insight in the first place?"*, this is the source to pull.

> "Starting from the future and working backwards is the second key to finding a breakthrough."

### Heresy vs. Contrarianism — the 2020 essay

[*The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups*](https://medium.com/@m2jr/the-heresy-of-breakthrough-startups-289f12fb5930) contains Maples's cleanest distinction between empty contrarianism and substantive heresy.

- **Contrarianism** disagrees with the crowd.
- **Heresy** replaces the crowd's assumption with a *better explanation*.

This essay also introduces the four stress-test questions the skill uses in `heuristics.md`: **Refusal, Heresy, Inflection, Demo.** Breakthroughs are *experienced*, not merely argued.

Quotes to have on hand:
> "If what you're doing doesn't sound like heresy to someone, it's probably too similar to what's already been done."
> "Uber-for-X derivatives imitate a model of success without finding a new way to fix a real frustration."

### Delta 4 — from *Starting Greatness*

Maples's rule of thumb for whether a new product will actually displace an incumbent. Rate the new experience 1–10 and the incumbent 1–10; the delta needs to be **≥4** for switching cost to be worth it. Below 4, customers stay put — no matter what they say in surveys.

**Where he introduced it:** *Starting Greatness* podcast episodes (2019–2022), long before the 2024 book, and referenced in [The Generalist — Letters to a Young Investor](https://www.generalist.com/p/mike-maples-jr-1).

**When to use it:** any time the user proposes a product replacing an existing behavior. Force honest 1–10 scores on both sides and compute.

### Founder-Future Fit — a Maples addition to PMF conversation

Product-Market Fit is necessary but not sufficient. Maples adds **Founder-Future Fit** — the alignment between a founder's skills, motivations, and networks and the future they're building. A hot market with the wrong founder rarely produces a breakthrough.

**Where he developed it:** *Starting Greatness* episodes and Floodgate essays. Foregrounded in the 2024 book but the concept predates it.

**When to use it:** when the user is evaluating a founder (as investor) or when a founder is evaluating themselves. Ask: what about *you* — specifically — gives you a structural edge over anyone else attacking this opportunity?

### Thunder Lizards and Atomic Eggs — the Floodgate mental model

Maples's investor-facing vocabulary. **Thunder Lizards** are the hyper-disruptive companies (Twitter, Twitch, Lyft, Tesla) that reshape their categories. **Atomic Eggs** are the earliest-stage bets Maples is hunting — the tiny origins of what become Thunder Lizards.

**When to use it:** investor conversations, portfolio-construction questions, "what do you actually look for?" pitches.

**Reference:** [Floodgate — Mike Maples Jr bio](https://www.floodgate.com/team/mike-maples-jr).

### Three heuristics for PMF (also from *Starting Greatness*)

Maples articulated three heuristics for maximizing the odds of PMF:
1. Find the right North Star.
2. Offer a Delta 4 experience.
3. Don't fool yourself.

These predate the 2024 book and remain live guidance. Reference: episodes at [https://greatness.floodgate.com/](https://greatness.floodgate.com/).

### The Rachleff 2x2 (attributed, not coined by Maples)

Maples cites and popularized **Andy Rachleff's non-consensus-and-right 2x2**. Rachleff (Wealthfront co-founder, Benchmark Capital co-founder, Stanford lecturer) is Maples's most-cited peer on the "you must be non-consensus AND right" point. When invoking the 2x2, attribute to Rachleff and credit Maples for the popularization.

## Post-book material (2024 → )

### The rebrand — Starting Greatness → Pattern Breakers podcast

Coinciding with the book, Maples rebranded his podcast from *Starting Greatness* to *Pattern Breakers*. The archive of *Starting Greatness* episodes remains — some of the richest single-conversation Maples material exists there (Andreessen, Hoffman, Systrom, Krieger, Sarah Leary). Ongoing episodes under the new brand continue to feature new interviews and Maples's own "breakthrough lessons" solo episodes.

Live URL: [https://greatness.floodgate.com/](https://greatness.floodgate.com/)

### The Pattern Breakers Substack

Maples launched [https://patternbreakers.substack.com/](https://patternbreakers.substack.com/) alongside the book. Essays here extend the book's arguments and apply them to current situations (AI as inflection, incumbent vulnerability, founder mindset). Cadence varies — check periodically.

### Book positioning — as endorsed by Steve Blank

Notably, Steve Blank (Lean Startup co-creator) endorsed *Pattern Breakers* as *"the most important start-up book of the last ten years."* This matters because a naive read of Maples might position Pattern Breakers as anti-Lean. Blank's endorsement signals they should be read as complementary at different phases: **Pattern Breakers is upstream of Lean.** Find the right insight first (Maples), then iterate to PMF on it (Blank/Ries).

### The book's structure

*Pattern Breakers* is organized in three sections:

1. **Pattern-Breaking Ideas** — Inflections, Insights, Movements, and the frame that ideas without all three rarely produce breakthroughs.
2. **Pattern-Breaking Actions** — Movement-building, Storytelling (founder as guide), Disagreeableness, the Implementation Prototype method.
3. **No Limits** — overcoming self-imposed constraints on thinking and acting.

If the user wants to go deeper on any single element, this is the map — and Ch. 3 (Insights) plus the Implementation Prototype chapter are usually the most operationally dense.

### Peter Ziebelman's contribution

Co-author Peter Ziebelman is a Stanford GSB lecturer on entrepreneurship and general partner at Palo Alto Venture Partners. His voice in the book adds pedagogical structure and academic framing to Maples's investor perspective. Worth acknowledging: this is a joint work, not a solo Maples book. When citing, credit both.

## Refinements and evolutions

### From *Starting Greatness* to *Pattern Breakers* — evolving the language

Compare the older vocabulary to the newer:

| Older (Starting Greatness era, 2019–2022) | Newer (Pattern Breakers book, 2024+) |
|---|---|
| Breakthrough insight | Insight (as one of three elements) |
| Backcasting (standalone device) | Backcasting (integrated with Inflection/Insight identification) |
| Delta 4 (single heuristic) | Delta 4 remains — applied to test the experience gap once the insight is in place |
| Thunder Lizards | Pattern Breakers (as founder archetype) |
| Non-consensus and right (Rachleff) | Same — but now framed as the test for Insight in the three-element model |

The book didn't replace the earlier devices — it integrated them into a single three-element idea + three-element action model.

### AI as the current-cycle inflection

Post-book essays and podcast episodes engage with AI as *the* current-cycle inflection. Maples's discipline on this is consistent: "AI" is not the inflection; the *specific new capability at a specific cost point unlocking a specific new customer job* is the inflection. Founders who invoke AI vaguely get pushed to specificity.

## Adjacent frameworks Maples has commented on

- **Christensen's Disruption Theory** — Maples treats Christensen as a respected predecessor. Disruption focuses on the incumbent's dilemma; Pattern Breakers focuses upstream on what makes the disruption possible now (the inflection). Compatible, at different altitudes.
- **Thiel's *Zero to One*** — closest philosophical peer. Thiel's "secret" ≈ Maples's "insight". Both prize non-consensus bets. Maples adds the specific mechanic of inflection + backcasting.
- **Lean Startup (Ries/Blank)** — surface tension, real complementarity. Lean's iterate-to-PMF is downstream of finding a breakthrough insight. Blank's endorsement of *Pattern Breakers* is the tell.
- **Blitzscaling (Hoffman)** — further downstream. Blitzscaling is what you do once you've found a breakthrough insight and need to capture the market before anyone else catches up.
- **JTBD (Christensen / Ulwick / Moesta)** — Maples doesn't attack JTBD; frames it as downstream methodology useful *once* you have an insight into which job is being reframed by an inflection.
- **Playing to Win (Martin)** — downstream. PtW cascades once you know your winning aspiration. Pattern Breakers is about whether the breakthrough exists at all.
- **Good Strategy/Bad Strategy (Rumelt)** — complementary at different altitudes. Rumelt's diagnosis finds the crux; Pattern Breakers asks whether the crux is powered by an inflection or by present-anchored assumptions.

## Frameworks Maples has effectively pushed against

- **Pattern matching (as investor practice)** — the whole thesis is a rebuttal to this.
- **Trend-following as strategy** — trends produce optimization; only inflections produce breakthroughs.
- **Contrarianism-as-identity** — heresy with a better explanation is the substantive version.
- **Uber-for-X / Notion-for-Y derivatives** — imitation without insight.
- **"AI wrapper" thinking (2024–2026)** — the current-cycle consensus dressed up.
