# Pattern Breakers — Method

> The canonical description of Maples's framework in his own terms. Fidelity is the point — Pattern Breakers is opinionated, and softening any of the three elements collapses the method into generic "how to evaluate a startup idea" language.

## Definition

Maples's operating framing:

> "Great startup founders are pattern breakers. They offer solutions that depart from old methods." — *Pattern Breakers* (2024)

And on breakthrough builders specifically:

> "Breakthrough builders are visitors from the future, telling us what's coming. They seem crazy in the present but they are right about the future." — Maples, essays and podcast, recurring

Two words in that framing are non-negotiable:

- **"Pattern breaker"** — the idea, and the founder, must break the pattern of what's been done. Optimizing within the pattern is not a breakthrough; it's a business.
- **"Visitor from the future"** — the founder isn't extrapolating from the present. They're already living somewhere the rest of us haven't reached yet, and telling us what they see.

## The three elements of a pattern-breaking IDEA

Maples insists breakthroughs require *all three*. Missing any one is a signal that the idea isn't yet a breakthrough — and iterating on the surface won't produce one.

### 1. Inflection

**What it asks:** What specific external event created a *new capability that didn't exist before*, at what specific moment, via what specific mechanism?

**Not:** a trend, a macro theme, a rising line on a chart, "AI is big", or "consumer behavior is changing." Those are backgrounds. An inflection is a tipping-point event that unlocks a genuinely new possibility space.

**Author's words:**
> "Inflections should be understood even before markets." — [How to build a breakthrough](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06)

**Trend vs. Inflection — the test Maples uses:**
- **Trend:** solar panel costs decline ~10% per year.
- **Inflection:** the moment solar becomes cheaper than grid electricity for a given customer segment.

Trends produce optimization opportunities. Inflections produce breakthrough opportunities. The difference is not size — it's *whether something newly possible now exists that wasn't possible before*.

**Recurring examples Maples reaches for:**
- Lyft: iPhone 4s ships with a GPS chip precise to ~1 meter → coordinated peer-to-peer transportation becomes newly possible.
- Twitch: broadband quality reaches a threshold where live game-streaming is watchable → *and* gaming culture reaches mainstream mass simultaneously.
- Airbnb: Facebook Connect enables trust signals + smartphone GPS enables coordination → renting a stranger's home becomes psychologically viable.
- Okta: SaaS proliferation + browser as universal client → identity management as a new layer becomes necessary.

**Anti-move Maples warns against:** waving at a macro shift without naming the specific mechanism. *"AI changes everything"* is not an inflection. *"With inference cost of $X per million tokens, a customer support agent that would have been $Y/query now costs $Z, making it viable for SMB budgets"* — that could be an inflection. Specificity is the tell.

### 2. Insight

**What it asks:** What nonobvious truth do you have about how this inflection can be harnessed to radically change human capacity or behavior — a truth that most other smart people either don't know or don't believe?

**Not:** an opinion, a bet, a feature list, a market observation, or "customers want X." An insight is *earned* (through direct immersion), *nonobvious* (most people either don't see it or disagree), and *right* (borne out by future reality even if the present rejects it).

**Author's words:**
> Insights are "nonobvious truths about how one or more inflections can be harnessed to radically change human capacities and behaviors." — *Pattern Breakers*

**The non-consensus-and-right 2x2** (Andy Rachleff's framing, which Maples cites and popularized):

|                     | **Consensus**             | **Non-Consensus**          |
|---------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Right**           | Commodified — the market prices it in. No excess returns. | **The breakthrough zone.** Where outlier startups live. |
| **Wrong**           | Delusional consensus — mass mistake. | Just crazy. Or too early. |

Only the **non-consensus AND right** quadrant produces breakthroughs. Both conditions matter:
- Consensus and right = you'll build a fine business but competitors will arbitrage the returns.
- Non-consensus but wrong = you're just weird.
- Non-consensus and right = the market rejects you initially and rewards you later.

**How Maples talks about earning an insight:**
- **Living in the future** — direct immersion in emerging behavior. Andreessen at NCSA seeing the browser is the archetype. Founders should be users of tomorrow's technology today, not researchers of yesterday's.
- **Earned secrets** — knowledge won through hands-on experimentation and proximity, not from decks or reports.
- **Interviewing seers** — spending time with people who already live in the future scenario you're building toward.

**Test for a real insight:** could you write it as a sentence that would make a sophisticated observer say "I disagree" or "that's crazy" — while a second sophisticated observer, one who's also living in the future, says "you're right"?

### 3. Idea

**What it asks:** What is the specific product or service you'll build to implement the insight?

**Important:** the idea is downstream of the insight. Iterating on the idea does not fix a weak insight. Founders often confuse the two, and spend years iterating a product that was built on a consensus insight.

**Author's words:**
> "An idea is a specific product; an insight is the nonobvious truth powering it." — *Pattern Breakers*

**Maples on the failure mode:**
> "Most founders make the mistake of trying to think of a start-up idea and then let their opti­mism convince them it's worth pursuing. But trying to think of a start-up idea sets founders on a path that relies on the rules of the present."

The corollary: start with the inflection and the insight. The idea then falls out.

## The three elements of pattern-breaking ACTION

Having a pattern-breaking idea is necessary but not sufficient. The founder also has to *act different*. Maples: *"Pattern-breaking founders don't succeed by blending in or sticking to the usual paths."* Three mechanisms:

### 4. Movement

**What it asks:** Are you building a community of early believers around a higher purpose — one that distinguishes clearly "the world that is" from "the world that can be"?

**Not:** a marketing campaign, a brand community, or a customer base. A movement has believers, not just users. Believers tolerate rough product because they want the future you're building.

**Author's words:**
> A movement is "a group of people joining forces to redefine the future." — *Pattern Breakers*

**Tesla:** the mission is accelerating sustainable-energy adoption, not out-competing Ford. Believers bought a Model S in 2013 knowing the charging network was thin — because they wanted the future.

**Lyft's pink mustache:** community identity, not just ride-share.

**Test for a real movement:** would the early believers keep believing if the product were half-broken for a year? If yes, it's a movement. If no, it's a customer base.

### 5. Storytelling

**What it asks:** Does your narrative position the customer as the hero on a transformative journey, with you as the guide?

**Author's words:**
> "The founder is the mentor, not the hero." — *Pattern Breakers*, on the hero's-journey framing (Obi-Wan, not Luke).

**Why this matters:** founders who make themselves the hero of the story fail to recruit believers, because believers don't want to help *your* journey — they want to join *their own*.

### 6. Disagreeableness

**What it asks:** Are you willing to sustain resistance from critics, incumbents, present-anchored advisors, and (sometimes) governments?

**Author's words:**
> "Pattern-breaking founders must choose to prioritize their mission for the long term, even if it means that the present will attack their status." — *Pattern Breakers*

**Careful:** disagreeableness is a tool, not a personality. *"Extreme or gratuitous disagreeableness is damaging."* Maples names Jobs and Musk as founders who used disagreeableness productively — as a shield against conformity pressure, not as a stance.

## The mindset — Backcasting

Maples's central mental move, developed pre-book on Medium and made canonical in the 2024 book.

**Forecasting** stands in the present and projects forward. Assumes the world stays mostly the same. Suitable for optimization. Fatal for breakthroughs.

**Backcasting** stands in the future and works backward to the present. As Maples puts it:

> "Starting from the future and working backwards is the second key to finding a breakthrough." — [How to build a breakthrough](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06)

**The three-step backcasting process:**

1. **Identify inflections.** What technology, adoption, regulatory, or belief inflections are reshaping possibility right now?
2. **Envision potential futures.** Maples divides these into three categories to expand imagination:
   - **Plausible** — non-obvious but logical (Okta's unified cloud identity).
   - **Possible** — emerging from lived experience (Andreessen and the web browser).
   - **Preposterous** — seemingly crazy (Musk on EVs and rockets).
3. **Gather breakthrough insights.** By interviewing "seers" living in future scenarios, not by extrapolating from surveys of the present.

**Why the reversal matters:** *"most of your friends, advisors, and even investors are useless [at spotting breakthroughs] because they inhabit the present."* Backcasting is the operational move that gets you out of the present's gravity.

## Integration

These pieces must reinforce each other. Concretely:

- Your **inflection** must be what makes the **insight** newly possible — not something you name after the fact to dress the pitch up.
- Your **insight** must be the reason a **movement** would form — believers gather around a truth about the future, not around a feature list.
- Your **idea** must be what implements the insight — not a separate product concept you've retrofitted.
- Your **backcasting** must have preceded the idea — if you started with the idea and then wrote the inflection into the story, the story might sound good but the discovery didn't happen in Maples's mode.

If any element floats free of the others, you have a pitch, not a pattern break.

## Sequence of application

Maples does not prescribe a strict order — the elements are a matched set, not a to-do list. In practice for the assistant:

1. **Start with the specific idea on the table.** What is the user actually proposing?
2. **Run the three-element test on the idea.** Inflection specific? Insight non-consensus AND right? Movement plausible? For each, either name the answer or name the gap.
3. **If any element is missing, don't paper over it.** Name the gap. Ask what the user would put there — or push back that the idea may not yet be a breakthrough.
4. **If all three are present, stress-test each.** Delta 4 on the experience (see `post-book.md`). The four questions: Refusal, Heresy, Inflection, Demo (see `heuristics.md`).
5. **Match Maples's voice.** Historical arc → specific mechanism → question back to the user.

## What this method is NOT

Maples is emphatic about the negative definition. This method is NOT:

- **A pitch-scoring rubric.** Filling in three boxes doesn't produce a breakthrough. The framework describes what breakthroughs look like from the outside; it doesn't manufacture them.
- **A substitute for iteration once you have PMF.** Pattern Breakers is upstream. Once the insight is validated and the movement is forming, iterate hard.
- **A validation exercise for a decision already made.** Founders love to describe their existing plan in Pattern Breakers language. If nothing about the plan changes after the exercise, the exercise didn't happen.
- **The same as contrarianism.** *"Contrarianism disagrees with the crowd; heresy replaces the crowd's assumption with a better explanation."* Being different is not enough.
- **The same as Disruption (Christensen).** Christensen focuses on the incumbent's dilemma. Maples focuses upstream on why the disruption is possible now (the inflection).

**Author's words on the trap:**
> "If what you're doing doesn't sound like heresy to someone, it's probably too similar to what's already been done." — [The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups](https://medium.com/@m2jr/the-heresy-of-breakthrough-startups-289f12fb5930)

The critique is that most startup advice — including much of what passes for lean/agile orthodoxy — teaches founders to iterate from a consensus starting point. That produces businesses, not breakthroughs. Pattern Breakers is Maples's alternative.
