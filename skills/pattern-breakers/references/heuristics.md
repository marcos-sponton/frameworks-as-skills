# Pattern Breakers — Heuristics, Do's, Don'ts, Gotchas

> The practical devices — Maples's "small operational tricks" — that separate applying the framework well from the pattern-matching, trend-chasing, iterate-your-way-there mode that dominates most startup thinking. Attribution is precise: this comes from the book, this from a 2024 podcast, this from a 2019 Medium essay.

## Do's

### Start with the inflection, not the idea

Maples's most-repeated correction to founders. Founders default to "I have an idea, is it good?" — which anchors the analysis in the present. Correct order: identify the inflection first, then the insight, then the idea.

**How to apply:** for any startup idea on the table, immediately ask *"what specifically became newly possible, at what moment, via what mechanism?"* If the answer is a macro theme ("AI is big"), you don't yet have an inflection — you have a background condition.

**Author's words:**
> "Most founders make the mistake of trying to think of a start-up idea and then let their opti­mism convince them it's worth pursuing. But trying to think of a start-up idea sets founders on a path that relies on the rules of the present." — *Pattern Breakers* (2024)

### Test insights against the non-consensus-and-right 2x2

Andy Rachleff's frame, popularized by Maples. For any insight the user proposes, ask two questions in order:

1. **Is this non-consensus?** Would most sophisticated observers disagree, or is this the received wisdom?
2. **Is it right?** What evidence do you have that the future will vindicate you — not just that you feel strongly?

Both conditions must be met. Consensus + right = commodified. Non-consensus + wrong = crazy. Non-consensus + right = the breakthrough zone.

**How to apply:** ask the user to write the insight as a single sentence a sophisticated observer would push back on. If no one would push back, the insight isn't non-consensus enough.

**Source:** Andy Rachleff, quoted by Maples across essays and *Starting Greatness* episodes. See also *Pattern Breakers* Chapter 3.

### Backcast, don't forecast

Maples's signature mental move. When the user says "given today's market...", nudge them to stand in the future first.

**How to apply:**
1. Ask the user: *"Imagine it's five to ten years from now and this bet worked. What does the world look like?"* Force specificity — no vague futurism.
2. Then: *"What had to happen between now and then for that world to be real?"* — that's the backcast path.
3. Look for the inflection embedded in the path. If none exists, the future imagined isn't earned — it's wishful.

**Author's words:**
> "Starting from the future and working backwards is the second key to finding a breakthrough." — [How to build a breakthrough](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06)

**Source:** [How to build a breakthrough — the secret of Backcasting](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06)

### Live in the future — earn the insight through immersion

Insights don't come from surveys of current customers. They come from immersion in emerging behavior. Andreessen at NCSA. The gamers who watched Justin Kan stream well before mainstream livestreaming existed. Airbnb's founders renting out their own apartment.

**How to apply:** ask the user *"how much of your week is spent with people already living in the future you're building toward?"* If the answer is "not much", the risk is that the insight is borrowed, not earned.

**Author's words:**
> "Interviewing seers living in future scenarios" is the input to breakthrough insights — not surveying present customers. — *Pattern Breakers*

### Use the Delta 4 test on the experience

Rate the new experience 1–10. Rate the incumbent's experience 1–10. The delta must be **≥4** for the switching cost to be worth it. Below 4, the customer stays put — even if they say they'd switch.

**How to apply:** for any product replacing an existing behavior, force the user to score both experiences honestly and compute the delta. If the delta is 1–3, the pitch is asking customers to switch for marginal gain; that rarely produces movements.

**Source:** Maples on the *Starting Greatness* podcast and multiple essays. Introduced well before the 2024 book.

### Use Implementation Prototype, not MVP

Maples's alternative to the MVP concept. An Implementation Prototype is a discovery tool designed to reveal:
1. Which specific benefits customers experience as essential (not just nice).
2. Which specific segments feel *desperation*, not interest.

**Canonical example:** Chegg's textbook rental discovery. Facing cash depletion, they built a fake checkout page for textbook rentals. Randomized rental prices $35–$75 to measure *desperation, not interest*. Checkout "crashed" — they never had to fulfill. Result: proved the willingness-to-pay curve and unlocked investor funding.

**How to apply:** when the user proposes an MVP, ask *"what specifically will this test — interest, or desperation? And how will you know?"* If it tests interest, redesign.

**Source:** *Pattern Breakers*, Ch. on Implementation Prototypes.

### Stress-test with Refusal, Heresy, Inflection, Demo

Four questions Maples uses to pressure-test a breakthrough claim. Breakthroughs are *experienced*, not merely argued.

- **Refusal:** would you literally refuse to sell this to certain customers because they wouldn't get it? (Pattern breakers polarize.)
- **Heresy:** does the insight sound heretical to at least some sophisticated observers? (If no, likely too consensus.)
- **Inflection:** can you name the specific inflection powering this now vs. any other time? (If no, no "why now.")
- **Demo:** can you make someone *feel* the difference in a demo, not just argue it? (Figma's live collaboration, Stripe's 8 lines of Ruby, Tesla's Autopilot — demonstrated, not explained.)

**Source:** [The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups](https://medium.com/@m2jr/the-heresy-of-breakthrough-startups-289f12fb5930).

### Recruit believers before customers

A movement forms from a small group of people who share the founder's view of the future. That group is upstream of the customer base — and often overlaps with early hires, early investors, early users.

**How to apply:** ask the user *"who are the first five people you need to convince, not to buy, but to believe? What's the story you'd tell them?"* If they don't have five, the movement isn't real yet.

**Source:** *Pattern Breakers*, Ch. on Movements.

## Don'ts

### Don't confuse a trend for an inflection

Maples's most vocal deletion. Trends are gradual continuations of established curves. Inflections are tipping points that unlock genuinely new possibilities.

**Author's words:**
> "Solar costs declining annually is a trend. A tipping point where solar becomes cheaper than grid electricity is an inflection." — paraphrased from multiple essays.

**How to redirect:** force the specific mechanism. When exactly did the newly-possible thing become possible? What was true before that moment, and what became true after? If you can't name the switch, it's a trend.

### Don't confuse being contrarian for being non-consensus AND right

Maples's cleanest single distinction. Contrarianism is empty — disagreeing with the crowd for its own sake. Heresy replaces the crowd's assumption with a *better explanation*.

**Author's words:**
> "Contrarianism disagrees with the crowd; heresy replaces the crowd's assumption with a better explanation." — [The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups](https://medium.com/@m2jr/the-heresy-of-breakthrough-startups-289f12fb5930)

**How to redirect:** ask *"what specifically do you believe that most smart people don't, and what makes you right?"* Both halves must have answers.

### Don't build an Uber-for-X or an X-for-Y

**What it looks like:** *"We're Uber for dog-walking."* *"We're the Notion of restaurant menus."* Imitating a shape without a new insight or inflection.

**Why it fails:** you inherit none of the inflection that powered the original (mobile + GPS + trust for Uber; block-based-editing + collaborative-cursors for Notion) but keep all the execution difficulty. And the incumbent shape is now well-priced by the market.

**How to redirect:** rewrite the pitch without naming any existing company. If it collapses without the analogy, there's no insight of your own.

### Don't hand-wave "AI changes everything"

The 2024–2026 version of the same trap. AI is an inflection background, not an inflection. The inflection lives in the *specific* new capability — what specifically became possible, for whom, at what cost, when.

**How to redirect:** force the user to complete the sentence *"before [specific moment], you couldn't [specific thing] because [specific constraint]. Now you can because [specific mechanism]."* If they can't fill in the blanks, the inflection is vague.

### Don't iterate to PMF from a consensus starting point

Maples doesn't attack Lean Startup directly (Steve Blank endorsed *Pattern Breakers*), but the frame is a direct challenge. Iteration compounds a strong insight; it doesn't manufacture one. Founders who start from a consensus insight and iterate rarely produce breakthroughs — they produce fine businesses that get arbitraged.

**Author's words:**
> "If what you're doing doesn't sound like heresy to someone, it's probably too similar to what's already been done." — *The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups*

**How to redirect:** run the non-consensus-and-right 2x2 on the *starting* insight, not the current product. If the starting insight was consensus, iteration won't rescue it.

### Don't rely on present-anchored advisors, friends, or investors

**Author's words (paraphrased):**
> Most of your friends, advisors, and even investors inhabit the present and are structurally poor at identifying breakthroughs. — [How to build a breakthrough](https://medium.com/@m2jr/how-to-build-a-breakthrough-3071b6415b06)

**How to redirect:** feedback from present-anchored people is signal about the present, not about the future you're building. Weight accordingly. Also — actively spend time with people already living in the future scenario you're building toward.

### Don't confuse a movement with a marketing campaign

A movement has believers who tolerate rough product because they want the future. A marketing campaign has attention that dries up when the discount ends. Building a "movement" without a non-consensus insight underneath it is theater.

### Don't retrofit the framework onto a decision already made

Founders (and investors) love to describe a plan they already believe in using Pattern Breakers vocabulary. If nothing about the plan changes after the exercise, no real application happened. It was validation theater.

**How to check:** at the end of the session, ask *"what did you change your mind about? What are you going to do differently on Monday?"* If nothing, the exercise wasn't real.

### Don't confuse being first with being right

First-mover framing is a comfort blanket. What matters is the **Goldilocks moment** — not too early (Justin.tv on mainstream livestreaming) and not too late (Quibi on short-form mobile video). Being first without an inflection powering the *now* just makes you the science project incumbents cite when they enter later.

## Gotchas (things that go wrong even when you think you're doing it right)

### The "AI wave" collapse

**What it looks like:** every pitch invokes AI as the inflection. Fine — but which specific capability, at which specific cost point, unlocking which specific customer job?

**How to check:** if the pitch would still make sense with "AI" replaced by "cloud" or "internet", the inflection isn't specific enough.

### Insight described as opinion

**What it looks like:** *"We believe X is the future."* That's a bet, not an insight. An insight is a nonobvious *truth* — something demonstrable, not just held.

**How to check:** can you show early data, a working demo, or seer testimony (people already living in the future scenario) that supports the claim? If you can only argue for it, it's an opinion.

### Confusing product-market fit with founder-future fit

**What it looks like:** founder finds a market pulling on the product. Great — but does the *founder* fit the future being built? Do their skills, motivations, and networks give them a structural edge over anyone else attacking this opportunity?

**Founder-Future Fit is Maples's addition** to the PMF conversation. A hot market with the wrong founder rarely produces a breakthrough. See [The Generalist — Letters to a Young Investor](https://www.generalist.com/p/mike-maples-jr-1).

### The "obvious in hindsight" trap

**What it looks like:** the insight sounds obvious now — of course people would rent their apartments to strangers on the internet, of course people would want to watch other people play video games. But it wasn't obvious then.

**Why it fails as evaluation:** if you're evaluating a *future* breakthrough with today's hindsight, you'll reject anything that sounds crazy — which is exactly what a real breakthrough sounds like in the present.

**How to check:** would you have funded Twitch in 2011 based on the pitch alone? Would you have booked Airbnb #1? If not, and yet those turned out to be breakthroughs — recognize that the current "sounds crazy" reaction may be signal.

### Movement theater

**What it looks like:** "we're building a movement" without the underlying insight and inflection. A pink mustache without a Lyft.

**Author's words:** movements form around a shared truth about the future. Without the truth, there's no movement — just a logo.

### The Goldilocks trap

**What it looks like:** the founder is right about the future, but wrong about the timing. Justin.tv was right that mainstream people would livestream — but too early. They pivoted to Twitch, which caught the right timing on a different segment.

**How to check:** what specific evidence tells you *now* is the moment vs. two years from now or two years ago? If the answer is "it feels like now", you may be off on timing.

### Consensus disguised as heresy

**What it looks like:** the founder thinks they're heretical, but the insight is actually widely held in their bubble. VCs pattern-match on "cybersecurity + AI" — so a pitch that feels heretical to the founder ("nobody's doing X!") is actually the current consensus among the last 50 pitches the investor heard.

**How to check:** who's the audience? Heresy is measured against the smart consensus, not against the founder's immediate circle.

## Pro tips (accelerators — small devices that punch above their weight)

### The "Why now?" filter

For every idea, force a specific answer to the two-word question *"why now?"* — meaning what specifically about *this moment* makes this newly possible or newly urgent. Vague answers ("the world is changing") = no inflection. Specific answers ("since [specific event] in [specific year], [specific new capability]") = candidate inflection.

### The "seer test"

Name three people already living in the future this bet assumes. Are they real people you can call? Do they use tomorrow's technology today, or do they just talk about it? If you can't name three, the founder isn't yet immersed.

### Ask what would falsify

For any insight, ask *"what would you observe that would make you abandon this?"* If nothing would, the insight is a faith commitment, not a testable claim.

### Steal the demo test

If you can build a demo that makes a skeptic *feel* the difference in 30 seconds, you probably have a real insight. If you can only explain it in a deck, you probably don't. Figma, Stripe, Tesla Autopilot — the demo *was* the argument.

### Weight investor and advisor feedback asymmetrically

Feedback that says *"this is too risky, too weird, wouldn't work"* from present-anchored observers is not signal about the future. Feedback that says *"the inflection you're pointing to isn't actually happening"* is signal. Distinguish these.

### Look for polarizing early reactions

Maples: *"People should love or hate them at first. Neutral reactions rarely lead to breakout success."* Tepid interest across the board is worse than mixed love/hate.

## Anti-patterns (the "bad breakthrough claims" Maples explicitly names)

### Uber-for-X derivatives

**What it looks like:** *"We're the Uber of..."*
**Why it fails:** inherits the shape without the inflection, and the shape is now over-supplied and well-priced.
**Author's words:**
> "Uber-for-X derivatives imitate a model of success without finding a new way to fix a real frustration." — *The Heresy of Breakthrough Startups*

**How to redirect:** kill the analogy in the pitch. Force the user to explain the insight without borrowing another company's shape.

### "Big market, we could get some of that"

**What it looks like:** market entry justified by market size — the classic bad TAM slide.
**Why it fails:** market size doesn't produce a breakthrough. An inflection producing a *new possibility* does.
**How to redirect:** rewrite the entry rationale as *"customers here now [need/want/can] X, which they couldn't before [inflection], and we uniquely [insight]."* If the sentence doesn't complete, don't enter.

### Contrarian for contrarian's sake

**What it looks like:** the pitch's identity is "we disagree with everyone" without a specific better explanation.
**Why it fails:** disagreement is not an insight.
**How to redirect:** name the specific assumption you're overturning and the specific replacement that's better and more explanatory.

### Present-solving with a future coat of paint

**What it looks like:** the product actually solves a present-day problem, but the pitch dresses it in future-facing language.
**Why it fails:** dressing doesn't change what you're building. Iterate to PMF on the present-day problem — but don't call it a breakthrough.
**How to redirect:** be honest about what phase you're in. Pattern Breakers is upstream of PMF. If you have PMF on a real problem, celebrate that and iterate — don't wedge it into Pattern Breakers vocabulary.

### The consensus AI wrapper

**What it looks like:** *"We use AI to [do X that already exists] better."*
**Why it fails:** if 50 other startups can build the same wrapper the same week, you're in the consensus quadrant. The market will price this in.
**How to redirect:** name what specifically becomes newly possible with AI in your case that isn't possible in the other 49 wrappers.

## Common misapplications (people saying they're doing Pattern Breakers but aren't)

### Filling in three boxes as a pitch exercise

The framework becomes a slide template someone fills in for a pitch, then goes back to building the same product they were going to build anyway. This is pattern-breaking theater.

**Test for the real thing:** did any decisions change after the exercise? If no, no real application happened.

### Treating the three elements as sequential

Founders sometimes try to "finish" the inflection analysis before touching the insight, or lock in the insight before thinking about the movement. All three are matched. A change in one forces re-testing the others.

### Using the framework to validate the current path

Same trap as with any strategy framework. If the exercise produces no discomfort and no changes, either the founder is brilliant and rare, or the framework was defanged.

### Skipping the "act different" side

The book has two halves — ideas and actions. Many implementations honor the ideas half (Inflection/Insight) and hand-wave the actions half (Movement/Storytelling/Disagreeableness). But without action, the idea stays in a deck.

## Language and vocabulary — say this, not that

Small phrasing shifts Maples has made explicit across essays and interviews:

| Instead of | Use | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Trend | Inflection | Trends are gradual; inflections are tipping points that unlock new capability |
| Contrarian | Non-consensus and right | Contrarianism is empty; the right half of the phrase carries the load |
| MVP | Implementation Prototype | MVP measures interest; Implementation Prototype measures desperation |
| Product-market fit (alone) | Founder-Future Fit + PMF | PMF is necessary but not sufficient for a breakthrough |
| First-mover | Goldilocks moment | First without the right timing = science project |
| Big market | Newly possible market | Size doesn't create breakthroughs — new possibility does |
| Iterate to PMF | Start with the insight, then iterate | Iteration compounds a strong insight; it doesn't manufacture one |
| Customer base | Movement / early believers | Believers tolerate rough product; customers don't |
| Vision statement | Backcast from the future | Visions describe; backcasts operate |
| Marketing campaign | Storytelling (guide, not hero) | Founder as Obi-Wan, customer as Luke |
| Contrarian bet | Heresy with a better explanation | Heresy has substance; contrarianism doesn't |
| Pattern matching (as investor) | Pattern breaking | Pattern matching hunts past-winner shapes; pattern breaking hunts future-winner substance |
| "AI changes everything" | The specific new capability + cost + segment + moment | Specificity is the tell of a real inflection |
