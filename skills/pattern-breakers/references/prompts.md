# Pattern Breakers — Prompt templates

> Copy-paste templates users can adapt. Each starts with a user situation and invokes the skill in a shape Claude or Codex can execute well.

## Stress-test my startup idea

```
I want to stress-test {{my idea}} using Pattern Breakers.

Context:
- What we're building: {{one-sentence description}}
- Founder(s) and their story: {{who are you, what earned expertise do you bring}}
- Where we are: {{pre-launch / MVP / early traction / raising / etc.}}
- Current thinking on why now: {{what you'd tell an investor}}

Run the three-element test on the idea:
1. What's the specific inflection powering this NOW — the specific moment/mechanism, not a macro theme?
2. What's the non-consensus insight — the truth we hold that most smart people either don't see or don't believe?
3. What's the movement — who are the first believers and why would they care?

Push back hard when I hand-wave. If any element is missing or weak, say so — don't paper over it. Cite Maples/Pattern Breakers when introducing a specific device.
End with a question I have to answer.
```

## Distinguish inflection from trend

```
I'm looking at {{macro shift — e.g., AI, biotech, sustainable energy}} as the basis for a startup bet.

Help me distinguish trend from inflection here:
- What specifically became newly possible, at what specific moment, via what specific mechanism?
- What was true before that moment and what became true after?
- If I can't name a specific moment or mechanism, I have a trend — not an inflection. Tell me straight.

Then: name the strongest inflection candidates hiding inside this macro shift, and stress-test each against Maples's specificity bar.
```

## Test whether my insight is non-consensus AND right

```
My insight for {{context}} is: "{{insight in one sentence}}."

Test it against Andy Rachleff's non-consensus-and-right 2x2:
1. Is this non-consensus? Would most sophisticated observers in this space disagree — or is this the received wisdom?
2. Is it right? What evidence — early data, working demo, seer testimony from people already living in the future scenario — supports this?

Both halves matter. Consensus + right = commodified. Non-consensus + wrong = crazy. Non-consensus + right = the breakthrough zone.

Push me. Where's the evidence that I'm non-consensus, not just in my own bubble? Where's the evidence I'm right, not just convinced?
```

## Critique a startup pitch

```
Here's a pitch. Critique it using Pattern Breakers.

{{paste pitch / one-pager / memo}}

Specifically:
1. What's the claimed inflection? Is it a real tipping-point that unlocked a new capability, or a trend / macro invocation?
2. What's the claimed insight? Is it non-consensus AND right? Or is it consensus dressed up, or contrarian for its own sake?
3. What's the movement? Is there a believer group, or just a customer base?
4. Does the pitch pass the Demo test — could I make a skeptic feel the difference in 30 seconds?
5. Does the pitch pass the Refusal / Heresy tests?
6. Is this an Uber-for-X or an "AI wrapper" trap in disguise?
7. Where's the strongest point worth doubling down on, and where's the weakest?

Be direct. Cite Maples where relevant.
```

## Am I about to iterate to PMF from the wrong starting point?

```
I've been iterating {{product}} for {{how long}} without hitting PMF.

Apply Maples's diagnostic:
1. What was the STARTING insight I built this on? (Not the current iteration — the original one.)
2. Was that starting insight non-consensus AND right? Or was it consensus all along?
3. If the starting insight is weak, iterating on the surface won't rescue it — is that what's happening?
4. What would a real inflection powering this idea look like? Do I have one, or am I building on a trend / macro theme?
5. What's my Founder-Future Fit — do my skills, motivations, and networks give me a structural edge here?

Tell me honestly whether I should keep iterating, pivot on the insight, or step back.
```

## Backcast from the future

```
Help me backcast {{context}} instead of forecasting.

1. Imagine it's 5-10 years from now and this bet worked spectacularly. What does the world look like? Force specificity — not vague futurism.
2. What had to happen between now and then for that world to be real? Trace the path.
3. What's the inflection embedded in that path — the specific moment / mechanism that unlocks the new possibility?
4. Am I early, late, or at the Goldilocks moment? What evidence tells me now is the moment?
5. If I couldn't find an inflection in the path, the imagined future is wishful, not earned. Tell me.

Sit in the future. Don't project from the present.
```

## Evaluate the movement side (act different)

```
For my breakthrough bet on {{idea}}, help me think about the ACTION side of Pattern Breakers, not just the idea side.

1. Movement — who are the first 5-10 early believers I need to convince, not to BUY, but to BELIEVE? What's the story I'd tell them? Are they real people I can name?
2. Storytelling — in my narrative, am I the hero or the guide? (The founder should be the guide; the customer or believer is the hero.)
3. Disagreeableness — am I willing to sustain resistance from critics, incumbents, and present-anchored advisors? Am I confusing productive disagreeableness with gratuitous confrontation?
4. Which incumbent strength does my movement turn into a weakness (judo-master move)?

Cite Maples on movements. Don't let me get away with movement theater — real movements have believers, not just customers.
```

## Investor-side — evaluating a pitch I received

```
I'm considering investing in {{startup}}. Here's the pitch: {{paste}}.

Apply Pattern Breakers from the investor side:
1. What's the specific inflection? Is it a real tipping-point moment, or a founder waving at a macro trend?
2. What's the founder's insight? Is it non-consensus AND right — and how do I know they're right vs. just convinced?
3. Founder-Future Fit — do THEIR skills, motivations, and network give them a structural edge here, or would any competent founder be equivalent?
4. Movement potential — are there early believers, or just early customers?
5. What would falsify their thesis? What signal would tell us they're wrong within 12-18 months?
6. Am I pattern-matching this against a past winner? If so, kill the analogy and evaluate on substance.

Give me a hold-hold-fold recommendation with reasoning. Cite Maples where relevant.
```

## AI-as-inflection specificity drill

```
I'm building {{something that uses AI}}. Help me pass Maples's specificity test on AI as an inflection.

Force these completions:
- "Before {{specific month/year}}, you couldn't {{specific capability}} because {{specific technical/cost constraint}}."
- "Now you can because {{specific mechanism — model capability, cost per token, latency, context window, tool use, etc.}}."
- "The specific customer segment that becomes newly enabled is {{segment}} because their prior blocker was {{blocker}}."
- "This is not an inflection if {{competitors could build the same wrapper the same week — check whether this is true}}."

Then diagnose: is this a real AI-driven inflection, or an AI-wrapper trap dressed up? Don't be nice — be right.
```

## Compare Pattern Breakers vs. an adjacent framework

```
I'm deciding whether to use Pattern Breakers or {{other framework — e.g., Zero to One, Lean Startup, Playing to Win, Rumelt's kernel, Christensen's Disruption}} for {{my situation}}.

Help me pick. If the answer is "use both, in this sequence", tell me the sequence. If the answer is "neither, use something else entirely", tell me that too.

Consider phase: Pattern Breakers is upstream of PMF. Lean/Continuous Discovery is at PMF. Playing to Win is post-PMF at strategic-choice altitude. Blitzscaling is at capture-the-market altitude.
```
