# Pattern Breakers — Applications

> When Pattern Breakers fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where Pattern Breakers fits well

- **Founder evaluating whether an idea is a real breakthrough.** The three-element test is exactly the shape of this question.
- **Investor evaluating a pitch or portfolio bet.** Maples wrote the book at least partly to make his own investing legible; it maps directly onto pitch evaluation.
- **Answering "why now?" for a specific idea.** Pattern Breakers's inflection concept is the strongest available frame for the *why now* question.
- **Distinguishing a trend from an inflection.** Constant early-stage confusion — the framework has a clean test for it.
- **Testing whether an insight is non-consensus AND right.** The 2x2 (via Rachleff) is the tool.
- **Deciding whether to commit years (or capital) to a bet.** The stress tests (Refusal, Heresy, Inflection, Demo) exist for exactly this decision.
- **Critiquing a startup pitch or memo.** The three elements give you three diagnostic lenses.
- **Post-launch check — has the story stayed a pattern break?** Founders drift into optimization; the framework re-anchors on whether the original breakthrough logic still holds.
- **Reasoning about AI (or any macro shift) as an inflection.** Discipline to force specificity — which capability, at what cost, for whom, when.
- **Deciding whether to pivot when things aren't working.** Pivot-diagnosis: is the insight still non-consensus AND right, or was it consensus all along and iteration won't rescue it?
- **Assessing Founder-Future Fit for yourself or someone else.** Beyond PMF — do the founder's skills, motivations, and networks match the future they're building?

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **Optimizing an existing product with proven PMF.** Pattern Breakers is upstream of PMF. Once you have PMF, use continuous discovery, Lean/Iterative product management, or Playing to Win for strategic choice.

- **Growth-stage scaling and GTM design.** Once you've captured a real breakthrough, capture the market — use Blitzscaling (Hoffman), Predictable Revenue (Ross), or the SaaStr / OpenView playbooks. Pattern Breakers doesn't cover the "how do we scale to $100M ARR" problem.

- **Diagnosing what's broken in a company.** For "why isn't the current business working?", use Rumelt's *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* — his kernel front-loads diagnosis. Pattern Breakers presupposes you know what you're building.

- **Corporate strategy for a mature company.** Playing to Win (Martin) is the better tool for the "where do we play, how do we win" question when the company already exists and has capabilities.

- **Portfolio prioritization across many bets.** The framework evaluates one bet at a time. For ranking N options, use ODI (Ulwick) or Wardley Mapping.

- **Purely operational or execution questions.** *"How do we ship faster?"* isn't a Pattern Breakers question.

- **When the user just wants a summary of the book.** Give them the [book link](https://www.amazon.com/Pattern-Breakers-Start-Ups-Change-Future/dp/1541704355). Don't run the framework at them.

- **When the user is looking for validation of a decision already made.** Founders love this trap. Push back — or run the framework honestly with them, expecting the answer might be "this isn't yet a breakthrough."

## Adjacent frameworks — when to reach for a different one

| If the user's situation is... | Reach for... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-PMF startup iterating a validated insight | Lean Startup / Continuous Discovery | Iteration compounds a strong insight — Pattern Breakers is upstream, Lean is downstream |
| Product-market fit search (once insight is real) | Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres), Superhuman PMF Engine (Rahul Vohra) | These are the operational tools for the PMF-search phase |
| Diagnosis of what's actually broken | Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis / guiding policy / coherent action) | Rumelt front-loads diagnosis; Pattern Breakers front-loads insight |
| Corporate strategy cascade for existing company | Playing to Win (Martin) | PtW cascades once you know your aspiration and market; Pattern Breakers is about whether the breakthrough exists |
| Understanding industry structure and competitive dynamics | Porter's Five Forces | Complementary structural analysis; downstream of Pattern Breakers |
| Growth-stage scaling and capture | Blitzscaling (Hoffman) | Downstream of Pattern Breakers by phase |
| Positioning (marketing) | April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* | Positioning is downstream of the insight |
| Deep philosophical framing of non-consensus bets | Thiel's *Zero to One* | Closest philosophical peer — read alongside Pattern Breakers |
| Disruption analysis for incumbent decisions | Christensen's *Innovator's Dilemma* | Incumbent-side view; Pattern Breakers is founder/investor-side |
| Job-to-be-done specification | JTBD (Christensen / Ulwick / Moesta / Kalbach) | Once you have the insight, JTBD helps specify how to serve it |
| Business model design | Osterwalder Canvas | Useful downstream artifact of a chosen bet |
| Portfolio prioritization across many ideas | ODI (Ulwick), Wardley Mapping | Ranking N options; Pattern Breakers evaluates one at a time |

## How Pattern Breakers composes with other frameworks

- **Pairs well with Thiel's *Zero to One*.** Both prize non-consensus bets. Thiel's "secret" ≈ Maples's "insight". Read together, they reinforce.

- **Pairs well with Rumelt's *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*.** Rumelt's kernel finds the diagnosis (crux); Pattern Breakers asks whether the crux is powered by a real inflection. Use Rumelt to *find the problem*, Pattern Breakers to *check the breakthrough logic*.

- **Pairs well with Lean Startup (Ries) and *Four Steps to the Epiphany* (Blank).** Not opposites — sequential. Pattern Breakers upstream (find the right insight); Lean downstream (iterate to PMF on the insight). Steve Blank's endorsement of Pattern Breakers is the strongest signal that these tools are complementary at different phases.

- **Pairs well with Christensen's Disruption Theory.** Christensen: incumbent's dilemma. Maples: what makes the disruption possible now. Compatible at different altitudes.

- **Pairs well with JTBD.** JTBD specifies which job is being reframed by an inflection. Use JTBD once you have an insight; Pattern Breakers to check whether the insight is real.

- **Pairs well with Andy Rachleff's writing.** Rachleff's non-consensus-and-right 2x2 is a Maples-adjacent tool the book explicitly credits. If the user wants more on the 2x2, point to Rachleff's Wealthfront and Stanford GSB material.

- **Playing to Win is downstream by phase.** Once the breakthrough insight is validated and the company exists, Playing to Win cascades the strategic choices.

- **Blitzscaling is downstream by phase.** Once the breakthrough is in place and the market opens, Blitzscaling captures it.

## Frameworks Maples has pushed against

- **Pattern matching (investor default)** — the whole thesis rebuts this.
- **Trend-chasing / vague macro invocation** — trends produce optimization, only inflections produce breakthroughs.
- **Uber-for-X / Notion-for-Y derivatives** — imitation without insight.
- **Contrarian-as-identity** — being different is not enough; heresy needs a better explanation.
- **"AI wrapper" thinking (2024–2026)** — the current-cycle consensus dressed up.
- **Iterate-your-way-to-a-breakthrough from a consensus starting point** — iteration compounds a strong insight; it doesn't manufacture one.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work as breakthrough strategy, redirect gently but clearly (see `heuristics.md`).

## Where in the lifecycle Pattern Breakers lives

```
   [Idea search]  →  [Insight discovery] →  [PMF iteration]  →  [Growth/scale]  →  [Mature strategy]
                        ↑                        ↑                    ↑                   ↑
                  PATTERN BREAKERS         Lean Startup /       Blitzscaling         Playing to Win
                                          Continuous Discovery                        Rumelt
```

Pattern Breakers sits at the earliest phase — before iteration begins, before PMF is chased, before scaling. It answers *is there a real breakthrough here worth committing to*, and if not, it's honest about that.
