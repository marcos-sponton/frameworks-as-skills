## Stripe — Product Operations at scale

**Where Perri uses it:** *Product Operations* (with Denise Tilles, 2023). [Lenny's guide with Perri](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-product-operations) covers the arc.

**The situation:** Stripe scaled its PM function from tens to hundreds of PMs across products while maintaining evidence-based decision making at every altitude.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- **Data & Insights pillar** — Stripe built the internal analytics infrastructure that lets PMs query customer behavior across product lines without asking data-eng for a custom pull.
- **Customer & Market Insights pillar** — dedicated research ops team recruiting and running interviews at scale.
- **Process & Governance pillar** — a coherent operating rhythm across PM verticals so a PM in one area could understand what was happening in another without escalation to a VP.

**Why the case is instructive:** demonstrates that Product Ops is not a bureaucracy — it's the infrastructure that lets a large PM function stay fast. Stripe is the poster case for "removes obstacles" rather than "adds process."

## Uber — Product Operations under speed constraints

**Where Perri uses it:** *Product Operations* (2023).

**The situation:** Uber's PM function operated under extreme speed and scale constraints (multiple geographies, multi-sided marketplace, regulatory heterogeneity).

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Product Ops as the *decoupling mechanism* that lets local teams make local decisions without breaking global coherence.
- The tension between "move fast" and "make evidence-based decisions" as the design problem Product Ops solves at Uber's scale.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows that Product Ops matters more, not less, at high speed. The naive expectation is "we're too fast for process" (which Perri names as an anti-pattern in the podcast). Uber shows the opposite — the faster you go, the more Product Ops earns its keep.

## Fidelity — Product Operations in a legacy enterprise

**Where Perri uses it:** *Product Operations* (2023).

**The situation:** Fidelity Investments — an incumbent financial services firm modernizing its product function.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Product Ops as the vehicle for cultural shift from project-management-style PM to outcome-driven PM in a large enterprise.
- Governance pillar as the coherent operating rhythm that replaced the previous "waterfall + quarterly steering committee" cadence.

**Why the case is instructive:** counters the assumption that Product Ops is only for tech-native scale-ups. Fidelity shows it works — and matters — inside a legacy enterprise where the PM function has to be *created*, not just optimized.

## Citigroup e-Solutions — strategic-alignment failure

**Where Perri uses it:** [Why are we making Product Managers the 'one throat to choke'? — Substack, Jun 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/why-are-we-making-product-managers).

**The situation:** Citigroup e-Solutions in the early 2000s — a digital-transformation initiative that had all the vocabulary (strategy, roadmap, PMs) but lacked the strategic alignment between the digital function and the business units it was supposed to serve.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- The PMs got made accountable for outcomes they couldn't drive — the "one throat to choke" pattern in early form.
- The strategic vacuum above them meant every roadmap became a negotiation with business unit leaders, not a deployment of a shared strategy.
- Cautionary tale for anyone standing up a Product function inside a large incumbent without exec-level Strategic Intent in place first.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows the "one throat to choke" pattern isn't new — it's a recurring failure mode when accountability is assigned before authority is granted or strategy is deployed.

## Airbnb / Brian Chesky — the Founder Mode foil

**Where Perri uses it:** [Are We Getting Rid of Product Managers? — blog, Jul 2023](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2023/7/7/are-we-getting-rid-of-product-managers) and [My Thoughts on Founder Mode — Substack, Sep 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/my-thoughts-on-founder-mode-and-why).

**The situation:** Brian Chesky's 2023 restructuring at Airbnb — reduced the traditional PM function and moved product decisions closer to the founder + design. Chesky later coined "Founder Mode" (via Paul Graham's essay) as a scaling doctrine.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Foil for her argument that PMs are not going away — the *function* remains essential; what changes is who does it and how.
- Counter-argument to Founder Mode as a *general* doctrine: Chesky's move worked at Airbnb because of Airbnb's specific design-led culture and Chesky's specific involvement. It doesn't generalize.

> "Just because you made it from 0 to 1 doesn't mean you inherently know how to scale." — [Sep 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/my-thoughts-on-founder-mode-and-why).

**Why the case is instructive:** shows Perri using a specific high-profile move as the trigger for a broader diagnostic about founder-role scaling.

## Meta / Mark Zuckerberg — the Founder Mode counter-example

**Where Perri uses it:** [My Thoughts on Founder Mode — Substack, Sep 2024](https://melissaperri.substack.com/p/my-thoughts-on-founder-mode-and-why).

**The situation:** Zuckerberg as founder who has scaled Meta over 20+ years by *actively learning and adapting* — surrounding himself with experienced operators (COOs, product leaders, infrastructure leaders) while retaining strategic ownership.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Counter to Founder Mode: not every founder has to become a control-freak to succeed at scale.
- Model for founder evolution — from 0→1 hands-on to 1→N operating-structure-builder.
- The pattern is "founder + strong operators around them", not "founder alone".

**Why the case is instructive:** shows Perri using the same category (long-tenure founder at scale) with an *opposite* pattern to break Founder Mode's claim to generality.

## MURAL — Elaina O'Mahoney (Product Thinking Ep 265)

**Where Perri uses it:** Product Thinking podcast Ep 265 (guest interview).

**The situation:** MURAL (collaborative-visualization SaaS) — Elaina O'Mahoney as CPO scaling a product-led org through the transition from hyper-growth to mature enterprise product.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Modern-era example of a CPO who runs the full Four Dimensions playbook.
- Shows Product Ops at a mid-scale SaaS (not just Stripe-scale) — proves the frame works below Fortune 500 headcount.

**Why the case is instructive:** modern, mid-scale, actively-in-progress. Good exemplar when the user is at similar scale and wondering "is this frame for us?"

## Freshworks — Srinivasan Raghavan (Product Thinking Ep 259)

**Where Perri uses it:** Product Thinking podcast Ep 259 (guest interview, Dec 2025).

**The situation:** Freshworks CPO on how to integrate AI into a mid-size B2B SaaS product function without falling into the build trap.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- AI decisions run through the same 4-tier deployment (Strategic Intent → Product Initiative → Options).
- Product Ops instrumentation (Pillar 1) becomes even more important as AI-driven features create harder-to-measure outcomes.
- Warning against "AI as strategy" — AI is a capability inside the strategy, not the strategy itself.

**Why the case is instructive:** the freshest live case for AI + PM. Anchors the abstract 2026 AI-in-PM discourse in one CPO's operational reality.

## Natural Cycles — Magda Armbruster

**Where Perri uses it:** Product Thinking podcast (guest interview).

**The situation:** Natural Cycles (FDA-cleared digital contraception app) — Magda Armbruster running a PM function inside a regulated consumer-health product.

**How Perri uses the case:**
- Regulated product context shows that the Problem Roadmap works even where compliance dictates part of the delivery schedule.
- Discovery + validation cadence must integrate regulatory constraints without becoming a waterfall.

**Why the case is instructive:** proof point that Perri's frame doesn't require greenfield SaaS. Reach for it when a user has a regulated or high-stakes product context and worries the frame is too "consumer software" for them.

## Anonymized client work — Produx Labs (2010s consulting engagements)

**Where Perri uses it:** most concrete PM diagnostics in *Escaping the Build Trap* (2018) come from her Produx Labs consulting engagements — ~8 years across Fortune 500 + SaaS scale-ups.

**How Perri uses these:** she names patterns, not clients. The Waiter PM archetype, the roadmap-as-contract failure, the Product Owner-as-order-taker pathology all come from actual engagements but are anonymized.

**Why this matters for the skill:** when the user's own situation feels too specific to fit a public case, ground the diagnosis in "this pattern shows up across the F500 + SaaS scale-ups Perri has worked with" — you're not inventing; you're pointing to the anonymized composite.
