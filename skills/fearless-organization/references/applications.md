# The Fearless Organization — When to Use, When Not, Adjacent Frameworks

> Edmondson's framework is opinionated. It is also not a universal tool. This file names where her method fits, where it doesn't, and how it relates to adjacent frameworks — so the assistant can either apply it or point at the better tool.

## When the Fearless Organization framework fits

Edmondson's method is at its most useful in these situations:

- **Diagnosing why a team has gone quiet.** The 2×2 with accountability locates the team (Comfort / Anxiety / Apathy / Learning); the leader toolkit prescribes the response.
- **Designing or redesigning a post-mortem, incident retro, or blameless review.** The failure taxonomy (Basic / Complex / Intelligent) types the failure before determining response.
- **Deciding how to respond to a specific failure.** Basic → checklist. Complex → system redesign. Intelligent → celebrate learning.
- **Introducing psychological safety to a leadership team.** The 2×2 with accountability is the anti-misread structural device — use it as the introduction so the "PS as being nice" caricature doesn't take hold.
- **Auditing a "we're a psychologically safe culture" claim.** Look for the practices (Framing, Situational Humility, Proactive Inquiry, Structures, Responding Productively, sanction predictability), not the slogan.
- **Rewriting incident-response protocols or engineering excellence rituals.** The framework tells you what conditions the protocols need to produce.
- **Building learning-heavy teams (R&D, novel product work, high-uncertainty environments).** Intelligent failure celebration is a core capability.
- **Working through a Boeing- / Columbia- / Wells Fargo- / Volkswagen-style organizational failure.** The Anxiety Zone diagnosis is Edmondson's canonical case.
- **Facing pressure to lower standards in the name of "being kind" or "not creating anxiety."** The framework's whole structure is designed to reject this false choice.
- **Onboarding a new leader who wants to build the conditions for voice from day one.** Framing + Situational Humility + Proactive Inquiry is the day-one leader toolkit.

## When the Fearless Organization framework does NOT fit (or fits poorly)

- **Legal / HR / compliance situations.** If harassment, discrimination, or safety-of-life is at stake, the framework informs but does not replace HR / legal counsel. Direct the user to their HR partner or attorney, not to a 2×2.
- **Individual coaching for anxiety, trauma, or clinical mental health.** PS is a team-level condition, not therapy. Route to appropriate professional support.
- **Purely technical / strategic questions with no team, culture, or interpersonal dimension.** "How should we architect this service?" is not a Fearless Organization question. Don't force the framework onto it.
- **Deep personal / therapeutic contexts.** If a user is asking about grief, burnout, or serious mental health situations at work, the framework isn't the primary tool. Suggest EAP resources or a therapist.
- **A single manager-report feedback conversation** — that's Radical Candor territory. Route there, or compose (Fearless Organization for the team condition, Radical Candor for the conversation).
- **Cultures where the framework's calibration doesn't survive translation.** The axes (PS, accountability) are universal, but the calibration of what "voice" looks like varies by culture. In some contexts, voice is raised through intermediaries or in writing rather than directly and publicly. Adjust calibration; don't drop the axes.
- **When the user is fishing for permission to lower a standard.** See the misread guard in `heuristics.md`. Route them back to the Learning Zone (both PS AND accountability high, not one without the other).

## Adjacent frameworks — when each is the better tool

### Radical Candor (Kim Scott)

- **Kim:** manager-report feedback dyad. Care Personally + Challenge Directly. Individual behavior.
- **Amy:** team-level condition that enables voice. Psychological Safety + Accountability. Systemic behavior.
- **The bridge:** Kim's Care Personally + Challenge Directly maps almost perfectly onto Amy's high PS + high accountability. Both authors spend most of their airtime warning against the misread that drops one of the two axes (Kim: dropping Care produces Obnoxious Aggression; Amy: dropping accountability produces the Comfort Zone).
- **When Kim is better:** the specific feedback conversation with a specific direct report.
- **When Amy is better:** the team-level condition that makes those conversations possible.
- **Compose:** run PS at the team level (Amy) + Radical Candor at the manager-report level (Kim) + Career Conversations (Russ Laraway, via Kim). Nearly the full management stack.
- See [[radical-candor]] for the deeper treatment.

### Five Dysfunctions of a Team (Patrick Lencioni)

- **Lencioni:** pyramid model — trust → conflict → commitment → accountability → results. Trust as foundation.
- **Edmondson:** PS as the belief that permits interpersonal risk-taking. Similar substrate concept but Amy is more precise about what "trust" means (safety for specific behaviors), grounds it in research, and pairs it explicitly with accountability.
- **When Lencioni is better:** offsite / team-building work with a group that responds to narrative (fable) more than to research.
- **When Edmondson is better:** any context where research rigor, precise diagnostics, and structural intervention matter (leadership team retros, org-wide culture initiatives, healthcare / high-stakes environments).
- **Compose:** Lencioni for the diagnostic conversation; Edmondson for the operational work of building PS + accountability at the team level.
- See [[five-dysfunctions]] once built.

### Radical Transparency (Ray Dalio / Bridgewater)

- **Dalio:** radical transparency — full disclosure, recorded meetings, public rating apps ("Dot Collector"), culture of "getting in sync" through relentless debate.
- **Edmondson:** PS is about voice without retribution, not mandatory disclosure of everything to everyone. She has been careful not to conflate the two.
- **Edmondson's implicit critique:** radical transparency without accountability calibration and without leader psychological safety practices can produce the Anxiety Zone at scale — surveillance masquerading as candor.
- **Do NOT conflate them.** When a user invokes both, name the distinction: PS is about voice without retribution; radical transparency is about mandatory disclosure. They are not synonyms.

### DORA / Accelerate + Westrum culture typology

- **Westrum (1988):** Pathological / Bureaucratic / Generative culture typology. Generative culture = high cooperation, messengers welcomed, novelty-seeking, cross-functional bridging.
- **DORA (Forsgren, Humble, Kim):** Westrum's generative culture is one of the strongest predictors of software delivery performance.
- **Edmondson connection:** generative culture ≈ high PS + high accountability + learning orientation. DORA's software-delivery finding is a domain-specific application of Edmondson's org-wide claim.
- **When DORA is better:** engineering / software delivery performance measurement.
- **When Edmondson is better:** the leader behaviors and structures that produce the underlying condition.
- **Compose:** Edmondson at the leadership-behavior level, DORA at the software-delivery level. Both point to the same substrate.
- See [[dora-accelerate]] for the deeper treatment.

### High Output Management (Andy Grove)

- **Grove:** managerial output = output of your team + output of teams you influence. 1:1s, task-relevant maturity, meeting design, staff meetings.
- **Edmondson:** the conditions under which Grove's mechanics actually generate voice, learning, and performance. Grove is the plumbing; Edmondson tells you whether water actually flows through it.
- **When Grove is better:** you're designing the manager mechanics from scratch — 1:1 cadence, staff meeting design, task-relevant maturity assessments.
- **When Edmondson is better:** the mechanics exist and the question is what conditions they should produce.
- **Compose:** Grove's 1:1s are one of the strongest structures for building PS at the manager-report level. Grove + Edmondson + Kim Scott is a natural triad.
- See [[high-output-management]] once built.

### Scaling People (Claire Hughes Johnson)

- **CHJ:** operating manual for building high-functioning companies as they scale. Emphasizes context-setting, feedback rituals, and leader clarity.
- **Edmondson:** the substrate CHJ's rituals produce. CHJ's operating cadence is designed to produce Learning Zone conditions.
- **When CHJ is better:** you're operationalizing a specific set of rituals (context memos, 1:1 templates, feedback cadence).
- **When Edmondson is better:** you're diagnosing why the rituals aren't producing the effect they should.
- **Compose:** natural pairing — Edmondson for the "why," CHJ for the specific "how."
- See [[scaling-people]] once built.

### Nonviolent Communication (Marshall Rosenberg)

- **NVC:** observation → feeling → need → request. Focuses on emotional language and needs literacy.
- **Edmondson:** team-level condition that permits interpersonal risk-taking. Not primarily about emotional language.
- **When NVC is better:** intimate relationships, deeply emotional conflicts, situations where the primary problem is naming feelings and needs.
- **When Edmondson is better:** workplace team conditions, org-level culture, structural intervention.
- **Compose:** NVC vocabulary can support the "kind and clear" delivery a Learning Zone requires. Someone fluent in NVC will find Framing and Proactive Inquiry easier to execute.

### Crucial Conversations (Patterson, Grenny, McMillan, Switzler)

- **Crucial Conversations:** STATE method. Focus: high-stakes conversations where opinions vary and emotions run strong.
- **Edmondson:** the team-level conditions that make each hard conversation smaller because they happen constantly.
- **When Crucial Conversations is better:** you have one specific hard conversation, right now, and need a tactical script.
- **When Edmondson is better:** you're designing the culture that makes hard conversations routine rather than crisis-level.
- **Compose:** Crucial Conversations gives you the tactical script for a single hard conversation; Edmondson gives you the operating system that makes each one smaller.

### Playing to Win (Roger Martin) / Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Richard Rumelt)

- **Different problem entirely.** These are strategy frameworks (what to do at the org level). Fearless Organization is a management framework (how to design the team conditions that let people execute).
- **The composition failure to name:** a great strategy executed in an Anxiety Zone team gets partial credit at best. Wells Fargo had clear strategy — the execution collapsed the strategy into a scandal. Boeing 737 MAX had clear commercial strategy; the Anxiety Zone execution made it lethal.
- **When each is better:** if the user is asking about competitive positioning, market choice, or where-to-play — that's Martin or Rumelt, not Amy.
- **Compose:** Fearless Organization is *inside* the org that has a Playing to Win strategy. They operate at different layers and don't conflict.
- See [[playing-to-win]] for the deeper treatment.

### Learning Organization (Peter Senge)

- **Senge:** five disciplines — personal mastery, mental models, shared vision, team learning, systems thinking.
- **Edmondson:** operationalizes what "team learning" actually looks like in practice — the specific team learning behaviors (information sharing, asking for help, seeking feedback, discussing errors, experimenting).
- **When Senge is better:** framing the org-wide aspiration and the systems-thinking lens.
- **When Edmondson is better:** the specific team-level practices and diagnostics.
- **Compose:** Edmondson is the operational specification of Senge's "team learning" discipline.

## Adjacent frameworks NOT to conflate

- **PS ≠ Radical Transparency.** Called out above. Most common confusion in tech circles.
- **PS ≠ "being nice."** Called out throughout `heuristics.md`. The single biggest misread.
- **PS ≠ absence of pressure.** Learning Zone has high accountability. High pressure is compatible with PS; retribution is not.
- **PS ≠ consensus decision-making.** Voice is invited; leaders still decide.
- **PS ≠ engagement.** Engagement surveys measure something else. PS lives at team level in specific behaviors.
- **PS ≠ team-building.** Offsites don't build PS. Leader behavior + structures + consequences over time do.
- **PS ≠ DEI initiative.** PS is a learning-and-performance framework with DEI side-benefits. Framing it primarily as DEI narrows adoption.
- **PS ≠ HR wellness program.** PS lives in the operating layer, not the wellness layer.
- **Fearless Organization Scan ≠ engagement survey.** The Scan measures PS at team level using validated instrument; engagement surveys measure org-wide averages that hide the team-by-team variation where PS actually lives.

## When to compose the Fearless Organization framework with another framework

- **Manager giving hard feedback in a team where PS is low** — Edmondson's framework for the team condition + Kim Scott's Radical Candor for the specific conversation.
- **Engineering culture that hits deadlines but has high burnout / turnover** — likely Anxiety Zone (Edmondson's diagnosis) + DORA / Westrum culture assessment + Grove's 1:1 mechanics as the structural intervention.
- **Post-mortem culture that's blameless but never learns** — Edmondson's failure taxonomy + Westrum's generative culture as the target + specific post-mortem templates.
- **New leader building a team from scratch** — Edmondson's leader toolkit (Framing / Situational Humility / Proactive Inquiry / Structures) + Grove's 1:1 mechanics + Kim Scott's Radical Candor as the manager-report layer + Career Conversations (Laraway).
- **Org-level cultural transformation** — Edmondson's team-level framework + Kim Scott's Radical Respect (BUL) for the bias/prejudice/bullying dimensions + a strategy layer (Playing to Win, Rumelt) so the culture serves a real strategic bet.
- **Failure response after a major incident (Columbia / Boeing / Wells Fargo style)** — Edmondson's failure taxonomy + Anxiety Zone diagnosis + specific structural interventions (whistleblower protection, sanction predictability, near-miss reporting) + external investigation / regulatory response as needed.

## Rule of thumb

If the question is:
- "Why has my team gone quiet?" → Fearless Organization (diagnose zone; run leader toolkit).
- "How do I respond to this specific failure?" → Fearless Organization (failure taxonomy: Basic / Complex / Intelligent).
- "How do I give this feedback to my report?" → Radical Candor (Kim Scott).
- "How do I win this specific hard conversation?" → Crucial Conversations.
- "How do I run my 1:1s?" → Grove + Edmondson (Grove for structure, Edmondson for what conditions the 1:1 should produce).
- "How do I grow my direct reports?" → Career Conversations (Russ Laraway, via Kim).
- "Should I fire this brilliant jerk?" → Fearless Organization + Radical Candor + Netflix keeper test — all point to yes.
- "How do I structure my team's decisions?" → GSD wheel (Kim) + Edmondson's Proactive Inquiry.
- "How do we build a blameless post-mortem process?" → Fearless Organization (failure taxonomy is essential — blameless does not mean unanalyzed).
- "What's our competitive strategy?" → Not Fearless Organization. Use Playing to Win or Rumelt.
- "How do we measure our software delivery culture?" → DORA / Accelerate (Westrum), with Fearless Organization as the leader-behavior layer.
