# The Fearless Organization — Invocation Templates

> Ready-to-adapt prompt shapes for common tasks the user brings to this skill. Not scripts — starting points. The assistant should adapt to what the user actually says.

## 1. "My team has gone quiet in meetings / retros / 1:1s. What's going on?"

**The shape of the assistant's response:**

1. **Define the problem in Edmondson's terms.** Silence is data — it means voice isn't happening. That's the psychological safety diagnostic, not a personality diagnostic about the team members.
2. **Locate the team on the 2×2.** Ask: what are the accountability / standards conditions? If high standards + quiet team → likely Anxiety Zone. If low standards + quiet team → likely Apathy Zone. If unclear standards + warm team → possibly Comfort Zone that's just starting to notice something is off.
3. **Diagnose leader behaviors.** In the last 4 weeks:
   - Have you done Framing — named the interdependent, uncertain nature of the work?
   - Have you practiced Situational Humility publicly — said "I don't have the answer" or "we've never been here before"?
   - Have you asked specific proactive-inquiry questions, or generic ones?
   - How have you responded the last time someone brought bad news?
4. **Prescribe the leader move that's most missing.** Usually one of the four above is the biggest gap.
5. **End with a concrete ask.** "What's one specific proactive-inquiry question you can ask in your next meeting, directed at a specific person?"

## 2. "How do I introduce psychological safety to our leadership team without it sounding soft / touchy-feely / like a wellness thing?"

**The shape:**

1. **Never introduce PS in isolation.** Introduce the 2×2 with accountability from the first slide. Name all four zones.
2. **Anchor in a hard case.** Boeing 737 MAX or Wells Fargo. These are Anxiety Zone cases — high standards, low PS, catastrophic outcomes. The framework is not soft; it's what prevents the next Boeing.
3. **Define PS with the negation.** Edmondson's line: *"Psychological safety is not about being nice. It's about giving candid feedback, openly admitting mistakes, and learning from each other."* Read it verbatim.
4. **Name the target: Learning Zone.** High PS + high standards. The goal is not to make things nicer — it's to make it possible to learn as fast as the work demands.
5. **Give the leader toolkit.** Framing / Situational Humility / Proactive Inquiry / Structures / Responding Productively. Concrete, actionable.
6. **Introduce the Fearless Organization Scan.** Measurement at team level. Something to actually track, not a values-page slogan.

## 3. "We had a failure. How do I respond?"

**The shape — this is a failure-taxonomy question:**

1. **Diagnose the failure type FIRST.**
   - **Novel territory + hypothesis-driven + opportunity-motivated + as small as possible?** → Intelligent failure. Response: celebrate the learning. Extract the insight. Run the next experiment.
   - **Known territory + single cause + preventable?** → Basic failure. Response: checklist, standardization, training. Don't run a workshop; fix the process.
   - **Familiar territory + multiple interacting causes?** → Complex failure. Response: system redesign, defense-in-depth, near-miss reporting. NOT "who screwed up?"
   - **Clear misconduct?** → Sanction case, not failure-taxonomy case. Sanction it. PS does not shield bullies, harassers, or code-of-conduct violators.
2. **Match the response to the type.** Treating all failures the same is blame culture in a lab coat.
3. **Own your part.** If it's a Basic failure, ask why the checklist wasn't in place — that's the leader's job. If it's Complex, ask what system change is needed. If Intelligent, celebrate publicly.
4. **End with the specific commitment.** Who does what by when to prevent the next one (Basic/Complex) or run the next experiment (Intelligent).

## 4. "I've heard we have a 'psychologically safe culture' but nobody speaks up. What's happening?"

**The shape:**

1. **PS is a team-level condition, not an org-wide claim.** Measured by behavior, not by slogan. If nobody's speaking up, PS is low regardless of what's on the values page.
2. **Diagnose the specific team behaviors:**
   - Are team learning behaviors happening (information sharing, asking for help, seeking feedback, discussing errors, experimenting)?
   - If not — PS is low. The values page is decoration.
3. **Audit leader practices:**
   - Framing memos before initiatives?
   - Situational humility publicly modeled?
   - Proactive inquiry that's specific, not generic?
   - Sanction predictability — have bullies been sanctioned, promoted, or ignored?
4. **The gap is the problem.** If the practices don't match the claim, the claim is misleading. Fix the practices or remove the claim.
5. **Consider the Fearless Organization Scan.** Measurement at team level tells you where PS actually lives and where it doesn't.

## 5. "I want to run better post-mortems / blameless retros. How?"

**The shape:**

1. **Blameless does not mean unanalyzed.** Skip the analysis and you get Apathy Zone with a nice name.
2. **The template:**
   - What actually happened? (facts, not blame)
   - What failure type was this? (Basic / Complex / Intelligent — cite the taxonomy)
   - What system or process change prevents the next one? (specific commitment, owner, deadline)
   - What is the learning we take forward? (what changes in how we work)
3. **The failure-type step is where most retros fail.** Without it, Basic failures don't get the process fix they need, Complex failures don't get the system redesign, and Intelligent failures don't get celebrated.
4. **Reward the surfacing of near-misses, not just failures.** The team reporting 10 near-misses per quarter is running defense-in-depth. The team reporting zero has either perfect execution or a suppression problem.
5. **Publish the follow-through.** Retro without visible action teaches the team the retro is theater.

## 6. "One of my top performers is a jerk to teammates. What do I do?"

**The shape — this is a sanction case + PS case:**

1. **PS is not shielded from misconduct.** In Edmondson's frame: bullies destroy PS for the whole team. The team's collective output — which depends on PS — drops far more than the bully's individual output justifies.
2. **Diagnose the pattern.** Bullying is intentional, repeated use of in-group status or power to harm or humiliate (Kim Scott's BUL definition, aligned with Edmondson's stance). One-off aggression under stress is different from a pattern.
3. **If it's a pattern:** sanction. Predictably. Publicly enough that the team sees behavior has consequences.
4. **Kim's rule, Amy's rule:** *"Do not promote bullies. It's that simple."* Promoting a bully is a public statement that PS is optional.
5. **The signal cost of keeping them:** every other team member learns that behavior doesn't matter. Best people leave. Institutional knowledge bleeds out.
6. **End with the hard question:** "Are you willing to let this person go if the pattern continues? If not, you're keeping them regardless of what you say about PS."

## 7. "How do I do proactive inquiry without it feeling forced?"

**The shape:**

1. **Specificity is the antidote to forced.** Generic questions ("any thoughts?") sound like theater because they are. Specific questions ("Priya, you've done more of these migrations than anyone here — what's the concern we're not weighting enough?") sound like real invitations because they are.
2. **Ask a specific person, not the room.** Diffuse invitations diffuse response.
3. **Ask about specific risks, not general feelings.** "If this fails in 6 months, what's the reason?" > "How are you feeling about this?"
4. **Wait.** Silence after a question is often when the person is actually deciding whether to answer honestly. Don't fill it.
5. **Respond productively to whatever comes.** Thank them for the input specifically. Don't defend. Engage the content.
6. **Repeat over months, not weeks.** Trust is built by consistent behavior, not one Framing memo.

## 8. "How do I introduce accountability without triggering the 'you're breaking psychological safety' pushback?"

**The shape — this is the Comfort Zone → Learning Zone transition:**

1. **Name the misread directly.** *"Psychological safety and accountability are not a trade-off. They are two separate dimensions, and we need both high."* Cite Edmondson's TEDxHGSE line if useful.
2. **Show the 2×2 with all four zones.** Point at Comfort Zone (high PS + low accountability). Say: "This is where we are. Nothing is shipping. That's not safe — that's stagnant."
3. **Frame the target explicitly.** Learning Zone. High PS + high standards. The move is to add the accountability axis, not to reduce the PS axis.
4. **Anchor in Edmondson's cases.** Pixar Braintrust: brutal feedback + norm-protected space. Ford under Mulally: hard truths + safe reporting. Both are Learning Zone exemplars where high standards and high PS coexist.
5. **Specific commitments matter more than framing.** What's the specific standard being raised? By when? How is progress measured?
6. **If the pushback continues,** the pushback IS the low-accountability side of the diagnosis. The Comfort Zone team is defending the Comfort Zone. Naming that dynamic (kindly) is often part of the intervention.

## 9. "I just witnessed a near-miss / suppressed concern / red flag at my org. What do I do?"

**The shape:**

1. **This is data. Take it seriously.** Suppressed concerns are what Boeing engineers had; what Columbia's Rodney Rocha had; what Wells Fargo's front-line workers had. Every catastrophe starts as a suppressed near-miss.
2. **Diagnose the pattern.** Is this a one-off (a bad meeting) or systemic (voice regularly suppressed)? One-off is a moment; systemic is Anxiety Zone.
3. **If systemic — what's your leverage?**
   - You're the leader → run the leader toolkit. Framing, situational humility, proactive inquiry, sanction predictability. Structural changes to how concerns get raised.
   - You're not the leader → escalate through appropriate channels (skip-level, HR, whistleblower channels if severe). Document. If the channels don't work, that's more data about the culture.
4. **If severe / safety-of-life / legal violations** — this is not a PS conversation, it's an escalation / legal / whistleblower conversation. Route to appropriate professional counsel.

## 10. "How do I explain psychological safety in a way that doesn't sound like touchy-feely HR-speak?"

**The shape — this is the introduction / positioning question:**

1. **Start with a hard case.** Columbia. Boeing 737 MAX. Wells Fargo. These are what suppressed voice actually costs. Not soft; catastrophic.
2. **Define with Edmondson's precision.** *"A belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns, or mistakes."* Precise; not therapeutic.
3. **Immediately negate the misread.** Not being nice. Not lowering standards. Not anything-goes.
4. **Show the 2×2 with accountability.** The framework is about high performance + high candor, not comfort.
5. **Point at the operational tool.** The Fearless Organization Scan. Team-level measurement. Data.
6. **Attribution.** This is Harvard Business School Novartis Professor Amy Edmondson's research, replicated across 20+ years and validated by Google's Aristotle project. Not a values-slogan.

## 11. "Is this really an intelligent failure or am I rationalizing?"

**The shape — the taxonomy check:**

1. **Run the four tests. All must be true.**
   - **Novel territory?** If someone else has done this exact thing before → not novel. Reclassify as Basic (if you skipped known steps) or Complex (if the system produced the failure).
   - **Hypothesis-driven?** Did you have a specific reason to expect it might work? If it was a guess → not intelligent.
   - **Opportunity-motivated?** Were you pursuing a specific opportunity, or just tinkering? Tinkering isn't intelligent failure; it's play (which has its place, but different frame).
   - **As small as possible?** Was the experiment scaled to learn, or was it a big bet? A big bet that fails is not an intelligent failure — it's a big bet that failed.
2. **If all four hold:** it's an intelligent failure. Celebrate the learning. Extract the insight. Run the next experiment.
3. **If any fail:** be honest about the classification. Basic failures get checklist fixes. Complex failures get system redesign. Rationalizing a Basic failure as intelligent lets the pattern repeat.

## 13. "How does this apply in [remote / hybrid / cross-cultural / high-stress] context?"

**The shape:**

1. **The axes are universal.** Psychological safety + accountability. Interpersonal risk-taking as the anchor concept.
2. **The calibration is not.** What "voice" looks like varies. In a high-power-distance culture, voice may be raised through a trusted intermediary, in writing, or in a small group before the large one. In a remote / async context, voice may live in written channels more than in synchronous meetings.
3. **Adjust the calibration; don't drop the axes.**
4. **Structural interventions matter more, not less, in remote / hybrid / cross-cultural contexts.** Framing memos. Written pre-reads. Async input channels. Round-robin turn-taking. Anonymous concern-surfacing.
5. **Point at Edmondson's podcast archive** for specific applications — her post-2020 material has addressed remote and hybrid explicitly.
