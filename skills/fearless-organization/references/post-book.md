## The "psychological safety ≠ nice" doubling-down (2019-present)

After the 2018 book landed, the biggest misread was that PS = comfort / niceness / lowered standards. Edmondson has spent most of her post-book airtime pushing back against this. **This is the single most important post-book theme, and this skill's misread guard exists because of it.**

Key defenses:

### "Psychological Safety ≠ Anything Goes" (blog post)

- **URL:** [amycedmondson.com/psychological-safety-≠-anything-goes/](https://amycedmondson.com/psychological-safety-%E2%89%A0-anything-goes/)
- **What it says:** explicit refutation of "lowering standards, giving up on accountability, or 'wrapping teams in cotton wool'" (the last phrase from Dan Cable of LBS, which Edmondson quotes approvingly).
- **What it introduces:** the operational rule that PS lives in the "upper right quadrant" where PS and accountability are both high. Sanction of misconduct is a feature of a psychologically safe org, not a violation of one.

### The 2×2 with Accountability

The most important structural refinement Edmondson has added post-book. In the 2018 book, the 2×2 is present but underweighted; in the post-book teaching (TED talks, podcasts, blog posts, HBR pieces), it is the anchor structure. Four zones:

- **Learning Zone** — high PS + high accountability. The goal.
- **Comfort Zone** — high PS + low accountability. The "PS as nice" misread lived out.
- **Anxiety Zone** — low PS + high accountability. Boeing 737 MAX, Wells Fargo, Volkswagen.
- **Apathy Zone** — low PS + low accountability. Coast-to-retirement.

**Never present PS in isolation.** The 2×2 with accountability is the anti-misread structural device. It is the difference between the framework being understood and being caricatured.

## Right Kind of Wrong (2023) — the failure taxonomy is now core method

Edmondson's biggest post-book intellectual project. *Right Kind of Wrong: The Science of Failing Well* won the FT & Schroders Business Book of the Year 2023.

### What Right Kind of Wrong added to the Fearless Organization system

The Fearless Organization covers "how to build the conditions for voice." It does not, by itself, tell leaders how to respond to failures — which is the highest-leverage moment for either building or destroying PS. *Right Kind of Wrong* filled that gap.

The three failure archetypes:

- **Basic failure** — preventable, known territory, single cause. Response: checklists, standardization, training.
- **Complex failure** — familiar territory, many interacting causes. Response: system redesign, defense-in-depth, near-miss reporting.
- **Intelligent failure** — novel territory, hypothesis-driven, opportunity-motivated, as small as possible. Response: **celebrate the learning.**

Verbatim:
> "Not all failures are created equal."
> "An intelligent failure is the right kind of wrong."
> — *Right Kind of Wrong*, 2023

**Why this matters for the skill:** the biggest culture-killing move a leader makes is treating all failures as blameworthy. The Fearless Organization gives you the diagnosis (low PS); *Right Kind of Wrong* gives you the taxonomy (which failures are learning, which are execution, which are systemic). Treat the 2023 book as core method, not add-on.

### Precursor: "Strategies for Learning from Failure" (HBR, 2011)

- **URL:** [hbr.org — the article behind the paywall; multiple summaries available](https://hbr.org/2011/04/strategies-for-learning-from-failure)
- **The blameworthy-to-praiseworthy spectrum** — Edmondson's earlier version of the taxonomy. Failures range from deviance and inattention (blameworthy) through system failure and complexity to exploratory testing (praiseworthy).
- **The 2011 finding:** most leaders overweight the blameworthy end and underweight the praiseworthy end. Result: they punish 80% of failures when only 2-5% actually warrant it. That's what breaks PS.
- *Right Kind of Wrong* (2023) is the mature version of this early framework, rebuilt around three archetypes with clearer diagnostic tests.

## The Fearless Organization Scan (2019-present)

Edmondson's productized diagnostic. The tool that operationalizes the framework.

- **URL:** [fearlessorganizationscan.com](https://fearlessorganizationscan.com/)
- **The Psychological Safety Index (PSI)** — validated survey based on Edmondson's research.
- **Three levels:**
  - **Individual (free):** self-reflection scan.
  - **Team PSI:** team-level assessment. **This is where PS actually lives** — the framework measures at the team level.
  - **Enterprise PSI (EPSI):** org-wide measurement, with participation in the annual Global Benchmark or custom solutions.
- **The 2025 Global Benchmark** — annual report on PS across participating organizations.

**Why this matters:** the Scan is the productized version of the framework. Consultants, HR leaders, and executives use it to move from "we should have PS" to "here's where we are." Any leader serious about applying the framework beyond one team should be aware of the tool.

## The intelligent-failure origin story as post-book pedagogy

Edmondson tells the story of her own PhD research more explicitly in *Right Kind of Wrong* than she did in *The Fearless Organization*. The story:

- As a PhD student, she designed a study expecting that better hospital teams would have fewer medication errors.
- Data showed the opposite: better teams reported MORE errors.
- She spent months confused, then realized: better teams weren't making more errors, they were more willing to REPORT them.
- The variable she was measuring was psychological safety.

Published as: **"The Intelligent Failure that Led to the Discovery of Psychological Safety"** — [Behavioral Scientist essay](https://behavioralscientist.org/the-intelligent-failure-that-led-to-the-discovery-of-psychological-safety/).

**Why it matters:** the story does double duty. It explains where the concept came from. It also demonstrates the intelligent-failure taxonomy applied to Edmondson's own research. She uses it as the canonical case in most recent talks.

## Post-2023 emphasis: leader-first, not bystander-first

Similar arc to Kim Scott's Just Work → Radical Respect rewrite. Edmondson's mature position: **PS is a leader-designed condition, not an emergent property.** Individual contributors and bystanders can practice speaking up, but the system-level failure requires system-level intervention, and only leaders can design that.

This shows up in her post-2020 writing and speaking:
- Increased emphasis on the leader toolkit (Framing / Situational Humility / Curiosity / Structures / Responding Productively).
- More attention to structural interventions (blameless post-mortem templates, near-miss reporting systems, sanction predictability).
- Less attention to "how individual contributors can be brave" — because that's an unfair burden if the system doesn't support them.

## Where Edmondson's thinking has evolved most since 2018

- **The accountability axis** — in 2018 the 2×2 with accountability was present; in the post-book teaching it is anchor structure. The framework is always presented with both axes now.
- **The failure taxonomy** — the 2023 book crystallizes what was scattered in HBR articles since 2011. The three archetypes (Basic / Complex / Intelligent) are now core method.
- **Leader-first framing** — the responsibility for PS is now placed squarely on leaders (who design the system) rather than on individuals (who navigate it).
- **The misread defense** — "PS ≠ being nice" is Edmondson's most-repeated post-book line. The volume of the defense tells you how common the misread is.
- **Cross-cultural calibration** — post-2020 podcast episodes address how the framework applies (or fails) in different national/regional cultures. The axes are universal; the calibration of what "voice" looks like is not.
- **Structural interventions** — increased focus on specific structures (blameless post-mortem templates, near-miss reporting, sanction protocols) rather than exhortation.

## What Edmondson has NOT changed her mind about

- The definition of psychological safety. Unchanged since 1999.
- The interpersonal-risk-taking anchor. Unchanged.
- The team-level focus. Unchanged.
- The rule that PS is not niceness has only intensified — she defends it harder now than she did in 2018.
- The importance of specific leader behaviors (Framing, Situational Humility, Proactive Inquiry). Unchanged.
- The rule that leaders must sanction clear misconduct — reinforces PS, does not violate it. Non-negotiable.
