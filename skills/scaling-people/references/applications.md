# Scaling People — When to Use, When Not, Adjacent Frameworks

> *Scaling People* is opinionated and template-heavy. It is also NOT a universal tool — it's a scale-org tool for the 20-to-1000+ people zone. Below the zone, most of it is premature. Above the zone, it composes with more specialized frameworks. This file names where Claire's method fits, where it doesn't, and how it relates to adjacent skills — Claire being unusually generous in crediting her sources, this file is unusually load-bearing.

## When Scaling People fits

Claire's method is at its most useful in these situations:

- **A scaling company (roughly 20 → 1,000+ people) building the people-operating-system for the first time.** The primary target audience.
- **A leader in a scaling company who needs the specific artifacts** — founding documents, team charters, personal operating manuals, interview scorecards, review templates.
- **An experienced operator joining a new company** who needs to compress the "how to work with me" onboarding overhead.
- **A COO / VP People / Chief of Staff / Head of Ops** designing the operating cadence, review process, or compensation calibration.
- **A hiring manager designing a new interview loop** — especially for executive hires.
- **A manager doing reference calls** on a senior candidate.
- **A manager managing a low performer** through the four-phase escalation.
- **A team lead planning an offsite** — Claire's public toolkit maps directly.
- **A team lead diagnosing why the team is stuck** — the 4-principles diagnostic + Tuckman-style team-state assessment.
- **A leader designing a re-org or restructure** — the "what are we optimizing / what price are we paying?" frame.
- **A founder transitioning from doing-everything to context-not-control** — the whole book is designed for this.

## When Scaling People does NOT fit (or fits poorly)

- **Pre-PMF startup with 3–10 people.** Most of the operating cadence is premature. Claire explicitly warns not to force a mission statement in month one. The personal operating manual and the "say the thing" principle still apply; the rest can wait. Point them at first-principles startup material instead.
- **Legal / HR-compliance situations.** If harassment, discrimination, or safety is at stake, Claire's method informs but doesn't replace legal / HR counsel. Direct the user to their HR partner or attorney, not to a template.
- **Pure strategy questions** (where to play, how to win, market positioning). These are strategy frameworks — [[playing-to-win]], [[good-strategy-bad-strategy]], [[7-powers]], [[obviously-awesome]]. *Scaling People* assumes the strategy is decided and asks how to organize people to execute it.
- **Product-decision rituals** (writing a PR/FAQ, running a six-page memo debate). Amazon's [[working-backwards]] is the specialized tool.
- **Single-page company alignment artifact** the whole org can memorize. Salesforce's [[v2mom]] is the specialized tool.
- **Weekly OKR ritual** (Monday commitments / Friday wins). Christina Wodtke's [[radical-focus]] is the specialized tool.
- **Diagnosing a specific piece of feedback** to see if it landed as care+challenge or as attack. Kim Scott's [[radical-candor]] 2×2 is the specialized tool — Claire's method is the *system* around feedback, not the diagnostic of a single move.
- **First-principles design of manager mechanics** (1:1 leverage math, task-relevant maturity, meeting-type theory). Andy Grove's [[high-output-management]] is the load-bearing foundation Claire herself builds on.
- **Pattern-recognition on org-wide dysfunction** where the diagnosis is unclear (are we in a complex domain? Complicated? Chaotic?). Dave Snowden's [[cynefin]] is the specialized diagnostic.
- **Founder-market fit / where to bet the company** for a very early team. [[pattern-breakers]], [[four-fits]] are specialized.

## Adjacent frameworks — when each is the better tool

### Andy Grove — [[high-output-management]]

**The relationship: Grove is Claire's load-bearing infrastructure.** She cites him openly.

- **Grove** — managerial output = output of your team + output of teams you influence. First-principles on 1:1s, task-relevant maturity, meeting-type theory, leverage math.
- **Claire** — the templates and rituals that fill in Grove's structure. 1:1s become the venue for hypothesis-based coaching. Grove's OKRs get FOCUS(S) criteria. Grove's mgmt leverage extends into founding documents + operating cadence.
- **When Grove is better:** you're designing the manager mechanics from first principles, or the org has no manager tradition yet.
- **When Claire is better:** the mechanics exist and you need the specific templates, docs, and failure-mode diagnostics.
- **Compose:** Grove is the load-bearing plumbing; *Scaling People* is the furnished interior. Almost always compose them.

### Kim Scott — [[radical-candor]]

**The relationship: Claire endorses; the two live in overlapping feedback space with different units of analysis.**

- **Kim's method** — the 2×2 (Care Personally + Challenge Directly) as the diagnostic for a *single piece of feedback*. Confession-first pedagogy. Care Personally is load-bearing.
- **Claire's method** — hypothesis-based coaching + informal feedback culture + formal reviews + four-phase escalation — the *system* around feedback. Less taxonomic than Kim's 2×2; more procedural.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** the specific conversation is the unit — you need to diagnose whether a piece of feedback landed as care+challenge or as OA/RE.
- **When Claire is better:** the operating rhythm around feedback is the unit — you need the review process, the informal feedback culture, and the escalation framework.
- **Compose:** Radical Candor is the *quality* of a single feedback move; Claire is the *system* that makes many good feedback moves happen. Nearly always compose them for feedback-heavy sessions.

### Marc Benioff — [[v2mom]]

**The relationship: Overlapping goal-setting territory; different granularity.**

- **V2MOM** — Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles, Measures. Salesforce's single-page alignment tool. Read the whole org.
- **Claire** — founding documents (mission + long-term goals + values + team charters) + FOCUS(S) goal criteria. More decomposed, more explicit at role/team level.
- **When V2MOM is better:** you want a single-page company-level artifact the whole org can memorize and reference.
- **When Claire is better:** you need the full doc set with role-specific charters, plus the operating cadence around them.
- **Compose:** run V2MOM at the company level; use Claire's team charters at the sub-org level. They stack.

### Christina Wodtke — [[radical-focus]]

**The relationship: Both endorse OKRs; different specialization.**

- **Wodtke** — the OKR mechanic + the specific weekly cadence (Monday commitments / Friday wins) that makes quarterly OKRs bite.
- **Claire** — endorses OKRs but is framework-agnostic. Shifted Stripe from 6-month to quarterly, but her position: *"the framework matters less than the clarity of the goals and the repetition in their communication."*
- **When Wodtke is better:** you need the specific weekly OKR ritual to make quarterly goals actually bite.
- **When Claire is better:** you need OKRs inside a larger operating system (charters + reviews + comp + hiring aligned).
- **Compose:** Wodtke's weekly OKR ritual inside Claire's larger cadence. They stack.

### Amazon / Bezos — [[working-backwards]]

**The relationship: Claire endorses written-memo culture; different decision layer.**

- **Working Backwards** — the PR/FAQ ritual + the six-page memo before-the-meeting. Product-decision ritual specifically.
- **Claire** — memo-first business-review culture + written-culture principle. Not product-specific.
- **When Working Backwards is better:** the question is *what product to build*.
- **When Claire is better:** the question is *how to run the org that builds it*.
- **Compose:** WB is the product-decision ritual; Claire is the meta-org that repeats WB reliably. Compose when running product-heavy orgs.

### Fred Kofman — Conscious Business

**The relationship: Claire cites Kofman as source for victim/player, left-hand column detoxification, and the underlying ownership philosophy.**

- **Kofman** — the philosophical roots. Read Kofman if you want the deeper "why."
- **Claire** — Kofman operationalized for a working COO. Templates and rituals that make Kofman's principles bite at scale.

### Ben Horowitz — The Hard Thing About Hard Things

**The relationship: Complementary registers.**

- **Horowitz** — war stories + tactical rules for CEO moments (layoffs, wartime CEO, demoting a friend). Reactive.
- **Claire** — the system that reduces the number of Horowitz-moments by baking honesty and structure in early. Preemptive.
- Claire endorses Horowitz's "the struggle" and extends it: *"the more senior you become, the more creative reality gets at finding ways to beat you up every day."*
- **Compose:** read both. Claire preempts, Horowitz survives.

### Elad Gil — High Growth Handbook

**The relationship: Same audience, different form.**

- **Gil** — curated interview compilation + tactical playbook. Broad coverage (fundraising, board mgmt, PR, legal, people).
- **Claire** — deeper and more system-oriented on the people-and-operating-system slice. Was interviewed for Gil's book.
- **Compose:** Gil for coverage across scale-org topics; Claire for depth on the people slice.

### Camille Fournier — The Manager's Path

**The relationship: Function-specific vs. function-agnostic.**

- **Fournier** — engineering-manager-specific career ladder and manager-craft handbook.
- **Claire** — function-agnostic; templates translate across sales, product, ops, engineering.
- **When Fournier is better:** engineering-manager-specific questions (tech-lead-to-EM, EM-to-Director, on-call, incident retros).
- **When Claire is better:** cross-functional templates and general manager craft.
- **Compose:** often bundled in engineering-leader reading lists.

### Playing to Win / Good Strategy Bad Strategy — [[playing-to-win]] / [[good-strategy-bad-strategy]] / [[7-powers]] / [[obviously-awesome]]

**The relationship: Different problem entirely.**

- These are strategy frameworks (what to do at the org level).
- *Scaling People* is a management framework (how to work with the people who execute it).
- **When each is better:** if the user is asking about competitive positioning, market choice, or where-to-play — those are strategy skills, not Claire.
- **Compose:** *Scaling People* is *inside* the org that has a strategy. They operate at different layers and don't conflict.

### Cynefin (Dave Snowden) — [[cynefin]]

**The relationship: Diagnostic vs. operational.**

- **Cynefin** — diagnosis of what kind of problem you're in (Clear / Complicated / Complex / Chaotic).
- **Claire** — operational templates that assume you know what kind of problem you're in.
- **When Cynefin is better:** the diagnosis of "what kind of problem is this?" is the actual question.
- **When Claire is better:** you know the problem is a management/people-system problem and need the specific tool.

## Adjacent frameworks NOT to conflate

- **Scaling People ≠ Radical Candor.** Overlap on feedback; different unit of analysis. Kim's is the 2×2 for a single move; Claire's is the operating system around many moves. Cross-link, don't merge.
- **Scaling People ≠ Working Backwards.** Both endorse written culture; different domain. WB is product-decision-specific.
- **Scaling People ≠ V2MOM.** Both are goal artifacts; different granularity. V2MOM is single-page company; Claire's founding docs decompose.
- **Scaling People ≠ Good Strategy Bad Strategy.** Different layer. Strategy vs. operating system.
- **Scaling People ≠ a personality-assessment book.** Claire uses MBTI/DISC/Enneagram as starting points for shared vocabulary, not identity. Don't turn Chapter 1 into a personality workshop.
- **Scaling People ≠ Radical Transparency (Dalio / Bridgewater).** Claire's "say the thing you think you cannot say" is bounded by "separate the person from the it." Radical Transparency at Bridgewater tends to drop that boundary. Kim Scott has pushed back on the conflation; Claire's method inherits Kim's caution.

## When to compose Scaling People with another framework

- **Scale-org building a feedback culture from scratch** → *Scaling People* (system) + [[radical-candor]] (single-move quality) + [[high-output-management]] (1:1 mechanic).
- **Scale-org designing the goal-setting cadence** → *Scaling People* (FOCUS(S) + operating cadence) + [[radical-focus]] (weekly OKR ritual) + [[v2mom]] (single-page company artifact).
- **Scale-org running product-heavy decisions** → *Scaling People* (memo culture, operating cadence) + [[working-backwards]] (product-decision ritual).
- **Founder navigating scale + strategy simultaneously** → *Scaling People* + a strategy skill ([[playing-to-win]] or [[good-strategy-bad-strategy]] or [[7-powers]]).
- **Executive hiring — a very senior role** → *Scaling People* (reference drill + followership test) + [[radical-candor]] (Care Personally investment for exec-in-onboarding).
- **Manager managing a low performer** → *Scaling People* (four-phase escalation) + [[radical-candor]] (the specific feedback delivery quality).
- **Diagnosing org-wide dysfunction** → [[cynefin]] (what-kind-of-problem-is-this?) + *Scaling People* (which piece of the OS broke?).
- **Building a new team from scratch** → *Scaling People* (team charter + personal operating manual for everyone) + [[radical-candor]] (norms for the feedback culture).

## Rule of thumb

If the question is:
- "How do we build our people-operating-system at scale?" → **Scaling People.**
- "How do I write my personal operating manual?" → **Scaling People.**
- "How do I run this hiring loop / reference call?" → **Scaling People.**
- "How do I manage this low performer?" → **Scaling People** (four-phase escalation) + **[[radical-candor]]** (delivery quality).
- "How do I give this specific piece of feedback?" → **[[radical-candor]]** primary; **Scaling People** for the operating context.
- "How should we design our 1:1s from first principles?" → **[[high-output-management]]** primary; **Scaling People** for the specific templates.
- "How should we do our OKRs?" → **[[radical-focus]]** for the weekly ritual + **Scaling People** for the larger cadence.
- "What single-page artifact aligns the whole company?" → **[[v2mom]].**
- "How should we make this product decision?" → **[[working-backwards]].**
- "What is our competitive strategy?" → **[[playing-to-win]]** / **[[good-strategy-bad-strategy]]** / **[[7-powers]]** — not Claire.
- "What is our positioning?" → **[[obviously-awesome]]** — not Claire.
- "What kind of problem are we even in?" → **[[cynefin]]** — not Claire.
