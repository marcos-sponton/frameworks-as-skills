# Scaling People — Invocation Templates

> Ready-to-adapt prompt shapes for the common tasks a user brings to this skill. Not scripts — starting points. Each entry ends with the specific artifact (template, opener, question) Claire would give.

## 1. "Help me write my personal operating manual."

**The shape of the assistant's response:**

1. **Frame it as a working doc, not a personality quiz.** Claire's version has three sections; adapt to your role.
2. **Walk the sections:**
   - **Section 1 — Work preferences.** Communication channels per message type. Response timeframes. Contact norms. Preferred meeting formats.
   - **Section 2 — Behavioral style.** Decision-making default. Behavior under stress. Deep-work times. Management style with new vs. established work.
   - **Section 3 (optional) — Personal context.** Important dates. Accomplishments the manager should know about.
3. **Give Claire's own example line** — *"I am not a micromanager necessarily, I'm gonna give you space to do your work. But the first time that we're doing something, I will get really into the details."* — to model the register: specific, honest, useful.
4. **Ask the user for the specific inputs** — go through the sections and produce a draft in their voice. Don't write generic copy.
5. **End with the pinning + refresh rules.** Pin it somewhere everyone can find. Refresh annually. Cite it when work-style mismatches surface.

**The template opener:**
> "Here's how to work with me. This is a working document — expect it to shift as we work together. Section 1: how I like to communicate. Section 2: how I make decisions and behave under stress. Section 3: things it helps to know about me personally."

## 2. "Help me design a hiring loop for [role]."

**The shape:**

1. **First: what does year-one success look like?** If you can't articulate it, the interview loop will be diagnosing the wrong thing. Force this before designing questions.
2. **Design the rubric first, the loop second.**
   - Role-specific skills (2–3 dimensions).
   - Values alignment.
   - Learning-organism attributes (curiosity, resilience, ownership).
   - Work-style / collaboration signals.
3. **Assign dimensions to interviewers** — each interviewer owns one or two. The loop covers all dimensions without overlap.
4. **Scorecard per interviewer** — score against the rubric, with evidence, not vibes.
5. **Add the bar-raiser role** if you're doing structured hiring — one person on every loop who gatekeeps the standard, not the specific hire.
6. **Claire's signature questions** to include:
   - Self-awareness: *"If I interviewed [reference name] next, what would they say it's like working with you?"*
   - Learning orientation: *"Tell me about a time you worked on something you knew you wouldn't enjoy."*
   - Ownership: *"A project that failed and what role you played in it. How do you recover from failure?"*
   - Curiosity: *"What have you changed your mind about recently? Why?"*
7. **The debrief rule:** discuss with evidence, not with impressions.

**The check for the manager:** *"Who owns the final decision on this hire?"* Answer must be a specific person, and it must be the hiring manager.

## 3. "Coach me through this reference call."

**The shape — the reference-check drill:**

1. **Are you doing this yourself?** If you were about to delegate to the recruiter — stop. Claire's line: *"If you are delegating the references, you're making a mistake."*
2. **Ask candidate permission before back-channel calls.** *"Would you be comfortable if I talked to them very confidentially?"*
3. **Prime the referee with role context.** Describe the current situation, year-one success indicators, the capabilities required. Then: *"Do you think they'll thrive in this?"*
4. **The ranking question.** *"Are they top 50%, top 20%, top 10%?"* Follow-up: *"Does that mean they're top 5%?"*
5. **The coaching question.** *"What can I do as their manager to help them succeed?"* Immediately opens up performance, blind spots, development.
6. **For exec references — the followership question.** *"When they moved, who followed them?"* Or: *"Would you follow them to a new company?"*
7. **Push for a recent reference.** *"Is there someone who left your company recently who could talk about them?"*
8. **Back-load the back-channels** — resist confirmation bias.

**The check for the user:** *"What would change your mind about this hire?"* If nothing, you're doing validation calls, not reference checks.

## 4. "My team is stuck. Diagnose it."

**The shape — the 4-principles diagnostic:**

1. **Ask which of the four is the actual gap:**
   - **Self-awareness / mutual awareness gap** — do people know how each other work? Personal operating manuals shared?
   - **Something not being said** — is there a compounding-tension pattern in team meetings?
   - **Management-vs-leadership confusion** — is the team stuck on execution (management problem) or stuck on direction (leadership problem)?
   - **Operating system break** — which specific mechanism (goal-setting, review rhythm, decision rights, hiring loop) is failing?
2. **Ask Claire's diagnostic question:** *"Which part of the operating system broke — and how do we return to it?"* Not *"who screwed up?"*
3. **Tuckman-check the team state.** Forming / Storming / Norming / Performing — different intervention per stage.
4. **If it's a "say the thing" problem** — help the user draft what needs to be said, using the neutral-distance line: *"From my seat, I have a couple of things I'm worried about."*
5. **If it's an operating-system problem** — help the user identify the specific mechanism to fix (team charter refresh, review cadence, decision-type declaration).

**End with:** *"What's one specific mechanism you're going to change in the next two weeks?"*

## 5. "Help me manage this low performer."

**The shape — the four-phase escalation:**

1. **First: where are you in the sequence?**
   - **Phase 0** — Pattern recognition (under 3 weeks). Notice; don't act. Test context vs. trend.
   - **Phase 1** — Align on challenge (1–3 conversations, ~2 weeks). Name the pattern; hear their perspective.
   - **Phase 2** — Agree on next steps (1–2 conversations, ~1 week). Concrete changes, explicit timeline.
   - **Phase 3** — Create action plan (1–3 months). Formal PIP + parallel backup planning.
2. **The Phase 1 opening line** (Claire's direct version): *"I am worried this is not working out for you."*
3. **Precede with hypothesis-based coaching if you're still in Phase 0/1.** *"Here's what I'm noticing — does this match your experience?"* Let them self-assess first.
4. **Separate the person from the "it."** *"I'm not criticizing you, I'm criticizing [the specific work / behavior]."*
5. **Check the Bob test (Kim Scott, endorsed by Claire).** If the person would say *"why didn't you tell me?"* — you failed. Firing after unwarned failure is Ruinous Empathy compounded into cruelty.
6. **Parallel-track backup planning starts in Phase 2.** Don't wait until Phase 3 to start considering successor, adjacent role, or exit.

**End with:** *"Which phase are you in right now? What's your specific move for this week?"*

## 6. "Help me write my team charter."

**The shape:**

1. **Frame as a living doc, not a wiki tombstone.**
2. **Walk the sections** (see template in `references/method.md`):
   - Team name + mission (one sentence).
   - Customers (who this team serves — internal, external, or both).
   - Strategy (3–5 year, connected to company mission).
   - Goals (this year / quarter, FOCUS(S)-compliant).
   - What we will do (scope).
   - What we will NOT do (anti-scope — as important as scope).
   - Team norms (meeting cadence, decision rights, comms defaults).
   - Owner + last updated.
3. **Force the anti-scope.** *"What are we NOT going to do?"* — this is where most teams over-commit and under-deliver.
4. **Run FOCUS(S) on the goals** before adopting: Focus, Objectively assessable, Challenging but possible, User-oriented, States not activities, Sensitivity and specificity.
5. **Refresh annually.**

**End with:** *"Who owns this charter, and when is the next refresh scheduled?"*

## 7. "Help me plan an offsite."

**The shape (borrowing from Claire's public toolkit):**

1. **First: does the team need an offsite?** Assess team state (Tuckman). A Storming-stage team may need a different intervention.
2. **What's the objective?** Claire's three legit objectives:
   - Transform a work group into a cohesive team.
   - Evaluate progress + plan future priorities.
   - Encourage long-term strategic thinking.
3. **Design the task-vs-team ratio deliberately.** Setting metrics = task-focused. Team-building = team-focused. Design the split before the agenda.
4. **Structure with:**
   - Planning checklist (nothing overlooked).
   - Set objectives + agenda.
   - Facilitator guide.
   - Icebreaker.
   - Welcome + check-in exercise.
   - Working sessions.
   - Check-out exercise (each person: one takeaway + one worry).
5. **Point the user at [Claire's Offsite Toolkit](https://docs.superhuman.com/@clairehughesjohnson/claires-offsite-toolkit)** for the actual templates.

**End with:** *"What's the one specific outcome the team should walk away with?"*

## 8. "Help me draft this hard feedback."

**The shape:**

1. **First: what's the hypothesis?** Not the verdict. *"Here's what I'm noticing — does this match your experience?"* is the opener.
2. **Test the Care Personally axis (borrow from [[radical-candor]]).** Have you invested in the relationship enough that this feedback can land? If not, that's the work first, not the delivery.
3. **Separate the person from the "it."** *"I'm not criticizing you, I'm criticizing [the specific behavior/work]."*
4. **Use the neutral-distance line.** *"From my seat, 10,000-to-20,000 feet away, I have a couple of things I'm worried about."*
5. **Draft the feedback with:**
   - The observation (specific, behavioral, not personality).
   - The impact (what happened as a result).
   - The hypothesis / question (not the verdict).
6. **End with an ask** — what would need to change, by when, and how you'll know.
7. **After the conversation:** ask *"Did I say that well? Did it land as caring or as harsh?"* — the recipient's answer diagnoses the delivery.

**Do NOT** draft the feedback if:
- The user is fishing for permission to unload on someone they dislike.
- The user hasn't done the Care Personally investment.
- The user is skipping hypothesis-based coaching and jumping straight to verdict.

Redirect using the "separate the person from the it" move.

## 9. "Help me write our founding documents."

**The shape:**

1. **Are you actually ready?** If you're pre-PMF or in the first 30 days of a new company, Claire's warning: *"Listen for the crystallization of what you're trying to be."* Don't force the mission statement early.
2. **If you're ready — walk the four in order:**
   - **Mission** (why the company exists — one concise sentence).
   - **Long-term goals** (3–5 year plan; Stripe called them "long-term goals" publicly, not "vision," because concrete framing forces specificity).
   - **Values** (behaviors — test: *would you fire someone for violating them?* If not, they're marketing).
   - **Team charters** (per team; kept short; refreshed annually).
3. **The values test.** For each value: *"What behavior would violate this? Would we actually fire someone for it?"* If both answers aren't clean, keep working.
4. **The mission test.** Could a new hire in month one state the mission back to you in one sentence? If not, the mission isn't concise enough.
5. **Refresh cadence:** annually, with the leadership team.

**End with:** *"Who owns each doc? When is the annual refresh?"*

## 10. "Help me design our operating cadence."

**The shape:**

1. **Ask what's actually broken.** Add structure at the moment of a break, not preemptively.
2. **Claire's recommended layers:**
   - Charters (founding docs) — annual refresh.
   - OKRs — quarterly (Claire shifted Stripe from 6-month to quarterly; framework matters less than clarity + repetition).
   - Company metrics targets — annual.
   - Business reviews — variable per team; written memos first.
   - Performance reviews — semi-annual or annual; multi-source; calibrated.
   - 1:1s — weekly.
3. **Cross-link:** for the weekly OKR ritual, point at [[radical-focus]]. For 1:1 mechanics, point at [[high-output-management]]. For single-page company alignment, point at [[v2mom]].
4. **Warning:** *"You need planning structures earlier than you think"* — but don't over-engineer. Trigger is *"we broke this way once,"* not *"we might break this way later."*

**End with:** *"Which layer are you adding first? What break is triggering it?"*

## 11. "We're re-org-ing. Help me think through it."

**The shape:**

1. **Frame:** *"There's no such thing as an optimal org structure."* You're picking trade-offs.
2. **The question:** *"What are we optimizing? What price are we paying?"* Names the trade-off; makes the decision reversible if the price gets too high.
3. **In hypergrowth:** re-orgs every 3–6 months are normal. Frame as iteration, not failure.
4. **Communication rules:**
   - State the specific problem the re-org solves.
   - State what you're optimizing.
   - State what you're giving up.
   - Give people clarity on their specific role + manager + team + charter.
5. **Team-level:** refresh every affected team charter before / immediately after the re-org.

**End with:** *"What are you optimizing? What price are you paying? Are you willing to pay it?"*

## 13. "Should we hire a Chief of Staff?"

**The shape** (Claire has a specific view here):

1. **Claire dislikes the Chief of Staff title** for its ambiguity. Undefined scope produces confusion.
2. **If the actual need is Business Operations** — consider a BizOps team instead. Swiss army knife team that tackles undefined problems. Typically needed at PMF.
3. **A great BizOps lead can grow into COO.** Chief of Staff often can't.
4. **If you do hire a Chief of Staff** — define the scope explicitly and time-bound the role. Otherwise it drifts into either shadow-executive or admin-overhead territory.

**End with:** *"What's the specific problem this hire would solve? Could BizOps solve it better?"*

## 14. "I'm a new leader joining a scaling company. What's my first-30-days playbook?"

**The shape:**

1. **Write your personal operating manual in week one.** Share with your team + cross-functional peers + skip-levels.
2. **Read all the founding docs** — mission, long-term goals, values, team charters.
3. **1:1 with every direct report** — no agenda; listen. Ask about their operating manual (or ask them to write one).
4. **1:1 with cross-functional peers** — understand hand-offs and shared work.
5. **Skip-levels within 60 days.**
6. **Don't restructure in the first 90 days.** Diagnose the operating system first.
7. **When you do act — start with the piece of the OS that's most broken.** Claire's principle #4.

**End with:** *"What's the most important thing you want to know about how this org actually works — that's not written down?"*
