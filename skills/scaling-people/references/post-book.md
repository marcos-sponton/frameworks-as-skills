## What has NOT changed since the book

Claire's core method is stable — the book is recent enough that she hasn't publicly refined or reversed positions. Continuities to date:

- The 4 operating principles are unchanged.
- FOCUS(S), the founding documents structure, the operating cadence, the hypothesis-based coaching model, the four-phase low-performer escalation — all as in the book.
- The personal operating manual ("Working with X") continues to be her most-cited artifact and the one she most consistently recommends.
- The reference-check drill and the "learning organism" filter continue to be the moves she leans on hardest in interviews.

## Where Claire has amplified or extended in interviews

### On victim / player (Tim Ferriss 2024)

The book covers victim/player in Chapter 3 (hiring) as an interview-loop filter. In the Tim Ferriss appearance she extends the concept as a *personal-development* lens — not just a hiring signal, but a lifetime practice.

- *"People who can't stop being victims, and the other gap is — I call it 'self-awareness gap,' where they think they are the best in the world."* Both are the hardest gaps to close in adults.
- Rules-as-liberation frame — *"Good rules can be liberating."* Extends the book's operating-system principle from the org level to the individual level.

### On saying no cleanly (Tim Ferriss 2024)

Not deeply in the book, but developed in the interview:

- *"I'm going to do a probability assessment. The chance that I'm going to invest/make a donation are sub five percent. No. No for you, no for me. And you don't have to think about it ever again."*
- Better to give a clear, quick rejection than false hope that requires repeated follow-ups.
- Related: *"I am just honest about, 'I can't do this well, and I think you want someone at their best. It's not going to be my best.'"*

### On coaching vs. directing (Tim Ferriss 2024 + First Round Review 2023)

The book has this; the podcasts sharpen it:

- *"There's a reason these people are leaders. Most of the time they're 80 percent of the way there. They're just not confident in their instinct. And so my job is not to tell them what to do or how to do it; it is to build their confidence in their instinct and then, yeah, we can brainstorm the last 20 percent."*
- *"People do not learn by being told answers... you're going to lead them through learning with you and they are going to get to the answer and you are going to celebrate them doing that."*
- *"Be an explorer, not a lecturer."*

### On the Collison brothers' hiring process — for Claire herself (First Round Review 2023)

This is post-book color that the book itself doesn't cover in detail:

- Patrick "relentlessly researched" and pursued exceptional talent.
- Asked references detailed questions, then: *"Who have you worked with better than this person?"* Maps talent networks systematically.
- Mixed strong opinions with genuine curiosity about the candidate — not a hard sell.
- Sent Claire a vintage copy of *Computer Lib* — signaling passion and knowledge, not just recruiting effort.
- Multiple interview rounds with company-wide all-hands Q&A session.
- Practiced working together — drove to office to write company goals together.
- Portfolio assembly over perfect fit — hired her as "Chief of Business Operations" (not COO) to show respect to Billy Alvarado. Portfolio grew over years.
- Asked: *"What do we need? What are your career aspirations? What might you bring we didn't know we needed?"*

Model for how to run exec-hiring diligence: not selling, investigating a match.

### On executive-hiring blind spots — her own (First Round Review 2023)

- *"I was making the assumption, okay, if you got to be this successful in your career, you're gonna adapt."* Reality: adaptation is not guaranteed.
- Her theory on why her exec-hiring track record was weaker than her other hiring: she was *"trying to be liked"* and not pushing hard enough on diligence.
- Some executives from scaled environments never actually recruited themselves — they need teaching: *"Show them how to recruit."*
- Followership as signal: strong executives inspire others to work with them across roles. *"I brought people into the company"* — absence of that pattern is a leadership problem.

### On the "learning organism" as her single most predictive attribute (First Round Review 2023)

The book names it; the interview makes it central:

- *"Is the person a learning organism? Are they curious? Are they a learner?"*
- Different from ambitious people who achieve others' definitions of success and then stop learning.
- Test in the interview:
  - Curiosity — can they go deep? Do they introspect?
  - Resilience — how do they respond under pressure?
  - Ownership — victim vs. player.
  - Results orientation — track outcomes, not process.
- Sample questions from the book, re-emphasized in the interview:
  - *"Tell me about a time you worked on something you knew you wouldn't enjoy."*
  - *"A project that failed and what role you played in it."*
  - *"How do you recover from failure?"*

### On cross-industry management (First Round Review 2023, LEADERS 2023)

Post-book Claire has been pushing on how *Scaling People* generalizes beyond tech:

- Cross-industry cases she uses: Dan Cohen (Met Museum President), Dominique Crème (Michelin-star chef), Zannya Mittenbottles (The Economist editor), Sam Hagood (UCSF Chancellor), Reid Hoffman (feedback culture story).
- Point: management is universal across industries — respect, trust, self-awareness, ability to learn and adapt. The templates translate.

### On the personal operating manual — how it went viral inside Stripe (Fortune, Lenny 2023)

The book covers the template; the Fortune article + Lenny podcast add the story:

- Claire originally wrote *"Working with Claire"* at Google as a private doc.
- Brought it to Stripe when she joined.
- Made it open. People started asking *"Well, why don't we have more of these?"*
- Organic adoption inside Stripe — many people wrote their own.
- Fortune quote (Claire): *"You're actually completely cutting off all of that studying and reading between the lines and jumping right to work."*

### On the goal-framework question (Tom Tunguz office-hours notes)

- Claire's actual view is framework-agnostic: *"The framework type matters less than the clarity of the goals and the repetition in their communication."*
- She shifted Stripe from 6-month planning to quarterly OKRs — but the shift wasn't ideological, it was operational (the shorter cadence let them re-plan when the market moved).
- Metrics categories: input / operational / strategic — separate dashboards, separate cadences.

## Amplifications from external interviewers

Interviewers have pushed Claire on specific edge cases; her answers here are useful context:

### Newcomer podcast (Eric Newcomer, 2023)

- On management fundamentals vs. innovation: *"Countries with good management education and practices tend to have better economic outcomes"* (Patrick Collison's line, which convinced her to lean on proven practices rather than reinventing).
- On feedback directness (from Google engineers): appreciated their bluntness ("That's a terrible idea") but warned that feedback *"so direct that it feels like an attack"* causes people to stop listening. Neutral-distance framing: *"From my seat, 10,000-20,000 feet away, I have a couple of things I'm worried about."*
- Self-management: diagnose personal energy patterns by tracking good days and bad days on a calendar for a month.
- On leadership challenges: *"The more senior you become, the more creative reality gets at finding ways to beat you up every day"* — referencing Ben Horowitz's "the struggle."

### McKinsey Author Talks (2023)

- On what most companies get wrong at scale: they scale operations but not people-practices. The people-side falls behind because it's harder to measure and easier to defer.
- On the founder-to-scale-executive transition: the shift from doing everything to *"context, not control."*

### Slush 2023 (with Harry Stebbings)

- On the founder's role in scaling: the founder should remain involved in hiring through product-market fit stage, and the founder's specific decisions (senior hires, stopping projects, values calls) cannot be delegated.
- Multi-product strategy: requires identified *"hinge points"* to maintain customer coherence. Without hinge points, companies *"ship their org chart as a product."*
