# Play Bigger — Examples

> The dense case table the authors use publicly. When the assistant is teaching or diagnosing, reach for these — not generic "here's an example" prose. The case IS the lesson.

## Master case table

| Company | Category they designed | Signature move | Notes / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Salesforce** | Cloud CRM / "End of Software" | Marc Benioff hires actors to stage a mock protest outside Siebel's user conference. Siebel starts defending itself. Press runs with the story. Salesforce is legitimized as the category challenger the moment the incumbent acknowledges them. | The canonical Lightning Strike. Frame: "software is over." POV owned by CEO. |
| **Airbnb** | Home sharing | Named a category the market didn't have (peer-to-peer accommodation). Reframed hotels as the incumbent to displace. New languaging ("hosts", "guests" — not "landlords" and "renters"). | Recurring across Lochhead materials. |
| **Uber** | On-demand transportation | Named "on-demand" as the category, not "taxi 2.0" or "ride-share app." The category name did the reframe. Result: ~25× Lyft in valuation — King economics. | The 76% dynamic in extreme form. |
| **VMware** | Server virtualization | Category-designed the market from a technology insight (hypervisor). Built the ecosystem (developer partners, ISV certifications, VMworld conference). | Play Bigger 2016 case. |
| **Qualtrics** | Experience Management (XM) | Named a new category ("XM") that reframed adjacent categories (survey tools, CX platforms, market research) as legacy sub-categories inside XM. | Category-name-as-Lightning-Strike. |
| **Amazon (AWS)** | Cloud infrastructure | Serial Category King. AWS is the canonical "category the incumbents didn't see coming." Design + languaging + ecosystem + Data Flywheel — the full method executed by a company that already had one King category (e-commerce). | Continuous Category Design (Corporate Playbook). |
| **IKEA** | Flat-pack self-assembly furniture | Category-designed a whole retail category the incumbents couldn't copy without cannibalizing themselves. Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma mechanism in action. | 2016 book. |
| **Apple (iPhone)** | Smartphone as the category, not "mobile phone with apps" | 93% of category profits — Category King economics at extreme. Naming, POV ("this changes everything"), Lightning Strike (Jobs' keynote), ecosystem (App Store). | Referenced across Category Pirates and *22 Laws*. |
| **Lululemon** | Athleisure | Named the category ("athleisure"). Owned it via ecosystem (yoga instructors, brand ambassadors, physical stores as community hubs). | *The 22 Laws of Category Design* (2023). |
| **Liquid Death** | Canned water | Languaging move — reframed bottled water as the incumbent, canned water as a new category (punk-rock branding, aluminum format, "murder your thirst" POV). Different, not better; new tribe. | Lochhead's 2025 favorite; used to teach the Bolt-on AI Trap by inversion. |
| **Purell (GOJO Industries)** | Hand sanitizer | Didn't invent the sanitizer chemistry — invented the *category*. Reframed the problem (hand hygiene between hand-washings) instead of just launching a product. | [Lenny's Podcast / Lochhead episode](https://lochhead.com/how-to-become-a-category-pirate-with-lenny-rachitsky/). |
| **Netflix** | Streaming (vs DVD rental vs cable TV) | Repeat Category Design across multiple waves — first DVD-by-mail-as-category (vs Blockbuster stores), then streaming-as-category (vs DVDs and cable), then originals-as-category. Continuous Category Design. | Category Pirates + Play Bigger. |
| **Tesla** | Electric vehicles (as a category — not "green cars") | Category-designed and Lightning-Strike'd repeatedly (Roadster launch, Model S, Cybertruck reveal, Battery Day). CEO-owned POV in extreme form. | Recurring across Follow Your Different. |
| **Siebel** | THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE | The incumbent that lost the End of Software category to Salesforce. Used throughout Play Bigger as the case of what happens when the King is displaced by a category-designer. | Play Bigger 2016. |
| **Blockbuster** | THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE | Lost to Netflix's category-designed DVD-by-mail (and then streaming) move. Classic Existing Market Trap. | Category Pirates. |
| **BlackBerry** | THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE | Lost to Apple's smartphone-as-category move. Was still the "leader in the mobile phone category" — but the category itself moved. | Play Bigger + subsequent materials. |
| **Slack** | Team messaging / workplace collaboration | Named the category, built the ecosystem (integrations, apps directory, developer platform). Later category-defended by acquisition. | Category Pirates. |
| **Snowflake** | Cloud data warehouse (as its own category — not "cloud version of Teradata") | Categorization discipline: refused to be positioned as "cloud data warehouse" alone; carved out "Data Cloud" as the category. | Category Design Advisors case content. |
| **Peloton** | Connected fitness | Named the category. Built the ecosystem (instructors, community, tech, hardware, subscription). Category-defended less well post-COVID — cautionary. | Category Pirates. |
| **HubSpot** | Inbound marketing | Named the category ("inbound"), wrote the book, built the certification ecosystem. Textbook Play Bigger execution. | Widely cited. |

## Cross-cutting pattern

Every case above follows the same shape:

> **The company reframed the problem → named the new category → committed a Lightning Strike → built the ecosystem → captured 60–95% of the new category's economics.** Everyone else in the "same market" fought over the residual ~24% and were eventually re-narrated as legacy.

The counter-examples (Siebel, Blockbuster, BlackBerry, in some ways Peloton) share the inverse shape: **they were the King of an old category the market was re-categorizing out from under them, and they defended the old category instead of designing a new one.** This is the Existing Market Trap in incumbent form.

## AI-era emerging cases (still forming as of 2026)

Play Bigger commentary in Category Pirates and Follow Your Different is actively tracking these:

- **OpenAI vs Anthropic vs Google Gemini** — the LLM/foundation-model category is still forming; multiple contenders framing the problem differently. Watch which "different, not better" framing wins.
- **AI-native software categories** (AI-native CRM, AI-native code review, AI research infrastructure, voice-first AI, agentic browsing, etc.) — Lochhead's 2025 material argues most incumbents are stuck bolting AI onto old categories while AI-native challengers are designing new ones.
- **Perplexity vs Google Search** — attempted category redesign ("answer engine" as the category, not "search 2.0"). Whether the languaging holds up is the question.
- **Cursor / Windsurf vs Copilot** — the "AI code editor as its own category" vs "AI assistant to old editor" framing is a live Bolt-on AI Trap test case.

These are moving too fast to codify — the assistant should treat them as "cases in flight" and either check `author-live-sources.md` for the latest Category Pirates writing on them, or WebFetch fresh material rather than quoting from memory.

## Personal-brand cases (Snow Leopard territory)

- **Christopher Lochhead** — himself; category designer of "Category Design." Named the discipline, wrote the book, built the ecosystem (Category Pirates, podcasts, follow-up books). The framework applied to its own author.
- **Ryan Holiday** — Stoicism as a modern practical philosophy category (arguably). Named a niche, took a POV, built an ecosystem (Daily Stoic).
- **Tim Ferriss** — Category-of-one podcaster / lifestyle-experiment author. *Niche Down* territory.
- **Category Pirates themselves** — the writing collaboration as a category ("mini-books on Substack") — self-application of Snow Leopard.

## How to use this table in a session

1. **Case-first teaching.** When the user asks "what does POV look like in practice?" — reach for Salesforce End of Software or Liquid Death. Don't teach the concept abstractly.
2. **Case-first diagnosis.** When the user shows their positioning and it sounds like BlackBerry defending "mobile phones," name the counter-example.
3. **Case as anti-pattern.** When the user proposes bolting AI onto an existing category, contrast with Liquid Death's inverse move — reframing the incumbent category as legacy.
4. **Cases in flight.** When the user asks about AI companies, note that the category-forming period is live — quote from recent Category Pirates / Follow Your Different rather than from the 2016 book.
