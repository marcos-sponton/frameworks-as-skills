# Play Bigger — Post-book material

> **This is the differential of the skill.** *Play Bigger* was published in 2016 — ten years ago. The method has substantially evolved since, and most of the current thinking lives NOT in the book but in the **Category Pirates** Substack (Lochhead + Eddie Yoon + Nicolas Cole + Bri Clark, twice-weekly since 2021), four Category Pirates books (2022–2023), and the 2025 prequel *The Existing Market Trap*. When the assistant only quotes the 2016 book, the skill is stale. Load this file whenever the topic touches modern category economics, AI-era category traps, personal-brand category design, the Data Flywheel, No Ocean, Languaging, Snow Leopard, or 22 Laws — none of which are in the 2016 book.

## What changed after the book — one-paragraph summary

The 2016 book established the discipline (Category Design, Category King, POV, Lightning Strike, Magic Triangle, TTMC / 76% economics). Post-book, the same authors — mostly Lochhead now, in collaboration with Eddie Yoon and Nicolas Cole under the Category Pirates banner — added: (1) the **Data Flywheel** as the modern source of category-king defensibility; (2) **Languaging** as a discipline distinct from marketing/copy; (3) **Snow Leopard** — Category Design applied to individuals (writers, creators, personal brands); (4) the **No Ocean Strategy** pushback on Blue Ocean; (5) the **22 Laws of Category Design** as a Ries-and-Trout-successor tightening of the method; (6) in 2025, Ramadan re-teamed with Lochhead for **The Existing Market Trap** (13 Deadly Sins) — the "prequel" that explains why great companies get stuck and how Category Design is the escape. The 2025 AI-era addition is the **Bolt-on AI Trap** — the specific way most AI companies are getting existing-market-trapped right now.

## 1. The Data Flywheel (Category Pirates, ~2022 onward)

**The single biggest post-book addition.** The 2016 book has the concept of a category flywheel (POV + Lightning Strikes + ecosystem + capital + data + talent). Category Pirates specified the *data* piece into its own distinct flywheel — because in the modern era, data is what makes a Category Kingship structurally uncatchable.

Category Pirates framing:

> *"The Data Flywheel is a system that accurately predicts the future so you can continue to design and dominate the category."*

The loop:

1. Category leadership generates **proprietary data** (usage, behavior, telemetry, transactions).
2. Proprietary data trains **proprietary models / predictions**.
3. Predictions let you **design the next wave** of the category before anyone else sees it.
4. Next wave reinforces category leadership → more data.

Economic backing:
- Wall Street rewards Category Queens/Kings with a Data Flywheel with ~**$4.82 of market cap per $1 of revenue growth** — ~**5× the typical company's multiple.** (Eddie Yoon research, cited in Play Bigger / Category Pirates materials.)

**Implication for how the assistant runs the method:** don't wait until year 5 to design the Data Flywheel. Ask on Day 1: "what proprietary data does this category generate, how do we capture it, and what does the prediction it enables let us do that nobody else can?"

**Contrast with the 2016 book:** the book treats data as one of six flywheel fuels. Category Pirates treats it as *the* structural moat of modern Category Kingship. If the user is operating a modern software / AI / marketplace / SaaS category, the Data Flywheel is closer to first-class than sixth-class.

## 2. Languaging (Snow Leopard, 22 Laws)

Category Pirates treats *language* as its own discipline — separate from copy, marketing, or branding.

From *Snow Leopard* (2022): the book includes the **Quick Guide to Languaging** — *"a model for inventing new language to get ideas to 'stick' — and the unfair advantage this creates."*

**The claim:** language does the category work. Naming the problem, naming the future, naming the "from" and "to" (Frotos) is not marketing polish — it's the mechanism by which the market's mind reorganizes. Same product + different languaging = different market position.

**AI-era version (2025):** the language you use matters more than ever. Calling your innovation an "AI assistant" or "AI copilot" positions it as secondary to an incumbent category. Declaring it a new category magnetizes the future. Same product, different languaging, different market outcome.

**How to apply:** when the user shows their homepage / one-pager / pitch, run a languaging audit:
- What words does the incumbent own? (Don't use them.)
- What words does the incumbent NOT have? (Own them.)
- Is the category name 2–3 words? (If not, sharpen.)
- Are the Frotos ("from-to's") named crisply?
- Does the product taxonomy use new words that only make sense inside the new category, or does it use the incumbent's taxonomy?

## 3. Snow Leopard — Category of One (2022)

The Category Pirates book *Snow Leopard: How Legendary Writers Create A Category Of One* extended Category Design from companies to **individuals** (writers, creators, thinkers, consultants, personal brands).

Core premise: *"Legendary writers, the ones who stand the test of time, create a category of one. They're snow leopards."*

**Category of one** = the individual analog of Category King. The person nobody else can be confused with, because they've named a niche, taken a POV, and made the languaging their own. Related earlier book: Lochhead + Heather Clancy, *Niche Down: How to Become Legendary by Being Different* (2018).

For the assistant: when the user is doing personal brand / consulting / creator work — this is the branch of the method to load. The seven personal-brand steps in `method.md` map to Snow Leopard.

## 4. No Ocean Strategy (Category Pirates)

Explicit pushback on *Blue Ocean Strategy* (Kim & Mauborgne). From the [No Ocean Strategy essay](https://www.categorypirates.news/p/no-ocean-strategy-why-sailing-for-e3f):

Category Pirates' five arguments against Blue Ocean:
1. The ocean assumption itself is wrong — there's no pre-existing space to find.
2. Blue Ocean over-credits incumbent-driven innovation.
3. Blue Ocean under-values technology's role in creating markets.
4. Superconsumers + data play larger roles than Blue Ocean acknowledges.
5. Category design vs product design — leadership requires building categories, not just companies.

Category Pirates' line:

> *"Pirates reject the premise! They don't just blindly accept the way it is."*

And:

> *"Categories make brands, not vice versa."*

**Composition rule:** if the user brings Blue Ocean thinking to a Category Design problem, name the pushback and reframe. Play Bigger and Blue Ocean carry different assumptions about what a "market" is — don't blend them uncritically.

## 5. The 22 Laws of Category Design (2023)

*The 22 Laws of Category Design: Name & Claim Your Niche, Share Your POV, And Move The World From Where It Is To Somewhere Different* (Category Pirates, 2023). Explicitly positioned as the Ries-and-Trout-successor for the modern era.

The 2016 book cites Ries & Trout's *22 Immutable Laws of Marketing* as foundational (Law of Leadership, Law of the Category, Law of the Mind, Law of Perception, Law of Focus, Law of the Ladder, Law of Division). The 22 Laws of Category Design is the Category Pirates' own numbered list.

**The book covers 22 Category Design principles that, when combined, can help a company become a Category King/Queen and make it impossible for someone else to "do what you do."** Detailed real-world cases: Apple's redefinition of the mobile-phone category, Lululemon's reign over athleisure, Liquid Death's rise above bottled water.

**For the assistant:** treat 22 Laws as the tightest post-book distillation. When the user wants a compact operational reference, this is the book to point at.

## 6. The Existing Market Trap + 13 Deadly Sins (2025)

*The Existing Market Trap: (a Primer) Escaping The 13 Deadly Sins that Destroy Companies, Careers and Portfolios* — Al Ramadan, Christopher Lochhead, Jason Wellcome, Mary Grice (2025). The prequel to Category Design.

**Central claim:**

> *"Markets are people. Categories are beliefs. A market is just a group of people who share a problem. A category is what they believe can solve it."*

**The Existing Market Trap:** most companies don't fail because they build bad products. They fail because **they don't build belief.** They build something new — and the market compares it to something old. In these existing markets, ~76% of value flows to the category king, leaving competitors fighting over ~24% regardless of product quality.

The book maps **13 Deadly Sins** that keep great companies stuck. (Full list in the book; representative sins include: building "better" not "different"; delegating POV to marketing; language-matching the incumbent; skipping the Lightning Strike; hiring mercenaries; treating the category as marketing; measuring against incumbent KPIs.)

**Escape:** category design — build belief in a new market, not incremental improvement of an old one.

**Diagnostic to run in a session:** *"Are we being compared to something old, and losing that comparison?"* If yes, load the Existing Market Trap framing before any of the Category Design steps.

## 7. The Bolt-on AI Trap (2025)

Lochhead's post-2023 flavor of the Existing Market Trap. Follow Your Different episode 415 (2025):

> *"If your strategy involves simply bolting on an AI assistant or copilot, you're making a pussy move and you're fucked."*

**The trap mechanism:** most AI-era companies are using AI to marginally improve *existing* products. They're calling their innovation an "assistant" or "copilot" or "for [Incumbent]" — languaging that positions them as secondary to the incumbent category. That languaging installs the incumbent as the category and the company as the accessory. Result: the incumbent captures the ~76%, the AI company captures scraps regardless of product quality.

**The escape:** design a new category, name it, take a POV that reframes the incumbent as legacy, run a Lightning Strike. Example Lochhead uses: **Liquid Death — canned water.** They didn't call themselves "premium bottled water" (that's competing in the incumbent's category). They named a new category ("canned water for the punk-rock crowd" — new format, new tribe, new language) and reframed bottled water as boring / legacy.

**For the assistant:** when the user is building an AI product and describing it as an assistant / copilot / AI-powered [Existing Thing], run the Bolt-on AI Trap check.

## 8. AI-era Category Design (2025, still emerging)

Two big themes in Category Pirates 2024–2026 writing:

- **Knowledge worker value is collapsing** — AI is compressing the value of doing routine knowledge work. The only durable position, per Lochhead, is *"at the edge, inventing net new knowledge and value."* Category Design (creating categories, not competing in them) becomes the survival strategy for knowledge-worker firms and individuals.
- **AI Native categories** — many AI Native categories (voice interfaces, AI agents, AI-native CRM, AI code review, AI research infrastructure, etc.) are actively being designed right now. The 2016 book didn't anticipate this — but the method applies cleanly. The specific move is: name the AI-native category on its own terms, not as "AI-powered [old category]."

This material is still emerging on Category Pirates and Lochhead's podcasts. Community PRs to `author-live-sources.md` welcome as it lands.

## 9. Reframing the four legacy laws people used to lean on

Post-book, Category Pirates has publicly refined or rejected several older laws:

- **"Minimum Viable Product"** — Category Pirates: *"Category Design Kills the Minimum Viable Product."* MVP thinking assumes the market pre-exists; category design assumes it doesn't.
- **"First mover advantage"** — Play Bigger already rejected in 2016; Category Pirates now more emphatic. First to *frame the problem* = first to King.
- **"Blue Ocean"** — see §4. Rejected via No Ocean Strategy.
- **"Positioning"** (as commonly taught) — Category Pirates draws a hard line between *positioning* (inside a category, Dunford's turf) and *category design* (creating the category itself). Not a rejection of Dunford — a clarification of scope. See `applications.md`.
