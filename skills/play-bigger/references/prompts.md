# Play Bigger — Prompt templates

> Copy-paste templates the user can adapt. Each starts with a user situation and invokes the skill in a shape the assistant can execute well. All templates assume the assistant applies the fidelity rules from SKILL.md — proper-noun vocabulary, "different not better", CEO-owned POV, case-first teaching, redirect if the problem is actually positioning (Dunford) or narrative (Raskin).

## 1. Design a new category from scratch

```
I want to design a new market category for {{product / company / idea}} using
the Play Bigger / Category Design method.

Context:
- What we've built or are building: {{describe in 3–5 sentences}}
- Stage: {{pre-launch / early revenue / scaling / repositioning}}
- Current market framing: {{how the market categorizes us today — even if we don't like it}}
- Incumbents / adjacent categories: {{who the market compares us to today}}
- Founding insight: {{market insight or technology insight — what did we see that others missed?}}
- Missionary commitment: {{is the CEO ready to be Chief Category Officer for 6–10 years?}}

Please walk me through the Category Design method:
1. Diagnose whether we're in the Existing Market Trap first.
2. Frame the problem — what problem are we actually naming?
3. Name the category (2–3 words) — challenge me if it's more than 3.
4. Sketch the POV: problem → ramifications → future → outcomes. Make it disputable.
5. Identify the Lightning Strike we could plan in 3–6 months — what are we hijacking?
6. Map the ecosystem — analysts, partners, developers, missionaries.
7. Sketch the Data Flywheel for year-5 defensibility.

When my answers sound like "we're better at X" or match an incumbent's language,
push back with the specific Play Bigger anti-pattern.
Cite sources — 2016 book vs. Category Pirates post-book — so I know which era.
```

## 2. Diagnose whether we're in the Existing Market Trap

```
I think we might be stuck in the Existing Market Trap. Here's our situation:

- Product/category framing today: {{how we describe ourselves}}
- Who we lose to and how: {{describe the last 3–5 lost deals}}
- Analyst / media / prospect language about us: {{what words do they use?}}
- Symptoms: {{prospects "get it" but don't feel urgency, sales cycle drags, deals default to
  "same as [Incumbent] but a bit different", etc.}}

Please:
1. Diagnose whether this is the Existing Market Trap (Ramadan + Lochhead, 2025).
2. Name which of the 13 Deadly Sins we're most likely committing.
3. Show me the escape route — what would designing a new category look like from where we are today?
4. Distinguish this from a positioning problem (which would send me to Dunford's method instead).
5. Be blunt. If we're stuck for the reason Lochhead calls "a pussy move" (bolting on AI as an
   assistant / copilot), say so.
```

## 3. Sharpen a POV

```
Here's our current Point of View (or the draft we're working on):

{{paste POV / homepage headline / one-page category story / pitch-deck category slide}}

Please critique this using Play Bigger's method:
1. Does it frame the problem, or does it describe the product? (Product-first = disqualified.)
2. Does it name the ramifications of not acting?
3. Does it present a vision of the future — the "to" side of Frotos?
4. Does it explain the business outcomes of embracing the vision?
5. Is it disputable — could a reasonable person disagree with the framing?
   (If nobody would disagree, it's a slogan, not a POV.)
6. Would this POV fit unchanged on our closest competitor's homepage? (If yes, it's not a POV.)
7. Is the CEO ready to own it and "make it religion"?

Rewrite one version that lands cleanly against Play Bigger's structure. Keep the language
simple, short-sentence, provocative, and emotional — like a movie trailer.
```

## 4. Plan a Lightning Strike

```
We want to plan a Lightning Strike for {{category we're designing}}.

Context:
- Category name (2–3 words): {{name}}
- POV in one paragraph: {{paste}}
- Timeline: {{how many months out are we willing to commit resources for?}}
- What we could hijack: {{upcoming industry conferences, analyst reports, competitor events,
  market moments, seasonal timing}}
- CEO commitment: {{is the CEO ready to commit political capital and not waver?}}
- Ecosystem readiness: {{do we have analysts / partners / customers who'd amplify?}}

Please help me plan the strike using Play Bigger's rules:
1. Content — is what we're planning big, bold, different enough to cut through?
2. Problem-first / product-second — are we evangelizing the problem or the product?
3. Roles — CEO, Strike Master Controller, Strike Leader, Chief Hijacker, Event Planner. Who plays what?
4. What are we hijacking? Name the specific moment / conference / news cycle.
5. What materials do we need to codify?
6. Signals — what will tell us the strike landed (analysts using our category name,
   competitors defending themselves, prospects volunteering the language)?

Push back if this looks like a launch event with catering rather than a real Lightning Strike.
Remind me: "if the CEO wavers or hedges or doesn't commit resources, the strike will fail."
```

## 5. Name a category (the 2–3-word test)

```
I'm trying to name the category for {{our thing}}.

Current candidates:
- {{Candidate 1}}
- {{Candidate 2}}
- {{Candidate 3}}

Context:
- Problem we frame: {{paragraph}}
- Incumbent's category name: {{what the market currently calls it}}
- Frotos: from {{today's state}} to {{future state}}

Please:
1. Test each candidate against Play Bigger / Category Pirates naming criteria:
   - 2–3 words?
   - Owns language the incumbent doesn't?
   - Big enough that other companies could plausibly enter (i.e., not just our product name)?
   - Signals a category, not a feature?
   - Doesn't language-match the incumbent's category?
2. If none of the candidates land, propose 3 alternates using the Languaging principle
   (Snow Leopard — Category Pirates, 2022).
3. Show worked examples of category names that did the work: "home sharing" (Airbnb),
   "on-demand transportation" (Uber), "athleisure" (Lululemon), "canned water"
   (Liquid Death), "Experience Management" (Qualtrics).
```

## 6. Compose Play Bigger with Dunford's positioning

```
I've done Category Design work using Play Bigger — we've named the category, we have a POV,
and we're planning the Lightning Strike. Now I need to position our individual product lines
inside the category we're designing.

Category: {{name}}
POV: {{one paragraph}}
Product lines / SKUs we need to position: {{list}}

Please:
1. Confirm the category is real / accepted enough for positioning work to make sense
   (if the category isn't landing yet, positioning inside it is premature).
2. Switch to April Dunford's positioning method for the individual product lines
   (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value themes → best-fit customer →
   market category / frame of reference).
3. Keep the category-level POV as the parent context.
4. Flag any conflict between Play Bigger's category framing and Dunford's positioning
   framing — they can pull apart if not sequenced carefully.

If you determine that Play Bigger and Dunford together are producing muddled outputs
(different answers to "what category are we in?"), name it and recommend which to prioritize.
```

## 7. Compose Play Bigger with Raskin's Strategic Narrative

```
We've designed the category (Play Bigger). Now we need the sales-narrative artifact — the deck,
the CEO all-hands, the investor pitch story — that renders the category change into a buyer
story.

Category: {{name}}
POV: {{paragraph}}
Frotos: from {{old state}} to {{new state}}
Founding insight: {{what we saw that others missed}}

Please build the 5-part Strategic Narrative (Raskin) on top of the Category Design:
1. Name a Big, Relevant Change in the World → derived from our Frotos "from" side.
2. Show Winners and Losers → who thrives in the new category, who becomes legacy.
3. Tease the Promised Land → derived from our POV's "vision of the future".
4. Introduce Features as Magic Gifts → our product taxonomy, framed as the buyer's tools.
5. Present Evidence → early customer proof, ecosystem signals, category traction.

Match Raskin's voice: case-first, buyer-world-focused, product enters at step 4, enemy is
the old mindset (not competitors). Keep the Play Bigger vocabulary (Category, POV, King,
Missionary) in the narrative where it lands, but don't force it — Raskin's voice takes over
for the narrative artifact.
```

## 8. Escape the Bolt-on AI Trap

```
We're building an AI-era product. I'm worried we're describing it as an "AI assistant" or
"AI copilot" for [existing thing] — which is exactly what Lochhead calls the bolt-on AI trap.

Current framing:
- What we've built: {{describe}}
- How we describe it publicly: {{paste homepage / one-liner / pitch}}
- Incumbent we're being compared to: {{name}}

Please:
1. Diagnose whether we're in the Bolt-on AI Trap (Lochhead, 2025).
2. Show me the languaging move — how is our current framing installing the incumbent as
   the category and us as the accessory?
3. Reframe: what's a new category name that reframes the incumbent as legacy?
4. Contrast with Liquid Death's inverse move — they didn't call themselves "premium bottled
   water"; they named a new category ("canned water") and reframed bottled water as boring.
5. Sketch a POV that puts the incumbent's category on the losing side of the Frotos.
```

## 9. Personal-brand Category of One (Snow Leopard)

```
I want to design a category of one for my personal brand / consulting / writing / creator
work using the Snow Leopard method (Category Pirates, 2022).

Context:
- Who I am: {{background}}
- What I do today: {{current positioning}}
- Who I'm currently compared to: {{competitors / peers}}
- The insight I've got: {{market or technology insight — what do I see that others don't?}}

Please walk me through Snow Leopard + Niche Down (Lochhead 2018):
1. Articulate the problem I solve — reputation for solving a specific problem beats generic "better."
2. Design my three legs: skills / capabilities / category opportunity together.
3. Develop my POV — what I am, what makes me different, why people care.
4. Build my ecosystem — audience, partners, community.
5. Plan my Lightning Strikes — deadlines that trigger action.
6. Apply the Languaging principle from Snow Leopard — what new language should I own?

Push back if my positioning matches an existing category (e.g., "consultant", "coach",
"writer" without a niche) — the whole point of Snow Leopard is category of one.
```

## 10. Critique an existing category strategy

```
Here's our current category strategy / one-pager / investor narrative:

{{paste}}

Please critique it against Play Bigger's method. Specifically:
1. Are we designing a category, or competing inside one? (Play Bigger vs. positioning confusion.)
2. Is the category named in 2–3 words? Is the name doing work?
3. Is the POV disputable? Or is it category-generic?
4. Are we CEO-owning it, or is this a marketing artifact?
5. Is there a Lightning Strike planned? Or are we hoping the category installs itself?
6. Is there an ecosystem strategy?
7. Is there a Data Flywheel design for year-5 defensibility?
8. Are we in the Existing Market Trap or the Bolt-on AI Trap?
9. Which of the 13 Deadly Sins (Existing Market Trap, 2025) are we committing?

Be blunt. Cite whether each critique comes from the 2016 book or from post-book
Category Pirates material (Data Flywheel, 22 Laws, Existing Market Trap).
```
