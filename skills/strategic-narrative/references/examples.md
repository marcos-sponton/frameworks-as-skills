## The Zuora canonical case

**Where Raskin uses it:** [The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen (2016)](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0), [This Is How a CEO Tells a Great Story](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/this-is-how-a-ceo-tells-a-great-story-5d158620cec9), [Lenny 2023](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-power-of-strategic-narrative), Raskin's own podcast ([Tien Tzuo episode](https://andyraskin.com/podcast/10-zuora-ceo-tien-tzuo/)).

**The situation:** Zuora was selling billing/subscription-management software. Standard SaaS pitch: our software does X, Y, Z. Then Tien Tzuo made the *subscription economy* the story.

**The 5 parts applied:**
- **Old game:** buying software (and cars, and consumer products) as one-time purchases. Product-centric business models.
- **Change in the world:** the *subscription economy* — buyers now prefer recurring relationships.
- **Winners and losers:** Fortune 500 mass-extinction chart (companies that failed to adapt) vs. subscription-native winners (Netflix, Salesforce) + rejuvenated incumbents.
- **Promised Land:** *"Turn customers into subscribers."* Concrete criteria — recurring-revenue infrastructure, customer records built around subscribers, subscription analytics.
- **Magic Gifts:** Zuora's customer-record model, order-to-revenue engine, subscription analytics — reframed as *capabilities you need to win the new game.*
- **Evidence:** customer proof of orgs that made the transition.

**Multi-channel:** Tzuo told the same story on Mad Money w/ Cramer, in the sales deck, on the website, in investor pitches, and eventually as a book (*Subscribed*). Same story, six registers.

**Instructive move:** the deck opens with the change (subscription economy), not with the company. Product enters in the second half. Every feature is anchored to a specific obstacle to reaching the Promised Land.

## Salesforce — "End of Software"

**Where Raskin uses it:** [The Making of a Great Strategic Narrative](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-making-of-a-great-sales-narrative-978938b3926) and Lenny 2023.

**The situation:** Salesforce launched into a market dominated by Siebel/Oracle on-prem CRM. Enterprise software = install, integrate, maintain, upgrade.

**The narrative:**
- **Old game:** enterprise software as install-and-maintain. IT owns the pain.
- **Change in the world:** business apps should just work — like consumer web apps do.
- **Promised Land:** business software that behaves like a website — always current, no installs, no upgrade cycles.
- **Magic Gifts:** cloud delivery, subscription pricing, API integrations.
- **Enemy:** the *old mindset* that enterprise software has to be complicated. (Not Siebel specifically; the mindset Siebel represented.)

**Instructive move:** the "No Software" logo (red circle with a diagonal slash through "SOFTWARE") is the reframe rendered as visual identity. The narrative extends into the brand.

## Uberflip — the Blockbuster → Netflix reframe

**Where Raskin uses it:** [The Making of a Great Strategic Narrative](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-making-of-a-great-sales-narrative-978938b3926).

**The situation:** Uberflip was pitching a "content-experience delivery" feature set. Buyers were nodding but not moving. Then they reframed around the *engagement economy* (a shift borrowed from Marketo) with a Blockbuster-to-Netflix analogy.

**The narrative:**
- **Old game:** marketers push content out, hope buyers find it, measure clicks.
- **Change in the world:** buyers now expect content experiences that adapt to them (Netflix-style), not one-size-fits-all catalogs (Blockbuster-style).
- **Promised Land:** *"Own the journey."* Actively guide buyers through pipeline with curated content experiences.
- **Magic Gifts:** Uberflip's content hub, personalization, sales-enablement content packs.
- **Evidence:** customer proof of orgs seeing engagement lift.

**Instructive quotable moment:**
> "Holy shit, I need this." — Uberflip prospect Mike Latty, hearing the reframe for the first time.

That reaction is the pattern-response Raskin looks for. The features hadn't changed; the frame had.

## Gong — "Goodbye opinions. Hello reality."

**Where Raskin uses it:** Lenny 2023, various LinkedIn posts.

**The situation:** Gong sells revenue-intelligence software — records sales calls, analyzes conversations, surfaces patterns. Standard pitch: features. Reframe: revenue leadership based on data, not opinions.

**The narrative:**
- **Old game:** revenue leaders make decisions based on gut, VP intuition, forecast opinion, war-room debate.
- **Change in the world:** conversation data is now capturable and analyzable at scale. Reality can replace opinion.
- **Promised Land:** a revenue org where decisions are grounded in what customers actually said, not what people think they said.
- **Enemy:** the *old mindset* of opinion-based revenue leadership. (Not Chorus, not ExecVision. The mindset those competitors also represent.)
- **Magic Gifts:** Gong's call recording, deal intelligence, deal boards.

**Instructive move:** the "Goodbye opinions. Hello reality." tagline compresses the whole narrative into two beats. It's the reframe-as-headline pattern.

## SpotMe — discovery-first sales enablement

**Where Raskin uses it:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

**The situation:** SpotMe sold event technology. New deck used the *change in in-person + digital engagement* as opener. But the more important move was operational — reps used the change slide as a discovery opener.

**The move:**
- Rep opens the pitch by naming the shift in event/engagement.
- Rep pauses and asks: *"How is this shift playing out for you?"*
- Prospect voluntarily opens up about their world — the challenges they're facing with hybrid events, engagement metrics, attendee experience.
- Rep now has "gold" customer language — used to close this deal AND to refine the narrative for the next one.

**Instructive move:** the change slide isn't just the opening of the pitch. It's a *discovery tool*. This is how narrative work becomes sales enablement.

## Zaius (now Optimizely Data Platform) — the rollout case

**Where Raskin uses it:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

**The situation:** CDP/marketing-tech. New deck reframed around a shift from *campaign-centric* to *customer-centric* marketing. But the deck was landing poorly with sales.

**The critical move:** Raskin brought sales VP Steve Angoff into the creation room. Deck went from 2/5 to 4.5/5 in Angoff's rating.

**Instructive move:** the narrative work is CEO-owned but sales must be co-authors, not the audience of a rollout. Adoption is a lagging indicator of inclusion. If sales wasn't in the room, don't expect the deck to be used.

## SkySlope — real-estate transaction management

**Where Raskin uses it:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

**The situation:** SkySlope was reframing around a shift in broker economics. When it came time to unveil the new narrative internally, the CEO didn't just present — she brought a broker (a board member) into the room to validate that the Promised Land felt real to the buyer.

**Instructive move:** validate the Promised Land with an actual buyer *inside the internal unveil*. If the buyer nods, the org believes the narrative more.

## Foxglove — game vs. way (robotics)

**Where Raskin uses it:** [LinkedIn: Old Game vs. Old Way (2023)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_sometimes-people-boil-down-my-strategic-narrative-activity-7131327379045576704-YwbE).

**The situation:** Foxglove sells robotics debug tooling. Robotics is shifting from lab POCs to production deployment at scale. Their initial framing was Old Way ("debugging robotics by looking at logs") / New Way ("modern visualization tools").

**The reframe:** Old Game ("robotics lives in the lab, POCs are enough") / New Game ("robotics lives in production, scale is the real hurdle"). Foxglove is the tooling for the New Game.

**Instructive move:** Old Way / New Way pushed toward "our tool vs. their tool." Old Game / New Game pushed toward "the market has shifted; robots need to run in production now; here are the tools you need."

## Turing — narrative refresh across pivots

**Where Raskin uses it:** referenced in Story Rules recap and Lenny 2023 as an example of narrative evolution as strategy evolves.

**The situation:** Turing pivoted its business multiple times (from Jobs marketplace → developer talent → AI-adjacent) and each pivot required refreshing the narrative.

**Instructive move:** narrative is not static. When the strategy shifts (or the market shifts under the strategy), the narrative refreshes. Turing's iterative work is the counter-example to treating a narrative as a one-time output.

## 360Learning — collaborative learning

**Where Raskin uses it:** referenced in Lenny 2023 (CEO Nick Hernandez).

**The situation:** LMS market. Shift from top-down corporate training to collaborative learning (peers teaching peers).

**The narrative move:** enemy = the *old mindset* that L&D is centralized experts producing content for passive employees. Promised Land = orgs where the best learning comes from peers and the LMS surfaces that.

**Instructive move:** the enemy is a mindset in the L&D discipline, not a competitor LMS. Movement, not category.

## OneTrust — privacy as strategic imperative

**Where Raskin uses it:** [nektar summary](https://nektar.ai/the-strategic-narrative-playbook-lessons-on-how-stories-align-companies-and-win-markets/).

**The situation:** OneTrust sells privacy/compliance tooling. Standard pitch: compliance checkbox. Reframe: privacy as strategic imperative.

**The narrative:**
- **Old game:** privacy = compliance checkbox. Legal handles it. Cost center.
- **Change in the world:** trust is now a strategic differentiator; regulators multiplied; enterprise buyers demand it.
- **Promised Land:** trust infrastructure that's a strategic asset, not a compliance cost.
- **Magic Gifts:** OneTrust's privacy management, third-party risk, ethics/compliance modules.

**Instructive move:** repositioning a compliance product as strategic infrastructure via a shift in how buyers should *think about* privacy.

## Slack — work OS

**Where Raskin uses it:** [nektar summary](https://nektar.ai/the-strategic-narrative-playbook-lessons-on-how-stories-align-companies-and-win-markets/).

**The situation:** Slack could have been positioned as "chat for teams." Instead, it was positioned as a *work OS* — work happens *in* Slack, not around it.

**The narrative:**
- **Old game:** work happens in email, docs, apps; chat is a side conversation.
- **Change in the world:** work is now conversational and integrated; the chat surface IS the work surface.
- **Promised Land:** an org where work happens *in* Slack — apps, integrations, notifications, decisions all flow through it.

**Instructive move:** category matters less than movement. "Chat" is a category; "work happens in Slack" is a movement.

## The recurring reframing pattern

Across every case above, the same pattern:

> The client was pitching features → Raskin makes them name a shift the buyer already feels → the deck opens with that shift, not the company → prospects react with *"I need this"* instead of *"nice to have."*

This is the Uberflip Mike Latty *"Holy shit, I need this"* pattern, and it's what Raskin's method is optimizing for.
