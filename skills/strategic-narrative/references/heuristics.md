# Strategic Narrative — Heuristics, Do's, Don'ts, Gotchas

> The practical devices — the tests Raskin has explicitly named — that separate a strategic narrative from features-first pitching dressed as story. Attribution is precise: this comes from the 2016 essay, this from a 2023 LinkedIn post, this from the Lenny episode.

## Signs your company doesn't have a strategic narrative

Composite from the Lenny 2023 episode + the [foundercoho interview](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys) + the [nektar summary](https://nektar.ai/the-strategic-narrative-playbook-lessons-on-how-stories-align-companies-and-win-markets/):

- Sales, marketing, and product each describe the company differently.
- The pitch opens with the company, its logos, its investors, or "we are the leader in X" — not with a change in the buyer's world.
- Prospects say "cool tool" but don't feel urgency.
- Reps rely on their own custom decks — the "official" deck sits unused.
- The CEO can't tell the story in <2 minutes.
- Every new hire re-writes the pitch.
- A long messaging doc exists; nobody uses it. *"Nobody uses them."*
- The word "problem" appears more than the word "world."
- The investor deck and the sales deck tell different stories.
- Recruiting materials sound like they're for a different company than the sales materials.

If more than three of these are true, the org doesn't have a strategic narrative — it has a features-first pitch with story decoration bolted on. Diagnose before you rewrite.

## Do's

### Open with the change in the buyer's world

Not the company. Not the logos. Not the investors. The undeniable shift creating stakes for the buyer.

**How to apply:** the first slide of the deck names the change. The first paragraph of the website names the change. The first sentence of the all-hands names the change. Same change everywhere.

**Author's words:**
> "What attracts human attention is change." — Robert McKee, quoted in [The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0)

### Harvest customer language for the change

Don't invent the shift from a whiteboard. Invent it from customer calls.

**How to apply:** use the change slide as a discovery-opener with prospects: *"How is this shift playing out for you?"* Their words become the language of the narrative. The SpotMe pattern.

**Source:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

### Test the Promised Land against buyer-world criteria

If the Promised Land reads like your feature set, you've smuggled the product in early.

**How to apply:** for each element of the Promised Land, ask: *"Could a competitor also help the buyer reach this?"* If yes, the Promised Land is honest — it's about the buyer's world. If no, you've defined the Promised Land as your product, not as the buyer's aspiration.

### Reframe every feature as a Magic Gift

For each feature that makes the deck, complete the sentence: *"This is the tool the buyer needs to overcome [specific obstacle to the Promised Land]."* If you can't complete it, cut the feature.

**Test:** when a rep demos, do they explain features as *the buyer's tools for crossing* or as *our capabilities*? First is Raskin. Second is chest-beating.

### Involve sales leadership in creating the narrative

Handing sales a new deck without inclusion → adoption fails. Raskin cites the Zaius / Steve Angoff example: rating went from 2/5 to 4.5/5 only after the sales VP was in the creation room.

**How to apply:** the sales leader is a co-author, not a rollout audience.

**Source:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

### Refresh the narrative on a market shift

Narratives age. If the change you named is now common wisdom, the urgency is gone.

**How to apply:** re-test the narrative every 18–36 months. Turing's multiple pivots are Raskin's example of narrative evolving as strategy evolves.

## Don'ts

### Don't lead with logos, investors, or "we are the leader in…"

Chest-beater opening. Prospect files you under "vendor pitching me."

**How to redirect:** cut the first three slides and rewrite the first slide as "here's the shift happening in your world."

### Don't use "old way / new way" framing

Sounds close, works differently.

**Author's words:**
> "'Old way/new way' encourages you to talk primarily about *product*. 'Old game/new game'…encourages you to build the narrative around a shift taking place in the *buyer's head*."
> — [LinkedIn, Nov 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_sometimes-people-boil-down-my-strategic-narrative-activity-7131327379045576704-YwbE)

**Old Way / New Way** — invites feature comparison. "Their old thing did X; our new thing does Y."
**Old Game / New Game** — invites mindset shift. "Buyers used to think X; now they think Y." Product enters later.

### Don't name a competitor as the enemy

Guarantees you look petty and same-shaped.

**Author's words:**
> "Your buyer's enemy is not competitors. It's not even a problem the buyer faces, at least in the traditional sense. Rather…your buyer's enemy is an *old mindset* that has become a road to ruin."
> — [LinkedIn, Oct 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_one-part-of-my-strategic-narrative-framework-activity-7126247639502782464-LtXQ)

**How to redirect:** name the mindset the competitor represents, not the competitor. Gong doesn't say "beat Chorus." Gong says *"Goodbye opinions. Hello reality."* The enemy is opinion-based revenue leadership.

### Don't confuse setup with education

Dumping industry stats to prove the change is real → the buyer disengages before the emotional hook lands.

**Author's words:**
> "Your primary goal in setting context is not educating prospects, but engaging them emotionally."
> — [Is the "Greatest Sales Deck" Great for Savvy Prospects?](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/is-the-greatest-sales-deck-great-for-savvy-prospects-d5de48fb2f94)

**How to redirect:** cut the industry data. Keep the sentence that makes the buyer feel the shift.

### Don't make the Promised Land your feature set

Most common misapplication. The Promised Land = what the buyer wants to be able to say/do; NOT what your product does.

**How to redirect:** rewrite the Promised Land using only buyer-world nouns. If your product name appears, restart.

### Don't introduce features before the Promised Land

Reflex among founders and product marketers. Kills the narrative arc.

**How to redirect:** whatever your deck's slide 2 currently is, ask: is the Promised Land already established? If not, you're too early.

### Don't delegate narrative to marketing or an agency

Fatal to CEO ownership.

**Author's words:**
> "The CEO has to be the decider… this story really is the strategy."
> — Lenny 2023

**How to redirect:** CEO in the room, or don't run the exercise.

### Don't bolt story onto an existing feature pitch

Not additive. Requires rebuild.

**How to redirect:** treat the current deck as scrap. The 5-part narrative is one story, not a wrapper around a features-first deck.

### Don't be complete — be provocative

**Author's words:**
> "The most powerful narratives are always 'wrong,' in that they've sacrificed completeness for brevity."
> — [dylan.tweney interview](https://dylan.tweney.com/the-art-of-strategic-narrative-a-conversation-with-andy-raskin-2/)

**How to redirect:** if a smart critic wouldn't push back on any part of your narrative, it's a slogan. Sharpen until it's *disputable*.

## Gotchas (things that go wrong even when you think you're doing it right)

### The "we named a change but nobody feels it" trap

Common when the change is named on a whiteboard, not extracted from customer calls.

**Test:** show the change slide to five prospects cold. If four out of five say "yes, we're feeling that," you're on it. If three say "not really" — you named a fake change.

### The Promised Land as feature list in disguise

Wording sounds buyer-world; the underlying nouns are your product's.

**Test:** strip every product-adjacent noun from the Promised Land statement. Does it still describe the buyer's aspiration? If it collapses, it was product-defined.

### The "old way / new way" reflex

Teams read the framework and immediately draw a 2x2 with old-way features on the left and new-way features on the right. Product comparison, not narrative.

**Test:** does the "new" side of your framing name a shift *in how the buyer thinks*? Or does it name a shift *in features available*? The former is Old Game / New Game. The latter is Old Way / New Way.

### The competitor-as-enemy reflex

Team can't stop naming the biggest competitor.

**Test:** replace the competitor's name with "the mindset that _____." If the sentence still makes sense, you had the mindset all along. If it doesn't, you were doing chest-beating, not narrative.

### Setup that educates instead of engaging

Team defends every data point. "But this stat is important." Doesn't matter — the buyer isn't in learning mode; they're in "does this matter to me?" mode.

**Test:** read the setup out loud. Does it feel like a documentary or a wake-up call? Only the second is doing narrative work.

### The bolted-on-story trap

Team keeps the old feature-first deck and adds a "story slide" at the front.

**Test:** does the whole deck read as one story from start to finish? Or does the story disappear once features arrive? Second = bolted on.

### The "we handed sales the new deck and adoption is 0%" trap

Sales wasn't in the creation room.

**Fix:** Zaius / Angoff example — restart with sales leadership co-authoring. Adoption is a lagging indicator of inclusion.

## Tests for whether the narrative is working

Six tests Raskin has cited:

1. **The pattern-response.** Prospects respond with *"How quickly can we get started?"* — not "let me think about it." ([How Great Sales Pitches Drive Urgency](https://medium.com/the-mission/how-great-sales-pitches-drive-urgency-e35aa1069de3))
2. **The discovery-opener works.** The rep names the change and the prospect *voluntarily* opens up about their world. (SpotMe pattern.)
3. **The champion can pitch internally.** The deck gives them a story they can retell to their CEO without your rep in the room.
4. **The same-story test.** Sales, product, and CS all describe the company the same way in under 30 seconds.
5. **The movement test.** Investors, hires, and customers all recognize the same "movement" — one story, four audiences.
6. **Disputable purpose.** A reasonable person could disagree with your framing. If nobody would disagree, you have a slogan, not a movement. (From the Gong "Goodbye opinions, hello reality" post cluster.)

## Anti-patterns Raskin explicitly names

### The Arrogant Doctor

"You have a problem. I have the cure. Here's why mine is better."

**Why it fails:** the buyer hasn't accepted they have your definition of the problem. You've skipped straight to the sell without earning the diagnosis.

**How to redirect:** be a **Humble Awakener** — someone who names a change that already happened in the buyer's world. Not "you have a problem," but "the world has changed, and here's what it means."

**Source:** [foundercoho interview](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys).

### The Chest Beater

Pitch that centers the company: our vision, our history, our funding, our leadership, our category leadership.

**Why it fails:** the buyer's world doesn't appear. Nothing to attach to emotionally.

**How to redirect:** rewrite everything to be about the buyer's world. Your company appears at step 4 as the source of Magic Gifts, not as the protagonist.

### The features-first pitch with story decoration

"Here are our 12 features. Also, here's a customer anecdote."

**Why it fails:** the anecdote doesn't fix the frame. The frame is still features-first.

**How to redirect:** treat the current deck as scrap. Rebuild around the 5 parts.

### The completeness trap

"Let's make sure we cover everything about what we do."

**Why it fails:** *"The most powerful narratives are always 'wrong,' in that they've sacrificed completeness for brevity."*

**How to redirect:** cut ruthlessly. The narrative sacrifices completeness for emotional force.

## Common misapplications (people saying they're doing strategic narrative but aren't)

### Writing better copy without changing the frame

The messaging gets crisper. The frame is still chest-beater. Not narrative work.

### Treating positioning as narrative

A well-worked April Dunford positioning brief isn't a narrative. It's the input to a narrative. Both matter; they're different.

### Copying Zuora's deck slide-for-slide

*"Harvest language about what's at stake directly from customers."* The template is not the method.

### Naming a change nobody feels

Whiteboard-invented shifts don't land. The change must be one the buyer already senses.

### Stopping at the sales deck

If the website, all-hands, investor pitch, and recruiting story don't tell the same story, the narrative isn't the strategy — it's just a deck.

## Language and vocabulary — say this, not that

Small phrasing shifts Raskin has made explicit since 2016:

| Instead of | Use | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Old Way / New Way | Old Game / New Game | Game invites mindset shift; way invites feature comparison |
| The enemy is [competitor] | The enemy is [the old mindset] | Competitor = petty; mindset = movement |
| Category | Movement | Category is a taxonomy slot; movement is people moving together |
| Value proposition | Promised Land | Value prop is a claim; Promised Land is a destination the buyer wants |
| Feature | Magic Gift | Feature is a spec; Magic Gift is the buyer's tool for crossing |
| "We help X do Y" | "The world has changed. Here's what it means for you." | The first is chest-beating; the second earns attention |
| "We are the leader in [category]" | Name the shift, not your rank | Rank isn't a story |
| Storytelling | Strategic narrative | Storytelling ≠ narrative; narrative ≠ decoration |
| Messaging document | Sales deck as true north | Messaging docs *"nobody uses"* |
| Educating the prospect | Emotionally engaging the prospect | Setup is not for information; it's for stakes |
