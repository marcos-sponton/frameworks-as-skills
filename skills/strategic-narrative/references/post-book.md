# Strategic Narrative — Refinements since the 2016 essay

> **This is the differential of this skill.** The 2016 essay [*The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen*](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0) laid down the 5-part framework. Since then, Andy Raskin has published:
> - ~10 canonical follow-up essays in *Firm Narrative* and *Mission.org* Medium publications
> - Multiple LinkedIn micro-essays per week clarifying specific pieces of the method
> - The Pavilion 2023 talk *Goodbye Product. Hello Movement.* (and the essay of the same name)
> - Podcast appearances including Lenny Rachitsky (2023) — the most current articulation of the framework
>
> Raskin has **no book** and **no Substack**. His refinements are scattered across the sources above. Most Claude responses about "the greatest sales deck" pull from the 2016 essay alone and miss almost a decade of refinement. This file captures the material that has updated, clarified, or reversed pieces of the original method.

## Refinements and reversals

### From "Old Way / New Way" to "Old Game / New Game" (2023)

The 2016 essay didn't yet contain this framing. Post-2016, teams started boiling the framework down to "old way vs. new way," which pushed them toward product-comparison thinking. Raskin corrected explicitly:

**Author's words:**
> "Sometimes people boil down my strategic narrative framework as 'old way / new way'… 'Old way/new way' encourages you to talk primarily about *product*. 'Old game/new game'…encourages you to build the narrative around a shift taking place in the *buyer's head*."
> — [LinkedIn, Nov 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_sometimes-people-boil-down-my-strategic-narrative-activity-7131327379045576704-YwbE)

The distinction:
- **Old Way / New Way** — invites feature comparison ("their old thing did X; our new thing does Y").
- **Old Game / New Game** — invites mindset shift ("buyers used to think X; now they think Y"). Product enters later.

Use "Old Game / New Game" in the skill's outputs. If the user reaches for "Old Way / New Way," redirect explicitly with attribution.

### "Name the Enemy" clarified (2023)

The 2016 essay implied the enemy was the status quo or competitors. Practitioners over-read this as "name your competitor as the villain." Raskin corrected:

**Author's words:**
> "One part of my strategic narrative framework that's often misunderstood: your buyer's enemy is not competitors. It's not even a problem the buyer faces, at least in the traditional sense. Rather…your buyer's enemy is an *old mindset* that has become a road to ruin."
> — [LinkedIn, Oct 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_one-part-of-my-strategic-narrative-framework-activity-7126247639502782464-LtXQ)

The enemy is:
- Not a competitor (that reads petty and same-shaped).
- Not a specific customer problem (too small — a job for troubleshooting, not narrative).
- **An old mindset** — a way of thinking that used to work and no longer does.

Gong's *"Goodbye opinions. Hello reality."* is the archetypal example. The enemy is opinion-based revenue leadership, not Chorus or ExecVision.

### "Goodbye Product. Hello Movement." (2023)

The 2016 essay put "product enters at step 4" but didn't yet name the reframe as **movement**. The 2023 Pavilion talk and companion essay do:

**Author's words:**
> "'Goodbye product' does not mean your product is unimportant. It does not mean you don't talk about features, speeds and feeds. It means don't start there."
> — [Goodbye Product. Hello Movement.](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/goodbye-product-hello-movement-b06df08e02b8)

The move: instead of positioning the company as *in a category* ("we are the leader in X SaaS"), position it as *leading a movement* — a group of buyers, employees, investors all moving from an old mindset to a new one. Movement > category.

Related LinkedIn post: *"[Goodbye category. Hello movement.](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_goodbye-category-hello-movement-got-this-activity-6838485447627436032-3-v8)"*

Category = a taxonomy slot. Movement = people moving together. The latter is what you want.

### "Arrogant Doctor" → "Humble Awakener"

Raskin has named two personas explicitly. The **Arrogant Doctor** is the anti-persona; the **Humble Awakener** is the prescribed one.

- **Arrogant Doctor:** "You have a problem, I have the cure, here's why mine is better."
  - Skips the diagnosis-earning step. Buyer hasn't accepted the problem framing.
- **Humble Awakener:** "The world has changed. Here's what that means for you."
  - Earns the diagnosis by naming the shift the buyer already senses.

**Source:** [foundercoho interview](https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/the-strategist-behind-silicon-valleys).

Use this framing when the user's pitch feels like they're prescribing without earning. Rename what they're doing (Arrogant Doctor) and prescribe the alternative (Humble Awakener).

### Setup is for emotional engagement, not education (post-2016 clarification)

The 2016 essay talked about "naming a change in the world" but didn't make explicit that the setup's purpose is emotional, not informational. Post-2016 essay clarified:

**Author's words:**
> "Your primary goal in setting context is not educating prospects, but engaging them emotionally."
> — [Is the "Greatest Sales Deck" Great for Savvy Prospects?](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/is-the-greatest-sales-deck-great-for-savvy-prospects-d5de48fb2f94)

This matters because founders default to educating (dumping industry stats to prove the change is real). Educating is the wrong job. The job is: make the buyer *feel* that something has shifted and staying put is dangerous.

### The "Chest Beater" (post-2016 named anti-persona)

Third named persona, alongside Arrogant Doctor and Humble Awakener. The **Chest Beater** = self-promoting CEO pitch style — company-centric, feature-centric, history-and-funding-centric.

Sinek's Why-first pitch, when applied badly, reads as Chest Beater in Raskin's frame. The Golden Circle centers the company's origin; Raskin's setup centers the buyer's world.

## New material developed after the 2016 essay

### The "disputable purpose" test

For movement-vs.-slogan discrimination. From the Gong *"Goodbye opinions. Hello reality."* post cluster.

A slogan is something everyone agrees with (harmless, unmemorable). A movement's purpose is *disputable* — a reasonable person could push back on it. That disputability is what makes it a movement instead of a slogan.

**Test:** could a smart critic argue with your framing? If no, sharpen it. If yes, you have a purpose worth building a movement around.

### The multi-channel expansion (Zuora canonical extension)

The 2016 essay focused on the sales deck. Follow-up essays have expanded the frame to *"strategic narrative shows up everywhere the org communicates."* From [This Is How a CEO Tells a Great Story](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/this-is-how-a-ceo-tells-a-great-story-5d158620cec9):

Tien Tzuo told the *subscription economy* story on:
- The Zuora sales deck (canonical)
- The Zuora website
- CNBC's Mad Money with Jim Cramer (as CEO, on live TV)
- Investor pitches (same 5 acts)
- Recruiting materials
- Eventually, a book: *Subscribed*

Same story, six registers. This is what "strategic narrative is the strategy" looks like in practice.

### "Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales" — the rollout method

Post-2016 addition. The 2016 essay didn't address rollout; the practice essay did.

**Key move:** sales leadership must be co-authors of the narrative, not the audience of the rollout. Raskin cites the Zaius / Steve Angoff example — deck rating went from 2/5 to 4.5/5 only after Angoff was in the creation room.

**Discovery-opener as sales-enablement device:** the change slide isn't just the opening of the pitch. It's a discovery tool. Reps use it to ask *"How is this shift playing out for you?"* — generating "gold" customer language that in turn refines the narrative. SpotMe pattern.

**Anti-pattern:** handing sales a new deck without their involvement guarantees zero adoption. Adoption is a lagging indicator of inclusion.

**Source:** [Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/selling-your-new-sales-deck-to-sales-a7dc032a57bb).

### "Air cover" — org-wide reinforcement

Post-2016 vocabulary. Sales rolling out a new narrative alone will fail. Marketing, CS, product, and CEO must all reinforce the same story in their channels. That reinforcement is *air cover* — sales isn't left holding the story alone.

### "Life or death stakes" as the register for step 2

The 2016 essay named "winners and losers." Post-2016 sharpening: the emotional register should be *life or death*. If the stakes feel merely inconvenient, loss aversion doesn't fire. Zuora's Fortune 500 extinction chart works because "extinction" is life-or-death vocabulary, not "underperformance."

## Movement over category (the 2023 through-line)

Multiple post-2016 essays converge on the same point: **stop trying to create a category; lead a movement.**

- **Category creation** = defining a new market slot and claiming leadership of it. Analyst-oriented, taxonomy-oriented.
- **Movement leadership** = defining a shift in how buyers think, and getting them to move with you.

**Author's words:**
> "Goodbye category. Hello movement." — [LinkedIn, 2021](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_goodbye-category-hello-movement-got-this-activity-6838485447627436032-3-v8)

The essay [To Create a New Category, Name the New Game](https://medium.com/firm-narrative/to-create-a-new-category-name-the-new-game-70c2e55edc2e) resolves the tension: even category creation requires naming the *game*, not the category itself. Category is downstream of the movement, not the object of the pitch.

## Frameworks/framings Raskin has explicitly pushed back on

- **"We help X do Y"** — features-first, no shift.
- **"We are the leader in [category]"** — chest-beater, no stakes.
- **"Our value prop is…"** — treats value prop as narrative; they aren't the same thing.
- **"Old way / new way"** — encourages product-comparison thinking.
- **Naming the competitor as the enemy** — the enemy is the *old mindset* the competitor represents, not the competitor.
- **Long messaging documents** — *"nobody uses them."*
- **Storytelling-as-decoration** on claims of superiority — the point isn't to make your claim sound nicer, it's to change what the buyer thinks the story is about.
- **Setup as education** — the setup's job is emotional engagement, not information transfer.
