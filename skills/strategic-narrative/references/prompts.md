# Strategic Narrative — Prompt templates

> Copy-paste templates users can adapt. Each starts with a user situation and invokes the skill in a shape Claude can execute well.

## Build a 5-part strategic narrative from scratch

```
I want to build a strategic narrative for {{my company / product}}.

Context:
- What we do: {{describe in 2-3 sentences}}
- Who we sell to: {{ICP — role + company profile}}
- Current pitch opens with: {{first 30 seconds of your usual pitch}}
- The prospects who bite fastest tend to say: {{typical positive reactions}}
- The prospects who go cold tend to say: {{typical objections or crickets}}

Please build the 5-part strategic narrative using Andy Raskin's method.

Walk me through:
1. Name the change in the world (buyer-world, not industry stats)
2. Winners and losers (life-or-death register, not inconvenience)
3. The Promised Land (buyer-defined aspiration, not our feature set)
4. Magic Gifts (features as buyer's tools for crossing)
5. Evidence (customer stories that reprise the arc)

When I default to features-first or "we help X do Y" or naming a competitor as the enemy, push back explicitly and reframe using Raskin's vocabulary.
Anchor recommendations in a case (Zuora, Uberflip, Gong, SpotMe, etc.).
```

## Critique a sales deck I already have

```
Here's our current sales deck (or the outline of it):

{{paste content or attach}}

Please critique it against Andy Raskin's 5-part strategic narrative. Specifically:

1. Does the deck open with a change in the buyer's world, or with the company / logos / investors?
2. Are the stakes life-or-death, or merely inconvenient?
3. Is the Promised Land buyer-defined, or is it secretly our feature set?
4. Do the features read as Magic Gifts (buyer's tools for crossing) or as our capabilities?
5. Does the Evidence step reprise the arc, or is it a logo wall?
6. Which anti-patterns is this closest to: Chest Beater, Arrogant Doctor, features-first-with-story-decoration?
7. What's the single strongest move worth doubling down on?

Reference specific Raskin essays or LinkedIn posts when relevant.
```

## Name the change in the buyer's world

```
I'm stuck on step 1 of Raskin's 5-part narrative — naming the change in the buyer's world.

Context:
- What we do: {{describe}}
- Three quotes from recent customer/prospect calls where they described what's different about their world:
  1. {{quote}}
  2. {{quote}}
  3. {{quote}}

Help me extract the *shift* those quotes reveal. Push me toward:
- Old Game / New Game framing (not Old Way / New Way)
- A shift the buyer already senses (not one we invented on a whiteboard)
- Emotional register that fires loss aversion (not industry stats)

Give me 2-3 candidate framings and press me on which one is disputable enough to be a movement, not a slogan.
```

## Define the Promised Land

```
Help me define the Promised Land for our narrative.

Context:
- Our product: {{describe}}
- The change we've named in step 1: {{paste}}
- Our current attempt at the Promised Land: {{paste}}

Push me on:
1. Is this buyer-defined or product-defined? Would a competitor's product also help the buyer reach it?
2. Is it concrete enough that a buyer could imagine themselves there, or is it abstract vision-speak?
3. Does it use buyer-world nouns only? (Strip every product-adjacent noun and see if it still stands.)
4. Is it a destination worth crossing to, or a nice-to-have?

Reference the Zuora "Turn customers into subscribers" example or another case that mirrors mine.
```

## Reframe our features as Magic Gifts

```
Help me reframe our features as Magic Gifts.

Context:
- Our Promised Land: {{paste}}
- Our top 5-8 features: {{list}}

For each feature, complete this sentence: "This is the tool the buyer needs to overcome [specific obstacle to the Promised Land]."

If I can't complete the sentence, tell me the feature doesn't belong in the deck.
For each Magic Gift, help me phrase it as *buyer's capability*, not *our feature*.
```

## Translate the narrative into sales enablement

```
We have a 5-part strategic narrative built (see below). Now help me translate it into sales enablement so reps actually use it.

Our narrative:
{{paste 5-part outline}}

Push me on:
1. How does the change slide become a discovery opener? (SpotMe pattern — "How is this shift playing out for you?")
2. What does the champion need in the deck so they can retell the story internally without us in the room?
3. What air-cover assets do we need (website, one-pagers, ROI calculators) that tell the SAME story so sales isn't alone?
4. How do we roll this out to sales without triggering the Zaius / Angoff adoption failure? (Who needs to be co-authors, not audience?)

Reference Raskin's rollout essay ("Selling Your New Sales Deck to Sales").
```

## Refresh the narrative for a market shift

```
Our current strategic narrative is 2-3 years old. The market has shifted — the change we named is now common wisdom, and prospects nod through the setup without feeling urgency.

Current narrative:
{{paste 5-part outline}}

What's happening in our market now:
{{describe the shift that's happened since we wrote this}}

Help me refresh the narrative:
1. What's the NEW change in the world that our current narrative doesn't yet name?
2. Does the Promised Land still hold, or has it evolved?
3. Are the Magic Gifts still positioned against the right obstacles?
4. What Evidence do we need now that we didn't need before?

Reference Turing's iterative narrative work if the shift is a full pivot.
```

## Diagnose whether we have a strategic narrative at all

```
Help me diagnose whether we actually have a strategic narrative, or just a pitch deck with story decoration.

Signals from my org:
- Sales, marketing, and product each describe the company differently: {{yes / no + evidence}}
- The pitch opens with: {{what actually shows up on slide 1}}
- Prospects typically respond with: {{the actual pattern-response we get}}
- Reps rely on their own custom decks: {{yes / no}}
- CEO can tell the story in <2 minutes: {{yes / no}}
- A long messaging doc exists that nobody uses: {{yes / no}}

Apply Raskin's diagnostic tests. If we don't have a narrative, tell me the biggest gap to close first.
```

## Design a movement, not a category

```
We're being pushed to "create a category" for what we do. Help me think through whether we should design a movement instead, in Raskin's frame.

Context:
- What we do: {{describe}}
- The category we could try to create: {{name}}
- The shift in the buyer's world we could name: {{describe}}

Apply Raskin's Goodbye Category / Hello Movement move:
1. What's the difference between calling this a new category vs. naming a movement?
2. Is our "purpose" disputable — could a smart critic argue with it? (Movement test.)
3. Reference the *To Create a New Category, Name the New Game* essay — if we're going to create a category, do it via naming the game.
4. What would the movement look like across sales, hires, investors — one story, three audiences?
```

## Compare strategic narrative vs. an adjacent framework

```
I'm deciding whether to work through Raskin's strategic narrative or {{other framework — April Dunford positioning, Roger Martin Playing to Win, Rumelt kernel, Marty Cagan product vision, StoryBrand}} for {{my situation}}.

Help me pick. If the answer is "use both, in this sequence", tell me the sequence.
If the answer is "neither, use something else entirely", tell me that too.

Reference Raskin's own compatibility comments where he's made them.
```

## Push back on a Chest-Beater draft

```
Here's a draft narrative / deck opening I'm not happy with — I think it's Chest Beater but I need help articulating why:

{{paste}}

Diagnose using Raskin's frame:
1. Where does the company appear vs. where does the buyer's world appear?
2. Is this Arrogant Doctor (prescribing before earning the diagnosis) or Chest Beater (centering the company)?
3. What's the specific first move to fix it? (Where does slide 1 need to start?)
4. Reference the Raskin quote(s) that name this anti-pattern.
```
