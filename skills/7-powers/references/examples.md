## Helmer's own cases (from the 2016 book and interviews)

### Netflix — Counter-Positioning → Scale Economies → Branding

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book), Acquired 2020, Lenny 2024. His most-used single case.

**The situation:** Netflix started as DVD-by-mail, then transitioned to streaming, then invested in originals.

**How the cascade of Powers applied:**
- **Origination (~2007–2012):** *Counter-Positioning* vs. Blockbuster. Streaming cannibalized Blockbuster's retail rentals + late fees; Blockbuster's economically rational response was to preserve their high-margin business as long as possible. Collateral damage was severe enough that Blockbuster went through denial → ridicule → fear → anger → capitulation.
- **Takeoff (~2013–2020):** *Scale Economies* on content spend. Investing billions in originals only works if you can amortize across a ballooning global subscriber base. Smaller rivals face prohibitive share-gain cost to match Netflix's per-subscriber content investment.
- **Stability (2020s):** *Branding* solidifies — Netflix becomes shorthand for streaming.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows Powers compounding across the three stages. Also shows Counter-Positioning as a *bootstrap* Power that gives the challenger space to build the durable Powers (Scale, Branding) during Takeoff and Stability.

### Vanguard vs. Fidelity — canonical Counter-Positioning

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** Jack Bogle's index-fund model cannibalized active-management fees, which were Fidelity's core revenue.

**Why the case is instructive:** Fidelity had every capability to launch index funds. They wouldn't — because doing so would cannibalize the active-management business that paid the bills. Classic Counter-Positioning: incumbent constrained by their own P&L.

### Pixar — Cornered Resource (the Brain Trust)

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** Pixar's creative process centered on the "Brain Trust" — a group of directors who reviewed each other's films in candid, structured sessions. Documented in Ed Catmull's *Creativity, Inc.*

**Why the case is instructive:** the Brain Trust passes Helmer's five screens for Cornered Resource — idiosyncratic (Pixar-specific), non-arbitraged (couldn't be bid away), transferable (produced the outcome), ongoing (produced hit after hit), sufficient (was the durable input, not one among many). Rare qualifying case; most claimed Cornered Resources fail sufficiency.

### Toyota — Process Power (with NUMMI as evidence)

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book), multiple podcasts.

**The situation:** Toyota Production System is documented in extreme detail, taught in every business school, and published in books like Womack & Jones's *The Machine That Changed the World*. Despite this transparency, competitors have not fully replicated it.

**NUMMI as the standing evidence:** NUMMI was the joint venture where GM had inside access to TPS on GM's own soil. Even with insider access, GM couldn't propagate TPS across its plants. That's the evidence Process Power is Power (hysteresis + causal ambiguity), not just documentation.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows the difference between operational excellence (mimicable — the treadmill) and Process Power (hysteresis + causal ambiguity — durable). If TPS were operational excellence, GM would have replicated it via NUMMI. It didn't.

### Bausch & Lomb — Cornered Resource (spin-casting soft contact lens tech)

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** B&L's spin-casting technology for soft contact lenses was legally/technically exclusive for a period.

**Why the case is instructive:** clean example of Cornered Resource with Fiat as the Barrier (patent + technical exclusivity). Compare to Pixar's Brain Trust: same Power type, different Barrier flavor.

### Adobe — Switching Costs (Helmer's own client)

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** Adobe was Helmer's consulting client. Creative Suite (later Creative Cloud) built switching costs through workflow lock-in, file-format dominance, and team learning-curve investments.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows Switching Costs as *cumulative on repeat purchases* — Adobe's Power grew with each additional Creative Cloud tool a designer or team adopted. Doesn't apply to new customers who haven't yet adopted; does apply on every renewal.

### LinkedIn, Facebook — Network Economies

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** each user's value from the network rises with each additional user; past the tipping point, chicken-and-egg dynamics defeat challengers.

**Why the case is instructive:** classic Network Economies. Also useful for contrast: many products claim "network effects" but fail the material + tipping test. LinkedIn/FB meet both bars.

### Walmart — Scale Economies

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** Walmart's cost curve on procurement, logistics, and store operations sloped down sharply with size, producing prohibitive share-gain cost for smaller rivals.

**Why the case is instructive:** clean Scale Economies. Contrast with "being bigger" — Walmart's scale had a *downward-sloping cost curve* with a steep enough slope to be prohibitive.

### Intel — Operation Crush as a Takeoff move

**Where Helmer uses it:** *7 Powers* (book).

**The situation:** the memory-to-microprocessor pivot, followed by Operation Crush (the 8086 sales campaign that locked in design wins). A Takeoff-stage move to secure Scale Economies + Switching Costs on the emerging PC platform.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows how Takeoff Powers must be locked in *during the window*. Intel scaled during the PC market's takeoff; the door closed for challengers once Intel's install base and design-win library became prohibitive to replicate.

### Dell vs. Compaq, Amazon vs. Borders, In-N-Out vs. McDonald's — Counter-Positioning cluster

**Where Helmer uses them:** *7 Powers* (book), various podcasts.

**Common pattern:** challenger invents a business model (direct-to-consumer PCs; online books with infinite shelf; small, quality-focused menu with no franchising) that the incumbent's existing business economically cannot copy. Incumbent's cannibalization risk exceeds their upside from copying.

**Why the cluster is instructive:** Helmer uses these to teach the *shape* of Counter-Positioning across industries. The invention differs; the Barrier mechanism (collateral damage on incumbent's P&L) is identical.

## Post-book cases (Second Invention research)

### Amazon → AWS — the archetypal second business

**Where Helmer uses it:** [Acquired — How to Build an AWS-Like Second Business (2023)](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/interview-hamilton-helmer-chenyi-shi-on-how-to-build-an-aws-like-second-business).

**The situation:** Amazon's second business (AWS) was built alongside the first (retail). AWS developed its own Powers (Scale Economies on compute infrastructure, Switching Costs on developer/enterprise workflow) — independent of retail's Powers.

**Why the case is instructive:** the "second invention" pattern in action. Most companies at PMF never develop a second business with its own Power. Amazon is the case Helmer + Shi return to as the pattern-setting example.

### Nintendo — a century of second inventions

**Where Helmer uses it:** Acquired 2023, Trium Group interview.

**The situation:** Nintendo started as a playing-cards company (1889), moved to toys, then to video-game hardware/software (Famicom/NES, 1983 onward). Multiple second inventions over a century.

**Why the case is instructive:** long-horizon example of the pattern. Shows that second-invention capability, if institutionalized, can produce serial Power across generations of a company.

### Nvidia → CUDA — chip business + developer ecosystem business

**Where Helmer uses it:** Acquired 2023, Trium Group interview.

**The situation:** Nvidia's chip business is Scale Economies (R&D amortization). CUDA is a *second* business layered on top — developer ecosystem lock-in (Switching Costs) + patent/IP + community as Cornered Resource + Network Economies among developers.

**Why the case is instructive:** shows Powers stacking on a second business, and shows CUDA specifically as the durability engine for Nvidia's AI-era position. Also serves as the closest thing to Helmer's own commentary on AI-era competitive dynamics (though the case predates the current AI wave in its treatment).

## Third-party applications — *tagged as applied, NOT Helmer's own writing*

The following cases are useful analytic applications of Helmer's framework by others. Tag them as *applied* when using — Helmer has not personally written or discussed them at length. Adam D'Angelo said 9 of 10 founders mis-assert their advantages; the same discipline applies to us applying Helmer's framework to companies he hasn't written about.

### TSMC — Process Power (applied)

**Applied by:** industry analysts, not Helmer.

**The claim:** TSMC's Process Power sits in leading-edge foundry manufacturing (3–5nm and below). Public data supporting the claim: ~60% of global chip foundry share, ~90% of leading-edge share, 40–45% operating margins vs. Samsung Foundry's ~10–15%.

**Why the analytic application holds:** hysteresis (decades of process refinement) + causal ambiguity (competitors including Intel and Samsung have failed to fully replicate). Passes the 3 S's for Process Power.

**Caveat:** Helmer has not personally analyzed TSMC in the public record. Use as a strong *applied* case.

### ASML — Cornered Resource (applied)

**Applied by:** industry analysts, not Helmer.

**The claim:** ASML is the sole producer of EUV lithography equipment for leading-edge chip manufacturing. Barrier = Fiat (technical exclusivity plus international-consortium legacy). Fits Cornered Resource's five screens.

**Caveat:** *applied*, not from Helmer directly.

### Nvidia in the AI era — Scale Economies + Switching Costs + Cornered Resource (applied)

**Applied by:** industry analysts.

**The claim:** Nvidia's AI-era Power sits in stacked Powers — Scale Economies (chip R&D amortization at unprecedented volume), Switching Costs (developer investment in CUDA), and Cornered Resource (patent + ecosystem + CUDA IP). Multiple Powers stacked = strong durability signal.

**Caveat:** Helmer's own commentary on AI-era companies specifically is thin in the public record. The case is a strong *applied* one but should be tagged accordingly.

## Cases from adjacent frameworks Helmer has referenced

- **Buffett's economic castles / moats** — Helmer explicitly credits Buffett's metaphor as the informal parent of 7 Powers. When users think in "moats," translate to Powers.
- **Christensen's disruption** — Counter-Positioning is Helmer's tighter reformulation. Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma cases (Digital Equipment, US Steel, mainframe→mini→micro) map onto Counter-Positioning with collateral-damage-on-incumbent as the crisper mechanism.
