# 7 Powers — Post-book material

> **This is the differential of this skill.** The 2016 book *7 Powers: The Foundations of Business Strategy* laid down the taxonomy of seven Powers. Since then, Helmer's post-book work has focused on **Dynamics** (how to *get* Power) rather than on adding to the taxonomy itself. The seven remain seven. What's changed is the theory of invention, the sequencing model, and the co-research with Chenyi Shi on second businesses and platforms.
>
> Because Helmer does not publish essays on Medium/Substack, the post-book record lives in podcast transcripts, Strategy Capital's Media page, and one Trium Group interview. This file collects what's there.

## The taxonomy has NOT been renamed or added-to

Explicit for anyone expecting an "8th Power": the seven remain seven. Refinements have been to **Dynamics** (stages, sequencing, invention, second-business patterns), not to the taxonomy.

## Refinements and new material

### Statics vs. Dynamics — the most emphasized post-book distinction

The book introduced the distinction. Helmer's post-book teaching leans on it much more heavily. He returns to it in almost every podcast appearance.

- **Statics** — *having* Power creates value. Diagnostic. Easy in mature companies.
- **Dynamics** — *getting* Power. Requires invention. Not planable in advance.

**Author's words:**
> "'Getting there' (Dynamics) is completely different from 'being there' (Statics)."
> — Helmer, *7 Powers*

The post-book emphasis: most strategic writing (including Porter) is Statics-only. 7 Powers is dual — the taxonomy for Statics, the three-stage sequencing for Dynamics.

### The three-stage business phase model

The book gestured at stages; the post-book work formalized them. Every Power lives in a specific stage:

- **Origination** (pre-takeoff) — invention window. Only **Counter-Positioning** and **Cornered Resource** are available. Barrier available: Collateral Damage / Fiat.
- **Takeoff** (explosive growth) — window to lock in **Scale Economies**, **Network Economies**, **Switching Costs**. Barrier available: Share Gain Cost-Benefit.
- **Stability** (post-explosive; typically <30–40% market growth) — **Branding** and **Process Power** emerge from sustained action. Barrier available: Hysteresis.

**Implication Helmer stresses:** if the window is missed, that Power is off the table. You cannot build Network Economies after a competitor has already tipped the market.

**Source:** discussed most explicitly on Acquired (2020), NFX (2021), and Lenny (2024).

### "Second Invention" — the biggest post-book intellectual project

Book-in-development with **Chenyi Shi** at Strategy Capital. Reframes what companies must do after PMF.

**Framing (Chenyi Shi, via Trium Group interview):**
> "The first invention is product-market fit, but developing power is a different muscle... The second invention is getting power with product-market fit."

**Empirical thread:** most companies that reach PMF never develop Power. The ones that persist and compound do a *second invention* on top of PMF — building a Power (usually Scale Economies, Network Economies, or Switching Costs during Takeoff; occasionally Branding or Process Power in Stability). The "second invention" is deliberate, hard, and not automatic.

**Source:** [Trium Group — Strategic Power: A Conversation with Hamilton Helmer and Chenyi Shi](https://thetriumgroup.substack.com/p/strategic-power-a-conversation-with)

### Second-business research (AWS-like patterns)

An offshoot of Second Invention. Studies patterns where a company builds a *second* business alongside its first — one that develops its own Power. Most companies fail at second acts; a small number compound.

**Canonical patterns studied:**
- **Amazon → AWS** — retail (Scale + Counter-Positioning) plus AWS (Scale + Switching Costs).
- **Nintendo** — playing cards → toys → video games/consoles. Multiple second inventions over a century.
- **Nvidia → CUDA** — chip business plus a developer-ecosystem business that layered Switching Costs + Cornered Resource on top.

**Source:** [Acquired — How to Build an AWS-Like Second Business (2023)](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/interview-hamilton-helmer-chenyi-shi-on-how-to-build-an-aws-like-second-business)

### Platforms and Power

Extension of the framework to platform businesses. Focus: platforms often have Network Economies + Switching Costs stacked, with Scale Economies emerging as the platform matures. But platforms also have a specific failure mode — when the network doesn't tip, or tips against you.

**Source:** [Acquired — Platforms and Power with Hamilton Helmer and Chenyi Shi (2022)](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/platforms-and-power-with-hamilton-helmer-and-chenyi-shi)

### "Power Dynamics" — the investing operationalization

Strategy Capital's proprietary investing method — the operational sibling of 7 Powers. Hunts for the **Value Moment**: the point at which uncertainty about market size and Power radically diminishes, producing a step-change in cash-flow transparency. That transition is where Strategy Capital wants to invest.

Note: Strategy Capital does not publish quarterly letters, investor memos, or essays publicly. Filings are 13F/SEC only. What's public is the framework's *shape*, not specific investments beyond regulatory disclosures.

**Source:** [Strategy Capital About](https://strategycapital.com/about/)

### "Invention is the mother of Power"

Post-book Helmer emphasizes invention as the source of Dynamics much more than the book did. Three flavors of invention that produce Power:

1. **Product invention** — a new offering (Pixar, Bausch & Lomb).
2. **Business-model invention** — a new way of capturing value (Vanguard, Netflix, Dell).
3. **Process invention** — a new way of producing (Toyota, TSMC applied).

Planning refines execution. It does not summon invention.

### The "Power Progression"

A term Helmer uses in the 2024 Lenny episode:
> "There's a thing called power progression. There are times when certain types of power are available. The path to power is where the rubber meets the road."

The Power Progression maps to the three-stage sequencing but adds a temporal urgency: at each stage, only certain Powers are on the table; miss the stage and the Powers close.

## AI-era commentary — noteworthy caveat

**Honest flag:** research surfaced almost no *direct* Helmer commentary on AI competitive dynamics as such. If a user asks "what does Helmer say about AI moats?", the truthful answer is: Helmer himself has said very little publicly on AI specifically.

**What exists in the record:**
- The Trium Group interview (2023–2024) touches AI in passing but is not AI-focused.
- The Lenny 2024 episode discusses AI briefly.
- Second Invention research covers Nvidia/CUDA as one of the cases — the AI-adjacent case in the corpus.

**Third-party applications of 7 Powers to AI companies (not Helmer's own writing — tag accordingly):**
- **Nvidia** — Scale Economies (CUDA ecosystem investment) + Switching Costs (developer lock-in) + Cornered Resource (patent + ecosystem).
- **TSMC** — Process Power (3–5nm foundry with 60% global share, 90% at leading edge, 40–45% operating margins vs. Samsung foundry's 10–15%).
- **ASML** — Cornered Resource (EUV lithography monopoly).
- **OpenAI / Anthropic** — mixed claims; genuinely open whether they have Power beyond Scale Economies (compute) and early Brand recognition.

If the skill is asked to analyze AI companies through 7 Powers, the honest move is: use the framework, tag the case as *applied* rather than *Helmer's*, and flag that Helmer has not personally written the case.
