# Demand-Side Sales — Post-Book Material

> Material Moesta published or said AFTER *Demand-Side Sales 101* (2020). The primary text gave us the Forces, the timeline, and the Switch Interview applied to sales. Since then, two books and ongoing public appearances have expanded the method.

## Learning to Build (2022) — the innovation lens

*Learning to Build: The 5 Bedrock Skills of Innovators and Entrepreneurs* is Moesta's most personal book. It applies the demand-side lens to innovation and product development rather than sales. It's also his origin story — dyslexia, Detroit, learning by observation rather than reading, and the mentors who shaped him (Christensen, Taguchi, Deming, Willie Hobbs Moore).

### The Five Bedrock Skills

Moesta's thesis: successful innovators practice five skills, most of which are not taught in business schools.

1. **Empathetic Perspective** — the ability to see the world through the customer's context, not your own. Not sympathy ("I feel bad for you") — perspective-taking ("I can see what you're struggling with and why your current approach makes sense to you"). This is the foundation that makes Switch Interviews possible.

2. **Uncovering Demand** — finding where struggling moments exist and where energy for progress is building. Demand is not created by marketing — it's discovered by listening. The struggling moment is the seed; uncovering demand is the act of finding the seed.

3. **Causal Structures** — understanding *why* things happen, not just *what* happens. Moesta's insistence on causation over correlation is here formalized as a skill. Causal structures are built by reconstructing timelines (Switch Interviews) and mapping forces (the Forces diagram). Moesta traces this to his training with Taguchi (design of experiments) and Deming (variation and systems).

4. **Prototyping to Learn** — building to discover, not building to ship. Moesta distinguishes prototyping (learning what to build) from development (building what you decided). The demand-side lens applies here: prototype to test whether your understanding of the Forces is correct, not to test whether the feature works.

5. **Making Tradeoffs** — the discipline of choosing what NOT to do. Every product that tries to serve multiple contradictory Jobs fails (InVideo). Making tradeoffs requires knowing the Job well enough to know which features increase Pull and which increase Anxiety.

**Skill guardrail:** these five skills are not a sequence. They're practiced in parallel, iteratively. The order in the book is pedagogical, not operational.

**Source:** *Learning to Build* (2022, Lioncrest); SlideShare presentations (BoSON21, BoSEU23); Business of Software talks.

### The Deming/Taguchi lineage — what it adds

*Learning to Build* makes explicit a lineage that's mostly implicit in DSS101: Moesta's training in quality engineering (Taguchi methods, design of experiments) and systems thinking (Deming). This matters because:

- **Taguchi:** Moesta's approach to "causal structures" — understanding root causes through controlled variation — comes from Taguchi's DOE methodology, adapted to customer research. A Switch Interview is, in a sense, a qualitative design-of-experiments: you hold some variables constant (the purchase was made, the product was adopted) and reconstruct what varied (the context, the forces, the timeline).

- **Deming:** Moesta's insistence on systems over interventions — the idea that sales, marketing, and CS are one system, not three departments — echoes Deming's systems thinking. The buying timeline is a system; the funnel is a local optimization that breaks the system.

- **Willie Hobbs Moore:** physicist, first African American woman to earn a PhD in physics. Moesta cites her as a formative influence on how to think about structure and pattern in seemingly messy data.

**Source:** *Learning to Build*; Digestible Deming podcast (June 2026) — the most explicit public conversation about this lineage.

## Job Moves (2024) — the career lens

*Job Moves: 9 Steps for Making Progress in Your Career* (w/ Ethan Bernstein + Michael Horn, HarperCollins, Nov 2024). USA Today bestseller. Applies the demand-side lens to career decisions — treating a job change as a "switch" that follows the same Forces and timeline as any other purchase.

### The Four Quests — why people leave

Moesta's research (1,000+ interviews with professionals about their career transitions) revealed four distinct quests — the "why" of a job switch:

1. **Get Out** — escape burnout, depletion, or a toxic situation. The Push is overwhelming; Pull is almost anything that's "not here."
2. **Take the Next Step** — growth, advancement, new skills. Push is boredom or ceiling; Pull is a specific growth vector.
3. **Regain Control** — autonomy over time, responsibilities, or scope. Push is overextension; Pull is boundaries.
4. **Realign** — refocus on core competencies after drifting into roles that don't fit. Push is misalignment; Pull is "doing what I'm actually good at."

**Practical insight for hiring teams:** if you're recruiting someone on a Get Out quest, your Pull barely matters — they're running *from*, not *to*. If you're recruiting someone on a Realign quest, your Pull must be very specific about the *type* of work.

### Key concepts from Job Moves

- **Features vs. Experiences.** Salary, title, company brand = features (static, over-indexed on). Day-to-day activities and interactions = experiences (dynamic, actually determine satisfaction). Aim for 40-50% of time on energizing work. People over-hire on features and under-evaluate experiences.

- **Energy audit.** Daily/weekly tracking of what energizes vs. depletes. The energy audit is a self-administered diagnostic — the career equivalent of a Forces diagram on your own situation.

- **Jobcation.** A low-demand recovery job to reset while exploring the next real move. Not a detour — a deliberate recovery strategy when Get Out energy is high and clarity about Pull is low.

- **Career Story Template:** "Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that... Until finally... Ever since that day..." — a narrative structure for articulating career progress.

- **Switch Interview on yourself.** Reconstruct your own past job changes using the Switch Interview method. Pattern-recognize across your own career: what quest have you been on? What Forces drove each switch? What did you learn about your own preferences that you keep forgetting?

### What Job Moves adds to the method

Job Moves is the demand-side lens applied *inward*. The method is the same — Forces, timeline, struggling moments, progress — but the "product" being hired is a job, and the "customer" is the professional. This extension validates the universality of the method (it works on career decisions, not just product purchases) and adds new vocabulary (quests, energy audit, jobcation) that enrich the core framework.

**Source:** *Job Moves* (HarperCollins, 2024); Lenny's Podcast #2 (Feb 2025); Selling from the Heart podcast (Aug 2025); INDUSTRY 2025 keynote.

## Recent appearances and refinements (2025-2026)

### INDUSTRY 2025 — "Rethinking Hiring, Retention & Career Progress Like a Product"

Moesta presented the demand-side lens applied to HR and talent management. Key addition: organizations should treat their jobs as "products" that employees "hire" to make progress. Retention becomes a Forces problem — what's the Push (what's broken?), what's the Pull (what's attracting them elsewhere?), what's the Anxiety (what would they lose by leaving?), what's the Habit (what keeps them comfortable staying?).

This is Moesta's most explicit expansion into organizational territory — JTBD not just for products and careers, but for talent systems.

**Source:** [INDUSTRY 2025 keynote (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QeFDn3Mri4g).

## Method evolution — what has changed since DSS101

The core method (Forces, timeline, Switch Interview) has remained stable. What has evolved:

1. **Application breadth.** DSS101 was sales-focused. Learning to Build extended to innovation. Job Moves extended to careers. INDUSTRY 2025 extended to talent management. The method is becoming a general-purpose demand-side lens, not a sales technique.

2. **The five bedrock skills.** Learning to Build formalized what was implicit in DSS101: the underlying skills (empathetic perspective, uncovering demand, causal structures, prototyping to learn, making tradeoffs) that make the method work. These are now the pedagogical frame for the Re-Wired Group's coaching programs.

3. **The Deming/Taguchi connection.** Learning to Build and the Digestible Deming appearance (2026) made explicit what was implicit: Moesta's approach to causation comes from quality engineering, not from business theory. This is a lineage worth understanding for practitioners who want to go deeper.

4. **Career application.** Job Moves proved the method works on self-directed decisions, not just product-buying decisions. The Four Quests are a genuinely new contribution — they categorize the *type* of Push that drives a career switch.

5. **Organizational territory.** The INDUSTRY 2025 keynote marks a push into HR/talent management — "treat your jobs as products employees hire." This extends the method from individual career decisions (Job Moves) to organizational talent systems.

**What has NOT changed:** the core axiom (people hire products to make progress), the four Forces (Push/Pull/Anxiety/Habit), the buying timeline (six stages), the Switch Interview method (recruit recent buyers, follow the story, no discussion guide), and the fundamental distinction between supply-side and demand-side thinking.
