# Demand-Side Sales — Prompt templates

> Copy-paste templates users can adapt. Each starts with a user situation and invokes the skill in a shape the assistant can execute well.

## Map the Four Forces on a buying decision

```
I want to understand why customers are (or aren't) buying {{product/service}}.

Context:
- What we sell: {{describe the product/service}}
- Who buys it: {{describe recent buyers — role, situation, what triggered the purchase}}
- What they were doing before: {{the current solution or workaround they had}}
- What's frustrating us: {{low conversion, slow sales cycle, churn, etc.}}

Please map the Four Forces of Progress:
1. **Push** — what's broken about how they're handling this today? What struggling moment triggers the search?
2. **Pull** — what vision of progress do they imagine? Not features — what does their improved life look like?
3. **Anxiety** — what are they afraid of with our product? Learning curve? Integration risk? Social risk? What almost stops them?
4. **Habit** — what keeps them comfortable where they are? What workarounds have become invisible?

Then tell me: which force is the equation breaking on? Are we over-investing in Pull (features) while ignoring Anxiety and Habit?

Use Moesta's language. Cite sources when you introduce a concept.
```

## Plan a round of Switch Interviews

```
I want to run Switch Interviews to understand why people buy {{product/service}}.

Context:
- We have {{number}} recent buyers/churned customers available to interview.
- Our product does: {{one-line description}}
- What we're trying to learn: {{specific question — e.g., "why do they choose us over competitor X", "why does the sales cycle take 6 months", "why do 30% churn in month 2"}}

Please help me plan the interviews:
1. **Recruiting criteria** — who should I interview? Recent buyers? Churned customers? What segments should I cover?
2. **Sample size** — how many interviews, in how many rounds?
3. **Interview structure** — walk me through the structural sequence (First Thought → ongoing use). What questions open each section?
4. **Micromoves** — remind me of the key interviewing techniques (play back incorrectly, Columbo move, "tell me more" vs. "why?").
5. **Debrief plan** — how should I structure the post-interview debrief with my team?
6. **Quality checks** — what should I look for in my notes to know I got below the pablum layer?
7. **Common mistakes** — what anti-patterns should I watch out for?
```

## Diagnose why customers aren't switching

```
We have a product that's objectively better than what customers are currently using, but they won't switch.

Our product: {{describe}}
The current solution they're using: {{describe — include workarounds}}
What we've tried: {{e.g., "we've added features, run ads, offered free trials"}}

Apply Moesta's Forces:
1. Is there enough Push? Is the current solution *unacceptable*, or just *inconvenient*?
2. Is our Pull actually about progress, or are we listing features?
3. What Anxiety are we creating? Is adding features making Anxiety worse?
4. What Habit are we underestimating? What invisible comfort does the current solution provide?
5. Where does the equation break? Which force should we work on?

Be specific. Don't give me generic advice — tell me what's likely happening with these specific Forces and what to do about it.
```

## Redesign sales around the buying timeline

```
Our current sales process is built around our internal workflow (lead → qualify → demo → proposal → close). I want to redesign it around how customers actually buy.

Context:
- What we sell: {{describe}}
- Current sales cycle length: {{e.g., "3-6 months"}}
- Biggest friction point: {{e.g., "they go dark after the demo", "they can't get internal buy-in"}}

Apply Moesta's buying timeline:
1. **First Thought** — what struggling moment triggers the search? What are we doing (or not doing) to make the struggle visible?
2. **Passive Looking** — are we teaching or pitching? What language are customers learning at this stage?
3. **Active Looking** — are we expanding possibilities or trying to close? Are we letting them play?
4. **Deciding** — are we presenting three options? Do we have an "albatross"? Are we helping them articulate why they'd choose us?
5. **First Use** — does the customer feel progress fast? What's the "first win"?
6. **Ongoing Use** — where do new struggles appear? When does the next cycle start?

Tell me what's different about this approach vs. our current process. Where are we likely mismatched — doing the wrong thing at the wrong stage?
```

## Find the real competition

```
We think our competition is {{obvious competitor(s)}}. But conversions aren't improving despite building a better product than them.

Help me find the REAL competition using Moesta's method:
1. What would customers hire to make the same progress if our product didn't exist?
2. What are they currently doing to handle the struggling moment? (This is the real competitor — even if it's "nothing" or "a spreadsheet".)
3. Where does the "fire" event happen — what would they fire if they hired us?

Think Casper vs. ZzzQuil, not Casper vs. Tempur-Pedic. Think milkshake vs. banana, not milkshake vs. smoothie.

Context about our product: {{describe}}
What our customers say they were doing before: {{if known}}
```

## Analyze a product launch that isn't gaining traction

```
We launched {{product}} {{time period}} ago and adoption is below expectations.

What we expected: {{describe expected adoption}}
What's happening: {{describe actual adoption}}
What we've tried: {{list interventions}}

Apply the demand-side lens:
1. Is there a real struggling moment driving demand? Or are we creating supply without demand?
2. Are we selling features (supply-side) or visualizing progress (demand-side)?
3. Map the Forces: what's the Push that should drive people to us? Is it strong enough? Is our Pull about features or about progress? What Anxiety are we creating? What Habit are we underestimating?
4. Where are our potential customers on the buying timeline? Are they even at First Thought, or are we trying to close people who are still in Passive Looking?
5. Are we interviewing the right people? Should I be interviewing users of the products ours would replace?

Be honest. If the problem is "there's no struggling moment", say so.
```

## Apply Job Moves to a career decision

```
I'm considering a career move and want to think through it using Moesta's demand-side lens.

My current situation: {{describe role, what you do, how long you've been there}}
What's frustrating: {{describe the push — what's broken or draining}}
What I'm considering: {{describe potential new roles or directions}}
What's holding me back: {{describe anxiety and habit}}

Apply the Job Moves framework:
1. **Which quest am I on?** Get Out (escape), Take the Next Step (growth), Regain Control (autonomy), or Realign (refocus)?
2. **Map the Forces** on my current situation: Push (what's broken), Pull (what I imagine), Anxiety (what I'm afraid of), Habit (what's comfortable).
3. **Features vs. Experiences** — am I over-indexing on features (salary, title, company brand) and under-evaluating experiences (daily activities, interactions)?
4. **Energy audit** — what energizes me vs. what depletes me? Am I getting 40-50% energizing work?
5. **What would Moesta's "Switch Interview on yourself" reveal?** Look at my past job changes — what pattern do they show?
```

## Critique a marketing or sales approach

```
Here's our current marketing/sales approach: {{paste or describe}}

Critique it through Moesta's demand-side lens:
1. Is it supply-side ("look at our features") or demand-side ("here's the progress you'll make")?
2. Does it address a struggling moment, or does it assume people are already looking?
3. Which stage of the buying timeline is it designed for? Is it actually reaching people at that stage?
4. Does it increase Pull — or does it increase Anxiety (more features = more complexity = more to learn)?
5. Does it address Habit? Or does it ignore why people are comfortable where they are?
6. What would Moesta likely push back on hardest?
7. What would the Casper/ZzzQuil version of this look like? (i.e., targeting the real competitor, not the obvious one)
```

## Design three options for the Deciding stage

```
A customer is in the Deciding stage for {{product/service}}. I need to design three options per Moesta's method.

Context:
- What the customer is trying to achieve: {{describe the progress}}
- Their biggest Anxiety: {{what almost stops them}}
- What they're comparing against: {{alternatives}}

Help me design:
1. **Option A (the albatross)** — designed to be eliminated first. Obviously wrong in one specific way. Its purpose is to give the customer a clear "not that" anchor.
2. **Option B** — the real recommendation. Addresses the core Job with the right balance of Pull and low Anxiety.
3. **Option C** — a stretch option. More ambitious, higher commitment. Will be chosen by customers with strong Push and low Anxiety.

For each option:
- What's included and excluded (and why — map to Forces)
- The "story" the customer tells themselves for choosing it
- How it helps them justify the decision internally

Remember: the customer eliminates one first, then compares the remaining two against the eliminated one.
```
