# High Output Management — live sources (the carrier index)

> **Andy Grove died on 2016-03-21.** Unlike other skills in this repo — where "live sources" means the author's Medium, Substack, YouTube — Grove's own corpus is closed. His method stays alive because named **carriers** publicly extend it.
>
> This file is the carrier index. It's the place the skill goes when the user's situation matches something a carrier has written about at length, so the skill can either point them at the specific piece or WebFetch it inline.
>
> **How to use in a session:** when the user has a specific situation (new-executive onboarding, OKR grading fight, one-on-one gone stale, IC-to-manager transition, high-growth operational cadence), scan the carrier-specific section for the matching piece.
>
> **Community PRs to this file are the fastest way to keep the skill current** — a new Horowitz essay, a new Hughes Johnson interview, a new Zhuo Substack post. Add them with topic tag + one-line takeaway + URL.

## Grove's own corpus (closed — for canonical reference)

Because Grove is gone, this is the finished set. The `year` is when it went into the world.

### Books
- **1983** — [*High Output Management*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/72467/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove-former-chairman-and-ceo-of-intel/) (Random House). **2015 reprint with Ben Horowitz foreword** (Vintage) — the canonical edition.
- **1996** — [*Only the Paranoid Survive*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/only-the-paranoid-survive-andrew-s-grove) (Currency/Doubleday). Strategic Inflection Points, 10X change, Helpful Cassandras.
- **2001** — [*Swimming Across: A Memoir*](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/andrew-s-grove/swimming-across/9780446679701/) (Warner Books). Hungarian refugee memoir; register-forming.
- **2001** — [*Strategy Is Destiny*](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Strategy-Is-Destiny/Robert-A-Burgelman/9780743215688) (with Robert Burgelman, Free Press). Academic account of Intel strategy.
- **1967** — *Physics and Technology of Semiconductor Devices* (Wiley). Grove's engineering textbook; still cited.

### HBR
- **November 1996** — [*Inside Intel*](https://hbr.org/1996/11/inside-intel) — Grove's companion piece to *OTPS*. The most-accessible primary-Grove HBR article.

### Stanford GSB
- Strategic Management course, co-taught with Robert Burgelman for ~25 years (~1991 until Grove's health declined). Case archive at Stanford; not publicly indexed as a portal. If the user is a Stanford GSB alum, this is a source they can access; otherwise the case-book form is *Strategy Is Destiny*.

## The carriers — where Grove's method lives in 2026

### Ben Horowitz — the direct successor (loudest carrier)

**Position:** a16z co-founder. Explicitly built his own work on Grove.

**Books:**
- [*The Hard Thing About Hard Things*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-hard-thing-about-hard-things-ben-horowitz) (HarperBusiness, 2014) — the CEO-under-fire book. Grove's TRM, one-on-ones, and performance-review directness are load-bearing.
- [*What You Do Is Who You Are*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/what-you-do-is-who-you-are-ben-horowitz) (HarperBusiness, 2019) — the culture book.

**The single most important Grove-related piece Horowitz has written:**
- [Andy — Introduction to *High Output Management*](https://medium.com/software-is-eating-the-world/andy-37e10d4780bc) — his foreword to the 2015 Vintage reprint, publicly reproduced. **Where most modern readers actually enter Grove.**

**Where Horowitz lives now:**
- [a16z author page](https://a16z.com/author/ben-horowitz/) — ongoing essays and podcast appearances.
- **The a16z Podcast** — recurring management episodes.
- Books and public appearances.

**Densest single Horowitz-on-Grove audio piece:**
- [Tim Ferriss Show, ep. 392 — Ben Horowitz transcript](https://tim.blog/2019/11/12/ben-horowitz-transcript/) (2019-11-12). The transcript is where Horowitz talks about Grove at length — what Grove taught him, why he thinks Grove is the best CEO Silicon Valley ever produced, specific stories.

**Topics to look here first:**
- New-executive onboarding (TRM applied to senior hires)
- One-on-ones under high-pressure conditions
- Firing / performance-review directness
- Culture as structural mechanics (not vibes)
- The CEO's mental toughness

### John Doerr — the OKR canonizer

**Position:** joined Intel 1974, took Grove's iMBO course 1975. Kleiner Perkins. Brought OKRs to Google 1999.

**Book:**
- [*Measure What Matters*](https://www.measurewhatmatters.com/) (Portfolio, 2018) — the OKR canon.

**Platform:**
- [What Matters](https://www.whatmatters.com/) — Doerr's ongoing OKR site. New content added periodically.

**Key pieces on the Grove lineage:**
- [Origin Story — Grove → Doerr → Google](https://www.whatmatters.com/articles/the-origin-story) — the canonical account of how iMBO became OKRs.
- [Operation Crush case](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/john-doerr-operation-crush) — Grove's 1979 pivot as an OKR-in-action case.
- [Faqs — OKR definition and examples](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example).

**Topics to look here first:**
- OKR mechanics (setting, cascading, grading)
- Case studies of OKR implementations (Google, Gates Foundation, Bono/ONE, MyFitnessPal)
- The Grove-to-Google lineage in detail

### Julie Zhuo — the first-time-manager translator

**Position:** ex-Facebook VP Design. Now co-founder of Sundial (product analytics).

**Book:**
- [*The Making of a Manager*](https://www.juliezhuo.com/book/manager.html) (Portfolio, 2019).

**Platform:**
- [Substack "The Looking Glass"](https://lg.substack.com/) — ongoing management essays.
- [juliezhuo.com](https://www.juliezhuo.com/) — hub.

**Topics to look here first:**
- IC-to-manager transitions (the classic low-TRM management moment)
- Feedback in creative/design/product contexts (where craft is subjective)
- Manager as coach vs. player
- Managing on small teams (Facebook design pod scale)

### Claire Hughes Johnson — the modern operational playbook

**Position:** ex-Stripe COO (2014–2021). Previously at Google in operating roles.

**Book:**
- [*Scaling People: Tactics for Management and Company Building*](https://press.stripe.com/scaling-people) (Stripe Press, 2023).

**Platform:**
- Advisor / board work. Occasional podcast appearances. [Stanford eCorner podcast — Scaling Operations and People](https://stvp.stanford.edu/podcasts/scaling-operations-and-people).

**Topics to look here first:**
- High-growth-startup operations (10× headcount growth per year)
- Performance-review cycles at scale
- Templates for one-on-ones, staff meetings, planning cycles (this is the most template-forward Grove-adjacent book)
- Values → behaviors → mechanisms translation

### Robert Burgelman — the academic carrier

**Position:** Stanford GSB. Co-taught Strategic Management with Grove for ~25 years.

**Book:**
- [*Strategy Is Destiny*](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Strategy-Is-Destiny/Robert-A-Burgelman/9780743215688) (with Grove, Free Press, 2001).

**Post-Grove-death piece:**
- [HBR — Remembering Andy Grove, the Teacher](https://hbr.org/2016/03/remembering-andy-grove-the-teacher) (2016-03-23).

**Topics to look here first:**
- Grove's teaching method (case-based, adversarial)
- Intel's strategy as an emergent process (bottom-up)
- Grove as an intellectual (not just an operator)

### Michael S. Malone — the biographer

**Position:** Silicon Valley journalist, biographer.

**Book:**
- [*The Intel Trinity: How Robert Noyce, Gordon Moore, and Andy Grove Built the World's Most Important Company*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-intel-trinity-michael-s-malone) (HarperBusiness, 2014).

**Topics to look here first:**
- Grove's biography beyond *Swimming Across* (which stops at age 20)
- The Intel context that produced *HOM*
- Grove's operational contribution vs. Noyce's vision and Moore's technology

### Clayton Christensen — the theoretical peer (deceased 2020)

**Post-Grove-death piece:**
- [HBR — What I'll Miss About Andy Grove](https://hbr.org/2016/03/clayton-christensen-what-ill-miss-about-andy-grove) (2016).

**Topics to look here first:**
- Grove and the Innovator's Dilemma (memory-to-microprocessor as canonical case)
- The intellectual respect between Grove and Christensen
