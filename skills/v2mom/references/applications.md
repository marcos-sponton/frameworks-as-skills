# V2MOM — When to use, when NOT, adjacent frameworks

> V2MOM is opinionated — it's built for organizational alignment, not for choosing what to do competitively. This file draws the boundary and points at the framework that better fits when V2MOM isn't the right tool.

## When V2MOM fits

V2MOM is the right tool when the user's problem is one of the following:

- **Annual company planning.** Every year, the CEO / founders need one artifact that (a) states the vision, (b) ranks the values, (c) commits to methods, (d) names the hard things, (e) sets measurable outcomes. V2MOM does exactly this.
- **Cascading a plan across the org.** V2MOM's cascade mechanism (corporate → function → team → individual, all published) is its strongest feature. If the user needs alignment at scale, V2MOM's cascade beats generic planning docs.
- **Personal V2MOM for a manager or IC.** Applied at individual scope, V2MOM turns generic OKRs into a values-grounded plan with obstacles named.
- **Project or event scope.** Salesforce's own Innovation Project V2MOM and Events V2MOM prove the framework scales down. If the user is running a specific project (product launch, campaign, offsite) and needs alignment across contributors, V2MOM works.
- **Post-acquisition or post-merger alignment.** Two orgs need one shared plan. V2MOM's five elements force the conversation about what unified vision and values look like, not just what synergy targets are.
- **Refreshing a stale planning process.** The team has been using OKRs for years, they've become mechanical, and the "why" has faded. V2MOM re-centers Values and Obstacles as first-class elements.
- **Small team, no formal planning process yet.** V2MOM works at 4 people (Salesforce's founding scale) as well as 75,000. If the team is < 20 people and hasn't formalized planning, V2MOM is a lightweight starting point.

## When V2MOM does NOT fit

V2MOM is the wrong tool when the user's problem is one of the following:

### Competitive strategy — "how do we beat X?"

V2MOM assumes you know what you're doing and need to align on it. It does not help you *decide* how to compete. For "should we go up-market or defend down-market?" or "what's our unfair advantage?" — you want **Playing to Win** (Martin) or **the kernel of strategy** (Rumelt), not V2MOM.

The move: run Playing to Win / Rumelt first to make the competitive choice, then use V2MOM to cascade the resulting plan across the org.

### Pre-PMF search

If the founder genuinely doesn't yet know what business they're building, V2MOM is premature. Vision → Values → Methods → Obstacles → Measures assumes there's something coherent to align around. Pre-PMF, the work is discovery, not alignment. Use a lean/discovery frame instead.

**Exception:** Benioff himself wrote a V2MOM in the *first weeks* of Salesforce, so scale isn't the issue. What matters is whether you can articulate a Vision that's a real choice, not a placeholder. If the Vision would apply equally to five different pivots you're considering, you're pre-choice.

### Pure operational execution

"How do we ship faster?" "How do we reduce our error rate?" "How do we hire more engineers per month?" — these are operational questions. V2MOM's Measures section can hold operational metrics, but the framework is overkill for a pure ops improvement conversation.

### Pure product-decision work

"Should we build feature X?" "How do we prioritize the backlog?" — use PR-FAQ / Working Backwards (Amazon) or a product-strategy framework. V2MOM isn't wrong here; it's just heavier than needed.

### The team can't sustain the cadence

V2MOM promises value only if it's referenced weekly in 1:1s and refreshed quarterly. If the surrounding org can't sustain that cadence (small team where planning is ad-hoc, or a team that historically ignores planning docs), the framework will underdeliver. Name that up front. Consider a lighter alternative (a one-page memo + weekly standup ritual) instead of drafting a beautiful V2MOM that dies on Notion.

## Adjacent frameworks and when each is the better tool

### OKRs (Doerr / Grove / Google)

**OKRs = Objectives + Key Results.** Two components: what you're trying to achieve, and how you'll measure it.

**V2MOM subsumes OKRs.** V2MOM's Measures section is functionally your Key Results, and V2MOM's Methods section is functionally what would go under an Objective. V2MOM adds Vision (context), Values (decision guidance), and Obstacles (risk-naming) — all of which OKRs omit.

**When OKRs are the better choice:**
- The team is fully aligned on vision and values and just needs sharper measurement discipline. OKRs are a lighter artifact.
- The team is coming from Google/Facebook/Intel lineage and OKR literacy is high. Retraining to V2MOM has switching cost.
- Measurement rigor is the sole gap. V2MOM's Measures section is comparable but V2MOM's real value comes from V/V/O — if those aren't the gap, OKRs are enough.

**When V2MOM beats OKRs:**
- The team has strong OKR discipline but drifts in vision and values. V2MOM reintroduces context.
- The team's "obstacles" are the real story (regulatory friction, hiring bottleneck, incumbent countermove). OKRs give you nowhere to name them.
- Cascade needs to be values-grounded, not just numeric. V2MOM does this natively.

**Don't run both in parallel.** Pick one. Maintaining V2MOM *and* OKRs means the team consults whichever is easier and ignores the other.

### VMV templates (Vision, Mission, Values)

**VMV is a culture artifact — hangs on a wall, shows up on About pages.** No methods, no obstacles, no measures. Static.

**When VMV is the better choice:**
- External-facing brand / recruiting content. VMV is what a marketing team writes for the careers page.
- The org has no planning process at all yet and needs to declare culture before it can plan. VMV first, V2MOM second.

**When V2MOM beats VMV:**
- Any time the doc needs to drive weekly decisions. VMV can't.

### Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)

**Four perspectives** — Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth. Measurement architecture.

**When Balanced Scorecard is the better choice:**
- Large, mature enterprise with distinct measurement domains that need discrete dashboards.
- The org's problem is *what to measure across perspectives*, not *what to do*.

**When V2MOM beats Balanced Scorecard:**
- Startup / mid-sized org. Balanced Scorecard's four perspectives are overkill.
- The team needs a single unified artifact, not four dashboards.
- Values and Obstacles are load-bearing (Balanced Scorecard omits both).

### Playing to Win (Martin & Lafley)

**5 questions about competitive strategy:** winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, management systems.

**When Playing to Win is the better choice:**
- The question is competitive: how do we win in this market? What's our unfair advantage? Where do we play vs. compete?
- The team is drafting a strategy from scratch and hasn't yet chosen how to compete.

**When V2MOM beats Playing to Win:**
- Competitive strategy is settled; the question is cross-org alignment on executing it.
- The org is not currently in a competitive-choice moment (they know their game; they need to align on running it well).

**Combined use:** run Playing to Win to make the competitive choice → run V2MOM to align the org around the resulting plan.

### Rumelt's kernel of strategy (diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent action)

**Kernel:** what is the pivotal challenge (crux)? What guiding policy addresses it? What coherent actions follow?

**When Rumelt is the better choice:**
- The team knows something is wrong but hasn't diagnosed the crux. Rumelt is diagnosis-first.
- The organization has "10 priorities and nothing's moving" — Rumelt's insistence on one crux breaks the paralysis.

**When V2MOM beats Rumelt:**
- The diagnosis is settled; the team needs cascadable alignment on the response.
- The problem is not "what's wrong?" but "how do we align on the plan?"

**Combined use:** Rumelt to diagnose the crux → V2MOM to align the org around the response.

### Amazon Working Backwards / PR-FAQ

**Product-decision tool.** Write the launch press release before building.

**When PR-FAQ is the better choice:**
- The question is product-shaped: "should we build X?" "what would X look like from the customer's view?"
- The team is at the product-discovery stage.

**When V2MOM beats PR-FAQ:**
- The product decision is made; now the whole org (product + eng + sales + marketing + support) needs to align on launching and scaling.

**Combined use:** PR-FAQ to decide *what* to build → V2MOM to align the org on *building it*.

### EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System) / Traction

**EOS** is a full operating system for SMBs — includes Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction (Rocks + Meetings).

**When EOS is the better choice:**
- SMB owner-operator context, especially services/trades businesses. EOS is designed for that shape.
- The team wants a turnkey operating system, not just a planning artifact.

**When V2MOM beats EOS:**
- Tech / knowledge-work orgs where cascade needs to reach ICs, not just leadership. V2MOM's individual-V2MOM mechanism is stronger.
- The team wants a lighter artifact, not a full OS.

### Ben Horowitz's "What You Do Is Who You Are" values framework

Not a planning framework — an argument that Values need to be enforced with specific, observable rules to become real. Complements V2MOM's Values section by giving you the mechanics of *how to make ranked values actually operate*.

## Decision heuristic (compressed)

Ask the user:

1. **What is the actual problem?**
   - Competitive choice → Playing to Win / Rumelt.
   - Diagnosis of what's wrong → Rumelt.
   - Product-decision → PR-FAQ.
   - Alignment across org on an already-chosen plan → V2MOM.
   - Measurement discipline only → OKRs.
   - Culture declaration for external audience → VMV.

2. **Can the org sustain V2MOM's cadence?**
   - Weekly 1:1 references + quarterly refresh + mid-year formal review. If yes, V2MOM. If no, name that and either fix the cadence or pick a lighter artifact.

3. **Do you already have OKRs?**
   - Running V2MOM in addition means maintaining two docs. Pick one. Either migrate OKRs into V2MOM's Measures section, or stay on OKRs and use V2MOM as a private context doc — but not both in parallel as authoritative artifacts.
