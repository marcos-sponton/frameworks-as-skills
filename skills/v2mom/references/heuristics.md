# V2MOM — Heuristics, Do's, Don'ts, Gotchas

> The practical rules — Salesforce Trailhead's tips, Benioff's own emphases, plus the 2023 controversy — that separate applying V2MOM well from producing a vision-slogan-and-KPI list that happens to have five headings.

## Do's

### Write it yourself

Benioff writes his own V2MOM every fiscal year, personally. Not delegated to chief-of-staff, not ghost-written. The exercise of writing forces the choices that make the document useful. A delegated V2MOM is a document; a personally-written V2MOM is a decision.

**Applies to individual V2MOMs too.** Trailhead explicitly warns against copy-pasting from your manager's. Your V2MOM should reflect *your* specific choices about how you'll advance the team's methods.

### Preserve the order — V → V → M → O → M

Vision constrains Values. Values constrain Methods. Methods define what Obstacles are relevant. Obstacles inform what Measures actually matter. Rearranging the order — e.g., starting with Measures and reverse-engineering upward — is the classic OKR trap V2MOM was designed to avoid.

**When someone drafts in the wrong order:** ask them to redo, in order. It's often surprising what changes when Values are named *before* Methods rather than after.

### Rank the values

Salesforce's Trust → Customer Success → Innovation → Equality → Sustainability is deliberate. When two values conflict on a specific decision, the higher-ranked one wins.

**Test for real ranking:** ask "which two of your values would conflict most often, and which wins?" If the team can't answer, the ranking is decorative.

### Every obstacle paired to a method

An obstacle without a corresponding method to address it is just complaining. The pairing forces the team to commit to overcoming the obstacle, not just noting it.

**Test:** for each obstacle, name the specific method (or new sub-method) that addresses it. If none, either add the method or drop the obstacle from the doc.

### Every measure paired to a method

A measure without a method to drive it is a wish. A method without a measure is a task list. They must pair.

**Test:** for each measure, name the method(s) that drive it. If none, either add the method or drop the measure.

### One page

Benioff's original 1999 V2MOM fit on an American Express envelope. Most working Salesforce V2MOMs fit on a single letter-size page. The constraint forces prioritization — if it doesn't fit, you haven't chosen.

**Not one page = you have not made choices.** The move is not "make it two pages"; the move is "cut."

### Publish internally

Cascade only works if V2MOMs are visible. Salesforce publishes every one of them on Chatter, searchable by employee. If your org has no internal channel where V2MOMs are visible to peers, you have a private planning doc, not a cascade.

### Refresh quarterly, review formally at mid-year

Context changes. A static V2MOM that hasn't been updated in six months is a lie about current priorities. Trailhead's cadence: quarterly team refresh, mid-year formal review, weekly reference in 1:1s.

**Trailhead's line to remember:** *"If it's not on the V2MOM it won't get done."*

### Write with beginner's mind — shoshin

Benioff, on Fortune Leadership Next (2024): *"Shoshin is the beginner's mind."* Applied to V2MOM: don't inherit last year's framing unchanged. Rewrite as if writing for the first time. If last year's Vision still resonates fresh, keep it — but the test is deliberate, not default.

## Don'ts

### Don't skip Obstacles

The single most common failure mode. People find Obstacles awkward — naming what could go wrong feels like admitting weakness, or looks like negative-framing culture. That awkwardness is exactly why the framework demands it. Every plan has obstacles; only some plans name them; only the ones that name them get to address them proactively.

**Anti-pattern to reject:** "We don't have any real obstacles right now." Every plan does. If the team can't name any, they haven't looked hard enough — or they're afraid to name what they see.

### Don't leave Values as a flat list

*"We value trust, innovation, and speed."* Fine as a poster, useless as a decision tool. If two of those conflict on a specific choice, which wins? Without ranking, no one knows, and the values do no decision work.

### Don't blame people or behaviors in Obstacles

**The 2023 Wellness Culture cautionary tale.** Benioff's FY24 V2MOM Obstacles section (Feb 2023) named: *"Wellness culture overpowered high performance culture during pandemic. Fear of escalations for people-related issues (burnout, psychological safety, equality, etc.) can make managers reticent to performance manage their teams."*

Employees called it tone-deaf on internal Slack; the line was removed within a week. Two lessons:

1. **V2MOM is public inside the company** — every word gets scrutinized in real time. That's a feature, not a bug, but it means writing carefully.
2. **Obstacles should name things to overcome, not people or values to blame.** "Slow hiring pipeline" is an obstacle; "wellness culture" is a values statement in obstacle-clothing. If it reads like a shot at a group of employees or a set of values, rewrite.

### Don't run V2MOM and OKRs in parallel

V2MOM's Measures section *is* your OKRs (if you must call them that). Running both means:
- Two docs to maintain.
- Team confusion about which is authoritative.
- The rich context of Values + Obstacles never gets referenced because everyone's in the OKR spreadsheet.

Pick one. Salesforce picked V2MOM. If you pick OKRs, use OKRs — but don't call it V2MOM.

### Don't clone your manager's V2MOM

Cascade means *alignment*, not *identity*. Your individual V2MOM should show how *you specifically* advance the team's methods, not restate what the team's V2MOM already says. Trailhead explicitly warns against this.

### Don't write more than one page

If your draft runs long, you haven't prioritized. Cutting is the exercise. Benioff's original fit on an envelope; yours should fit on a page.

### Don't inherit last year's V2MOM unchanged

Shoshin — beginner's mind. Rewrite each year deliberately. Copying last year's Vision because "it's still valid" is the same trap as delegating the writing: it removes the fresh thinking that makes the doc alive.

### Don't publish and forget

A V2MOM referenced only at the annual all-hands is a dead document. It should surface in weekly 1:1s, quarterly reviews, and any resourcing conversation. If your org can't sustain that cadence, name it up front and choose a lighter framework.

## Gotchas

### The "vision" people often write is actually a mission statement

A mission is *who we are and what we do*. A vision is *what does the better place look like when we succeed*. V2MOM asks for Vision, not Mission. If your Vision reads like "We are the leading provider of X for Y," it's a mission. Rewrite as: what does the world (or the customer, or the team) look like when we've succeeded?

### The "values" people write are often traits, not principles

*"We value curiosity, ownership, learning."* — those are personal traits. Values in V2MOM are principles that govern decisions: *"Customer trust before speed of shipping,"* *"Long-term customer success before short-term revenue."* Principles cut both ways — they tell you what you'll *give up* to honor them, not just what you like.

### Methods drift into aspirations

*"Be more customer-centric."* Not a method. *"Ship monthly customer advisory calls, rotate PM ownership."* — that's a method. Test: could you put it on a calendar?

### Obstacles drift into environmental commentary

*"The market is competitive."* Not an obstacle — that's a fact of your existence. *"Two competitors launched free tiers in Q4, pressuring our SMB pricing."* — that's an obstacle, and it points to a specific method (revise pricing, or double down on differentiator).

### Measures drift into activity metrics

*"Publish 50 blog posts."* is an activity, not a measure. *"Blog drives 20k MQLs per quarter."* is a measure of the activity's outcome. Trailhead's Progress vs. Completion Measure distinction: Progress = a target you're moving toward; Completion = binary, did-or-didn't. Both are legitimate; activity counts are neither.

## Common misapplications

### 1. "V2MOM = VMV template"

Someone reads Vision and Values, writes those, calls it a day. Result: a wall poster. The MOM (Methods-Obstacles-Measures) is what turns V2MOM from culture ornament into a plan.

**Diagnosis:** if your document has beautiful Vision + Values and thin Methods + Obstacles + Measures, you have a mission poster, not a V2MOM.

### 2. "V2MOM = OKR replacement with extra fluff"

Someone treats V→V as decorative and puts all the effort into Measures (=Key Results). Result: OKRs with a preamble no one reads.

**Diagnosis:** if your Values section reads like a paragraph you wrote at the last minute to fill the box, and your Methods section is empty because "the OKRs will drive the work," you have OKRs.

### 3. "V2MOM as one-time offsite output"

Team goes offsite, writes a V2MOM, pins it on a Notion page, never opens it again. Result: dead doc.

**Diagnosis:** ask "when was the last time this V2MOM was referenced in a decision?" If the answer is "at the offsite," it's dead.

### 4. "Individual V2MOM as clone of team V2MOM"

IC copies their manager's V2MOM and changes three words. Result: no personal accountability, no visible individual thinking, no cascade signal.

**Diagnosis:** compare the IC's V2MOM side-by-side with the team's. If >70% overlap, the IC didn't write theirs.

### 5. "Obstacles as CYA"

Team lists obstacles they're not planning to address, so that if things go wrong they can point to the doc. Result: hollow risk log.

**Diagnosis:** for each obstacle, ask what method addresses it. If none, either add a method or drop the obstacle.

### 6. "Measures as vanity metrics decoupled from Methods"

Team picks measures because they're easy to hit or already tracked, not because they reflect the methods. Result: green dashboard, wrong direction.

**Diagnosis:** for each measure, ask which method drives it. If the answer is "none, we just track it because we have the data," it doesn't belong on the V2MOM.

## Pro tips

### The 90-day new-hire rule

Salesforce policy: new hires wait ~90 days before publishing their first V2MOM. Enough time to know what their actual scope is. Applied to any org: if you're new to a role, don't write a full V2MOM in week one — you don't yet know what you're aligning to.

### Use V2MOM as the 1:1 frame

Trailhead's canonical use: managers reference the employee's V2MOM in every 1:1 as the frame for the conversation. What progress on Methods? Any Obstacle updates? Are Measures still tracking? This is what makes the doc alive.

### Use V2MOM for scope smaller than a year

Trailhead publishes an "Innovation Project V2MOM" module and Salesforce publishes an "Events V2MOM" template. V2MOM works at project or event scope, not just annual company-wide. When applying V2MOM to a smaller scope, keep the structure — just tighten the horizon of each element.

### The transparency test

If you'd be embarrassed for your team to see your V2MOM, either the V2MOM is wrong (and you should rewrite it) or your team's incentives are wrong (and that's the real work). At Salesforce, everyone can see everyone's V2MOM — the transparency is the mechanism that makes cascade real.

### Beginner's mind check every year

Before starting the annual V2MOM refresh, spend an hour re-reading it as if you'd never seen it. Where does last year's framing feel stale? Where does the vocabulary sound like autopilot? Rewrite those first.
