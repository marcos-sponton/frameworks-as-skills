# V2MOM — Worked examples

> Concrete V2MOMs at different scopes. The 1999 original (company-wide, 4-person startup) is verbatim from Benioff's American Express envelope. The others are illustrative — the shape and load-bearing rules are what matters.

## 1. The 1999 original Salesforce V2MOM (verbatim from the envelope)

Written by Marc Benioff in the opening weeks of Salesforce, on a large American Express envelope. Preserved by co-founder Parker Harris and framed as a gift on IPO day (2004). Considered the founding artifact of Salesforce's planning discipline.

**Vision:**
Rapidly create a world-class Internet company / site for sales-force automation.

**Values:**
1. World-class organization
2. Time to market
3. Functional
4. Usability
5. Value to customer / investor

**Methods:**
1. Hire the team
2. Finalize product specification and technical architecture
3. Rapidly develop to beta and production
4. Build partnerships with e-commerce, content, and hosting companies
5. Build launch plan
6. Develop exit strategy (IPO / acquisition)

**Obstacles:**
- Developer recruitment
- Product manager / business development hiring

**Measures:**
- Prototype is state-of-the-art
- High-quality functional system
- Partnerships online and integrated
- Regarded as leader and visionary
- "We are all rich"

**What to notice:**

- **One page.** Fits on an envelope. Total content is under 150 words.
- **Vision is a real choice.** "World-class Internet company for sales-force automation" — not a generic "we will disrupt CRM." Specific enough to constrain.
- **Values are ordered.** "World-class organization" first, then "time to market" — Benioff was signaling that if he had to trade org quality for speed, he'd protect org quality. Two decades later this ordering shows up as why Salesforce hires senior early and pays above market.
- **Methods are actions.** Every one starts with a verb. "Hire the team" not "attract world-class talent." Schedulable.
- **Obstacles are honest and specific.** At 4 people, the two things that would hurt Salesforce most were both hiring problems. Both paired to Method #1.
- **Measures are mostly binary.** Pre-revenue, you can't set ARR targets. Completion Measures dominate. The last one — "we are all rich" — is Benioff being Benioff, but it's also the ultimate outcome measure that shaped exit strategy.

## 2. Individual V2MOM — Content Marketing Manager (illustrative)

An IC-level V2MOM for a content marketing manager at a mid-sized SaaS company, aligned to a team V2MOM aligned to the corporate V2MOM. Illustrative, not from Salesforce.

**Vision:**
Become a trusted voice for RevOps leaders in mid-market SaaS, and position [Company] as the go-to platform for revenue teams that want AI-native workflows without vendor lock-in.

**Values:**
1. **Craft** — every piece ships when it's actually good, not when the calendar says so. Trumps volume.
2. **Customer voice** — real customer stories, quotes, and pain, always. No generic think-pieces.
3. **Ownership** — I ship, I measure, I revise. No handoffs where I could act.

**Methods:**
1. Ship two high-quality articles per week targeting RevOps leaders in 200-2000-employee SaaS orgs.
2. Interview four customers per quarter, publish two as case studies and use quotes across all content.
3. Co-produce quarterly launch campaigns with product marketing, owning the content pillar of each.
4. Manage the SEO backlog for RevOps topical cluster; hit six top-3 rankings this year.
5. Ghost-write two LinkedIn posts per week for the CEO from customer conversations I source.

**Obstacles:**
- Sales team is skeptical of content ROI and drops customer intros when quarter is tight. Countermeasure: build a lightweight "customer story of the month" ritual with sales leadership so intros stay warm.
- I don't own paid distribution; strong pieces underperform if the paid team doesn't amplify. Countermeasure: quarterly co-planning meeting with paid marketing.
- I'm the only content person; sick weeks = zero output. Countermeasure: 2-week content buffer at all times.

**Measures:**
- 20,000 monthly organic blog visits by end of FY.
- 40 marketing-qualified leads per quarter attributed to content.
- 6 published customer case studies by end of FY.
- 6 top-3 SEO rankings for target RevOps keywords by end of FY.
- CEO LinkedIn posts averaging 500+ reactions each.

**What to notice:**

- **Values do decision work.** "Craft trumps volume" — the IC has committed to *not* ship an article that isn't good, even if the content calendar says so. If pressed by their manager to hit weekly cadence with a weak draft, the ranked value gives them ground to push back.
- **Every obstacle has a countermeasure.** No obstacle listed without an action.
- **Every measure ties to a method.** MQLs → Method 1, 2, 4. Case studies → Method 2. SEO rankings → Method 4. CEO reactions → Method 5.
- **The V2MOM is *this IC's own choices*.** It doesn't restate the team's marketing V2MOM; it shows how this IC specifically advances it.

## 3. Project-scope V2MOM — Product launch (illustrative)

A V2MOM for a single product launch, running ~90 days. Illustrates that V2MOM works below the annual/company scope.

**Vision:**
Launch [Product] as the AI-native default for RevOps teams that have outgrown their spreadsheets but resist committing to Salesforce, capturing first-mover positioning before the two rumored competitor launches in Q4.

**Values:**
1. **Trust with early customers** — no promises we can't keep at launch. If a feature isn't ready, we don't demo it.
2. **Speed to signal** — better to launch small and learn than launch complete and guess.
3. **Cross-functional respect** — product, eng, marketing, sales, and CS make this call together.

**Methods:**
1. Ship the "waitlist demo" 4 weeks pre-launch to 30 warm prospects; capture objections into a shared doc daily.
2. Publish 6 launch-content assets (2 blog posts, 1 keynote video, 1 case study, 1 pricing page, 1 demo video) by launch date.
3. Train the SDR team on the ICP + top-3 objections through 3 role-play sessions in launch week -2 and -1.
4. Run the launch webinar with the CEO + one customer + one industry analyst as guests.
5. Set up the post-launch feedback loop: weekly customer call for first 4 weeks, product-eng-support triage every Monday.

**Obstacles:**
- Engineering commitments to another product line may slip our stability requirements. Countermeasure: weekly VPE sync starting launch week -6; escalation path defined.
- Sales team hasn't sold this ICP before; ramp is unclear. Countermeasure: pair each AE with a CS lead for first 10 deals.
- Two rumored competitor launches in Q4 could dilute press. Countermeasure: schedule launch 3 weeks before their earliest rumored date; secure exclusive with target publication.

**Measures:**
- 30 waitlist customers converted to paying design partners by launch +30 days.
- $500k ARR closed by launch +90 days.
- 3 launch-day press placements (target: TechCrunch, HN front page, one industry pub).
- CSAT for design partners: 8.5+/10 at launch +60 days.
- 90%+ SDR quota attainment on new-ICP calls by launch +45 days.

**What to notice:**

- **Same five elements at project scope.** The V2MOM structure is unchanged; the horizon is 90 days instead of a fiscal year.
- **Values are project-specific.** "Trust with early customers" is a rule specifically for a launch, where over-promising can poison future customer relationships even if it hits the number.
- **Obstacles are named specifically.** Not "the market is competitive" — actual specific obstacles for *this* launch.
- **Measures are timed.** Launch +30, +45, +60, +90. Explicit horizons for each measure.

## 4. What a bad V2MOM looks like (anti-example)

For contrast — a common failure mode:

**Vision:**
To be the most innovative platform in our category, empowering every business to unlock their data potential.

**Values:**
Innovation. Excellence. Customer obsession. Integrity. Teamwork. Diversity.

**Methods:**
1. Grow ARR from $50M to $100M.
2. Improve NPS.
3. Expand into EMEA.
4. Launch enterprise tier.
5. Reduce churn.
6. Invest in AI capabilities.
7. Build a stronger brand.
8. Achieve profitability.

**Obstacles:**
- The market is competitive.
- We're constrained by hiring.
- Sales cycles are getting longer.

**Measures:**
- ARR: $50M → $100M.
- NPS: 30 → 50.
- Gross margin: 65% → 75%.

**What's wrong:**

- **Vision fails the coin-flip test.** "Most innovative platform" — a customer looking at two products from your category can't tell which is more innovative. Not a real vision.
- **Six unranked values.** All flat. When Innovation conflicts with Excellence (ship the AI feature fast vs. audit it first), no one knows which wins. Values do no decision work.
- **Eight priorities.** Everything is a priority, so nothing is. Also, Method 1 (grow ARR) is a *measure* dressed as a method — how are you going to grow it?
- **Methods 1, 2, 5, 8 conflict with each other.** Grow ARR fast AND improve NPS AND reduce churn AND achieve profitability. Any two of those pull against the others. No prioritization.
- **Obstacles are environmental facts, not addressable obstacles.** "The market is competitive" is your permanent operating condition; it's not something to overcome. No countermeasures listed.
- **Measures are decoupled from Methods.** ARR ties to Method 1 (which is itself circular). NPS ties to Method 2 (which is also circular). Gross margin ties to nothing. No causal chain.

**Diagnosis: this is not a V2MOM. It's a mission statement + a priority list + a KPI dashboard, wearing V2MOM's five headings.**

**The fix:** restart from the Vision. Ask *"what does the better place actually look like — specifically enough that a customer would recognize it?"* Then rank values. Then decide which of the eight priorities are actually methods (verbs, schedulable) vs. which are outcomes (measures). Then name real obstacles with countermeasures.
