# Inspired — Marty Cagan and SVPG's Product Operating Model

Marty Cagan and Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG)'s opinionated frame for how product organizations should operate — distilled from *INSPIRED: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love* (Wiley, 2008; 2nd ed. 2018), *EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products* (Wiley 2020, with Chris Jones), *TRANSFORMED: Moving to the Product Operating Model* (Wiley 2024, with Lea Hickman, Chris Jones, Christian Idiodi, John Moore), and the ~weekly essay stream at [svpg.com/articles](https://www.svpg.com/articles/).

The frame is opinionated because Cagan is opinionated: the product operating model is *not* a process, *not* Scrum, *not* SAFe, *not* an "outcome-based roadmap." It is a set of first principles about how the best product companies actually work — empowered teams, product discovery before delivery, the four big risks attacked early with prototypes, product vision as a persuasion tool, product strategy as focus + insights + bets, and coaching as the primary leadership responsibility. Applying the frame means resisting the industry reflexes (rebrand POs as PMs, adopt SAFe, publish a roadmap in outcome clothing) and holding the line on what Cagan calls the first principles of the model.

## When this skill activates

**Use this skill when the user is:**
- Diagnosing why their product org ships features but outcomes stay flat (feature-factory diagnosis).
- Auditing a product organization against the Product Operating Model — team structure, PM role, discovery/delivery split, coaching, culture.
- Deciding whether/how to shift from a feature-team model to empowered product teams.
- Writing or critiquing a **product vision** (2–5yr software / 5–10yr device horizon) or a **product strategy** (focus + insights + bets).
- Replacing a roadmap with product vision + team-level objectives + high-integrity commitments.
- Running or designing **product discovery** — prototypes, experiments, attacking the four big risks early.
- Staffing an empowered product team (real PM + real designer + real lead engineer + 2–10 engineers; two-pizza rule).
- Planning a product transformation, including a **pilot team** as the "MVP for the full transformation."
- Distinguishing real product managers from **backlog administrators** / **product management theater** / relabeled scrum roles.
- Thinking about AI's impact on the product operating model — the productivity paradox, build-to-learn vs. build-to-earn.
- Debating product ownership, missionaries vs. mercenaries, or the mini-CEO myth.

**Do NOT use this skill when:**
- The org is **pre-product/market-fit**. Cagan's own advice: don't hire PMs before PMF. Reach for discovery-and-search frames (JTBD / Torres / Lean Startup / Ries).
- The question is about the **PM function's operational infrastructure specifically** — career ladders, product ops function design, PM-role incentives. Cagan endorses Perri here; reach for [[escaping-the-build-trap]] and use this skill as the umbrella.
- The question is about **B2B positioning / marketing / launch**. Reach for Lauchengco's *LOVED* directly, or Dunford's [[obviously-awesome]] for positioning.
- The question is about **firm-level competitive advantage**. Reach for [[7-powers]] (Helmer) or [[playing-to-win]] (Martin).
- The user just wants a summary of *Inspired*. Give the book link and don't run the frame.

If the situation is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question before diagnosing.

## The frame at a glance

Cagan does not offer a single linear method — he offers a set of *first principles* and *devices* that fit together. The three canonical altitudes:

1. **The Product Team.** Empowered, cross-functional (PM + designer + lead engineer + 2–10 engineers), given problems to solve and desired outcomes — not features to ship. Accountable for outcomes. Ownership across discovery AND delivery.
2. **The Product Discovery / Product Delivery split.** Discovery = build to learn (prototypes, experiments, attack the four risks: value / usability / feasibility / viability). Delivery = build to earn (commercial-quality, scale, reliability, security).
3. **Product Leadership.** Owns the **product vision** (2–5yr / 5–10yr, a persuasion tool, ideally a visiontype prototype video), the **product strategy** (focus + insights + placing bets + active management), and the **coaching** that develops the PMs and designers.

Together these form the **Product Operating Model (POM)** — the 2024 umbrella term from *Transformed*. Three dimensions:
- **How products are built** — small, frequent, reliable releases; ideally CI/CD.
- **How problems are solved** — cross-functional teams tackle problems, addressing all four risks.
- **How you decide which problems to solve** — customer-focused vision + insight-driven strategy.

The book *Transformed* systematizes the model into **20 First Principles** across three groups (Product Team, Product Strategy, Product Discovery/Delivery/Culture). See `references/method.md` for the full set.

## How to use this skill in a session

1. **Ask what altitude the user is at.** *Team-level* (PM role, discovery, delivery)? *Leadership-level* (vision, strategy, coaching)? *Org-level* (transformation, POM, pilot team)? Cagan's three books map to these altitudes. Get the altitude right before diagnosing. Load `references/method.md`.

2. **Diagnose the feature-team pattern by name.** If the symptoms match — prioritized feature lists, dates on unvalidated solutions, PMs writing user stories, "product owner" as the title, roadmap-as-sales-contract — name it: *this is a feature team, not a product team*. Don't soften. Load `references/heuristics.md` for the anti-pattern list.

3. **Locate the four big risks in whatever they're describing.** Value, usability, feasibility, viability. Which are they attacking early (in discovery) and which are they discovering late (in delivery, when it's expensive)? This is the single sharpest diagnostic Cagan gives. Load `references/method.md`.

4. **Push back on roadmaps as the artifact.** Even "outcome-based" roadmaps are still roadmaps in Cagan's view. The alternative is product vision + team objectives + high-integrity commitments — replace, don't fix. Load `references/method.md` and `references/post-book.md`.

5. **When the topic is transformation — reach for *Transformed* material, not *Inspired*.** The 20 First Principles, the pilot-team playbook, the three case studies in the book. Most Claude/Codex responses default to *Inspired*; the differential is *Transformed*. Load `references/post-book.md`.

6. **When the topic is AI — reach for the 2026 essays.** Productivity paradox, build-to-learn vs. build-to-earn, AI as multiplier not equalizer. Load `references/post-book.md` and `references/author-live-sources.md`.

7. **Match Cagan's voice when responding on his frame's behalf.** Direct, opinionated, first-person plural ("we", meaning SVPG and the best product companies). Contrast pairs. "The best product companies…" as authority move. Names anti-patterns without softening. Load `references/voice-and-tone.md`.

8. **Cite sources.** SVPG essays are dated and canonical; the three books are chapter-navigable. Attribution respects Cagan's work and lets the user go deeper. Load `references/sources.md`.

## Non-negotiables

- **Fidelity to Cagan.** This is his frame, not a generic product-management skill. Don't collapse it into "product best practices." Don't blend with Perri, Torres, or Martin unless the user explicitly asks — Cagan composes with those authors but the composition is opinionated (see `references/applications.md`).
- **Span all three books.** Most Claude/Codex responses about "Inspired" pull only from the 2008 book. The frame lives across *Inspired* (product-team + discovery), *Empowered* (leadership + coaching), and *Transformed* (POM + 20 First Principles + transformation). If the user's question is about transformation, the answer is not in *Inspired*.
- **Preserve the opinion.** Cagan is direct about anti-patterns (feature team, SAFe, backlog administrator, product management theater, roadmap-as-commitment). Softening the diagnosis to be diplomatic collapses the method. He is warm about humans, cold about patterns.
- **Attribution matters.** When quoting Cagan, cite (book chapter, SVPG essay URL, Lenny episode). When paraphrasing, name the source. This skill is a distillation, not a substitute for his writing or SVPG's transformation engagements.
- **Explicit uncertainty when he shifts.** Cagan has publicly reversed positions — the 2026 *Product Coaching and AI* essay is the signature example. Don't collapse 20 years of thinking into a flat voice. When his position has evolved (e.g., roadmaps, coaching, AI), name the shift and cite both the old and new position.
