# Inspired — Invocation Prompts

> Templates for common tasks the assistant will do inside this skill. Copy-paste these to start a session, or use them as scaffolds for your own invocation. All framed to preserve Cagan's opinionated voice.

## Team-level diagnostic

**"Diagnose whether we're a product team or a feature team."**

> Here's how our team runs: [describe team composition, how work arrives, how success is measured, roadmap format, PM role, discovery cadence, delivery cadence].
>
> Apply Cagan's product-team-vs-feature-team diagnostic. Name the pattern with attribution. Identify which of the four big risks we're attacking early (in discovery) vs. late (in delivery). Point at the specific SVPG essays / book chapters where our anti-patterns live. Don't sanitize — Cagan is direct about anti-patterns.

## PM role audit

**"Is my PM role real or is it product management theater?"**

> Here's my job description and what I actually spend my week on: [describe]. My title is [PM / Product Owner / other]. My reporting line is [to whom]. I'm accountable for [what].
>
> Apply Cagan's diagnostic. Am I a real product manager (accountable for value + viability), a backlog administrator, or in product management theater? Cite the Lenny 2024 episode + the four-risks essay. If I'm in the pattern, name what would have to change.

## Product vision review

**"Critique our product vision using Cagan's frame."**

> Here's our product vision: [paste].
>
> Apply Cagan's [Product Vision vs. Mission](https://www.svpg.com/product-vision-vs-mission/) test. Is this a vision or a mission statement? Does it have the right time horizon (2–5yr software / 5–10yr device)? Is it a persuasion tool — compelling, inspiring, empowering? Would it enable product teams to coordinate without micromanagement? If it's not doing the job, tell me what would.

## Product strategy audit

**"Audit our product strategy against Cagan's four requirements."**

> Here's our product strategy: [paste].
>
> Apply Cagan's four requirements: focus (are we saying no to enough), insights (from data / customers / tech / industry, not opinion), actions (converted into problems assigned to teams), active management (managing the portfolio without micromanaging). Reference Roger Martin's Playing to Win as the composable strategy frame at the corporate level, and Perri's fuzzy-strategy diagnostic if strategy is unclear at the exec level.

## Roadmap replacement

**"Replace our roadmap with Cagan's alternative."**

> Here's our current roadmap: [paste].
>
> Apply Cagan's position from [The Alternative to Roadmaps](https://www.svpg.com/the-alternative-to-roadmaps/) — replace, don't fix. Show me what the three replacement artifacts would look like for us: (1) product vision at the multi-year horizon, (2) business objectives per team (OKR-shaped, problems + desired outcomes), (3) high-integrity commitments for the rare cases requiring a date. Push back if I try to keep the roadmap in outcome clothing.

## Discovery cadence design

**"Help me stand up product discovery against the four big risks."**

> We're a [size / stage] product team. We currently do [describe current discovery cadence, if any]. Our team is [PM / designer / engineers count].
>
> Walk me through Cagan's product discovery. Start with the four big risks (value / usability / feasibility / viability) — which are we currently under-attacking, and how do we know? Prescribe a prototype-first cadence. Compose with Torres's OST and continuous discovery cadence if useful. Cite Cagan's [Product Discovery](https://www.svpg.com/product-discovery/) + [Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/) + [Build to Learn vs Build to Earn](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn/).

## Transformation planning

**"We want to move to the Product Operating Model. Where do we start?"**

> Our org: [size / stage / current model — feature team / SAFe / other]. Leadership commitment: [level]. Timeline: [what leadership wants].
>
> Apply Cagan's transformation playbook from *Transformed* (2024). Start with the pilot-team method from [The Politics of Pilot Teams](https://www.svpg.com/the-politics-of-pilot-teams/). Push back on any "transformation by memo" instinct. Walk me through: (a) pilot team composition (hand-picked bar-raisers, not average performers); (b) problem selection ("impressive but not impossible"); (c) success metric (business outcome); (d) what leadership behavior has to change; (e) common transformation traps. Cite the 20 First Principles as the constitution.

## Product management theater diagnosis

**"Is our PM function real or is it theater?"**

> Describe our PM function: [count / titles / what they own / what they're measured on / typical week]. Executives say we have a product organization; the outputs suggest otherwise.
>
> Apply Cagan's [product management theater](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/product-management-theater-marty) diagnosis from the Lenny 2024 episode. Are our PMs accountable for value + viability, or are they administering backlogs? Name the pattern. If it's theater, what would have to change — and separately, name the ZIRP context if relevant.

## AI + POM diagnostic

**"How should we think about AI in our product organization?"**

> Our org: [context]. Current AI adoption: [what tools / where deployed]. Executives believe AI will [expectation].
>
> Apply Cagan's [AI Productivity Paradox](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/) frame. Warn against AI-as-productivity-lever without operating-model change. Reference the 89%/6% stat. Apply the [Build to Learn vs Build to Earn](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn/) frame: if AI collapses delivery cost, discovery becomes the new bottleneck — and that's where most orgs are worst. Give me a concrete diagnosis of what's likely to happen in our org if we accelerate AI without changing the operating model.

## Composition prompt (Cagan + Perri)

**"Audit both the POM and the operational scaffolding underneath."**

> Our org: [context]. We think we've moved to the Product Operating Model but outcomes are still flat.
>
> Compose Cagan's frame (top-level POM: empowered teams, four risks, vision, strategy, coaching) with Perri's frame (operational scaffolding: PM career ladders, Product Ops Three Pillars, Four Dimensions audit). Point at [[escaping-the-build-trap]] for the Perri altitude. Identify whether the failure is at the POM level (top-down empowerment isn't real), the Perri level (no operational infrastructure supporting evidence-based decisions), or both.

## Composition prompt (Cagan + Torres)

**"Design our discovery cadence."**

> Our team: [size]. Product: [maturity]. Current discovery cadence: [describe, or "none"].
>
> Design a discovery cadence composing Cagan (four big risks, prototype-first, discovery vs. delivery split) with Torres (weekly customer interviews, Opportunity Solution Tree, product trio). Show what week 1, week 4, and week 12 would look like. Cite both authors. Push back on Cagan-alone (missing weekly cadence) or Torres-alone (missing operating-model context).

## Composition prompt (Cagan + Martin)

**"Move from corporate strategy to product operating model."**

> We just finished a Playing to Win exercise at the corporate level: [paste WA / WTP / HTW / capabilities / management systems].
>
> Translate that corporate strategy into the product operating model layer using Cagan's frame. Show how the corporate HTW cascades into product initiatives, into team-level problems + desired outcomes. Cite Cagan + Martin. Push back if the corporate strategy is too abstract to translate.

## Voice check

**"Rewrite this in Cagan's voice."**

> Here's a draft describing our product operating changes: [paste].
>
> Rewrite in Cagan's voice — direct, opinionated, first-person plural ("we at SVPG…" / "the best product companies…"), contrast pairs (product vs. feature team, outcomes vs. output, missionaries vs. mercenaries, discovery vs. delivery), named anti-patterns, single-sentence definitional resets. Warm about humans, cold about patterns. Don't sanitize.

## Skill entry template

If unsure which prompt to use, start with:

> Apply the Inspired skill (Cagan + SVPG POM) to my situation: [describe].
>
> Ask me one clarifying question before diagnosing — specifically about which altitude I'm at (team, leadership, org-level transformation) and whether we're pre- or post-PMF.
