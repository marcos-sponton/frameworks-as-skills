# Inspired — Applications

> When Cagan's Product Operating Model is the right frame, when it isn't, and how it composes with adjacent frameworks. The point: Cagan's frame is opinionated. Composing it well with other frameworks (Perri, Torres, Martin, Ries, Amazon Working Backwards, JTBD, Helmer) requires knowing what each contributes.

## When the frame fits

Reach for this skill when:

- Auditing an existing product organization against the empowered-team model.
- Diagnosing why a product org ships features but outcomes stay flat.
- Designing team charters, PM role definitions, or team structure.
- Writing or critiquing a product vision (2–5yr software / 5–10yr device horizon).
- Writing or critiquing a product strategy — focus, insights, bets, active management.
- Replacing a roadmap with vision + team objectives + high-integrity commitments.
- Standing up a discovery cadence against the four risks.
- Planning a POM transformation, including pilot-team design.
- Thinking about AI's effect on product operating model (productivity paradox, build-to-learn shift).
- Recovering a team from "product management theater" pattern (relabeled scrum roles).

## When it does NOT fit

Do NOT reach for this skill when:

- **Pre-product/market fit.** Cagan's own advice: don't hire PMs before PMF. The POM assumes a product and a scale where the pattern exists. Pre-PMF orgs need discovery/search frames — reach for JTBD (Christensen / Moesta / Kalbach), Torres's *Continuous Discovery Habits*, or Ries's *Lean Startup*.
- **Product marketing / launch / positioning.** Reach for Lauchengco's *LOVED* directly, or Dunford's [[obviously-awesome]] for positioning.
- **PM function's operational infrastructure specifically** — career ladders, Product Ops function design, PM incentives. Cagan endorses Perri here; reach for [[escaping-the-build-trap]] and use this skill as the umbrella above.
- **Firm-level competitive advantage.** Reach for Helmer's [[7-powers]] (Power taxonomy) or Rumelt's [[good-strategy-bad-strategy]] (diagnosis-first).
- **Corporate strategy cascade.** Reach for Martin's [[playing-to-win]].
- **Delivery cadence design specifically.** Shape Up (Singer) or DORA/SRE frames are more precise.
- **Individual PM career advice.** Empowered has some, but Lenny's newsletter has more specific career material.
- **Enterprise sales / customer-success ops.** Not the POM's domain.

## Adjacent frameworks — how they compose

### [[escaping-the-build-trap]] — Melissa Perri
- **Complementary. One altitude apart.** Cagan = organizational transformation / POM at the top level. Perri = operational infrastructure that makes the transformation stick + PM career development.
- **Cagan explicitly adopts Perri's definition of Product Operations:** *"the art of removing obstacles from evidence-based decision making."*
- **Perri does not compete on POM as a term.** Cedes to Cagan.
- **Rules:** if the user's question is about top-level transformation or the empowered-team model, reach for this skill. If it's about the PM function's operational scaffolding (Product Ops team, career ladders, Four Dimensions audit), reach for Perri. Use both if the situation is layered.
- **Watch for:** Perri's frame is more diagnostic-oriented (Four Dimensions as audit); Cagan's is more prescriptive (20 First Principles as constitution). Both are valid; don't collapse them.

### Continuous Discovery Habits — Teresa Torres
- **Highly compatible.** Cagan wrote the foreword to Torres's book.
- **Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree** and **weekly customer-interview cadence** are the operational cadence for Cagan's product discovery principle.
- **Torres coined "product trio"** — Cagan uses it approvingly.
- **Rules:** when the user's specific question is *how do we actually run continuous discovery week to week*, reach for Torres inside Cagan's frame. When the question is broader (team structure, leadership, strategy), stay in this skill.
- **Watch for:** OST is a tool, not the whole discovery method. Cagan's frame is bigger.

### Shape Up — Ryan Singer / Basecamp
- **Compatible on delivery discipline.** Shape Up's "shape the work before betting" and "appetite over estimate" are consistent with Cagan's rejection of Gantt-style roadmaps.
- **Shape Up is a delivery cadence discipline; Cagan's frame is broader** (vision, strategy, discovery, delivery, culture, leadership).
- **Rules:** if the user's specific question is *how do we structure 6-week cycles with clear appetite*, Shape Up is more precise. If it's about the overall operating model, stay in this skill.

### Playing to Win — Roger Martin ([[playing-to-win]])
- **Cagan cites Martin approvingly** for strategy-making. When Cagan says "product strategy," the underlying discipline (focus / insights / bets / active management) is Martin-compatible.
- **Composition:** Martin's cascade at the corporate level → Cagan's POM at the product-org level → team-level problems and outcomes flow down.
- **Rules:** if the strategic question is *should we enter this market, how do we win against these competitors*, reach for Martin. If it's *how does our product org actually operate against that strategy*, stay in this skill.

### Amazon Working Backwards — Colin Bryar & Bill Carr
- **Compatible on customer-first reasoning.** Working Backwards PR/FAQ starts from the customer end-state and reasons back — same discipline as Cagan's visiontype prototype.
- **Divergence:** Working Backwards is document-first (the PR/FAQ document). Cagan is prototype-first (the visiontype video). Both fit the POM.
- **Rules:** if the user is at Amazon or an org that uses PR/FAQ, don't fight it. Compose: PR/FAQ = the vision artifact; discovery + delivery = how you actually build.

### Jobs to Be Done — Christensen / Moesta / Kalbach
- **Compatible.** Cagan uses "customer problems" and "outcomes" as his primary units, not "jobs." But the underlying discipline (understand demand deeply before designing supply) is the same.
- **Rules:** a team using JTBD inside Cagan's discovery is fine. A team using JTBD *instead of* the POM is missing the operating-model layer.
- **Watch for:** JTBD advocates sometimes overclaim. It's a discovery lens, not a full operating model.

### Lean Startup — Eric Ries
- **Foundational compatible influence.** Cagan retains **build-measure-learn** and MVP concept.
- **Cagan treats "MVP" carefully** — prefers *prototype* for discovery and *release* for delivery, because MVP got degraded to mean "small first version to ship."
- **Cagan endorses Ries's newer *Incorruptible*** work on mission-locked governance (2026 essay [Great Products, Bad Companies](https://www.svpg.com/great-products-bad-companies/)).
- **Rules:** pre-PMF, Ries is more precise. Post-PMF, Cagan is the operating model.

### SAFe / Scaled Agile Framework
- **Explicit anti-endorsement from Cagan and Perri.** SAFe rewards output cadence and encodes command-and-control in scaled ceremony.
- **Rules:** if the org has adopted SAFe and calls it POM, name the mistake. If leadership is committed to SAFe, this skill can still help at the team level but the top-level frame is fighting SAFe.

### Scrum
- **Cagan is agnostic-to-hostile** when Scrum becomes the point (ceremonies as religion) rather than a vehicle.
- **Empowered teams sometimes use Scrum, sometimes don't.** Cagan's argument: the operating model is upstream of the process choice.
- **Rules:** don't debate Scrum vs. Kanban. Debate the operating model.

### 7 Powers — Hamilton Helmer ([[7-powers]])
- **Different altitude.** Helmer = firm-level competitive advantage taxonomy. Cagan = product-org operating model.
- **Composition:** strong POM is *necessary but not sufficient* for building Power. Power is what the POM's compounding advantage becomes over years. A feature-team company almost never builds Power at the product level; an empowered-team company can.
- **Rules:** if the user's question is *why do we win vs. competitors*, reach for Helmer. If it's *how does our product org operate to build that advantage*, stay in this skill.

### Good Strategy Bad Strategy — Richard Rumelt ([[good-strategy-bad-strategy]])
- **Cagan-compatible on the diagnosis-first move.** Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent action) is orthogonal to but compatible with the POM.
- **Rules:** when the user's problem is *what is actually wrong with our strategy*, reach for Rumelt. When it's *how does our product org operate against a strategy that's already in place*, stay in this skill.

### Obviously Awesome / Sales Pitch — April Dunford ([[obviously-awesome]])
- **Non-overlapping.** Dunford = positioning + sales conversation. Cagan = product operating model.
- **Composition:** Dunford's positioning informs the vision and strategy in Cagan's frame; Cagan's frame decides what to build against that positioning.
- **Rules:** distinct domains. Reach for both when the user's question spans product and go-to-market.

### Strategic Narrative — Andy Raskin
- **Non-overlapping.** Raskin = company-level narrative (name the change, promised land, magic gifts). Cagan = product-org operating model.
- **Composition:** Raskin's narrative can inform the product vision; the product vision is a specific artifact inside Cagan's frame.

## Composition rules

The three most-common compositions in real product-org work:

1. **Cagan + Perri** — POM as the umbrella (Cagan), operational scaffolding inside (Perri: Product Ops Three Pillars, PM career ladder, Four Dimensions audit).
2. **Cagan + Torres** — POM as the umbrella (Cagan), continuous discovery cadence inside (Torres: OST, weekly interviews, product trio).
3. **Cagan + Martin** — corporate strategy at the top (Martin's cascade), product operating model inside (Cagan).

If the user is doing all three at once, it's not overkill — it's usually where the actual work is. Load multiple skills but stay disciplined about which one owns which question.

## Anti-composition (don't do this)

- **Don't blend the POM with Scrum/SAFe language.** They fight each other.
- **Don't apply Cagan's discovery/delivery split pre-PMF.** Pre-PMF, there is no delivery in the "commercial quality" sense yet.
- **Don't treat "outcomes" from Cagan and "outcomes" from OKR-vendor talk as the same word.** OKR vendors often mean "quantified feature milestones"; Cagan means "customer or business behavior change."
- **Don't collapse Cagan and Perri.** They cede to each other on specific terms (POM to Cagan; Product Ops definition to Perri). Preserving the cession is part of the fidelity.
