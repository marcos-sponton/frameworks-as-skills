# Inspired — Material posterior a *Inspired*

> **This is the differential of this skill.** *Inspired* (2008, 2nd ed. 2018) laid down the Product Team, the four big risks, product discovery, and the product manager role. Since then, Cagan and SVPG have published:
> - **A second book:** *EMPOWERED* (2020, with Chris Jones) — the product leadership + coaching book.
> - **A third book:** *TRANSFORMED* (2024, with Lea Hickman, Chris Jones, Christian Idiodi, John Moore) — the transformation playbook, the 20 First Principles, and three first-person transformation case studies.
> - **A companion:** *LOVED* (2022, Martina Lauchengco with Cagan foreword) — the SVPG canon on product marketing.
> - **~Weekly SVPG essays** since 2007, actively growing — including canonical evergreen essays and 2024–2026 refinements.
> - **Podcast appearances** — most-referenced modern intro: Lenny Rachitsky's "Product management theater" (2024) tied to the *Transformed* launch, and "The nature of product" (2022).
> - **SVPG's own podcast** *Product Therapy* (Christian Idiodi + Lea Hickman + Chris Jones) — Cagan appears occasionally.
>
> Most model priors default to *Inspired* alone. This file captures the sixteen years of subsequent refinements — the leadership altitude, the transformation altitude, the 20 First Principles, the 2024–2026 essays, the AI-era position, and the explicit reversals Cagan has flagged. Organized so you can pull the specific piece you need.

## 2.1 EMPOWERED (2020, with Chris Jones) — the product leadership book

Full title: *EMPOWERED: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products.* Wiley 2020. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/EMPOWERED-Ordinary-Extraordinary-Products-Silicon/dp/111969129X) · [SVPG book page](https://www.svpg.com/books/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/).

Cagan wrote *Empowered* because *Inspired* left the product-leadership altitude thin. The complaint from readers of *Inspired* was: "We have PMs but they still can't produce outcomes — what's wrong at the leadership level?" *Empowered* is the answer.

### Core moves

- **Coaching is the primary responsibility of product leadership.** Managers of PMs, designers, and engineers should be coaches first. Career development for each person is a first-class management output — not extracurricular.
- **Staffing bar-raisers.** Hire ordinary people who are coachable and mission-aligned. Develop them into extraordinary ones. The hiring bar is about coachability + mission-fit, not superhero credentials.
- **Product vision + product strategy = the leadership deliverables.** Empowered teams get the "what problem"; leadership owns the "why we're trying to win here."
- **Evangelism as the leader's job.** The vision has to be sold internally, repeatedly. Not memoed once, then referenced.
- **Missionaries, not mercenaries.** John Doerr quote Cagan uses in ~every talk: *"We need teams of missionaries, not teams of mercenaries."* Missionaries believe in the vision. Mercenaries "basically build whatever they're told to." Load-bearing example: Amazon does not outsource core empowered-team functions because outsourcing manufactures mercenaries.
- **Product culture as the enduring moat.** Great products are the surface artifact; the underlying culture — psychological safety, principled disagreement, learning from failure, evidence over authority — is what compounds over years.

### Case studies in *Empowered*

Amazon, Google, Netflix, Apple as the modern archetypes. Deep on how each company staffs, coaches, and empowers.

### When to reach for Empowered material in a session

- User's question is about **product leadership** — how to lead a PM team, what a Head of Product should own, how to coach PMs.
- User's question is about **staffing** — hiring, PM career development, promotion criteria.
- User is trying to move a team from feature-team → empowered team and needs to know what leadership behavior has to change.
- User mentions "mini-CEO", "missionaries", "mercenaries", "coaching", "evangelism" — these are Empowered vocabulary.

## 2.2 TRANSFORMED (2024) — the transformation playbook

Full title: *TRANSFORMED: Moving to the Product Operating Model.* Wiley 2024. Co-authors: Lea Hickman, Chris Jones, Christian Idiodi, John Moore. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Transformed-Becoming-Product-Driven-Company-Silicon/dp/1119697336) · [SVPG book page](https://www.svpg.com/books/transformed-moving-to-the-product-operating-model/).

The book Cagan spent 2020–2024 developing because *Empowered* left the "how do we get there from here" question unanswered for legacy orgs. Each co-author led a real transformation at a large pre-Internet company; three first-person case studies are embedded.

### The Product Operating Model (POM) umbrella

*Transformed* introduces the term **Product Operating Model** as the umbrella for the SVPG frame. Definition from the accompanying essay [The Product Operating Model: An Introduction](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction/):

> "A conceptual model based on a set of first principles that leading product companies believe to be true about creating products. Its core purpose is achieving outcomes versus merely producing output."

Three dimensions of the POM (also in `method.md`):

1. **How products are built** — small, frequent, reliable releases; ideally CI/CD.
2. **How problems are solved** — cross-functional empowered teams tackle problems, addressing all four risks.
3. **How you decide which problems to solve** — customer-focused product vision + insight-driven product strategy.

### The 20 First Principles (Chapters 15–19)

The book systematizes the model into 20 first principles across three groups. Reference index: [Product Compass in-depth summary Part 1](https://www.productcompass.pm/p/product-model-first-principles-transformed-cagan).

**Product Team principles (4)**

1. **Empowered with Problems to Solve** — teams get customer/business problems + desired outcomes, not solutions.
2. **Outcomes over Output** — measured by results, not features shipped.
3. **Sense of Ownership** — teams own discovery AND delivery; clear purpose, autonomy.
4. **Collaboration** — cross-functional; psychological safety; disagree-and-commit.

**Product Strategy principles (4)**

5. **Focus** — "saying no to the hundred other good ideas."
6. **Powered by Insights** — from data, customer conversations, enabling technologies, industry trends.
7. **Transparency** — reasoning, data, stakeholder impact shared openly.
8. **Placing Bets** — strategy as a portfolio; some bets fail; multiple teams may attack the same problem in parallel.

**Product Discovery / Delivery / Culture principles (12)**

Cover: continuous discovery cadence, prototype-based validation, tackling the four risks early, small/frequent/reliable releases (CI/CD default), instrumentation and monitoring, customer-informed decisions, principled disagreement, learning culture, career development, trust-based relationships with stakeholders, missionary culture, evidence-based decision-making. Detail: *Transformed* Ch. 15–19.

### The pilot-team playbook

From *Transformed* + the 2025 essay [The Politics of Pilot Teams](https://www.svpg.com/the-politics-of-pilot-teams/).

**Cagan's canonical framing:** *"You can consider a pilot team as the MVP for a full transformation."*

**Rules:**

- **Hand-pick members** — bar-raisers from your strongest people. Not average performers. The pilot has to demonstrate what "good" looks like to an organization that has never seen it.
- **Choose a problem in the sweet spot: "impressive but not impossible."** Trivial problems don't prove anything. Impossible problems doom the pilot.
- **Learn quickly, cheaply, safely** — usually a quarter or two, just one or two teams, low blast radius.
- **Judge the pilot by business outcome** — something meaningful to the business, not by process compliance or ceremony.
- **Until you've completed a successful pilot, you don't even know what you don't know.**

### The three transformation case studies

The book's differentiator vs *Inspired* + *Empowered*: three first-person accounts of transformations at large pre-Internet companies, contributed by co-authors who led them. If the user is planning a transformation, these are the highest-density practical reference in the SVPG canon.

### When to reach for Transformed material in a session

- User's question is about **transformation** — moving from feature teams to the POM.
- User mentions "Product Operating Model", "POM", "20 First Principles", "pilot team", "transformation engagement."
- Org is large / legacy / pre-Internet in origin — Transformed's case studies are aimed here.

## 2.4 The Product Discovery / Delivery split — refined post-2018

Original *Inspired* laid out discovery and delivery as parallel tracks. Post-book Cagan hardened the distinction. The sharpest formulation is in the 2026 essay [Build to Learn vs Build to Earn](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn/):

- **Discovery = build to learn.** Prototype-based. 10–20+ prototypes per week is normal for a strong team. Tests the four risks against real users and stakeholders before writing production code. Unit of work is the *experiment*.
- **Delivery = build to earn.** Commercial-quality product that can be sold, serviced, supported, and run in production. Addresses scale, performance, fault tolerance, reliability, accuracy, privacy, security, operations, provisioning, internationalization. Unit of work is the *release*.

Credit: **Jeff Patton** for the "build to learn / build to earn" distinction. Cagan cites him explicitly.

**2026 argument:** AI is collapsing the cost of *delivery*, which shifts the bottleneck to *discovery*. Which is exactly what most orgs are worst at. See §2.7 below.

Companion essay: [Build To Learn FAQ](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-faq/) (2026-04-27).

## 2.5 The Alternative to Roadmaps — hardened position

Post-2018 hardened position from [The Alternative to Roadmaps](https://www.svpg.com/the-alternative-to-roadmaps/) and [Roadmap Alternative FAQ](https://www.svpg.com/roadmap-alternative-faq/).

Roadmaps are **replaced, not fixed**. Even "outcome-based" roadmaps are still roadmaps in Cagan's frame. The alternative:

- **Product Vision** — the multi-year holistic view.
- **Business objectives per team** (OKR-shaped) — problems to solve + desired outcomes.
- **High-Integrity Commitments** — for the rare specific cases requiring a date-based deliverable. Handled separately, sparingly, and **only after discovery has validated the solution**.

**Why this matters:** the roadmap-as-artifact conflates strategy, planning, and commitment. Splitting the three functions into three separate artifacts resolves the conflict without lying to stakeholders.

Cross-link to Perri: her two-roadmap model (outcome-driven internal + feature-driven external) is a *softer* refinement of the same critique. Cagan's position is harder — replace the artifact. See [[escaping-the-build-trap]] §2.4 for Perri's version and choose the one that matches the user's readiness for change.

## 2.6 Product Management Theater (2024) — the ZIRP-era diagnosis

From the Lenny 2024 episode [Product management theater | Marty Cagan (Silicon Valley Product Group)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/product-management-theater-marty).

**Cagan's diagnosis:** the industry is full of people with the PM title who are not doing product management. They are **"Agile coaches, product owners, product operations, or business analysts"** relabeled as PMs. They administer backlogs, run ceremonies, translate stakeholder requests into tickets. They are not accountable for **value** or **viability**.

**Root cause:** ZIRP-era over-hiring (2020–2022) created a surplus of ineffective PMs — "overpaid project managers." Post-ZIRP layoffs are exposing the pattern.

**Cagan's fix:**

- Return to the traditional product model where PMs drive discovery and deliver solutions generating value for customers AND business, while product leaders concentrate on strategy.
- Related coinage: **backlog administrator** — Cagan's dismissive term for a product owner in a delivery/scrum team who has no strategic ownership.
- Founder advice repeated in the episode: avoid hiring PMs too early — wait until post-product-market fit.

**When to invoke in a session:** any time the user describes a PM function that is doing ceremony without accountability for outcomes; any time the org has many PMs but no product organization.

## 2.7 AI + Product Operating Model (2025–2026)

Two load-bearing essays.

### Product Coaching and AI (2026-02-04) — the explicit reversal

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/product-coaching-and-ai/).

For 20 years SVPG's position was: great product people are developed through *human* coaching only. In 2026 Cagan changed the position.

**Cagan's new position:** foundation models grounded in the right operating context can now serve as **scalable, always-on product coaches**. This is presented explicitly as a reversal, not as consistency. The essay opens with the acknowledgement of what SVPG previously said and why it's different now.

**Why this matters for the skill:** Cagan is signaling that AI-native coaching (skills like this one, for example) has crossed a threshold he's willing to endorse. It's a rare public reversal — worth naming when relevant.

### The AI Productivity Paradox (2026-07-23) — the current flagship AI essay

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/).

**The paradox:** *"89% of executives say AI has increased the speed of work, but only 6% feel confident they can point to specific organization-wide AI ROI."*

**Cagan's diagnosis:** AI accelerates whatever operating model you already have.

- **Feature team + AI = faster feature factory.** More output; still no outcomes.
- **Empowered team + AI = faster discovery + faster delivery + widening advantage.** Strong product companies are widening the gap.

**Load-bearing quote he embeds** (attributed to Hilary Gridley): *"It's never been faster to build, which means it's never been easier to run 10 times faster in the wrong direction."*

**Combined with §2.4** — AI collapses delivery cost, which promotes discovery to primary bottleneck. Strong teams widen their advantage by running more discovery, not by shipping features faster.

**When to invoke in a session:** any time the user is asking "how do we adopt AI in our product organization?" — redirect to *"what's the operating model that AI is going to amplify?"* first.

## 2.8 The Product Model at Google (2025-12-19, with Elias Lieberich)

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-at-google/).

Deep case study of how Google runs the POM. Signal moves:

- **Product strategy = leaders identifying hard problems to solve better than competitors.** Famous examples: *"Search Is Terrible"* (PageRank), *"Search Ads Suck"* (AdWords), *"Driving is Too Dangerous"* (Waymo).
- **Broadcast the problems.** Rather than top-down assignment, Google leaders *broadcast* the problems and let teams volunteer. Multiple teams often work on the same hard problem in parallel.
- **Evidence over hierarchy.** *"It is an intellectual environment where options are weighed against the evidence, not job titles."*
- **Tech Lead Managers (TLMs)** — promoted from strong engineers, don't need a separate coordination layer; can review code, debate architecture, understand technical debt.
- **Group Product Managers (GPMs)** — define strategy alongside TLMs and coach product managers.
- **Punchline:** *"Empowered teams don't require less management; they require better management."*

**When to invoke in a session:** any time the user is auditing team structure or leadership; when the user believes "empowered = flat / self-organizing" (it doesn't).

## 2.9 Stakeholders and the Product Model (2025-12-01, with Chris Jones)

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/stakeholders-and-the-product-model/).

The stakeholder-relationship playbook for POM orgs. Three foundation areas:

1. **Share business context** — regulations, financials, partnerships, GTM strategy — so teams design solutions that work for the business, not just customers.
2. **Frame as problems, not solutions** — stakeholders bring problems with success metrics, not feature requests.
3. **Enable direct customer access** — teams talk to customers with proper governance, without stakeholder gatekeeping.

Core shift: from *"shipping roadmaps of features"* toward *"solving problems for customers and business, as measured by achieving business results."*

**When to invoke in a session:** whenever the friction is with stakeholders (sales, marketing, exec team) rather than inside the product function.

## 2.10 Great Products, Bad Companies (2026-06-30)

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/great-products-bad-companies/).

Cagan's 2026 argument at the corporate-governance altitude: great products are necessary but insufficient for company success. Successful product companies become targets for governance capture — individuals seeking short-term financial extraction infiltrate boards and leadership.

> *"Great products can actually lead companies to very bad outcomes."*

**Recommended reading in the essay:** Eric Ries's *Incorruptible* — on mission-locked governance structures designed to protect companies from capture. Cagan endorses.

**When to invoke in a session:** rarely inside a product-org session. But if the user is a founder thinking about long-term company preservation, this is the essay.

[Link](https://www.svpg.com/commercial-vs-internal-products/).
