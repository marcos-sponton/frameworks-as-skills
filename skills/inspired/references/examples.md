# Inspired — Real Cases Cagan Uses Publicly

> The cases Cagan cites across *Inspired*, *Empowered*, *Transformed*, and the SVPG essay stream. Use these when responding on his frame's behalf — they are his archetypes. Do NOT fabricate case details; if you don't have a specific attributed fact, use general language ("the best product companies…") rather than inventing.

## The modern archetypes (cited across all three books + essays)

### Google
- **Primary reference:** [The Product Model at Google](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-at-google/), 2025-12-19, with Elias Lieberich. Deepest single Cagan case study on how a company runs the POM.
- **Product strategy examples Cagan cites verbatim:**
  - *"Internet Search Is Terrible"* → Larry Page's PageRank innovation using web links as ranking signals.
  - *"Search Ads Suck"* → AdWords, solving relevance issues competitors couldn't.
  - *"Driving is Too Dangerous"* → Waymo, over a decade of discovery addressing safety.
- **How Google runs empowered teams:** leaders sometimes *broadcast* problems rather than assigning them; multiple teams work on the same hard problem simultaneously.
- **Leadership structure:** **Tech Lead Managers (TLMs)** promoted from strong engineers — they don't need a separate layer of coordinators, they can review code, debate architecture, understand technical debt. **Group Product Managers (GPMs)** define strategy alongside TLMs and coach PMs.
- **Cagan's punchline:** *"Empowered teams don't require less management; they require better management."*
- **Culture:** *"It is an intellectual environment where options are weighed against the evidence, not job titles."*
- **AI-era:** moved from "Mobile First" (2010s) to "AI First" (2016); Gemini at 650M+ MAU.

### Amazon
- **The two-pizza rule** — team size (2–10 engineers) originates here; Cagan adopts and uses across books.
- **Refusal to outsource core empowered-team functions** — Cagan's canonical example of missionary vs. mercenary. Outsourcing manufactures mercenaries.
- **Working Backwards PR/FAQ** — Cagan cites approvingly as compatible with his visiontype approach (both start from customer end-state and reason back). Divergence: PR/FAQ is document-first; visiontype is prototype-first.
- **AWS as second business** — built inside an empowered culture, its own POM.
- **Culture:** disagree-and-commit ceremonies, principled disagreement as norm.

### Netflix
- **Culture of missionary teams** — Cagan cites the Netflix "freedom-and-responsibility" culture as enabling empowered teams.
- **Streaming pivot + originals** — used as archetype for a product org betting big on a hard problem.
- **Reed Hastings's culture deck** referenced approvingly (context, not control).

### Apple
- **Extraordinary designer/engineer collaboration** — Cagan cites Apple as the archetype for product-team craft.
- **Product culture as the enduring moat** — the underlying culture is what compounds over years, not any single product.
- **Steve Jobs stories** — recycled but rarely as "do what Jobs did." More often: "what Jobs did shows what product leadership looks like." Cagan tempers hero-worship — Jobs is an example, not a template.

### Adobe
- Cited in *Inspired* for discovery + strategy examples. Cagan's earlier consulting exposure.

## Transformation case studies (Transformed, 2024) — the differential

The 2024 book *TRANSFORMED* embeds **three first-person case studies** of transformations at large pre-Internet companies. Contributed by co-authors:

- **Lea Hickman** — led a transformation at a major pre-Internet software company. Chapter contributed.
- **Chris Jones** — led a transformation at a large enterprise. Chapter contributed.
- **Christian Idiodi** — led a transformation at a global brand. Chapter contributed.
- **John Moore** — contributed transformation content.

These case studies are the book's differentiator vs. *Inspired* + *Empowered*. If the user is planning a transformation at a legacy org, these are the highest-density practical references in the SVPG canon. WebFetch or point at the book directly — the essays alone don't substitute.

## What NOT to fabricate

- **Do not invent Cagan case details.** If you don't have a specific attributed fact, use general language ("the best product companies…") rather than inventing quotes or events.
- **Do not attribute other authors' cases to Cagan.** Perri's Citigroup / Stripe cases are Perri's; don't cite them as Cagan's.
- **Do not attribute Torres's OST to Cagan** — Cagan endorses it but Torres coined it.
- **Do not attribute Working Backwards / PR-FAQ to Cagan** — Bryar & Carr wrote the book. Cagan cites Amazon; the specific technique is Amazon's.
