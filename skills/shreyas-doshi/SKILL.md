# Shreyas Doshi's PM Frameworks

Shreyas Doshi is a product leader (former PM at Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo -- Stripe's fourth PM, later its first PM manager) who has built one of the most widely-cited collections of product management frameworks through Twitter/X threads, his Substack newsletter, podcast appearances (including multiple episodes on Lenny's Podcast), and a Maven course on Product Sense. He does not have a published book; his body of work is distributed, evolving, and thread-native. This skill captures the full constellation of his frameworks plus their ongoing refinements.

## When this skill activates

**Use this skill when the user is:**
- Trying to prioritize their work and figure out where to spend energy (LNO framework).
- Preparing for a product launch and wants to identify risks before they happen (pre-mortems with Tigers/Paper Tigers/Elephants).
- Wondering why they feel busy but unproductive, or why their impact reviews are disappointing.
- Experiencing team conflict that seems like an execution problem but might be something else.
- Evaluating whether to build or research first, and how to think about that tradeoff.
- Deciding what customer problems to solve and wants to avoid the Focusing Illusion.
- Hiring or evaluating PMs and wants a framework for thinking about types and agency.
- Trying to communicate more effectively with executives vs. team members.
- Developing product sense or taste as a PM skill.
- Asking career questions about PM growth, what to focus on, or how to develop as a leader.

**Do NOT use this skill when:**
- The user wants a formal strategic choice cascade (Where to Play / How to Win) -- that's Roger Martin's Playing to Win territory. Shreyas operates at the PM/team level, not the corporate strategy level.
- The user needs a structured customer discovery method with interviews and opportunity trees -- that's Teresa Torres's Continuous Discovery Habits. Shreyas's CPSR complements CDH but doesn't replace it.
- The user is building a full organizational strategy document. Shreyas's frameworks are mental models for practitioners, not strategy architecture.
- The user wants a product development process or methodology (agile, Shape Up, etc.). Shreyas explicitly values judgment over process.

If the user's situation is at the edge, ask one clarifying question before applying a framework in full.

## The frameworks at a glance

Shreyas's frameworks are independent mental models, not a single integrated system. Each addresses a specific PM challenge:

- **LNO Framework** -- Classify every task as Leverage (10x impact), Neutral (1x), or Overhead (<1x). Spend disproportionate energy on L tasks.
- **Pre-mortems (Tigers / Paper Tigers / Elephants)** -- Before launch, imagine the project has failed. Classify risks using sticky vocabulary that creates psychological safety.
- **Three Levels of Product Work (Impact / Execution / Optics)** -- Most team conflicts arise because people are operating at different levels without recognizing it.
- **High Agency** -- "Finding a way to get what you want, without waiting for conditions to be perfect." Not aggression -- resourcefulness.
- **Execution Problems Are Strategy Problems** -- Most things labeled "execution problems" are actually strategy or culture problems in disguise.
- **Opportunity Cost vs ROI** -- Stop asking "is this worth doing?" Start asking "is this the best thing I could be doing?"
- **Three Types of Product Leaders (Operator / Craftsperson / Visionary)** -- Not career levels but operating modes.
- **Customer Problems Stack Rank (CPSR)** -- Ask customers to rank a problem against all their other problems, not discuss it in isolation.
- **The Antithesis Principle** -- Point truths inward (as warnings about your own defaults), not just outward (as tactics for influencing others).
- **Product Sense** -- "The ability to usually make correct product decisions, both macro and micro, even in the face of major ambiguity." Five components: empathy, simulation, strategic thinking, taste, creative execution.
- **The Focusing Illusion** -- People amplify the importance of whatever they're currently thinking about. Stack rank to counter it.

## How to use this skill in a session

1. **Identify the user's situation and match the right framework.** Shreyas's frameworks are modular -- don't dump them all. Load `references/applications.md` to match situation to framework.

2. **Apply the specific framework with fidelity.** Load `references/method.md` for the canonical definition. Use Shreyas's own terminology (Tigers, not "risks"; Leverage tasks, not "high-priority items").

3. **Challenge weak application using Shreyas's explicit anti-patterns.** Load `references/heuristics.md`. The most common failures: perfectionism-spraying across all tasks (LNO), running pre-mortems without follow-up action plans, confusing ROI with opportunity cost.

4. **Pull evolving material from the newsletter and podcasts.** Load `references/post-book.md` for how his frameworks have been refined over time -- the Antithesis Principle, Product Sense in the AI age, the Humility Trap.

5. **Use Shreyas's voice.** Direct, warm, thread-native. Personal experience as evidence. Sticky labels that do the teaching. Load `references/voice-and-tone.md` before writing in his mode.

6. **Attribute sources.** Cite the specific thread, podcast episode, or essay. Shreyas's work is distributed -- the user deserves to know whether an insight comes from his 2020 High Agency thread, his 2022 Lenny's Podcast appearance, or his 2026 Substack essay.

## Non-negotiables

- **Fidelity to Shreyas.** This skill is a distillation of Shreyas Doshi's work, not a generic PM skill. Do not blend with adjacent frameworks unless the user explicitly asks. His frameworks have specific vocabulary -- use it.
- **Match framework to situation.** Shreyas's frameworks are modular. Don't apply LNO when the user needs a pre-mortem. Don't apply CPSR when the user needs the Three Levels.
- **Attribution.** When quoting or paraphrasing, name the source. Distinguish between a 2020 Twitter thread, a 2022 podcast, and a 2026 Substack essay. The frameworks evolve.
- **Explicit uncertainty.** When Shreyas has refined a position (e.g., from "product sense" as informal concept to a precisely-defined five-component skill), name the evolution.
- **No process, only judgment.** Shreyas's frameworks are mental models for practitioners, not ceremonies or methodologies. If the user tries to turn them into a process, gently redirect: the value is in the thinking, not the template.
