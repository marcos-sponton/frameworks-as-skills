## LNO Effectiveness Framework

**Source**: Twitter thread (Jun 2021, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1399061782560350208), Lenny's Podcast (Jun 2022).

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "This framework improved the quality of my life as a PM & my work more than anything else I've encountered. The main insight is that all your tasks are not created equal." -- @shreyas, Twitter

### Definition

All tasks fall into three categories based on impact potential, not urgency:

### The three types

**Leverage (L) tasks** -- Create 10x to 100x returns on investment. These deserve your absolute best effort. Perfectionism is welcome here. These are the tasks where the difference between good and great work is enormous.

**Neutral (N) tasks** -- Create roughly 1x return. Required job functions done at standard efficiency. Often time-intensive but not ability-intensive (meeting notes, workspace management, routine updates). Should be completed efficiently to free time for L tasks.

**Overhead (O) tasks** -- Create less than 1x impact for the effort. Must be completed but don't require high quality. "Done is better than perfect." These are the tasks where most PMs waste energy by applying Leverage-level perfectionism.

### How to apply

1. Prefix every item on your to-do list with L, N, or O.
2. Audit your current time distribution against healthy targets (~40% L / 35% N / 20% O).
3. For L tasks: block deep work time, give your best effort, don't rush.
4. For N tasks: do them at "good enough" quality, efficiently.
5. For O tasks: minimize, automate, delegate, or timebox ruthlessly.

### The decision formula

**(Value created - Cost of your time) / Time taken**

The key question shifts from "Am I doing the right thing?" to "Is this the best thing I could be doing?"

**Key distinction:** LNO classifications shift. A task that was Leverage last week can become Neutral this week as priorities evolve. Reassess regularly -- this is a daily practice, not a one-time classification.

## Pre-mortems

**Source**: Twitter thread (Jan 2021, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1348520569721872384), Medium essay (https://medium.com/@shreyashere/how-to-use-pre-mortems-to-prevent-problems-blunders-and-disasters-6ecc6df6e22a), Coda/Superhuman Docs template (https://docs.superhuman.com/@shreyas/pre-mortems), Lenny's Podcast (Jun 2022).

Shreyas modified Gary Klein's standard pre-mortem because of a specific failure:

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "The standard pre-mortem meeting was very engaging while the team was in the room. But I frequently saw everyone forget about the progress made in the meeting." -- Shreyas Doshi, pre-mortem template

His innovation: a metaphorical lexicon that creates psychological safety and stickiness beyond the meeting.

### The three risk types

**Tiger**: A clear threat that will hurt us if we don't do something about it. Real, significant, requires mitigation.

**Paper Tiger**: An ostensible threat that you are personally not worried about (but others might be). Important to identify to avoid unnecessary panic and resource misallocation.

**Elephant**: The thing you're concerned the team is not talking about. The uncomfortable truth being avoided.

**Why the lexicon matters:** It creates "more psychological safety for team members to raise their concerns throughout the project's execution." Team members later naturally reference risks: "I recently thought of a Tiger..." -- which is far more natural and less pessimistic than "I have a concern about the project."

### One-hour meeting structure

1. **Kickoff and prompt (10 min)** -- Transport the team 3 months into the future where the project has failed spectacularly. Each participant must identify at least 2 Tigers plus any Paper Tigers and Elephants.

2. **Quiet brainstorming (10 min)** -- Silent individual ideation. No discussion. Write down risks using the Tiger/Paper Tiger/Elephant labels.

3. **Quiet voting and group discussion (30 min)** -- Everyone reads submissions and votes with up to 5 "+1s." Then the group discusses, shares reflections, and surfaces themes.

4. **Draft action plan (10 min)** -- Meeting leader shares top themes and proposes next steps.

### Post-meeting action plan (critical)

The meeting leader must create a follow-up document with:
- Top 3-5 Tigers and Elephants prioritized
- Verbatim threat descriptions (reassures participants their input mattered)
- Proposed mitigating actions for each
- A DRI (Directly Responsible Individual) accountable for each mitigation

**Optimal timing**: One to three months before launch -- enough context to identify real risks, enough time to address them.

**Key distinction:** Without the follow-up action plan and DRIs, the pre-mortem is theater. The meeting is not the output; the action plan is.

## Three Levels of Product Work

**Source**: Twitter thread (Aug 2021, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1427475060193857572), Lenny's Podcast, Knowledge Project #175, Amplitude conversation with John Cutler.

Shreyas calls this "a very fundamental framework that spans most product contexts."

### The three levels

**Impact** -- The long-term brand and business consequences. What matters strategically. C-level thinking. What will this do for the business, the user, the market position?

**Execution** -- The tactical delivery and day-to-day effort. Sprint planning, feature specs, bug fixes, release coordination. PM-level detail.

**Optics** -- How success is communicated and perceived. Building awareness and internal support. How stakeholders see the work.

### The core insight

Most organizational conflict arises because team members are operating at different levels without recognizing the mismatch.

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "If you come into this kind of product review as the product manager and you drown the group in all sorts of execution-level detail... you are going to lose the audience." -- Shreyas Doshi, Knowledge Project #175

> "Oftentimes conflict between people and teams arises mainly because we're not talking at the same level." -- Shreyas Doshi, Knowledge Project #175

### How to apply

The trick is figuring out at which level you ought to communicate with a given stakeholder:
- C-suite: Impact level. What does this mean for the business?
- Engineering team: Execution level. What are we building and how?
- Cross-functional partners: Often Optics level. How does this affect their work's visibility?

**Key distinction:** This is NOT a hierarchy where Impact > Execution > Optics. They are different planes of operation. A PM who only operates at Impact level and ignores Execution is ineffective. A PM who only operates at Execution level and ignores Impact is a project manager.

## High Agency

**Source**: Twitter thread (Jun 2020, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956836856393728), Knowledge Project #175.

### Definition

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "High Agency: finding a way to get what you want, without waiting for conditions to be perfect or otherwise blaming the circumstances." -- @shreyas, Twitter

High-agency individuals either push through adversity or actively reverse unfavorable conditions. It is "a prerequisite for making a profound impact in one's life & work."

### The Agency-Talent Matrix

- **Game Changers** -- Highly talented + high agency. Rare and expensive to hire.
- **Frustrated Geniuses** -- Talented but low agency. Eventually capitulate to systemic pressures.
- **Go-Getters** -- Can transition to game-changer status through developing high agency.

### Low Agency Bob vs High Agency Alice

A recurring teaching device: Bob externalizes problems ("that's someone else's responsibility"), Alice focuses on controllable factors and personal responsibility. Same talent, radically different impact and career trajectory.

### How to cultivate high agency

1. **Study foundational frameworks**: Circle of Influence versus Circle of Concern (Covey's 7 Habits). Focus on what you can control.
2. **Monitor self-talk**: Language patterns reveal and reinforce agency levels. Adjusting internal narratives strengthens high-agency thinking.
3. **Develop the ownership mindset**: Connected to "Jugaad" -- resourceful, creative problem-solving with limited resources.

### Hiring implications

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "Given a choice between hiring a frustrated genius and hiring a go-getter, if it is a role that has a high degree of ambiguity, I would rather hire the go-getter than the frustrated genius." -- Shreyas Doshi, Knowledge Project #175

> "Never compromise on Integrity, not even for Game Changers." -- @shreyas, Twitter

**Key distinction:** High agency is NOT aggression or bulldozing. It is resourcefulness. Agency without integrity is dangerous -- Shreyas makes this non-negotiable.

## Execution Problems Are Strategy Problems

**Source**: Lenny's Podcast (Jun 2022).

### The insight

Most things that look like execution problems are actually strategy or culture problems in disguise.

Before assuming your team has an execution issue, investigate:
- Is the strategy unclear or contradictory?
- Are teams misaligned on priorities?
- Are incentive structures working against the desired outcome?
- Is the culture suppressing honest feedback about direction?

### How to diagnose

When someone says "we have an execution problem," ask:
1. Is the goal clear to everyone involved?
2. Do people agree on the path?
3. Are there structural blockers (dependencies, incentives, resources) that would make even perfect execution fail?

If any answer is no, you have a strategy or culture problem, not an execution problem.

## Opportunity Cost vs ROI

**Source**: Twitter thread (Jun 2021, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1409893040089559041), Lenny's Podcast.

### The problem with ROI thinking

In a high-leverage role, hundreds of things will provide a positive ROI. So ROI doesn't differentiate. Worse: people with an ROI mindset naturally gravitate toward decreasing cost. A task with tiny cost will have very high ROI even if it creates low value.

### The reframe

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "Opportunity Cost = Value of optimal option - Value of chosen option. When we reprogram ourselves to think in terms of Opportunity Cost, we begin to instinctively explore the very high value alternatives that we would not have otherwise considered with an ROI mindset." -- @shreyas, Twitter

### How to apply

Stop asking: "Is this worth doing?" (ROI question -- almost everything is "worth doing")
Start asking: "Is this the best thing I could be doing?" (Opportunity cost question)

This forces you to consider what you're NOT doing by choosing this task. It surfaces the high-value alternatives that ROI thinking hides.

## Three Types of Product Leaders

**Source**: Twitter thread (Mar 2021, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144), 20 Product podcast with Harry Stebbings.

Not career levels but operating modes:

**The Operator** -- "It's about scale." Skilled at scaling teams and managing alignment. Focuses on systems, processes, hiring, organizational design.

**The Craftsperson** -- "It's all about the product." Driven by product insight, customer understanding, and quality. Focuses on UX, design decisions, product details.

**The Visionary** -- "It's about the future." Gifted at imagining what's next. Focuses on market shifts, long-term bets, category creation.

Everyone has a preference, and that preference influences everything -- how they run reviews, set goals, build teams, and evaluate success.

### Hiring implication

Founders should choose hires based on their startup's stage and needs, not based on which archetype sounds most impressive. An early-stage startup drowning in execution debt needs an Operator. One with a weak product needs a Craftsperson. One with no clear direction needs a Visionary.

## Customer Problems Stack Rank

**Source**: Twitter thread (May 2020, https://x.com/shreyas/status/1263890591349305346), OpinionX blog (https://www.opinionx.co/blog/shreyas-doshi-cpsr).

### The problem it solves

The Focusing Illusion: customers amplify the importance of whatever specific problem you're currently discussing with them. When isolated in conversation, every problem seems critical.

### The method

Instead of asking "how important is this problem?", ask customers to **stack rank** the problem against all other problems they currently face.

Get the CPSR from multiple personas (VP Support, VP Marketing, VP Sales, etc.). The ranking across personas reveals the true priority.

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "This Customer Problems Stack Rank will act as a great test of the TRUE priority of the customer problem & the product solution you're discussing." -- @shreyas, Twitter

### Real-world validation

A team tested CPSR with 600 target customers, ranking 45 problem statements. Their original focus problem ranked dead last. The top 7 problems revealed five similar pain points worth pursuing instead -- leading to multiple paying customers within a week and 3x improvement in landing page conversion.

## The Antithesis Principle

**Source**: Substack essay (Jul 2026), Knowledge Project #175.

### Definition

Truths about human nature can be viewed two ways: pointed **outward** (as tactics for influencing others) and pointed **inward** (as warnings about defaults to eliminate in yourself).

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "Everyone who's smart finds the outward tactic. Only the wise see the inward antithesis." -- Shreyas Doshi, Substack

### Examples

| Truth | Outward (tactic) | Inward (antithesis) |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|
| People learn better when entertained | Make your content entertaining | Develop ability to learn from un-entertaining content |
| Charisma influences decisions | Cultivate charisma to persuade | Build resistance to being swayed by others' charisma |
| First impressions matter | Craft favorable first impressions | Don't form fixed judgments from initial encounters |
| Good managers develop people | Become a competent manager | "Turn yourself into a person who can do really good work even without having a great manager" |

## Product Sense

**Source**: Maven course (https://maven.com/shreyas-doshi/product-sense), Substack essay (Mar 2026).

### Definition

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "The ability to usually make correct product decisions, both macro & micro, even in the face of major ambiguity." -- Shreyas Doshi

A learnable skill, not innate talent.

### Five components

1. **Strong empathy** -- Understanding unstated human needs beyond what data shows
2. **Excellent simulation skills** -- Identifying future possibilities through domain knowledge
3. **Stellar strategic thinking** -- Determining key segments and differentiators
4. **Great taste** -- Selecting optimal recommendations aligned with business goals
5. **Creative execution** -- Conceiving unique features competitors cannot match

### In the AI age

**Shreyas's own words:**
> "The only real long-term career moat for product people is how you can improve on the already-brilliant inputs and outputs that AI will provide for you." -- Shreyas Doshi, Substack (Mar 2026)

> "Tools have never been a significant source of alpha in product success and that is not changing with AI tools." -- Shreyas Doshi, Substack (Mar 2026)

## The Focusing Illusion

**Source**: Twitter threads, OpinionX blog, 5 Big Ideas analysis.

### Definition

People amplify the importance of whatever they are currently thinking about. In product: customers and teams treat the problem currently under discussion as the most important one, even when it isn't.

### The formula

**Success = Quality execution x Problem-solution fit x Problem importance**

Most teams optimize for the first two and completely miss the third. They build excellent solutions for unimportant problems.

### The antidote

Use the Customer Problems Stack Rank (CPSR) to force holistic prioritization. "Nobody goes out of their way to find solutions for a mild inconvenience."

## What these frameworks are NOT

- **They are not a methodology.** There is no "Shreyas Doshi Method" with steps 1 through 10. These are independent mental models you apply when the situation calls for them.
- **They are not process.** Shreyas explicitly values judgment over process. If you turn LNO into a mandatory daily standup exercise, you've missed the point.
- **They are not theory.** Every framework comes from practitioner experience at Stripe, Google, Twitter, and Yahoo. They were tested before they were tweeted.
- **They are not comprehensive.** Shreyas himself says: "Don't believe everything I write" and "Don't take everything and just implement it." These work in empowered-team environments; results vary in constrained contexts.
