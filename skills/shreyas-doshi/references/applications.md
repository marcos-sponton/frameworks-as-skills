# Shreyas Doshi's PM Frameworks -- Applications

> When to use which framework, when NOT to use it, and adjacent frameworks to reach for instead.

## When NOT to use Shreyas's frameworks

### Don't use when the user needs a strategic choice cascade
Shreyas's frameworks are practitioner-level mental models, not corporate strategy architecture. If the user is asking "Where should we play? How do we win? What capabilities do we need?", that's **Roger Martin's Playing to Win** territory.

**Redirect**: "Shreyas's frameworks operate at the PM/team level. For the strategic choice cascade you're describing, the Playing to Win skill is the better fit."

### Don't use when the user needs a structured discovery method
Shreyas's CPSR is a problem validation tool, not a complete discovery methodology. If the user needs continuous interviews, opportunity solution trees, and assumption testing, that's **Teresa Torres's Continuous Discovery Habits** territory.

**Redirect**: "The CPSR framework is great for validating problem importance. For the full discovery methodology -- weekly interviews, opportunity mapping, assumption testing -- use the CDH skill."

### Don't use when the user needs a product development process
Shreyas explicitly values judgment over process. If the user wants a shipping methodology (build cycles, cooldowns, pitches), that's **Shape Up** territory. If they want agile ceremony design, that's outside this skill entirely.

**Redirect**: "Shreyas's frameworks are mental models, not processes. For a structured build methodology, look at Shape Up."

### Don't use when the user needs organizational design or team topology
The Three Types and High Agency frameworks touch on hiring, but they're not org design tools. If the user is designing team structures, reporting lines, or platform vs stream alignment, reach for other frameworks.

### Don't use when the user needs a full competitive strategy
The Focusing Illusion and CPSR help validate problem importance, but they don't analyze competitive dynamics. For industry structure, moats, and positioning, reach for **7 Powers** (Hamilton Helmer), **Good Strategy Bad Strategy** (Rumelt), or **Obviously Awesome** (Dunford).

## Framework combinations that work well

### LNO + Opportunity Cost (daily prioritization)
Start with LNO to classify tasks, then apply Opportunity Cost thinking to the Leverage tasks specifically. "Of all the L tasks on my list, which has the highest opportunity cost if I don't do it today?"

### Pre-mortem + Three Levels (launch preparation)
Run the pre-mortem to identify risks, then classify the risks by level: Impact-level risks (strategic), Execution-level risks (delivery), and Optics-level risks (perception). Different DRIs for different levels.

### CPSR + Focusing Illusion (problem validation)
Use the Focusing Illusion diagnosis to recognize when you're falling for it, then apply CPSR as the specific antidote: stack rank the problem against all others.

### High Agency + Antithesis Principle (self-development)
Assess your own agency using the Agency-Talent Matrix, then apply the Antithesis Principle: where are you using "high agency" as an outward label while tolerating low-agency defaults in yourself?

### Three Levels + "Execution-as-Strategy" (conflict diagnosis)
When teams clash, first diagnose the level mismatch (are people operating at different levels?), then investigate whether the apparent execution problem is actually a strategy problem.

### Three Types + Product Sense (career development)
Understand your default archetype (Operator/Craftsperson/Visionary), then develop Product Sense components that compensate for your archetype's blind spots. Operators may need to develop taste and creative execution; Visionaries may need to develop simulation skills (operational reality).

## Adjacent frameworks and how they relate

### Teresa Torres -- Continuous Discovery Habits
**Relationship**: Complementary. CPSR validates problem importance before you invest in Torres-style continuous discovery. Torres provides the interview and opportunity mapping method; Shreyas provides the problem prioritization frame.
**Use Torres when**: You need a structured, ongoing discovery practice with weekly interviews and opportunity solution trees.
**Use Shreyas when**: You need to validate whether you're solving the right problem before investing in discovery.

### Marty Cagan -- Inspired / Empowered
**Relationship**: Aligned. Both emphasize PM agency, judgment, and empowered teams. Cagan provides the organizational model (empowered product teams); Shreyas provides the individual PM mental models.
**Use Cagan when**: You're designing how product teams should operate organizationally.
**Use Shreyas when**: You're developing individual PM skills and decision-making.

### Richard Rumelt -- Good Strategy Bad Strategy
**Relationship**: Shreyas's "execution problems are strategy problems" insight is Rumelt-adjacent. Both insist on diagnosis before action.
**Use Rumelt when**: You need to diagnose a pivotal strategic challenge and design a coherent response (the kernel).
**Use Shreyas when**: You're operating at the PM/team level and need practical mental models for daily work.

### Roger Martin -- Playing to Win
**Relationship**: Different operating altitude. Martin provides the strategic choice cascade for corporate/business-unit strategy. Shreyas provides practitioner-level frameworks.
**Use Martin when**: You're making where-to-play / how-to-win choices at the business level.
**Use Shreyas when**: You're making daily PM decisions about prioritization, risk, and communication.

### Hamilton Helmer -- 7 Powers
**Relationship**: Shreyas references Helmer's 7 Powers in his Product Sense essay. Helmer provides the competitive advantage framework; Shreyas provides the PM operational models.
**Use Helmer when**: You need to analyze or build durable competitive advantage.
**Use Shreyas when**: You need to make better product decisions day-to-day.

### Annie Duke -- Thinking in Bets
**Relationship**: Both emphasize better decision-making under uncertainty. Duke provides the decision process (resulting, probabilistic thinking); Shreyas provides PM-specific decision frameworks.
**Use Duke when**: You need a general framework for decision quality under uncertainty.
**Use Shreyas when**: The decision is specifically about product management work.

### Stephen Covey -- 7 Habits
**Relationship**: Shreyas explicitly references Covey's Circle of Influence vs Circle of Concern for developing high agency.
**Use Covey when**: You're working on personal effectiveness holistically.
**Use Shreyas when**: You're applying the agency concept specifically to PM work and hiring.
