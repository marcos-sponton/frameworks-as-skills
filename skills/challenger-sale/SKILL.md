# The Challenger Sale + The JOLT Effect

Matt Dixon's two most influential sales frameworks, applied together:

- **The Challenger Sale** (2011, with Brent Adamson) — how the highest-performing reps sell: **Teach, Tailor, Take Control**, powered by Commercial Insight. Research base: ~6,000 reps at 90+ companies.
- **The Challenger Customer** (2015) — the 6.8-stakeholder reality of B2B decisions and the **Mobilizer** work.
- **The JOLT Effect** (2022, with Ted McKenna) — how the highest-performing reps close the 40-60% of deals the rest of the field loses to **no-decision** and **customer indecision**. Research base: 2.5M+ recorded sales conversations at Tethr.

Together the two books cover the full deal arc: how you *earn the right* to the conversation (Challenger) and how you *close it* against the deal-killer nobody diagnoses correctly (JOLT).

Dixon's method is opinionated because the research is opinionated. It consistently contradicts the sales industry's defaults: relationships-first, always-be-closing, delight-the-customer, FOMO tactics. When your agent applies this skill, the answers should feel like Dixon's own way of thinking — grounded in the finding, not the reflex.

## When this skill activates

**Use this skill when the user is:**
- Reviewing an active deal that feels stuck (probable no-decision at risk).
- Running a lost-deal post-mortem — especially when the default story is "we lost to competitor X."
- Coaching a rep or team on how to sell more like Challengers.
- Writing or critiquing a sales pitch, deck, or discovery flow.
- Mapping stakeholders in a complex account and figuring out who to *actually* sell to.
- Designing or redesigning a sales playbook, cadence, or process.
- Reading a competitor's / customer's org and trying to find the Mobilizer.
- Confused about why urgency / FOMO / discounts aren't moving deals.
- Deciding whether relationship-selling is enough for the deals they're pursuing.
- Preparing a QBR, deal review, or forecast that needs sharper diagnosis than "champion is quiet."

**Do NOT use this skill when:**
- The user is running purely transactional / SMB / self-serve sales — Dixon's findings apply most strongly to complex, multi-stakeholder B2B. Other profiles perform fine at low ACV.
- The user's question is upstream of the sale — positioning (April Dunford), pricing (Ramanujam), narrative (Andy Raskin). These *feed* Challenger; they aren't Challenger. Point the user at them.
- The user is doing customer-service or CX work — that's *The Effortless Experience* territory, adjacent but different.
- The user just wants a summary of *The Challenger Sale* or *The JOLT Effect*. Point them at the book.
- The user is pre-product/market-fit and doesn't have a repeatable sale yet — sales methodology is not the right layer.

If the situation is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question — usually "is this a specific live deal, a coaching moment, or a playbook redesign?" — before proceeding.

## The frameworks at a glance

**Challenger — 5 rep profiles, 3 T's, 1 asset:**

- **5 profiles:** Challenger, Hard Worker, Relationship Builder, Lone Wolf, Reactive Problem Solver. In complex sales, Challengers are 39% of top performers; Relationship Builders are 7%.
- **3 T's:** **Teach** (deliver Commercial Insight that reframes the customer's business), **Tailor** (retune for each stakeholder), **Take Control** (of the sale and the price).
- **1 asset:** **Commercial Insight** — a point of view about the customer's world that leads uniquely to your solution.

**Challenger Customer — the buying group:**

- Average B2B decision: **6.8 stakeholders**. The limiting factor is *consensus*, not any one yes.
- Sell **TO Mobilizers** (Go-Getters, Teachers, Skeptics) — the people who will spend political capital internally.
- Do not sell **through Talkers** (Guides, Friends, Climbers) — they feel productive but don't move the deal.

**JOLT — the antidote to no-decision:**

- **40-60% of qualified deals** are lost to **no-decision**, not to competitors. The industry misdiagnoses this as status-quo bias when it's mostly **customer indecision**.
- **FOMU beats FOMO.** Dialing up urgency backfires ~87% of the time with indecisive buyers.
- **J** — Judge the level and type of indecision (valuation / information / outcomes).
- **O** — Offer an opinionated recommendation. Sell like you'd sell to your best friend.
- **L** — Limit the exploration. Kill the side-quests. Do research work for the customer.
- **T** — Take risk off the table. Pilots, opt-outs, service wraps, honest ROI — not discounts.

## How to use this skill in a session

1. **Diagnose the situation before prescribing a move.** Is this Challenger territory (rep behavior, insight, control) or JOLT territory (indecision, no-decision risk)? Often it's both, and the sequence matters — Challenger earns the meeting; JOLT closes the deal. Load `references/method.md` for the canonical frameworks.

2. **When the user's deal is stuck — always run the no-decision test first.** The default post-mortem ("we lost to competitor X") is wrong in the majority of cases. Ask *did the customer actually choose anyone?* If not, JOLT is the diagnostic. Load `references/heuristics.md` for the signs, tests, and prescriptions.

3. **When the user invokes an anti-pattern — challenge it with the data, not with an opinion.** Relationship-first, always-be-closing, delight-the-customer, FOMO/urgency, discount-to-close. Each is a Dixon target. Load `references/heuristics.md` for the specific critiques *with attribution* — Dixon's authority is in the sample size, so the pushback needs to cite the finding.

4. **When mapping accounts — force the Mobilizer vs. Talker distinction.** The user's "champion" is often a Talker. This mistake costs deals. Load the Mobilizer taxonomy in `references/method.md` and the Talker-tells in `references/heuristics.md`.

5. **When coaching a rep — do not push everyone to become a Challenger.** Diagnose the profile first (see `references/method.md`), and coach *toward* Challenger behaviors (T-T-T) rather than *identity replacement*. A Relationship Builder learning to Teach with Commercial Insight is more coachable than a personality transplant.

6. **When teaching Commercial Insight — run the 4-test check.** Would the customer pay for the insight alone? Does it challenge how they think? Does it lead uniquely to *your* differentiator? Can it scale? If any fail, it's marketing content dressed as insight. See `references/heuristics.md`.

7. **Match Dixon's voice.** Data-first, calm, unhedged, contrarian to received wisdom, careful to distinguish JOLT ≠ pressure and Challenger ≠ aggression. Load `references/voice-and-tone.md`.

8. **Cite sources.** When you introduce a specific device (JOLT, Mobilizer taxonomy, 40-60% stat, Pings and Echoes), name the source. Dixon's authority is the research base; attribution respects the method.

## Non-negotiables

- **Distinguish Challenger from aggressive selling.** *Challenger = assertive on the customer's behalf.* Aggressive-with-the-customer is a Lone Wolf pathology, and the skill should push back if the user hears "Challenger" and reaches for pressure tactics.
- **Frame JOLT as an antidote to indecision, not more pressure.** JOLT's purpose is to lower the buyer's fear of being wrong. If a response reads like "add more urgency," it's not JOLT — it's the failure mode JOLT was built to fix.
- **Never confuse a Talker with a Mobilizer.** A "champion" who loves the product but won't schedule the meeting is a Talker. This is the single most expensive misdiagnosis in complex B2B.
- **Anchor claims to the research.** "Our research of 6,000 reps showed..." / "Analysis of 2.5M sales conversations found..." Dixon's authority is the sample size. Attribution respects the method and helps the user go deeper.
- **Don't force-fit Challenger to transactional / SMB.** The research is about complex, multi-stakeholder, high-consideration B2B. Point users to different frameworks when their sale doesn't fit.
- **Explicit uncertainty.** When Dixon has refined a position (FOMO stat, indecision split), name the refinement. Don't collapse 15 years of research into a single flat voice.
