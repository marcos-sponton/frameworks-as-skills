# Hooked — Material After the Book

> **This is the differential of this skill.** Most treatments of the Hook Model stop at *Hooked* (2014). But Eyal has kept writing, speaking, and evolving for 12 years since — publishing *Indistractable* (2019), *Beyond Belief* (2026), hundreds of blog posts on nirandfar.com, Psychology Today columns, HBR articles, podcast appearances, and talks. This file captures that material with sources so you can verify.
>
> Organized chronologically within each theme so you can see how the author's thinking has evolved.

## Refinements and reversals

### From "build habits" to "the builder has a moral obligation"
**What changed:** In *Hooked* (2014), the Manipulation Matrix was Chapter 8 — a moral reflection at the end. Over the next five years, Eyal increasingly front-loaded the ethics question, culminating in *Indistractable* (2019) which is essentially the counter-manual to *Hooked*. The ethics dimension moved from epilogue to core thesis.

**When it appeared:** Evolved gradually 2015–2019; crystallized in *Indistractable* (BenBella Books, 2019).

**How this changes practice:** Practitioners applying the Hook Model today should treat the Manipulation Matrix as a **precondition**, not a reflection. Run it before designing the hook, not after shipping it.

### From "tech isn't addictive" to a more nuanced position
**What changed:** Early Eyal (2014–2017) pushed back strongly against the "tech is addictive" narrative, arguing that behavioral design creates habits, not addictions, and that personal responsibility matters. He hasn't reversed this position, but his tone has softened. *Indistractable* acknowledges that companies bear responsibility for designing ethically, and his public commentary increasingly recognizes the legitimate concerns about attention exploitation — while still rejecting the "tech = tobacco" framing.

**When it appeared:** Gradual shift visible in blog posts 2017–2019; fully articulated in *Indistractable* (2019) and subsequent podcast appearances.

**Author's words:**
> "Being indistractable does not mean you can't enjoy your technology. It means you use it on your terms, not theirs." — Nir Eyal, *Indistractable*, Introduction

**How this changes practice:** When discussing the Hook Model's ethics, present both sides honestly: Eyal still defends behavioral design as a legitimate discipline, but he no longer dismisses the critique that some companies abuse it. The full picture includes both *Hooked* and *Indistractable*.

### The shift from behavioral design to belief systems
**What changed:** *Beyond Belief* (2026) represents a significant expansion of Eyal's focus. While *Hooked* was about designing external behavior and *Indistractable* about managing one's own attention, *Beyond Belief* goes deeper into the cognitive layer — how beliefs shape what we see, attempt, and achieve. The connection to product design is more indirect: beliefs shape the internal triggers that drive the Hook cycle.

**When it appeared:** *Beyond Belief: The Science-Backed Way to Stop Limiting Yourself and Achieve Breakthrough Results* (Portfolio, March 2026), co-authored with Julie Li. Instant NYT bestseller.

**How this changes practice:** For practitioners doing trigger discovery (the 5 Whys), *Beyond Belief* adds a layer: the user's beliefs about themselves and the world shape which internal triggers fire and how they respond. A user who believes "I'm bad with money" has a different internal trigger landscape than one who doesn't.

## New material — added after Hooked

### Indistractable — the demand-side defense manual (2019)
The single most important post-*Hooked* publication. Where *Hooked* teaches builders how to create habit-forming products (supply-side), *Indistractable* teaches individuals how to resist unwanted hooks (demand-side). Full model documented in `references/method.md` under "The Indistractable Model."

Key concepts not in *Hooked*:
- **Traction vs. Distraction** — the fundamental axis of all behavior.
- **Timeboxing** — Eyal's recommended time management technique (schedule by values, not by tasks).
- **The 10-minute rule** — when an urge to distract hits, say "not yet" and wait 10 minutes.
- **Three pact types** — effort pacts, price pacts, identity pacts.
- **The "schedule maker" technique** — planning every minute of the day in advance based on personal values.

**Source:** *Indistractable: How to Control Your Attention and Choose Your Life* (BenBella Books, 2019). Won 2019 OWL Award; named Best Business & Leadership Book by Amazon.

### Nir Eyal's position on screen time and children
Post-*Indistractable*, Eyal has been vocal about children's technology use — pushing back against Jonathan Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* thesis while advocating for teaching children to be "indistractable" rather than imposing blanket bans.

**Source:** nirandfar.com articles including "The Real Culprit Behind Plummeting Children's Mental Health"; Psychology Today columns.

### Beyond Belief — the cognitive layer (2026)
Eyal's most recent book explores how beliefs (not just behaviors or attention) shape performance. While less directly product-focused than *Hooked* or *Indistractable*, it deepens the understanding of internal triggers — the belief system that generates the emotional states the Hook Model builds on.

Key concepts:
- Hidden assumptions filter perception, attempt, and achievement.
- Beliefs are not facts — they are tools that can be replaced.
- Practical methods for identifying and changing limiting beliefs.

**Source:** *Beyond Belief: The Science-Backed Way to Stop Limiting Yourself and Achieve Breakthrough Results* (Portfolio, March 2026), with Julie Li.

## Adjacent frameworks the author has since endorsed, critiqued, or connected to

### BJ Fogg's Behavior Model
Eyal explicitly builds on Fogg's B=MAT model in *Hooked* Chapter 3 and has continued to reference it in talks and articles. Eyal studied under Fogg at Stanford. The relationship is additive, not competitive — Fogg provides the behavioral mechanics, Eyal provides the product design framework.

### James Clear's Atomic Habits
Eyal has not publicly critiqued Clear's work, and the two occupy adjacent territory. Key difference: Clear focuses on personal habit formation (individual behavior change); Eyal focuses on product-driven habit formation (designing products that create habits in users). They compose well — a product designer might use Hooked for the product and Atomic Habits for their own behavior.

### Cal Newport's Deep Work / Digital Minimalism
Eyal and Newport have publicly disagreed. Newport advocates for radical reduction of technology use ("digital minimalism"); Eyal advocates for mindful use ("indistractable" — use tech on your terms). The disagreement is philosophical: Newport's view is that most tech is designed to exploit attention and should be minimized; Eyal's view is that tech is a tool and users should learn to manage their attention rather than avoid the tool.

### Jonathan Haidt's The Anxious Generation
Eyal has pushed back on Haidt's thesis that smartphones and social media are the primary cause of a youth mental health crisis. Eyal argues the evidence is more nuanced and that teaching children to be indistractable is more effective than banning devices.

**Source:** nirandfar.com articles; Psychology Today column "The Real Culprit Behind Plummeting Children's Mental Health"

## Examples added after the book

### The Bible App (YouVersion)
Eyal has used the Bible App as a post-*Hooked* example of habit-forming design applied to a traditionally non-tech domain. The app uses all four phases: trigger (daily verse notification), action (read one verse), variable reward (different content each day, community reactions), investment (highlights, bookmarks, reading streaks).

**Source:** Multiple talks and blog posts post-2014.

### Slack as a hook case study
In various podcast appearances, Eyal has analyzed Slack through the Hook Model lens: internal trigger (uncertainty about what colleagues are doing/saying), action (open Slack, scroll), variable reward (new messages, reactions — Rewards of the Tribe), investment (channels joined, message history, integrations set up).

**Source:** Multiple podcast appearances 2018–2024.

### Fitness and health apps
Eyal increasingly uses health and fitness apps (Fitbod, MyFitnessPal) as examples of ethical hook design — products where habit formation genuinely improves user outcomes. These examples reinforce the Facilitator quadrant of the Manipulation Matrix.

**Source:** nirandfar.com blog posts; LeanB2B interview.
