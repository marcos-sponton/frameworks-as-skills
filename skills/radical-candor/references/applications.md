# Radical Candor — When to Use, When Not, Adjacent Frameworks

> Radical Candor is opinionated. It is also not a universal tool. This file names where Kim's method fits, where it doesn't, and how it relates to adjacent frameworks — so the assistant can either apply it or point at the better tool.

## When Radical Candor fits

Kim's method is at its most useful in these situations:

- **Manager-to-report feedback.** The primary target. HHIPP, the 2×2 diagnostic, and the Care Personally axis all assume an ongoing working relationship with mutual investment.
- **1:1s and performance conversations.** The mechanism through which feedback lives day-to-day. If the 1:1 is a status update, no Radical Candor is happening.
- **Career conversations.** The Russ Laraway 3-conversation ritual is where Care Personally gets built over time.
- **Team decision-making processes.** GSD wheel — Listen → Clarify → Debate → Decide → Persuade → Execute → Learn. Applies to any recurring team decision.
- **Hiring / firing / promotion decisions.** Rock Star vs. Superstar diagnostics, the brilliant-jerk decision, the "do not promote bullies" rule.
- **Peer feedback and challenging up.** The framework applies to boss and peer relationships too — with more care about the Care Personally investment, since the power dynamic isn't in your favor.
- **DEI / bias / prejudice / bullying incidents.** BUL taxonomy + I/It/You statements from Just Work / Radical Respect.
- **Culture design at scale.** The two Radical Respect goals — "optimize for collaboration, not coercion" and "honor individuality, don't demand conformity" — apply as system-design principles.

## When Radical Candor does NOT fit (or fits poorly)

- **Legal / HR-compliance situations.** If harassment, discrimination, or safety is at stake, the framework informs but does not replace legal or HR counsel. Direct the user to their HR partner or attorney, not to a 2×2.
- **One-off vendor / contractor relationships with no ongoing Care Personally investment.** The framework requires an ongoing relationship for Care to compound. In one-off transactions, the mechanism doesn't exist. Direct feedback still applies, but "Radical Candor" is the wrong vocabulary.
- **Purely technical / strategic questions with no interpersonal dimension.** "How should we architect this service?" is not a Radical Candor question. Don't force the framework onto it.
- **Deep personal / therapeutic contexts.** If a user is asking about grief, trauma, or serious mental health situations at work, Radical Candor is not the right tool. Suggest EAP resources or a therapist.
- **Cultures where the framework's calibration doesn't survive translation.** The axes (Care, Challenge) are universal, but the calibration of what counts as "direct" varies by culture. In some contexts, "kind and clear" needs to be rebuilt from local norms — Kim addresses this in the podcast. The framework survives calibration; the axes don't survive being dropped.
- **When the user is fishing for permission to be blunt with someone they dislike.** See the voice guard in `heuristics.md`. This is not a Radical Candor situation — it's a self-diagnosis situation. Redirect.

## Adjacent frameworks — when each is the better tool

### Nonviolent Communication (Marshall Rosenberg)

- **NVC:** observation → feeling → need → request. Focuses on emotional language and needs literacy.
- **Radical Candor:** Care Personally + Challenge Directly. Focuses on manager-report power dynamic and workplace feedback specifically.
- **When NVC is better:** intimate relationships, deeply emotional conflicts, situations where the primary problem is naming feelings and needs.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** workplace feedback, ongoing manager-report relationships, org-wide culture questions.
- **Compose:** NVC provides the vocabulary for the "kind, clear, specific" delivery Radical Candor demands. Someone fluent in NVC will find HHIPP easy to execute.

### Crucial Conversations (Patterson, Grenny, McMillan, Switzler)

- **Crucial Conversations:** STATE method (Share facts, Tell your story, Ask for others' paths, Talk tentatively, Encourage testing). Focus: high-stakes conversations where opinions vary and emotions run strong.
- **Radical Candor:** the *ongoing* manager-report feedback relationship, not the one-off high-stakes conversation.
- **When Crucial Conversations is better:** you have to have one specific hard conversation, right now, and you need a tactical script for it.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** you're designing a feedback culture over months / years, not scripting one meeting.
- **Compose:** Crucial Conversations gives you the tactical script for a single hard conversation; Radical Candor gives you the operating system that makes each hard conversation smaller because they're happening constantly.

### Dare to Lead / Brené Brown

- **Brown:** vulnerability and shame research; leadership grounded in "rumbling" (leaning into hard conversations from a stance of vulnerability). "Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind."
- **Kim:** overlapping values (vulnerability is compatible with Radical Candor, and "I might be wrong" is the H in HHIPP). Kim's *"Kind and clear is kind. Unkind and unclear is unkind"* is close cousin to Brown's line.
- **When Brown is better:** the primary lever is the leader's own emotional courage and self-trust. Deeper personal work.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** the primary lever is the manager's specific feedback moves and team system design. More operational.
- **Compose:** Brown provides the *why* (courage requires vulnerability); Kim provides the *how* (specific move sequences: HHIPP, the 2×2 diagnostic, Career Conversations).

### Radical Transparency (Ray Dalio / Bridgewater)

- **Dalio:** full transparency, recorded meetings, public rating apps ("Dot Collector"), culture of "getting in sync" through relentless debate.
- **Kim:** **actively distinguishes.** Radical Transparency drops the Care Personally axis at scale — anyone can call anyone out anytime, publicly, with the assumption that transparency alone produces good outcomes.
- **Kim's critique:** without the Care Personally axis, "radical transparency" curdles into Obnoxious Aggression, which is exactly Bridgewater's reputation for being brutal.
- **Do NOT conflate them.** Kim has explicitly pushed back on the association in interviews. When a user invokes both, name the distinction: Radical Transparency drops the Care axis; Radical Candor makes it load-bearing.

### Netflix "keeper test" / no-brilliant-jerks (Reed Hastings)

- **Netflix:** "would you fight to keep this person?" as the performance filter; explicit "no brilliant jerks" rule; high performance density.
- **Kim:** strong agreement on the brilliant-jerks rule (a brilliant jerk retained is a public statement that Care Personally is optional). Sharper mechanism for *why* — they poison the Care axis for everyone.
- **When Netflix is better:** high-growth environments where role fit is dynamic and performance density is a strategic bet.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** ongoing manager-report development, not just the retention filter.
- **Compose:** Netflix's keeper test is a retention decision; Radical Candor's Career Conversations are a more relational alternative that runs continuously, not just at renewal.

### Andy Grove — High Output Management

- **Grove:** managerial output = output of your team + output of teams you influence. Focus on leverage, 1:1s, decision-making processes.
- **Kim:** Grove is her mechanical foundation. The 1:1 (Grove's core mechanism) is where much of Radical Candor happens. Kim cites Grove as canonical.
- **When Grove is better:** you're designing the manager mechanics from scratch — 1:1 cadence, task-relevant maturity, meeting design.
- **When Radical Candor is better:** the mechanics exist and the question is what to *do* inside them.
- **Compose:** Grove is the plumbing, Radical Candor is what flows through it.

### Camille Fournier — The Manager's Path

- **Fournier:** engineering-manager-specific career ladder and manager-craft handbook.
- **Kim:** framework layer above; Fournier: role-specific tactical layer.
- **When Fournier is better:** engineering-manager-specific questions (tech-lead-to-EM transition, EM-to-Director path, on-call, incident retros as a manager).
- **When Radical Candor is better:** cross-functional feedback and culture questions.
- **Compose:** often bundled in engineering-manager reading lists.

### Playing to Win (Roger Martin) / Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Richard Rumelt)

- **Different problem entirely.** These are strategy frameworks (what to do at the org level). Radical Candor is a management framework (how to work with the people who execute it).
- **When each is better:** if the user is asking about competitive positioning, market choice, or where-to-play — that's Martin or Rumelt, not Kim.
- **Compose:** Radical Candor is *inside* the org that has a Playing to Win strategy. They operate at different layers and don't conflict.

## Adjacent frameworks NOT to conflate

- **Radical Candor ≠ Radical Transparency.** Called out above. This is the most common confusion. Radical Transparency drops the Care axis.
- **Radical Candor ≠ "just being honest."** Called out above and in `heuristics.md`. If the recipient didn't feel cared for, the feedback was Obnoxious Aggression, regardless of the giver's intent.
- **Radical Candor ≠ "no filter" culture.** No-filter cultures are usually Obnoxious Aggression scaled to team level.
- **Radical Candor ≠ performance-review methodology.** Feedback lives in the moment, not in the quarterly review. If the review contains surprises, the manager failed HHIPP's Immediate.
- **Radical Candor ≠ conflict resolution.** Conflict resolution is a downstream method. Radical Candor's goal is to make most conflict resolution unnecessary by giving feedback continuously.

## When to compose Radical Candor with another framework

- **Manager giving hard feedback in a culturally-mixed team** — Radical Candor's axes + a cross-cultural communication frame (Erin Meyer's *The Culture Map* is a natural pair).
- **Manager running a high-stakes hard conversation** — Radical Candor's Care axis + Crucial Conversations' STATE script.
- **Manager building a feedback culture from scratch** — Radical Candor's 2×2 + Grove's 1:1 mechanics + Career Conversations.
- **Org-level cultural transformation** — Radical Candor's manager operating system + Radical Respect's leader responsibilities + a strategy layer (Playing to Win, Rumelt).
- **DEI incident response** — Radical Respect's BUL taxonomy + the org's actual HR / legal protocols. **The framework informs; it does not replace legal counsel.**

## Rule of thumb

If the question is:
- "How do I give this feedback?" → Radical Candor.
- "How do I win this specific conversation?" → Crucial Conversations.
- "How do I find the courage to have this conversation?" → Brené Brown.
- "How do I run my 1:1s?" → Grove + Radical Candor.
- "How do I structure my team's decisions?" → GSD wheel (Kim).
- "How do I grow my direct reports?" → Career Conversations (Russ Laraway, via Kim).
- "Should I fire this person?" → Radical Candor + Netflix keeper test.
- "How do I handle bias / prejudice / bullying?" → Radical Respect (BUL) + HR/legal.
- "What is my competitive strategy?" → Not Radical Candor. Use Playing to Win or Rumelt.
