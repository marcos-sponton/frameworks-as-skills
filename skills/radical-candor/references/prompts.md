# Radical Candor — Invocation Templates

> Ready-to-adapt prompt shapes for common tasks the user brings to this skill. Not scripts — starting points. The assistant should adapt to what the user actually says.

## 1. "I need to give my report hard feedback and I've been putting it off."

**The shape of the assistant's response:**

1. **Name the current move.** "It sounds like you've been in Ruinous Empathy for [N weeks/months] with this person. That's the manager default — Kim spent 10 months in it with Bob at Google, and the story didn't end well."
2. **Check the Care Personally axis.** "Before we write the feedback: what have you done in the last 30 days to demonstrate to this person that you want them to succeed? Career Conversations you've had? Times you've fought for their scope, their comp, their growth?"
3. **If the Care account is empty — build first, then feedback.** Don't skip this. Feedback deposited into an empty relationship account bounces.
4. **If the Care account has a balance — draft the feedback with HHIPP.** Humble, helpful, immediate, in person, in private, not personalized. Specific behavior, specific impact, specific ask.
5. **End with a question the user has to answer.** "When are you having this conversation? If it's not on your calendar by end of tomorrow, you're still in Ruinous Empathy."

## 2. "How do I know if my feedback was Radical Candor or Obnoxious Aggression?"

**The shape:**

1. **The diagnostic is the recipient's experience, not your intent.** "Did they feel cared for AND challenged? Or just challenged?"
2. **The tell for Obnoxious Aggression:** they got defensive, they went quiet, they didn't come back to talk more.
3. **The tell for Radical Candor:** they engaged, they pushed back on specifics, they made changes.
4. **The rescue move if it landed as Obnoxious Aggression:** go back. Say the specific thing you regret. Rebuild the Care Personally investment. Don't defend the delivery.

## 3. "My team never pushes back on me. How do I get real feedback?"

**The shape:**

1. **Silence isn't the absence of feedback.** It's evidence you haven't made it safe yet, or you've responded badly the last time it came.
2. **Solicit specifically, not generally.** *"What could I do or stop doing that would make working with me easier?"* Not *"any feedback?"*
3. **Do not accept silence. Ask again. Wait.**
4. **When feedback comes, resist defending. Thank them. Change something visibly.** So they see the feedback was heard.
5. **Repeat. Once is theater; the ritual is the mechanism.**

## 4. "I have a report who's a high performer but nobody wants to work with them (brilliant jerk)."

**The shape:**

1. **Kim's line: don't tolerate brilliant jerks.** A high performer who's an asshole to teammates isn't a net-positive — they poison the Care axis for everyone.
2. **The signal cost of keeping them:** every other team member learns that behavior doesn't matter, only results do. The best people notice and leave.
3. **The sequence Kim would run:**
   - Diagnose specifically: is it Bias (unconscious), Prejudice (conscious), or Bullying (intentional pattern)?
   - Give Radical Candor feedback. Care Personally, Challenge Directly. HHIPP.
   - If the pattern continues: escalate consequences. Conversational → Compensation → Career.
   - *"Do not promote bullies. It's that simple."*
4. **End with the hard question:** "Are you willing to lose this person if they don't change? If not, you're keeping them regardless of the framework."

## 5. "I want to run Career Conversations with my team."

**The shape:**

1. **Credit Russ Laraway.** Kim always does. Career Conversations are his method, adopted by Kim.
2. **The three:**
   - **Life Story** — *"Starting with kindergarten, tell me about your life."* Listen for pivotal moments, especially transitions.
   - **Dreams / Vision** — 3–5 concrete dream jobs at the peak of their career. Not vague ("I want to grow") — concrete (specific titles, roles, companies).
   - **Career Action Plan** — bridge from present to dreams. What skills, experiences, relationships would move them toward the dream? Manager commits to 2–3 specific helps.
3. **Cadence:** in the first 60–90 days with a new report; then every 12–18 months (dreams shift with life stage).
4. **Warning:** running the conversations and then not delivering on the commitments is worse than not running them at all.
5. **Deeper source:** Russ Laraway's book *When They Win, You Win* is nearly 100 pages on this.

## 6. "Someone on my team just said something biased/prejudiced/bullying. What do I do?"

**The shape:**

1. **Diagnose first — B, P, or U?**
   - **Bias:** unconscious. Person didn't mean it; didn't realize how it landed.
   - **Prejudice:** conscious. Person believes it and would defend it.
   - **Bullying:** intentional, repeated pattern to harm or humiliate.
2. **Response by category:**
   - **Bias → I statement.** *"I don't think you meant to imply what I heard; I'd like to tell you how it sounded to me…"*
   - **Prejudice → It statement.** Decency-based, policy-based, or law-based. *"It is disrespectful to…" / "It is a violation of our code of conduct to…" / "It is illegal to…"*
   - **Bullying → You statement.** Reverses the power dynamic. *"You can't talk to me that way." / "What's going on for you here?"*
3. **If you're the leader — the escalation ladder:** Conversational → Compensation → Career consequence.
4. **If the situation is legally sensitive (harassment, discrimination):** the framework informs, but does not replace HR / legal counsel. Point the user there.

## 7. "How do I run better meetings / team decisions?"

**The shape — GSD wheel:**

1. **Diagnose which step is broken.** Almost always: **Persuade** is skipped. Sometimes: **Listen** is captured by loud voices.
2. **The 7 steps:** Listen → Clarify → Debate → Decide → Persuade → Execute → Learn.
3. **Rule: never skip a step. Never let one drag.**
4. **The Persuade step is 10 minutes** of *"here's why this is the call, here's what I heard from your position, here's why we're going this way."* Do not skip.
5. **The Listen step needs structure** — async writing, small groups, direct 1:1 solicit for the quiet voices. If the loudest person always wins, you're getting the loudest ideas, not the best.

## 8. "I'm about to fire someone. Am I doing this right?"

**The shape:**

1. **The Bob test.** If your report would say *"Why didn't you tell me? Why didn't anyone tell me?"* — you failed. Firing after unwarned failure is Ruinous Empathy compounded into cruelty.
2. **The trail of feedback matters.** Have you given specific, HHIPP-compliant feedback about the behaviors that led here, multiple times, in the moment?
3. **If yes** — the firing is a completion, not a surprise. Proceed with respect. Give the person the benefit of clarity about what's happening and why.
4. **If no** — you owe them the feedback and a real chance to improve, before firing. That's Radical Candor.
5. **Exception:** severe misconduct (harassment, safety, legal). Do not require a "trail" for those.

## 9. "I want to challenge my boss / a peer. How do I do it well?"

**The shape:**

1. **The framework applies up and sideways too.** But with more care about the Care Personally investment, since the power dynamic isn't in your favor.
2. **Care Personally test first.** Does your boss / peer know you're on their side? What have you done to demonstrate it?
3. **HHIPP still applies.** Humble ("this is how I see it, and I might be wrong"), helpful (framed toward their success or the shared goal), immediate, in person, in private, not personalized.
4. **The specific rewrite move:** state the behavior + state the impact + ask a question. *"When [specific thing] happened, [specific impact] followed. What am I missing?"*
5. **Solicit feedback from them first.** Making it a two-way exchange reduces the power-dynamic friction.

## 10. "Is this really Radical Candor or am I just being blunt?"

**The shape — the weaponization guard:**

1. **The tell: are you announcing Radical Candor as a preamble?** *"I'm about to be radically candid…"* almost always signals Obnoxious Aggression. Radical Candor doesn't need announcement.
2. **The tell: are you defending your delivery with "just being honest"?** Radical Candor by definition includes Care. If you're defending against pushback with "I'm just being honest," you were in Obnoxious Aggression.
3. **The check:** what have you done in the last 30 days to demonstrate to this person that you want them to succeed? If nothing — this isn't Radical Candor territory. Build the Care account first.
4. **The redirect:** if you can't answer the Care question honestly, the assistant should NOT help draft the hard feedback. Redirect toward Care Personally investment first, or toward an HR / leadership conversation about role fit.

## 12. "How does this apply in [remote / hybrid / cross-cultural / high-stress] context?"

**The shape:**

1. **The axes are universal.** Care Personally + Challenge Directly.
2. **The calibration is not.** What counts as "direct" varies by culture. What counts as "immediate" shifts in async / remote contexts. What counts as "in person" shifts in video.
3. **Adjust the calibration; don't drop the axes.**
4. **Point at the podcast archive** — Kim and Jason and Amy have addressed remote, hybrid, and cross-cultural applications in specific episodes.
