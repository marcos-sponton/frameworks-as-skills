# Radical Candor — Method

> The canonical description of Kim Scott's method in her own terms. Fidelity is the point — Radical Candor's power comes from the load-bearing role of Care Personally, and softening it collapses the framework into "just be honest," which is exactly the failure mode Kim spends most of her airtime warning against.

## Definition

Kim's operating definition of Radical Candor:

> "Radical Candor is what happens when you put 'Care Personally' and 'Challenge Directly' together."
> — Kim Scott, *Radical Candor*, 2017/2019

Or, shorter:

> "Saying what you think while also giving a damn about the person you're saying it to."
> — Kim Scott, [What is Radical Candor?](https://kimmalonescott.medium.com/what-is-radical-candor-learn-the-basic-principles-in-6-minutes-50391b3ad76a)

Result: feedback that is **"kind, clear, specific and sincere."**

## The 2×2

Two axes:

- **Care Personally®** (vertical) — Kim calls this the "give a damn axis." Not "be nice." Actually care what happens to the person on the other side of the feedback.
- **Challenge Directly®** (horizontal) — willingness to say the hard thing.

Four quadrants (use the names verbatim — they're trademarked and the shared vocabulary is load-bearing):

|  | Challenge Directly (high) | Challenge Directly (low) |
|---|---|---|
| **Care Personally (high)** | **Radical Candor** | **Ruinous Empathy** |
| **Care Personally (low)** | **Obnoxious Aggression** | **Manipulative Insincerity** |

### Radical Candor (top-right)

Caring Personally while Challenging Directly. Kind and clear, specific and sincere.

**Author's words:**
> "Caring personally makes it much easier to do the next thing you have to do as a good boss, which is being willing to piss people off."
> — Kim, First Round Review, 2015

### Ruinous Empathy (top-left)

You care about the person, so you soft-pedal or withhold the hard thing to spare their short-term feelings. Vague praise, sugar-coated criticism, "everything's fine" when it isn't. **This is the manager default, not the exception.**

Kim's canonical example: Bob at Google. Warm engineer, subpar work, ~10 months of no direct feedback, ultimately fired. Bob's line on hearing the news:
> "Why didn't you tell me? Why didn't anyone tell me?"

Kim's diagnosis of herself: her kindness harmed Bob more than any candor could have.

### Obnoxious Aggression (bottom-right)

You challenge, but the recipient doesn't feel cared for. Kim also calls this **"brutal honesty"** or **"front stabbing."** This is the quadrant that gets confused with Radical Candor — the "just being honest" defense usually lives here.

**Author's words:**
> "Radical Candor is not brutal honesty. It is not front-stabbing. It is not a license to be a jerk."
> — [Radical Candor Is Not Brutal Honesty](https://kimmalonescott.medium.com/radical-candor-is-not-brutal-honesty-6cdeb04cac53)

### Manipulative Insincerity (bottom-left)

Neither care nor challenge. Backstabbing, political maneuvering, passive-aggression, insincere praise to someone's face + criticism behind their back. Corrosive and usually invisible until damage compounds.

## CRITICAL: Radical Candor is a measurement, not an identity

**The single most important rule of the framework**, and the one violated most often:

- The 2×2 measures **specific pieces of feedback**. "Was this thing I just said in the top-right quadrant?"
- The 2×2 does NOT measure **personality**. There is no such thing as "a Radical Candor person" or "an Obnoxious Aggression person."
- Same person, same day, can be in all four quadrants.

**Why this matters:** the moment someone treats the framework as identity, they stop measuring their moves. They start justifying pre-existing bluntness as "I'm just Radically Candid" (which is Obnoxious Aggression with a rebrand) or dismissing pushback as "you can't handle my Radical Candor" (which is doubling down on the Obnoxious Aggression). Always ask about the specific feedback, never about the person.

## HHIPP — the checklist for a Radical Candor delivery

Kim's mnemonic for what makes feedback fall in the top-right quadrant:

- **H — Humble.** You might be wrong. Frame as your perspective, not absolute truth.
- **H — Helpful.** The goal is to help them win, not to prove you're smart.
- **I — Immediate.** Timely, close to the event. Not saved for the annual review.
- **I — In person.** Face-to-face when possible.
- **P — Private (for criticism) / Public (for praise).**
- **P — Not personalized in content.** Critique the behavior, not the character. ("This slide had four data errors" — not "you're careless.")

**Verbatim:**
> "Radical candor is humble, it's helpful, it's immediate, it's in person — in private if it's criticism and in public if it's praise — and it doesn't personalize."

## The Care Personally axis is load-bearing

Kim spends more airtime defending the Care axis than the Challenge axis because it's the one people drop first. If you strip out Care and keep Challenge, you have Obnoxious Aggression. Full stop.

**Test before any hard feedback:**
- *"Does this person know I want them to succeed?"*
- *"What have I done in the last 30 days to demonstrate that?"*
- If nothing: build the relationship first, then give the feedback. Feedback deposited into an empty relationship account bounces.

The Sheryl-and-Kim "um" story is the canonical example. Sheryl's blunt line — *"When you say um every third word, it makes you sound stupid"* — landed as Radical Candor because Sheryl had built Care Personally over months (book-club invites, family flexibility). The same words from a stranger, or a boss who hadn't invested, would be Obnoxious Aggression.

## The GSD wheel — Get Sh*t Done

Kim's 7-step cycle for collaborative execution. **Rule: never skip a step, never let one drag on.**

1. **Listen** — create space for ideas to surface. Both loud and quiet team members need channels; loud voices alone give you the loudest ideas, not the best ideas.
2. **Clarify** — help ideas get sharper before they get judged. Kill the "half-baked" dismissal that lets people shoot down ideas before they're understood.
3. **Debate** — separate ideas from egos; make it about the idea's fitness. No personal shots.
4. **Decide** — someone decides. Don't consensus-stall.
5. **Persuade** — convince the losing side of the debate *why* this is the right call. **This is the step most managers skip and the one that determines whether execution is committed or reluctant.**
6. **Execute** (sometimes "Implement" in Kim's writing).
7. **Learn** — retrospect, feed learnings back into the next Listen.

**Author's words on Persuade:**
> "Expecting others to execute on a decision without being persuaded that it's the right thing to do is a recipe for terrible results."

## Career Conversations — Russ Laraway's 3-conversation ritual

Developed by **Russ Laraway** (Kim's Candor Inc. co-founder, ex-Google, ex-Twitter). Attributed openly by Kim; treated as canon. Kim credits Russ every time she teaches it, and this skill does the same.

Three conversations, spaced weeks apart, run by the manager. Not a substitute for performance reviews — this is the *strategic* career conversation, separate from tactical performance feedback.

### 1. Life Story

Opening line:
> "Starting with kindergarten, tell me about your life."

Listen for pivotal moments, especially transitions (why did they switch jobs, cities, fields?). Reveals values.

### 2. Dreams / Vision

What does the person's future look like at the peak of their career? Push for **3–5 concrete dream jobs** — specific titles, roles, companies. Not vague aspirations ("I want to grow"). Concrete alternatives ("I might want to be the head of design at a Series B climate startup, OR the founder of a specialized studio, OR a professor").

### 3. Career Action Plan

Bridge from present to dreams. What skills, experiences, relationships would move them toward the dream? Manager commits to helping with 2–3 specific ones — introductions, projects, mentorship, budget.

**Cadence:** run the three within the first 60–90 days with a new direct report; repeat every 12–18 months (dreams shift with life stage).

## Rock Stars vs. Superstars

Two shapes of high performance. **Both wanted. Both required for a healthy team.** NOT a synonym for "high potential vs. low potential" — that framing is exactly what Kim wants to kill.

- **Rock Stars** — gradual growth trajectory. World-class at their craft, deeply content, don't want promotion, don't want to manage. The "Rock of Gibraltar" of a team. Provide continuity, institutional knowledge, deep expertise. Kim's example: Taylor Malone (stability + fishing time).
- **Superstars** — steep growth trajectory. Would go stir-crazy in the same job in 12 months. Ambitious for scope, promotion, new problems. Provide innovation and organizational growth. Kim's example: Joe Hyde (empire-builder).

### Rules Kim insists on

- A team of all superstars is unstable — everyone gunning for the next promotion, no one anchoring.
- A team of all rock stars stagnates.
- Treating "wants promotion" as the marker of value systematically drives your rock stars out. Kim considers this one of her biggest historical mistakes.
- Trajectory shifts with life stage. Someone can be superstar → rock star (during a family season) → superstar again. Don't lock them in.
- **The way to know which one someone is: Career Conversations, not guessing.**

### How to reward each differently

- **Rock stars:** recognition, scope-deepening, expert-track paths, stability, protection from unnecessary re-orgs, credit for institutional knowledge.
- **Superstars:** new problems, promotion, scope-growth, stretch assignments, exposure.

## BUL — Just Work / Radical Respect (2021 → 2024)

Kim's framework for distinguishing three categories of workplace injustice + response tools. From *Just Work* (2021), rewritten as *Radical Respect* (2024, 40k words shorter, restructured to emphasize leader action).

### The three categories

- **Bias (B) — "not meaning it."** Unconscious. Automatic conclusions from stereotypes.
- **Prejudice (P) — "meaning it."** Conscious. Rationalized belief the person holds and would defend.
- **Bullying (U — from "bUllying") — "being mean."** *"The intentional, repeated use of in-group status or power to harm or humiliate others."*

### Response techniques by category

- **For Bias — "I" statements.** Invite perspective-taking without accusation.
  - *"I don't think you meant to imply what I heard; I'd like to tell you how it sounded to me..."*
  - Factual corrections: *"I am the person you are negotiating with. This is Jack, my summer intern."*
- **For Prejudice — "It" statements.** Establish the boundary between belief and behavior. Three types:
  - Decency-based: *"It is disrespectful to call a grown woman a girl."*
  - Policy-based: reference company code of conduct.
  - Law-based: *"It is illegal to refuse to hire women."*
- **For Bullying — "You" statements.** Reverse the power dynamic; put scrutiny on the perpetrator.
  - *"What's going on for you here?"*
  - *"You can't talk to me that way."*

### Roles by proximity to the incident

- **Person harmed** — chooses how to respond; not obligated to educate.
- **Upstander / observer** — steps in; language depends on B/P/U category.
- **Leader** — designs the system. Kim's escalation ladder for bullying:
  - **Conversational** consequence (direct feedback + warning).
  - **Compensation** consequence (no raises/bonuses for bullies).
  - **Career** consequence (no promotions; dismissal if behavior continues).

**Author's words:**
> "Do not promote bullies. It's that simple."

### Radical Respect (2024) — the two goals

The 2024 rewrite frames the operating system as two workplace goals held simultaneously:

- **Optimize for collaboration, not coercion.**
- **Honor individuality, don't demand conformity.**

Definition of respect Kim uses:
> "A regard for the feelings, wishes, rights, and traditions of others" — owed to everyone by default, not earned.

Distinct from admiration-based respect.

## Integration — the four moves working together

Kim's method isn't the 2×2 alone. It's the 2×2 as **diagnostic**, HHIPP as **delivery checklist**, GSD as **team decision-making OS**, Career Conversations as **retention + growth OS**, Rock Stars/Superstars as **talent-planning lens**, and BUL as **injustice-response OS**. In practice:

- **The 2×2** tells you what quadrant a piece of feedback is in.
- **HHIPP** tells you how to deliver so it lands in the top-right quadrant.
- **GSD** is where day-to-day feedback happens — inside a working process, not a quarterly review.
- **Career Conversations** build the Care Personally axis over time so that when hard feedback comes, the account has a balance to draw from.
- **Rock Stars/Superstars** tells you what feedback + growth opportunity each direct report actually needs.
- **BUL** tells you when the failure mode is not "bad feedback" but "structural injustice" and what to do about it.

The moves reinforce each other. A manager who runs Career Conversations well has Care Personally receipts to draw on when hard feedback is needed. A manager who runs the GSD wheel well is embedding feedback into every project, not saving it up. A manager who identifies a Rock Star correctly stops giving them "grow into management" feedback that would frustrate both sides.

## What this method is NOT

Kim is emphatic about the negative definitions:

- **NOT brutal honesty.** *"Radical Candor is not brutal honesty."* If it feels like brutality to the recipient, it's Obnoxious Aggression.
- **NOT a personality.** The 2×2 measures specific feedback moves, not people.
- **NOT permission to unload.** Announcing "I'm about to be radically candid" almost always signals Obnoxious Aggression.
- **NOT the annual review.** Feedback lives in the moment. If a report hears something at their annual review that they didn't hear when it happened, the manager failed.
- **NOT the same as Radical Transparency.** Kim explicitly distinguishes from Dalio's Bridgewater model — that model tends to drop the Care Personally axis and curdle into Obnoxious Aggression. See `references/applications.md`.
- **NOT a US-only cultural norm.** Kim addresses cross-cultural application in the podcast — the axes are universal, but the *calibration* of what counts as "direct" shifts by culture. The framework survives calibration; the axes don't survive being dropped.
