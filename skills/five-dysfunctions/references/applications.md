# Five Dysfunctions — When to Use, When Not, Adjacent Frameworks

> Lencioni's pyramid is opinionated and it is also not a universal tool. This file names where the method fits, where it doesn't, and how it relates to adjacent frameworks — so the assistant can either apply it or point at the better tool.

## When the Five Dysfunctions pyramid fits

- **Executive / leadership team dynamics.** The primary target. Lencioni's model is most consequential at the top of the org where collective results get set. The Advantage's first discipline is "build a cohesive leadership team."
- **Any team whose members share collective responsibility for outcomes.** Product teams, engineering teams, project teams — as long as the members need each other to succeed. Interdependence is required.
- **Diagnosing a team that keeps missing collective results.** Diagnose down the pyramid — the results problem is usually a symptom of a broken layer below.
- **Teams stuck at a specific pattern:** civil-but-nothing-happens (Absence of Trust), always-agree-then-hallway-relitigate (Fear of Conflict), consensus-stall (Lack of Commitment), manager-as-sole-enforcer (Avoidance of Accountability), individual-heroes-but-team-misses (Inattention to Results).
- **Leadership team off-sites, especially the first one for a new team or after a re-org / merger.** The pyramid gives the sequence for the off-site: trust exercises first, then productive-conflict practice, then commitment discipline, then peer accountability, then collective-results focus.
- **After the pyramid is stable, the rest of Lencioni's system.** Ideal Team Player for hiring; The Motive for the leader's own posture; Working Genius for role and genius-gap decisions; The Advantage's Four Disciplines for org-level cohesion.

## When the pyramid does NOT fit (or fits poorly)

- **Groups that aren't teams.** A set of independent contributors reporting to the same manager, without shared collective responsibility, is a group. The pyramid doesn't diagnose the same way. Force-fitting it creates artificial dysfunction diagnoses.
- **One-on-one manager-report feedback dynamics.** That's Kim Scott's Radical Candor territory. The pyramid works at team level; Radical Candor works at dyad level. See below.
- **Individual productivity, burnout, or role-fit questions.** Working Genius (WIDGET) is the right instrument, not the pyramid.
- **Strategic / competitive positioning questions.** Not a team-dynamics problem. Reach for Playing to Win (Roger Martin), Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Rumelt), 7 Powers (Helmer), or Positioning (April Dunford).
- **Legal / HR-compliance situations** (harassment, discrimination, safety). The framework informs; it does not replace HR/legal counsel. Direct the user to their HR partner or attorney.
- **Deeply cross-cultural teams where the calibration of "vulnerability" and "conflict" varies sharply.** The pyramid's logic survives translation; the specific practices may need cultural adaptation. Cite Erin Meyer's *The Culture Map* as the calibration lens.
- **When the user is fishing to label a colleague.** *"Is Susan an Absence of Trust person?"* is not a Five Dysfunctions question. The pyramid diagnoses team dispositions and specific behaviors, not personalities. Redirect.

## Adjacent frameworks — when each is the better tool

### Amy Edmondson — The Fearless Organization / psychological safety

- **Edmondson:** psychological safety = belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns, or mistakes. Research-based, empirical, hospital-team origins. Four-stage model (Inclusion Safety → Learner Safety → Contributor Safety → Challenger Safety).
- **Lencioni:** vulnerability-based trust = willingness to be exposed with one another. Framework-based, practitioner-derived. Downstream pyramid.
- **Same substrate, different vocabulary.** Edmondson's psychological safety and Lencioni's vulnerability-based trust are near-identical concepts named for different audiences. Both are the load-bearing foundation for team performance.
- **When Edmondson is better:** you need empirical research to convince a skeptical audience (executives, HR leaders trained in academic frameworks); you're doing organization-wide safety work at scale; you want the four-stage model of safety maturation.
- **When Lencioni is better:** you're working with a specific team; you want the sequential pyramid of what the foundation enables; you want a fable that installs the concept for a non-academic audience.
- **Compose:** Edmondson's research legitimizes the foundation; Lencioni's pyramid gives the operational sequence for what the foundation enables. Cite both when the audience needs both credibility and applicability. See `_research/patrick-lencioni.md` for the deeper distinction.

### Kim Scott — Radical Candor

- **Kim:** Care Personally + Challenge Directly. 2×2 matrix (Radical Candor / Ruinous Empathy / Obnoxious Aggression / Manipulative Insincerity). Focus on manager-report feedback dynamics.
- **Lencioni:** Trust → Conflict → Commitment → Accountability → Results. Focus on team-level dynamics, especially leadership teams.
- **Overlap on productive conflict.** Kim's "Challenge Directly without personalizing" is Lencioni's "productive ideological conflict, not personal." Kim's Ruinous Empathy is one form of Lencioni's Artificial Harmony scaled down to a dyad.
- **When Kim is better:** you're preparing a specific one-on-one hard conversation; you're diagnosing why your team doesn't push back on *you* specifically; you're running 1:1s.
- **When Lencioni is better:** you're diagnosing a team's collective disposition; you're running a leadership team off-site; you're designing team-level accountability rituals.
- **Compose:** Radical Candor is how individual managers give feedback inside a team the Five Dysfunctions is trying to build. Same operating stack, different layers. See the `radical-candor` skill in this repo for the deeper treatment.

### Roger Martin — Playing to Win

- **Martin:** strategy cascade (winning aspiration → where to play → how to win → capabilities → management systems).
- **Lencioni:** organizational health, not strategy. *The Advantage* argues smart (the cascade) is table stakes; healthy is the durable edge.
- **Not competing — different problem entirely.** Martin answers *what to do*; Lencioni answers *how the team that executes it works together*.
- **When Martin is better:** you're deciding strategy, positioning, or where to play; you're critiquing a strategy doc.
- **When Lencioni is better:** the strategy is set and the leadership team can't execute it because they can't hold the pyramid together.
- **Compose:** a company with a Playing to Win strategy AND a Five Dysfunctions leadership team is more likely to execute than one with the strategy alone. See the `playing-to-win` skill.

### Christina Wodtke — Radical Focus (OKRs + Team Health Monitor)

- **Wodtke:** OKRs + a specific weekly cadence + the Team Health Monitor (Wodtke's own instrument, adjacent to Lencioni's team assessment).
- **Lencioni:** the pyramid that has to hold for OKRs to stick. Wodtke's Team Health Monitor is spiritually adjacent to Lencioni's team assessment.
- **When Wodtke is better:** you're installing OKRs; you need the specific meeting rhythm and rituals.
- **When Lencioni is better:** the OKRs are already there but the team can't hold them because Trust or Commitment is broken.
- **Compose:** the team that runs OKRs from Wodtke's rituals needs Lencioni's pyramid to have Trust → Conflict → Commitment for the OKRs to stick. OKRs on top of Absence of Trust produce theater. See the `radical-focus` skill.

### Andy Grove — High Output Management

- **Grove:** managerial output = output of your team + output of teams you influence. Mechanisms (1:1s, task-relevant maturity, meeting types).
- **Lencioni:** what has to be true about the team for those mechanisms to work.
- **When Grove is better:** you're designing the manager mechanics from scratch — 1:1 cadence, meeting types, task-relevant maturity.
- **When Lencioni is better:** the mechanics exist and the team's disposition is what's broken.
- **Compose:** Grove is the plumbing; Lencioni is the water quality.

### Brené Brown — Dare to Lead

- **Brown:** vulnerability as leadership discipline, "rumbling with vulnerability" in hard conversations, "clear is kind, unclear is unkind."
- **Lencioni:** vulnerability-based trust as team-level substrate.
- **Same substrate, different scope.** Brown is deeper on the individual leader's inner work; Lencioni is broader on team dynamics that vulnerability enables.
- **When Brown is better:** the primary lever is the leader's own emotional courage and self-trust. Deeper personal work.
- **When Lencioni is better:** the primary lever is the team's collective disposition and specific team-level rituals.
- **Compose:** Brown gives the leader the inner work; Lencioni gives the team the operating sequence.

### Claire Hughes Johnson — Scaling People

- **CHJ:** operational manager craft, from Stripe / Google. Cites Lencioni explicitly (Five Dysfunctions and Ideal Team Player show up in the book).
- **Composition is native:** *Scaling People* sits on top of Lencioni's frameworks and adds operational detail (hiring rubrics, feedback templates, org-design patterns).
- **When CHJ is better:** you're a manager needing specific operational templates.
- **When Lencioni is better:** you're diagnosing a team disposition or setting the strategic direction of the team culture.

### Netflix "keeper test" / Reed Hastings' *No Rules Rules*

- **Netflix:** "would you fight to keep this person?" as the performance filter; explicit "no brilliant jerks" rule; high performance density.
- **Lencioni:** strong agreement on the brilliant-jerks rule — this is the Skillful Politician (Hungry + Smart, not Humble). Lencioni's Ideal Team Player is a broader diagnostic than the keeper test.
- **When Netflix is better:** high-growth environments where role fit is dynamic; a performance-density strategic bet.
- **When Lencioni is better:** ongoing team development, not just the retention decision.
- **Compose:** Netflix's keeper test is a retention decision; Lencioni's Ideal Team Player is a hiring rubric + Ongoing development lens. Both agree the Skillful Politician is disqualifying.

### Simon Sinek — Start With Why / Infinite Game

- **Sinek:** Why → How → What. Purpose-first leadership.
- **Lencioni:** Six Critical Questions from *The Advantage* start with "Why do we exist?" — same territory.
- **When Sinek is better:** you're doing brand or purpose work, especially externally-facing.
- **When Lencioni is better:** you're doing organizational cohesion work — Sinek gives the "why"; Lencioni gives the discipline to communicate and reinforce it across an organization.

### Kim Scott's Rock Stars / Superstars

- Similar spirit to Lencioni's Ideal Team Player + Working Genius: differentiation of who to reward how.
- **Compose:** treat Rock Star vs. Superstar as a growth-trajectory lens; treat Humble/Hungry/Smart as a hiring-fit lens; treat Working Genius as an energy-and-role-fit lens. All three can apply to the same person.

## Adjacent frameworks NOT to conflate

- **Five Dysfunctions ≠ personality typing.** MBTI, DISC, StrengthsFinder, Enneagram. The pyramid diagnoses team behaviors, not people. Do not blend.
- **Five Dysfunctions ≠ "team building."** A vague category that usually means "an off-site event." Lencioni's method is a set of ongoing team disciplines with specific practices. Do not use "team building" as a synonym.
- **Five Dysfunctions ≠ psychological safety.** Adjacent, not identical. Edmondson stays closer to the research; Lencioni names the downstream behaviors. Both are legitimate; the vocabularies are different for different audiences. See `_research/patrick-lencioni.md`.
- **Five Dysfunctions ≠ Working Genius.** Different diagnostics. Pyramid = how the team works together. WIDGET = what kinds of work each team member is suited for and where the team has genius gaps.
- **The Advantage ≠ the Five Dysfunctions book with a new cover.** *The Advantage* is a broader operating system where the pyramid is one of four disciplines. Reach for The Advantage when the question is org-level; reach for the pyramid when the question is team-level.
- **Reward-centered leadership ≠ selfish leader.** The Motive frames it as a *belief system about what leadership is*, not a character flaw. Reward-centered leaders can be well-intentioned people whose framing is wrong.

## When to compose Five Dysfunctions with another framework

- **Leadership team off-site design** — Lencioni's pyramid + specific meeting practices from *Death by Meeting* + Six Critical Questions from *The Advantage*.
- **Hiring for a leadership team** — Ideal Team Player (humble/hungry/smart) + Working Genius (WIDGET gap analysis) + the pyramid to shape the team's expected disposition.
- **Manager craft inside a team the pyramid is trying to build** — Radical Candor (Kim Scott) for 1:1s + High Output Management (Grove) for mechanics + the pyramid for team disposition.
- **Culture at scale** — The Advantage's Four Disciplines + Edmondson's psychological safety research (to legitimize) + Lencioni's team-level rituals (to install).
- **Strategy execution** — Playing to Win (strategy) + Five Dysfunctions (leadership team disposition) + Radical Focus / OKRs (execution cadence).
- **DEI / harassment / bullying** — Radical Respect / Just Work (Kim Scott's BUL taxonomy) + HR/legal protocols. Lencioni informs the team-health substrate; specific incident response is not his framework.

## Rule of thumb

If the question is:
- "How do we build a cohesive team?" → **Five Dysfunctions.**
- "How do I give this specific report feedback?" → **Radical Candor** (Kim Scott).
- "Why is my team stuck at [specific dysfunction]?" → **Five Dysfunctions** — diagnose the layer.
- "How do I run better meetings?" → **Death by Meeting** (Lencioni) + Five Dysfunctions to diagnose why meetings are broken.
- "How do I hire the right people?" → **The Ideal Team Player** (Lencioni, humble/hungry/smart).
- "Why am I exhausted at work?" → **Working Genius** (Lencioni, WIDGET).
- "How do I fix cross-department politics?" → **Silos, Politics and Turf Wars** (Lencioni) + Five Dysfunctions on each team.
- "Why can't the leadership team execute the strategy?" → **Five Dysfunctions** (pyramid) + **The Advantage** (organizational health).
- "How do I decide our competitive positioning?" → Not Lencioni. Use **Playing to Win** (Martin) or **7 Powers** (Helmer) or **Obviously Awesome** (April Dunford).
- "Why don't people speak up?" → **Fearless Organization** (Edmondson) + **Five Dysfunctions** (Absence of Trust → Fear of Conflict).
- "How do I know if I'm the right kind of leader?" → **The Motive** (Lencioni, reward-centered vs. responsibility-centered).
- "Should I fire this high performer nobody wants to work with?" → **The Ideal Team Player** (probable Skillful Politician).
- "How do I run OKRs on a team?" → **Radical Focus** (Wodtke) + **Five Dysfunctions** if the team can't hold commitments.
- "How do I handle bias / prejudice / bullying?" → **Radical Respect** (Kim Scott) + HR/legal.
- "Legal harassment question?" → Not Lencioni. HR / legal counsel first.
