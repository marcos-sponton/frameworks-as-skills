# Five Dysfunctions — Post-Book Material

> The material Patrick Lencioni has published *after* *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team* (2002) — 24 years of refinements, extensions, and adjacent frameworks that form one operating system. This is the differential of this skill: the base model knows the 2002 book; the skill knows what's happened since.

## Timeline

- **1998** — *The Five Temptations of a CEO* — Jossey-Bass. Precursor: five temptations that cause CEOs to fail. Three of them directly seed the 5 dysfunctions pyramid.
- **2000** — *The Four Obsessions of an Extraordinary Executive* — Jossey-Bass. Introduces "organizational health" as a concept. Four disciplines (build a cohesive leadership team, create clarity, overcommunicate clarity, reinforce clarity) — later expanded in *The Advantage*.
- **2002** — *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team: A Leadership Fable* — Jossey-Bass. The canonical fable. Kathryn Petersen at DecisionTech.
- **2004** — *Death by Meeting* — Jossey-Bass. Meeting-craft as leadership discipline. Argues meetings are boring precisely because leaders remove conflict.
- **2005** — *Overcoming the Five Dysfunctions of a Team: A Field Guide* — Jossey-Bass. Practitioner companion with tools, exercises, scripts.
- **2006** — *Silos, Politics and Turf Wars* — Jossey-Bass. Cross-functional dysfunction. "Thematic goal" as the unifying device.
- **2007** — *The Truth About Employee Engagement* (originally *The Three Signs of a Miserable Job*) — Jossey-Bass. Three causes of misery: anonymity, irrelevance, immeasurement.
- **2010** — *Getting Naked* — Jossey-Bass. Vulnerability applied to consulting client relationships.
- **2012** — *The Advantage: Why Organizational Health Trumps Everything Else in Business* — Jossey-Bass. **First non-fable book.** Consolidates the operating system. Four Disciplines + Six Critical Questions.
- **2016** — *The Ideal Team Player* — Jossey-Bass. Hiring/firing layer. Humble + hungry + smart.
- **2019** — *At The Table with Patrick Lencioni* podcast launches with co-host Cody Thompson. Weekly episodes since.
- **2020** — *The Motive* — Matt Holt Books. Leader-focus. Reward-centered vs. responsibility-centered leadership.
- **2022** — **20th Anniversary Edition** of *The Five Dysfunctions* — Jossey-Bass.
- **2022** — *The 6 Types of Working Genius* — Matt Holt Books. WIDGET framework. New product line + workinggenius.com.
- **Ongoing** — weekly podcast, LinkedIn cadence, Working Genius certification, Table Group consulting practice.

## The Advantage (2012) — the consolidation

The closest thing to a Lencioni "grand unified theory." Not a fable — his first straight practitioner book.

**Argument:**
> "The single, greatest advantage any company can achieve is organizational health."
> — *The Advantage*

**Smart vs. healthy:** organizations obsess over *smart* (strategy, tech, marketing, finance) because it's what MBA programs teach. But *healthy* (integrity, cohesion, clarity, minimal politics) is where the durable edge is — and it's underinvested in because it feels touchy-feely.

### The Four Disciplines of a healthy organization

1. **Build a cohesive leadership team.** The Five Dysfunctions pyramid applied to the executive team. **This is where the 2002 book lives inside the larger system.** The pyramid is discipline #1 of the four.
2. **Create clarity.** The leadership team answers the Six Critical Questions together, out of shared discussion, in one voice.
3. **Overcommunicate clarity.** Repeat the answers relentlessly across every channel. Lencioni's rule: any leader who thinks they're overcommunicating is probably just starting.
4. **Reinforce clarity.** Hard-wire the answers into hiring, performance management, compensation, recognition, promotion. If HR processes don't reflect the answers, the answers aren't real.

### The Six Critical Questions

The clarity artifact. The leadership team must be able to answer these in one voice:

1. **Why do we exist?** — the core purpose.
2. **How do we behave?** — the core values (aspirational vs. actual distinguished).
3. **What do we do?** — the business definition.
4. **How will we succeed?** — the strategic anchors.
5. **What is most important, right now?** — the thematic goal (a single time-bound rallying point).
6. **Who must do what?** — role clarity at the executive team level.

**"What is most important, right now?"** — the thematic goal from *Silos, Politics and Turf Wars* also lives here. A single, time-bound, cross-functional rallying point (usually 3–12 months). Not a strategic plan; not a mission. A single north-star answer to "if we win at one thing this quarter, what is it?"

### Key quote for the skill

> "Success is not a matter of mastering subtle, sophisticated theory but rather of embracing common sense with uncommon levels of discipline and persistence."
> — *The Advantage*

**When to pull from The Advantage in a session:**
- User is asking about org-level cohesion, not just team-level.
- User is a CEO thinking about company alignment.
- User is dealing with cross-department politics (add Silos content).
- User needs the Six Critical Questions to structure a strategy off-site.
- User is undervaluing "healthy" and overweighting "smart."

## The Ideal Team Player (2016) — the hiring layer

The 2002 book presumed you had the right people on the team. *The Ideal Team Player* is the diagnostic that sources them.

### The three virtues

- **Humble** — quick to point out others' contributions, slow to seek attention. Emphasizes team over self; defines success collectively.
- **Hungry** — self-motivated, diligent. Rarely has to be pushed. Constantly thinking about the next step.
- **Smart** — **interpersonally smart.** Common sense about people. Says the right thing at the right time; doesn't step on toes. **Not IQ; not technical smart.**

**All three virtues, at meaningful level, are required for an ideal team player.**

### The diagnostic power of missing one virtue

- **Humble + Hungry, not Smart → the Accidental Mess-Maker.** Works hard, wants to help, but keeps blowing up relationships. Doesn't read the room. Coachable.
- **Humble + Smart, not Hungry → the Loveable Slacker.** Easy to work with, does just enough. Rarely a fireable offense; slowly eats team velocity.
- **Hungry + Smart, not Humble → the Skillful Politician.** The most dangerous type. High individual performance, popular with leadership, poisonous to peers. Hardest to spot at hiring; hardest to remove later.

### The hiring rule

Missing Humble is disqualifying. Missing Hungry or Smart is coachable but hard. The Ideal Team Player is a hiring rubric, not an aspirational HR bullet.

### Assessment tool

Table Group publishes an Ideal Team Player assessment: [https://www.tablegroup.com/idealteamplayer/](https://www.tablegroup.com/idealteamplayer/)

**When to pull from Ideal Team Player in a session:**
- User is hiring, especially for a leadership team.
- User is deciding whether to fire a high performer nobody wants to work with (probable Skillful Politician).
- User is asking why a team keeps blowing up despite talent (probable Accidental Mess-Maker or Skillful Politician).
- User needs a rubric to justify a hiring or promotion decision.

## The Motive (2020) — the leader's why

Lencioni's response to years of watching leaders who technically understood the frameworks still fail. The failure wasn't skill — it was **motive**.

### The two motives

- **Reward-centered leadership.** Leadership is the payoff for hard work. Should be enjoyable, high-status, chosen work, freedom from mundane duties. The leader avoids what's uncomfortable and delegates the hard parts of leading.
- **Responsibility-centered leadership.** Leadership is a duty. Should be difficult, uncomfortable, in service of others. Personal gratification is a byproduct, not the point.

### The five omissions of reward-centered leaders

Reward-centered leaders systematically avoid these five duties — the same five that are needed to fix the pyramid:

1. **Developing the leadership team.** (The Five Dysfunctions pyramid, applied to the exec team.)
2. **Managing subordinates.** Real 1:1s, real feedback, real coaching — not status updates.
3. **Having difficult conversations.** Underperformance, culture violations, hard trade-offs.
4. **Running great meetings.** Not efficient meetings — productive-conflict meetings. See *Death by Meeting*.
5. **Communicating constantly and repetitively.** Overcommunication of clarity, from *The Advantage*.

### The diagnostic power

If a leader complains that these five things are hard, tedious, or beneath them — they're reward-centered. Responsibility-centered leaders expect the difficulty.

### Why this matters for the skill

**Diagnose the leader before you diagnose the team.** A reward-centered CEO cannot fix Absence of Trust because they won't be vulnerable themselves. Every Five Dysfunctions intervention on a reward-centered leader's team is trust theater. Fix the motive first.

**When to pull from The Motive in a session:**
- User is a leader complaining that the "people stuff" is exhausting.
- User is asking why a team can't be fixed despite prescribing all the right interventions.
- User is describing a leader who avoids the five duties.
- User is choosing whether to take a leadership role and asking what it's really like.

## The 6 Types of Working Genius (2022) — the WIDGET framework

The newest product line. Individual + team framework for productivity, satisfaction, and where people burn out. Launched with its own site + assessment + facilitator certification: [https://www.workinggenius.com](https://www.workinggenius.com).

### The six geniuses (WIDGET acronym)

Grouped into three stages of any work project:

**Ideation:**
- **Wonder (W)** — pondering, asking "what if?" and "why?" questions; sensing that something could be different / better.
- **Invention (I)** — creating original, novel ideas and solutions from scratch or from Wonder's questions.

**Activation:**
- **Discernment (D)** — assessing merit and workability; intuitive judgment on what works and what doesn't.
- **Galvanizing (G)** — inspiring, encouraging, mobilizing; getting people moving on an idea.

**Implementation:**
- **Enablement (E)** — providing support; helping others get an initiative off the ground.
- **Tenacity (T)** — pushing to meet ambitious goals; blowing through obstacles to finish.

### The rule of 2-2-2

Every person has:
- **2 geniuses** — energizing, joyful, sustainable.
- **2 competencies** — neutral. Can do them, doesn't love or hate them.
- **2 frustrations** — draining. Burns out doing them.

**Nobody is genius at all six.** Assuming someone should be — or hiring someone whose frustrations are the job — is a common failure mode.

### How it connects to team health

- When a person is in a role dominated by their frustrations → they burn out AND their work is worse.
- When a team lacks a genius in a critical stage → projects die there.
- **Common failure pattern:** heavy on Invention + Galvanizing, light on Discernment + Tenacity → lots of exciting starts, nothing finishes.
- **Another common pattern:** heavy on Tenacity + Enablement, light on Wonder + Invention → efficient execution of the wrong things.

### Working Genius and Five Dysfunctions — complementary, not competing

- **Five Dysfunctions** diagnoses *how the team works together* — trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, results.
- **Working Genius** diagnoses *what kinds of work each team member is suited for* and *where the team has genius gaps*.

Both can apply to the same team. Different failure modes. Reach for Working Genius when the failure is at a specific stage of work (ideation dies, projects don't finish); reach for Five Dysfunctions when the failure is in team dynamics (nobody says the hard thing, decisions don't stick, no accountability).

### Assessment + certification

- Individual assessment: $25 at [workinggenius.com](https://www.workinggenius.com).
- Team debrief: teams take the assessment together and map the team's genius coverage.
- Facilitator certification for consultants and coaches.

**When to pull from Working Genius in a session:**
- User is asking why a team keeps failing at a specific stage of work.
- User is asking why they personally are exhausted despite loving their job.
- User is designing a team and asking who to add.
- User is asking about individual productivity or burnout, not team dynamics.

## Overcoming the Five Dysfunctions (2005) — the field guide

The practitioner companion the 2002 fable didn't have. Exercises, scripts, worked examples, worksheets.

**Why it matters:** *The Five Dysfunctions* (2002) is a fable + theory appendix. It teaches you what the pyramid is; it doesn't hand you the exercises to run. The field guide does. Trust exercises, conflict-mining scripts, commitment-summary templates, accountability rituals.

**Rule for the skill:** if a user is going to actually do a Five Dysfunctions intervention, the field guide is prerequisite. The fable alone produces surface-level understanding.

Purchase: [Overcoming The Five Dysfunctions of a Team](https://www.tablegroup.com/product/overcoming-the-five-dysfunctions-a-field-guide/).

## Death by Meeting (2004), Silos Politics and Turf Wars (2006), Getting Naked (2010) — adjacent fables

- **Death by Meeting** — meetings are boring because leaders remove conflict. Four meeting types: daily check-in, weekly tactical, monthly strategic, quarterly off-site. Use when the user's team dynamics are stuck at the meeting-cadence level.
- **Silos, Politics and Turf Wars** — cross-functional dysfunction. "Thematic goal" as the unifying device across silos. Use when the user's problem spans departments.
- **Getting Naked** — vulnerability applied to consulting client relationships. Use when the user is a consultant / service provider asking about client trust.

These are not core Five Dysfunctions material but are part of the same operating system and can be reached for when the topic maps.

## The Five Temptations of a CEO (1998) — the precursor

Lencioni's first book. Five temptations that cause CEOs to fail. The three lowest temptations directly seed what became the pyramid four years later:

1. Choosing status over results → Inattention to Results.
2. Choosing popularity over accountability → Avoidance of Accountability.
3. Choosing certainty over clarity → Lack of Commitment (arguably).
4. Choosing harmony over productive conflict → Fear of Conflict.
5. Choosing invulnerability over trust → Absence of Trust.

**Read as archaeology, not primary reference.** The pyramid supersedes the temptations, but knowing the origin explains why Lencioni treats each dysfunction with such moral clarity — they're leadership temptations before they're team dynamics.

## Where Lencioni's thinking has evolved most since 2002

- **Post-book emphasis on organizational health, not just team dysfunctions.** *The Advantage* frames the pyramid as one of four disciplines — the individual-team frame gets embedded in the org-health frame.
- **The Ideal Team Player fills the hiring gap** the 2002 book presumed away. Humble + hungry + smart is the diagnostic for who to put on the team the pyramid is trying to build.
- **The Motive** shifted focus from team behaviors to leader psychology. Lencioni's admission that even the best framework fails without the right leader posture.
- **Working Genius** added an individual-productivity + team-genius-gap lens the pyramid never had. Not a replacement — an adjacent instrument.
- **The podcast** gives Lencioni a live, weekly voice — the framework applied continuously, refined publicly, updated for current situations (remote work, generational shifts, AI).
- **Language refinements:** *"vulnerability-based trust"* (2002) still holds; *"productive ideological conflict"* clarified over time; *"peer-to-peer accountability"* gets more emphasis in later work than it did in the 2002 book.

## What Lencioni has NOT changed his mind about

- **The pyramid is unchanged.** Five dysfunctions, same order, trademarked names. Stable across 24 years.
- **Vulnerability-based trust is load-bearing.** He defends it harder now than in 2002.
- **Artificial harmony is a failure mode, not a success.** He's more direct about this in the podcast than in the book.
- **Buy-in, not consensus.** Consistent across all publications.
- **First team = peers, not reports.** Consistent since introduced.
- **Organizational health trumps smart.** The 2012 argument in *The Advantage* has intensified, not softened.
- **The fable as pedagogy.** Every subsequent book (except *The Advantage*) uses the fable format. Lencioni's insistence that stories install frameworks in a way lectures don't.

## Where the skill should pull from post-book material

- **When the topic is org-level, not team-level** — reach for *The Advantage*'s Four Disciplines and Six Critical Questions.
- **When the topic is hiring, firing, or "brilliant jerk" decisions** — reach for *The Ideal Team Player*'s humble/hungry/smart.
- **When the topic is a leader who won't do the work** — reach for *The Motive*'s reward-centered vs. responsibility-centered diagnostic.
- **When the topic is individual productivity, burnout, or team genius gaps** — reach for *The 6 Types of Working Genius* (WIDGET).
- **When the topic is running better meetings** — reach for *Death by Meeting*'s four meeting types.
- **When the topic is cross-functional dysfunction** — reach for *Silos, Politics and Turf Wars* and the "thematic goal."
- **When the topic needs a fresh applied example** — search the podcast archive.
- **When the topic is "how do I actually run the pyramid intervention?"** — reach for *Overcoming the Five Dysfunctions* (2005 field guide).
