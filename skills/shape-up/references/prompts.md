# Shape Up — Invocation Templates

> Prompt templates for common Shape Up situations. Copy the shape, fill in the specifics. The templates are designed to prime the assistant to apply the method with fidelity — challenging weak framings rather than complying with them.

## Shape a pitch/package from scratch

> "I want to shape a bet on [FEATURE / PROBLEM]. Walk me through Shape Up's shaping — help me get to a Package with all five ingredients (Problem, Appetite, Solution, Rabbit Holes, No-Gos). Start by asking whether we've framed the problem first (whose problem, why now, business value). Push back on any part that isn't concrete enough."

## Critique a pitch/package written by someone else

> "Here's a pitch/package I've written [or here's one from a colleague]. Critique it against Ryan Singer's Shape Up method. Check: is the Problem a specific story showing why the status quo doesn't work? Is the Appetite stated and reasonable (small batch 1–2 weeks OR big batch full 6-week cycle)? Is the Solution at fat-marker / breadboard fidelity, or has it drifted into high-fidelity mocks? Are Rabbit Holes named? Are No-Gos named? Was it shaped with real technical depth in the room? Cite Singer at least once."

## Frame a problem before shaping

> "We have a [RAW IDEA / STAKEHOLDER REQUEST]. Before shaping a solution, help me frame the problem in Shape Up's terms. Whose problem? Why now? What's the business value? Would committing 6 weeks to this be worth it — and what would that mean specifically? Don't jump to solutions yet."

## Run or fix a Betting Table

> "We're running our first Betting Table [or our Betting Table isn't working]. Walk me through how Shape Up says this should work. What comes in (shaped packages, not slides). Who's in the room. What the decision criteria are. Why most packages should not be bet on. Push back if I describe anything that sounds like sprint planning."

## Decide whether to trip the Circuit Breaker

> "We're at the end of a 6-week cycle and the bet isn't done. We're being asked to extend. Help me think through the Circuit Breaker decision the way Ryan Singer would. What's the default (kill, not extend)? What would justify extension as an exception? What signal is 'uphill work at the deadline' actually giving us? Cite Singer / Chapter 14 explicitly."

## Diagnose a stuck Hill Chart

> "One of our scopes has been stuck near the top of the hill for two weeks. The dot isn't moving. Help me think about what this signals in Shape Up terms — is this uphill work still figuring itself out, is there a conceptual snag, is it a shaping failure surfacing? What should the manager's move be?"

## Adopt Shape Up in a non-Basecamp company

> "I want to bring Shape Up to my team. We're a typical B2B SaaS — separate backend/frontend/designer/QA/PM. Our PMs aren't technical. We have a JIRA backlog of hundreds of tickets. Leadership hasn't committed. Help me think through what's realistic — should we adopt the full method or borrow specific mechanisms? What does Singer's *Shaping in Real Life* material say about adaptations? Reference the 2025 *Common Pitfalls* piece and the *End-to-End* case study."

## Distinguish Shape Up from Scrum

> "My team runs Scrum. Someone read *Shape Up* and now wants us to 'do 6-week sprints.' Help me articulate the actual differences — cycles vs. sprints, appetite vs. estimation, no backlog vs. product backlog, circuit breaker vs. carry-over — so I can have a real conversation about whether we should switch or keep doing Scrum well. Don't blend the two."

## Size an appetite (not estimate)

> "Someone asked 'how long will [FEATURE] take.' In Shape Up terms, they should be asking 'how much time is this problem worth?' Help me set an appetite (small batch: 1–2 weeks; big batch: full 6-week cycle) and then design the shape to fit — not the other way around. If I catch myself estimating, call it out."

## Decide whether an idea is worth shaping at all

> "We have an idea. Before I invest in shaping, help me framing-check it: is this problem worth solving? Whose? Why now? What business value? If the answer isn't clear, tell me framing failed and to go do more discovery — don't let me shape a solution to an unframed problem."

## Draft a Package (2022+ term for Pitch)

> "Take me through drafting a full Package for [PROJECT]. Start with the framed problem (I'll paste it). Then walk me through appetite, solution at fat-marker fidelity, rabbit holes, and no-gos. Ask me clarifying questions when I'm being vague. Don't accept 'it should just be intuitive' as a solution."

## Check whether the work is shaped

> "Here's what my team has been working on for [X weeks]. Is it 'shaped' by Singer's definition — 'we can give this to someone to build and they will know what to do'? If not, what specifically is undershaped?"

## Argue for 'no backlog' internally

> "My team wants to rip out our backlog and adopt Shape Up's 'no backlog' stance. Help me make the argument to leadership. Cite Singer's Ch. 7 'Bets, Not Backlogs.' Address the common objections ('we need somewhere to capture ideas', 'stakeholders expect a roadmap'). And name honestly the political prerequisite — this requires leadership sponsorship, not just a team decision."

## Post-mortem a cycle that didn't ship

> "Our last cycle didn't ship. In Shape Up terms, we tripped the Circuit Breaker. Help me post-mortem: was this a shaping failure (undershaped work, missed rabbit holes)? A scoping failure (too much for the appetite)? A framing failure (wrong problem)? Or a genuine execution / conceptual snag surfaced mid-cycle? Reference Singer's Ch. 14 disposition — 'if the work is uphill, put the troubled project back in the shaping phase.'"

## Walk a case study

> "Walk me through Singer's 2025 gym-management case study end-to-end — how did framing lead to shaping, how did shaping surface a reframe, how did the build team decompose into 9 scopes, why 'wire functionality before high-fidelity design.' I want to see the adaptations for a non-Basecamp team explicitly."

## Compare Shape Up to Working Backwards

> "I'm choosing between Shape Up (Ryan Singer) and Working Backwards (Bill Carr / Colin Bryar / Amazon) for our product process. Both are 'structured pre-work before building' but they disagree on a lot. Help me understand where they differ — perspective of the artifact (customer future vs. builder's shape), cadence (fixed cycles vs. no fixed cadence), decision authority (Betting Table vs. CEO/Bezos-shaped review), backlog stance, progress mechanism. Which fits our situation better?"

## Reject a request that shouldn't be shaped

> "Someone wants me to 'write a pitch' for [MAINTENANCE / BUG / INFRA WORK]. In Shape Up terms, this isn't a feature bet — it doesn't belong in a cycle. Help me articulate why (Singer 2021: 'Shape Up is for features, not all development work') and what the right operating mode is instead (cool-down, or a separate maintenance track)."

## Voice check — writing on Singer's behalf

> "Draft [X] as if Ryan Singer wrote it — careful, systemic, sketch-driven, not polemical. Contrast quietly with Agile ('not estimation, appetite'), teach through the artifact, refuse to over-claim or sell adoption as easy. Don't quote DHH or Fried as if they were Singer."
