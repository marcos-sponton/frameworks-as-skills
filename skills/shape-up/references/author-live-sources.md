## Essay archive on ryansinger.co (chronological, freshest first)

**2026:**
- *What's the right level of detail when shaping?* — https://www.ryansinger.co/whats-the-right-level-of-detail-when-shaping/ — detail depends on document's purpose; approval-seeking = more detail, direction-giving = less.

**2025:**
- *End-To-End with Shape Up: A Real-World Case Study* — https://www.ryansinger.co/end-to-end-with-shape-up-a-real-world-case-study/ — the freshest worked example; gym-management software; adapts the method for a typical (non-Basecamp) team.
- *Common Pitfalls When Adopting Shape Up (and How to Avoid Them)* — https://www.ryansinger.co/pitfalls-when-adopting-shape-up/ — the densest adoption-failure catalog; names three failure modes (undershaped work, blurred framing/shaping, mixed reactive work).
- *When engineers say "that'll take months!"* — https://www.ryansinger.co/when-engineers-say-thatll-take-months/ — legacy systems as the hidden shape-breaker.

**2024:**
- *Not everyone needs to be talking to customers* — https://www.ryansinger.co/not-everyone-needs-to-be-talking-to-customers/ — mild pushback against Torres-style universal customer interviewing.
- *We did all this discovery… now how do we decide?* — https://www.ryansinger.co/discovery-how-to-decide/ — framing as the decision-making tool after discovery.
- *What's the unit of impact?* — https://www.ryansinger.co/whats-a-unit-of-impact/ — defining impact in terms of current business priorities.

**2023:**
- *The cost of not shaping* — https://www.ryansinger.co/the-cost-of-not-shaping/ — the hidden cost of skipping shaping.

**2022:**
- *Framing* — https://www.ryansinger.co/framing/ — **the single most important post-book essay.** Introduces the framing → shaping distinction absent from the 2019 book.

**2021:**
- *Shape Up is for features, not all development work* — https://www.ryansinger.co/shape-up-is-for-features-not-all-development-work/ — one of the earliest post-book clarifications; scope of the method.

## Related — 37signals / Basecamp / DHH / Fried

Not the primary source for the method (Singer is), but occasionally useful for the polemical register on Basecamp's product philosophy:

- **37signals (Basecamp) blog and site:** https://37signals.com
- **David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH) — world.hey.com:** https://world.hey.com/dhh — DHH's personal blog; polemical, occasional Shape Up mentions.
- **Jason Fried — world.hey.com:** https://world.hey.com/jason — Fried's personal blog.
- **Books by Fried + DHH (context for the 37signals worldview):** *Rework* (2010), *Remote* (2013), *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work* (2018) — the "no meetings, no estimates, no crunch" polemic that Shape Up operationalizes.

When quoting the "37signals point of view" — "we don't do sprints", "estimates are lies", "meetings are toxic" — that's DHH or Fried, not Singer. Attribute correctly.
