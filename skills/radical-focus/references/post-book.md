## Refinements and reversals

### From "cascading" to "alignment" — Wodtke's biggest position shift

The 2016 book still spoke about cascading OKRs. By 2025 Wodtke explicitly reversed:

- **Old (2016):** cascade from company → BU → team → individual.
- **New (2025):** *"Cascading creates bottlenecks. Instead, set one high-level OKR at the company or business unit level. Then empower teams to write their own OKRs."*
- **The mechanism:** company (or BU) sets ONE Objective. Teams write their own OKRs showing how they'll contribute. Leadership recommends, doesn't approve.

**Author's words:**
> "The best OKRs don't come from the top — they're co-created."

**Source:** [What I've Learned from 15 Years of Doing OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-ive-learned-from-15-years-of-doing-okrs/) (May 2025)

### From "everyone should do OKRs" to "not every team needs OKRs"

Another explicit reversal from the earlier posture.

- Service teams, support teams, teams without autonomy — OKRs will fail there. Wodtke now says: fix the autonomy first, or leave the team on KPIs + Health Metrics.
- The Elegant Hack version of this ("You Can't Handle OKRs") frames it as: OKRs are a **vitamin, not a medicine**. They preserve organizational health when done regularly; they don't cure a sick org.

**Author's words:**
> "OKRs are a vitamin, not a medicine." — paraphrase from Melissa Perri's *Product Thinking* podcast (2023)

**Source:** [You Can't Handle OKRs](https://cwodtke.medium.com/you-cant-handle-okrs-5465cf161e81)

### From scoring to learning

- **Old:** score each KR 0.0–1.0. 0.7 = success (Google's convention).
- **New (2025):** score, but don't get stuck on it. *"Instead of getting stuck on a 0.7 or 0.8 score, we focus on what worked, what didn't, and what we'll do differently."*
- The 5/10 confidence rule at the start of the quarter still holds. The score at the end is context; the retro is the point.

**Source:** [What I've Learned from 15 Years of Doing OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-ive-learned-from-15-years-of-doing-okrs/)

## New material — added after the book

### Health Metrics as anti-gaming guard — the "OpenAI code red" case

The single clearest post-book device. Formalized in 2025 using OpenAI as the exemplar.

**Mechanism:**
- OKRs are **pushed**. Health Metrics are **protected**.
- Health Metrics: revenue, customer satisfaction, code stability, team wellbeing, model reliability.
- Track green / yellow / red.
- Red = **code red** = pause OKR work, fix the health metric first.

**OpenAI case (2025):**
- OKRs at stake: advertising, shopping agents, health assistant, Pulse feature.
- Health Metric that turned red: model capability + reliability.
- Sam Altman's move: paused the OKR work, redirected to protect the Health Metric.
- Wodtke's read: *"That's Radical Focus in the wild. Knowing the difference between what to push and what to protect might save your company."*

**Source:** [What Sam Altman Just Taught Us About OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-sam-altman-just-taught-us-about-okrs-without-meaning-to/) (Dec 2025)

### Decoupling OKRs from approval workflows

Killed in 2024. This is one of the sharpest recent Wodtke positions.

**The kill:**
- Requiring leadership approval to set team OKRs is a trust issue in disguise.
- *"If you've hired great people and given them a clear strategy, you shouldn't need to approve every little thing."*
- Alignment reviews become bureaucratic gatekeeping; teams stall waiting for sign-off.

**The alternative:**
- Teams set their own OKRs.
- *"You can still get another set of eyes on your OKRs — but now it's recommended, not required."*
- Leadership's role shifts to strategy clarity, not OKR approval.

**Author's words:**
> "Waiting for approvals isn't just frustrating — it's a sign of something deeper. It's a trust issue."

**Source:** [Decoupling OKRs: It's Time to Let Go](https://eleganthack.com/decoupling-okrs-its-time-to-let-go/) (Nov 2024)

### KPIs vs OKRs — the dashboard / GPS distinction

The clearest single metaphor Wodtke uses to explain what OKRs are NOT:

- **KPIs = dashboard.** Steady-state, maintain-what-you-do metrics. *"They tell you if the engine's overheating, if you have enough fuel, if you're maintaining speed."*
- **OKRs = GPS to somewhere new.** *"You're driving somewhere you've never been."*
- **Health Metrics = the third category.** What you're protecting while pushing.

**Test question for a candidate OKR:** *"What change do you want to see this quarter?"* — not what to maintain perpetually. If the answer is "maintain X," it's a KPI or Health Metric, not an OKR.

**Author's words:**
> "OKRs aren't about maintaining anything. They're about transformation."
> "Being a high-performing team isn't a destination you arrive at. It's baseline."

**Source:** [KPIs Are Your Dashboard. OKRs Are Your GPS to Somewhere New](https://eleganthack.com/kpis-are-your-dashboard-okrs-are-your-gps-to-somewhere-new/) (Nov 2025)

### The Team Health Monitor — from *The Team That Managed Itself*

Not in *Radical Focus* itself; introduced in the 2019 sister book with Martin Eriksson. Works alongside Health Metrics.

- **Team Health Monitor** = diagnostic on the team itself (burnout, trust, clarity, autonomy).
- **Health Metrics** = diagnostic on the business (revenue, satisfaction, stability).
- Both run alongside OKR confidence. A team hitting OKRs while degrading either health signal is a leading indicator of collapse next quarter.

**Source:** *The Team That Managed Itself* (2019). Book summaries: [MAA1's review on Medium](https://maa1.medium.com/the-team-that-managed-itself-book-review-7d69b90090f8).

### Outcome thinking as prerequisite

Wodtke has published a specific ramp-up essay for teams stuck writing output OKRs.

- **Outcomes** = changes in the world (retention, revenue, satisfaction, behavior).
- **Outputs** = things you shipped (features, docs, campaigns).
- The KR must be an outcome. If it can be checked off a Jira board, it's an output.

**Source:** [Getting Started with Outcome Thinking](https://eleganthack.com/getting-started-with-outcome-thinking/) (Oct 2024)

### "Business as Usual" as vital work — 2026 refinement

The most recent essay from Wodtke on the balance between OKRs and everything else.

- Teams do OKR work + Business-As-Usual (BAU).
- Naming BAU as "not the OKR" implicitly demeans it, but BAU is often what keeps the business alive.
- Fix: name and protect BAU explicitly. Don't let the Objective become the only work that "counts."

**Source:** [Business as Usual Is a Terrible Name for Vital Work](https://eleganthack.com/business-as-usual-is-a-terrible-name-for-vital-work/) (Apr 2026)

### "In Praise of Guessing" — 2026 refinement

Recent nod to the fact that OKR-setting is not data-perfect.

- You will not know for certain what the right KR target is. Guess.
- The point isn't the guess; it's the practice of committing, testing, learning.
- Related to her framing of OKRs as **practice**, not planning.

**Source:** [In Praise of Guessing](https://eleganthack.com/in-praise-of-guessing/) (Jan 2026)

## Adjacent frameworks Wodtke has commented on

Compatibility notes from various essays and podcasts:

- **John Doerr — *Measure What Matters*** — respectful but distinct. Doerr's book is aspirational and cheerleading; Wodtke's is a manual. She rejects the individual-OKR examples that appear in Doerr.
- **Andy Grove — original Intel MBOs** — traces lineage. Grove's OKRs were team-level; the individual-OKR pathology came later.
- **Melissa Perri — *Escaping the Build Trap*** — highly compatible. Both anti-individual, both outcome-over-output. Perri interviewed her on *Product Thinking* (Jan 2023).
- **Roger Martin — *Playing to Win*** — Martin says OKRs can be an Enabling Management System for a strategy, not the strategy itself. Wodtke agrees: OKRs are the bridge from strategy to team work; they don't SET strategy.
- **Rumelt — *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*** — compatible. Rumelt's guiding policy sits upstream; OKRs help teams commit to coherent actions inside it.
- **EOS (Wickman) — *Traction*** — overlapping cadence rituals (L10 meeting ≈ Monday Commitments). Wodtke's method is lighter-weight; EOS is a heavier operating system covering hiring, roles, etc.
- **Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)** — adjacent; measures execution across dimensions. Wodtke uses Health Metrics as a lighter analog.

## Frameworks Wodtke has explicitly killed

- **Individual OKRs** — collapse into performance reviews.
- **Cascading** — creates bottlenecks; killed in favor of alignment.
- **Approval-based OKR workflows** — trust issue in disguise.
- **Quarterly-only OKRs (no cadence)** — a goal fantasy.
- **OKRs as KPIs** — conflation of push vs. protect.
- **Output KRs disguised as outcomes** — measure what changed, not what shipped.
