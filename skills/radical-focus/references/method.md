# Radical Focus — Method

> The canonical description of Wodtke's OKR method in her own terms. Fidelity matters — Wodtke's method is opinionated, and softening it collapses back into generic Doerr-flavored OKR advice.

## What Radical Focus is

Wodtke's operating definition, distilled across the book and 15 years of teaching:

> **OKRs are a practice — a habit of reflection, alignment, and growth.** They translate strategy into focused team work, run on a weekly cadence, and are protected by Health Metrics.
> — Christina Wodtke, *What I've Learned from 15 Years of Doing OKRs* (2025)

Two things in that definition are load-bearing:

- **"Practice."** OKRs aren't a document you fill out once a quarter. They live in the weekly rhythm or they die.
- **"Health Metrics."** The gaming problem is real. Any single-number KR can be optimized against the spirit of the thing. Health Metrics are the guard.

## The structure — one Objective, ~3 Key Results, per team, per quarter

Wodtke insists on parsimony. This is the "radical" in Radical Focus.

### The Objective

**Three qualities of a good Objective** (from [The Art of the OKR](https://eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr/)):

1. **Qualitative and inspirational.** *"The objective is designed to get people jumping out of bed in the morning with excitement."* Use the team's own language — if they say "pwn it" or "kill it," write "pwn it" or "kill it."
2. **Time-bound.** Achievable in the quarter. Not a strategic vision, not a north star.
3. **Actionable independently by the team.** If success depends on another team doing something, the boundary is wrong.

**Good Objectives Wodtke uses as examples:**
- *"Pwn the direct-to-business coffee retail market in the south bay."*
- *"Launch an Awesome MVP."*
- *"Transform Palo Alto's coupon-using habits."*

**Bad Objectives (these are actually KRs, or KPIs):**
- "Sales up 30%."
- "Double users."
- "Be a high-performing team." — *"That's baseline, not a destination."* (From [KPIs Are Your Dashboard](https://eleganthack.com/kpis-are-your-dashboard-okrs-are-your-gps-to-somewhere-new/), 2025)

### The Key Results

**How you know you met the Objective.** Quantitative, hard-edged, ~3 per Objective, chosen to **balance** competing dimensions so no single one can be gamed.

**Example — for "Launch an Awesome MVP":**
- 40% of users return 2× within one week
- Recommendation score of 8
- 15% conversion

Notice the balance: retention + satisfaction + conversion. Optimize for any one at the expense of another and the trade shows up in the numbers.

**The KR must be an outcome, not an output.** *"Shipped feature X"* is not a Key Result — it's a task on a roadmap. *"Users who used feature X returned 40% more"* is a Key Result. This distinction is where most first-time OKR implementations fail.

### The 5/10 confidence rule (Wodtke's version of the "70% rule")

Set KRs at the start of the quarter with a confidence of **5 out of 10** — a real 50/50 shot. Wodtke's tell:

> "If you are getting a funny little feeling in the pit of your stomach saying 'It would take a miracle to hit all three of these' — then you are probably setting them correctly."

Land at ~70% by end of quarter = right stretch. Land at 100% = too easy. Land at 20% = too hard or the KR was wrong.

**Author's words on the calibration:**
> "OKRs are like the advice 'Eat less and exercise more.' They are simple and hard."

## The Health Metric — the anti-gaming guard

This is Wodtke's most under-appreciated device and her clearest post-book contribution. Extended in [What Sam Altman Just Taught Us About OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-sam-altman-just-taught-us-about-okrs-without-meaning-to/) (Dec 2025).

**OKRs are what you *push*. Health Metrics are what you *protect*.**

- Health Metrics track **survival indicators**: revenue, customer satisfaction, code stability, team wellbeing, model reliability (in OpenAI's 2025 case).
- Status is **green / yellow / red**.
- Pick **~2 Health Metrics** per team, alongside the Objective.
- When a Health Metric goes **red → code red.** Pause OKR work. Fix the health metric first. This is the anti-gaming guard: you can't win the OKR by cratering the health metric.

**Author's words:**
> "OKRs are stretch goals. But not everything in your business needs to be pushed. Some things need to be protected. Knowing the difference might save your company."

**Canonical case:** OpenAI 2025. Sam Altman paused advertising, shopping agents, and health assistant OKRs when model capability + reliability (their health metric) turned red. That's the mechanic exactly.

## The cadence — Monday Commitments, Friday Celebrations

The operational engine. **This is where OKRs live or die.** From *The Team That Managed Itself* and every Wodtke essay on the topic since.

### Monday Commitments — the 4-quadrant meeting

Weekly team meeting (~30-60 min). Four quadrants on one page:

1. **Intention for the Week** — the 3-4 most important things this team will get done that move us toward the Objective. Concrete, doable, checkable next Monday.
2. **Forecast for the Month** — what's coming up in the next 3-4 weeks so teammates can help or unblock in advance.
3. **Status toward OKRs** — for each KR, confidence level (1-10). Discuss what changed since last week. Trending up? Down? Why?
4. **Health Metrics** — green / yellow / red status on each protected metric. Red triggers a conversation about pausing OKR work.

**The point of Monday:** hard conversations, honest confidence assessments, unblock ahead of time. Not a status theater.

### Friday Celebrations / Wins — the bragging session

Weekly team meeting (~30 min). Different tone.

- Demo what you can. Show, don't tell.
- Share wins — customer emails, small victories, cross-team assists.
- Learn what other teams are doing.
- **No hard conversations.** Wodtke: *"The Friday meeting is for fun and celebration. Tough discussions belong Monday."*

**Why the split matters:** without Friday, work becomes joyless. Without Monday, work becomes vague.

### Monthly retrospective + quarterly grade

- **Monthly retro:** what worked, what didn't, what to adjust. Not the OKRs themselves — the *practice* around them.
- **Quarterly grade:** score each KR 0.0–1.0. But (2025 refinement) **focus on learning, not the score.** *"Instead of getting stuck on a 0.7 or 0.8, we focus on what worked, what didn't, and what we'll do differently."*

## Team OKRs — not individual OKRs

Wodtke has been explicit on this for over a decade.

- **Team OKRs create shared accountability.** Everyone succeeds together or falls short together. That's the point.
- **Individual OKRs collapse into performance reviews.** People sandbag. HR grabs them. The measurement dominates the meaning.
- **Cascading is worse.** *"Cascading is you take your boss's Key Result and make it your Objective. I really hate this one."* — [You Can't Handle OKRs](https://cwodtke.medium.com/you-cant-handle-okrs-5465cf161e81)

**Author's 2025 refinement** — from [What I've Learned from 15 Years](https://eleganthack.com/what-ive-learned-from-15-years-of-doing-okrs/):

> "Cascading creates bottlenecks. Instead, set one high-level OKR at the company or business unit level. Then empower teams to write their own OKRs. The best OKRs don't come from the top — they're co-created."

The model: **alignment, not cascading.** Company/BU sets one OKR. Teams write their own OKRs that show how they'll contribute. Leadership recommends, doesn't approve.

## One Objective per team per quarter — the *radical* part

From [One Objective to Rule Them All](https://cwodtke.medium.com/one-objective-to-rule-them-all-1058e973bfc5):

- 5 company OKRs × 4 KRs = 20 data points. Add 3 team OKRs × 4 KRs = 32 things to remember. *"Every single person in the company knows what the company OKRs are in their heart. Which means they have to remember them."*
- **Signature move:** *"Do less, better."*
- **Whiskey funnel:** *"You don't pour whiskey through a leaky funnel into your flask and hope to get drunk."* Retention before growth. Sequence, don't parallel.
- **Frodo/Samwise:** *"We can't all be Frodo; some of us are Samwise."* Support roles matter; not every team leads every Objective.

The Objective is what you *lead* on this quarter. Everything else you do is business-as-usual + support for the Objective.

## OKRs vs KPIs — dashboard vs GPS

From [KPIs Are Your Dashboard. OKRs Are Your GPS to Somewhere New](https://eleganthack.com/kpis-are-your-dashboard-okrs-are-your-gps-to-somewhere-new/) (Nov 2025):

- **KPIs = dashboard.** Steady-state metrics of the thing you already do. Answer: *"How are we doing at the thing we do?"*
- **OKRs = GPS.** Transformation vector. Answer: *"We are here. We need to be there."*
- Test for a candidate OKR: **"What change do you want to see this quarter?"** If the answer is "we want to maintain X," it's a KPI (or a Health Metric). Not an OKR.

## Sequence of application

Wodtke's practical order:

1. **Confirm prerequisites.** Psychological safety, multidisciplinary teams, autonomy. Without these, don't do OKRs — fix these first.
2. **Set the company (or BU) Objective.** One. Qualitative. Inspirational.
3. **Teams write their own OKRs** in service of the company Objective. Alignment, not cascading.
4. **Pick 2 Health Metrics per team.** What are you protecting while pushing?
5. **Set the cadence** — put Monday Commitments and Friday Celebrations on the calendar for the whole quarter. Non-negotiable.
6. **Run for the quarter.** Confidence check every Monday. Wins every Friday. Monthly retro on the *practice*, not the OKRs.
7. **Grade at quarter end.** Score, but focus on learning. Write the next quarter's OKRs.

## What this method is NOT

Wodtke is explicit about the negative definition. This method is NOT:

- **A quarterly document.** OKRs without cadence = a fantasy.
- **A performance review tool.** Team-level, not individual. Never HR's toy.
- **A task list.** KRs must be outcomes, not outputs.
- **A KPI dashboard.** OKRs are transformation; KPIs are maintenance. Both matter — not interchangeably.
- **Universal.** Not every team needs OKRs. Service teams, dependent teams, teams without autonomy — do the fixing first, not the OKR-writing.
- **Strategy.** OKRs bridge strategy to team work. They don't replace the choice about what to compete on. If the strategy is broken, better OKRs won't fix it.

**Author's words:**
> "If you've read about OKRs and the first thing you think is 'we can't do that because we're remote / no one has control over their product / everyone is dependent on other teams / stakeholders need roadmaps' — STOP and work on those issues first."
