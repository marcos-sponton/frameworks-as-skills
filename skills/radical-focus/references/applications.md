# Radical Focus — Applications

> When Wodtke's method fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where Radical Focus fits well

- **Setting a team's OKRs for the first time.** The book + this skill are almost tailor-made for this — Objective structure, KR calibration, Health Metrics, cadence.
- **Diagnosing why existing OKRs aren't moving.** 9 out of 10 times the problem is missing weekly cadence. Wodtke's diagnostic pattern is fast.
- **Rewriting output-based KRs into outcome-based ones.** The "ship X" → "the change X was supposed to produce" translation is a Wodtke specialty.
- **Deciding whether to have OKRs at all.** Wodtke has explicit prerequisites (autonomy, psychological safety, multidisciplinary team). If they're missing, don't do OKRs — fix them.
- **Adding a Health Metric to guard against gaming.** The 2025 OpenAI "code red" case makes the mechanic vivid.
- **Reconciling KPIs, OKRs, and Health Metrics.** The dashboard / GPS / protect metaphor cleans up the usual mess.
- **Running quarterly planning that has to bridge to weekly team work.** The Monday commitments + Friday celebrations cadence is the bridge.
- **Coaching a manager who was told to "just do OKRs" and has nothing else.** Give them the operational method — the meeting cadence, the 4-quadrant page, the Health Metric guard.
- **Moving from individual OKRs (broken) or cascading (broken) to alignment (works).** Wodtke has explicit models for the transition.
- **Grading OKRs at end of quarter with a learning lens instead of a scoring lens.** Her 2025 refinement.

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **Strategy work — deciding what to compete on, where to play, how to win.** OKRs *translate* strategy; they don't create it. Wodtke is explicit: OKRs are the bridge, not the map. Redirect to [`playing-to-win/`](../../playing-to-win/) or [`good-strategy-bad-strategy/`](../../good-strategy-bad-strategy/).

- **Solo work with no team.** Wodtke's method is team-first. She has written about personal OKRs but they're a smaller / lighter version, and most of the ritual (Monday four-quadrant, Friday celebrations across teams) doesn't apply. Point users at simpler personal planning tools.

- **Pre-team, pre-product, pre-anything.** Wodtke: *"If you can't do OKRs because you're remote / no one has control over their product / everyone is dependent on other teams — STOP and work on those issues first."*

- **Performance management.** OKRs are team-level and about business outcomes, not individual assessment. If HR wants OKRs for performance reviews, that's the individual-OKR pathology in a suit — push back explicitly.

- **Roadmap prioritization across many potential features.** OKRs commit to one Objective per team per quarter; they don't rank a portfolio of feature bets. Use ODI (Ulwick), JTBD prioritization, or RICE for that.

- **Steady-state operations where you're not trying to transform anything.** OKRs are for change (GPS to somewhere new). Steady-state = KPIs + Health Metrics, not OKRs. Wodtke explicit on this.

- **When the user just wants a summary of the book.** Give them the [Amazon link](https://www.amazon.com/Radical-Focus-Achieving-Important-Objectives/dp/0996006028). Don't run the method at them.

## Adjacent frameworks — when to reach for a different one

| If the user's situation is... | Reach for... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Setting the strategy (not translating it) | [Playing to Win (Martin & Lafley)](../../playing-to-win/) or [Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Rumelt)](../../good-strategy-bad-strategy/) | OKRs are downstream of strategy; don't confuse them |
| Portfolio prioritization across many bets | ODI (Ulwick), JTBD prioritization, RICE | Cascade doesn't rank; it commits |
| Product discovery — finding the right problem | JTBD (Moesta, Kalbach, Ulwick), Continuous Discovery (Torres) | OKRs need a problem defined first |
| Positioning the product for a market | April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* | Positioning is downstream of HTW; adjacent to KRs |
| Building the strategic narrative | Andy Raskin's *Strategic Narrative* | Narrative is downstream of strategy + OKRs |
| Full operating system for a business (hiring, meetings, roles, etc.) | EOS Traction (Wickman) | EOS is heavier; overlaps on cadence rituals |
| Measuring execution across dimensions | Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton) | Sits alongside Health Metrics; broader instrumentation |
| Individual performance / development | 1:1s + coaching frameworks (Scott's Radical Candor, etc.) | Individual growth ≠ team OKRs |
| Change management once OKRs are set | Kotter, ADKAR | OKRs tell you *what* to change; not *how* to change the org |
| Retrospectives on team practices | Agile retros, [Team That Managed Itself](https://www.amazon.com/Team-that-Managed-Itself-Leadership/dp/0996006060) | Monthly OKR retro is on the *practice*, not the OKRs |

## How Radical Focus composes with other frameworks

- **Pairs well with [Playing to Win](../../playing-to-win/) (Roger Martin).** Martin explicitly says OKRs can be an **Enabling Management System** for a strategy — they translate the 5-question cascade (winning aspiration + WTP + HTW + capabilities + management systems) into what each team commits to this quarter. Sequence: strategy first (Martin), then OKRs (Wodtke) to execute.

- **Pairs well with [Good Strategy Bad Strategy](../../good-strategy-bad-strategy/) (Rumelt).** Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions) sits upstream; the "coherent actions" show up as OKRs at the team level.

- **Pairs well with Melissa Perri's *Escaping the Build Trap*.** Both anti-individual-OKR, both outcome-over-output, both team-first. Perri's product operating model is highly compatible with Wodtke's OKR method. Perri interviewed Wodtke on *Product Thinking* (Jan 2023).

- **Pairs well with Continuous Discovery (Teresa Torres).** Torres's opportunity-solution tree gives you the *why* behind an OKR; Wodtke's method commits to it and runs the cadence.

- **Compatible with JTBD (Moesta, Kalbach).** JTBD helps you understand the customer job; OKRs commit the team to moving specific outcome numbers related to that job.

- **Adjacent to EOS Traction.** EOS's L10 meeting resembles Monday Commitments. Wodtke's method is lighter-weight — pick EOS if you want a full operating system, Radical Focus if you want the OKR practice.

- **Balanced Scorecard as Health-Metric-heavy Radical Focus.** If your org already runs Balanced Scorecard, the Health Metrics layer maps to Scorecard's "protect" dimensions. Wodtke's OKR layer is the "push" dimension.

## Frameworks Wodtke has explicitly killed (see `heuristics.md` for details)

- **Individual OKRs** — collapse into performance reviews.
- **Cascading OKRs** — killed in favor of alignment.
- **Approval-based OKR workflows** — trust issue in disguise.
- **Quarterly-only OKRs with no cadence** — a goal fantasy.
- **OKRs as KPIs** — conflation of push vs. protect.
- **Output KRs disguised as outcomes** — measure what changed, not what shipped.
- **Universal simultaneous rollout** — pilot one team, learn, expand.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work, redirect gently but clearly, with the Wodtke-specific reason.
