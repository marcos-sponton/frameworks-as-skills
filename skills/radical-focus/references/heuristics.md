# Radical Focus — Heuristics, Do's, Don'ts, Gotchas

> The practical devices that separate applying Radical Focus well from doing generic OKR theater. Attribution is precise — this comes from the book, this from a 2024 Elegant Hack essay, this from her 2023 podcast with Lenny.

## Do's

### Write ONE Objective per team per quarter

Wodtke's most repeated principle. The math is the argument: 5 company OKRs × 4 KRs + 3 team OKRs × 4 KRs = 32 things a person has to remember and prioritize. That defeats the purpose.

**How to apply:** if the team wants 3 Objectives, force them to pick the one that would matter most if the other two didn't happen. That's the Objective. The rest is business-as-usual.

**Author's words:**
> "Do less, better."
> — *One Objective to Rule Them All*, Wodtke on Medium

**Source:** [One Objective to Rule Them All](https://cwodtke.medium.com/one-objective-to-rule-them-all-1058e973bfc5)

### Set KRs at confidence 5/10

The stretch test. If the team feels comfortable at the start of the quarter, the KRs are too easy. If they feel doomed, too hard.

**How to apply:** ask each teammate to rate confidence 1-10 privately, then discuss. If average is >7, tighten. If <3, back off.

**Author's words:**
> "If you are getting a funny little feeling in the pit of your stomach saying 'It would take a miracle to hit all three of these' — then you are probably setting them correctly."

**Source:** [The Art of the OKR](https://eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr/)

### Pick 2 Health Metrics — the things you protect

The anti-gaming guard. OKRs push; Health Metrics protect. Together they cover push AND protect.

**How to apply:** ask the team *"what would we destroy if we optimized purely for the Objective?"* Those are your Health Metrics. Track green / yellow / red. Red = code red = pause OKR work.

**Author's words:**
> "OKRs are stretch goals. But not everything in your business needs to be pushed. Some things need to be protected. Knowing the difference might save your company."

**Source:** [What Sam Altman Just Taught Us About OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-sam-altman-just-taught-us-about-okrs-without-meaning-to/)

### Run Monday Commitments every week — the 4-quadrant page

The single operational discipline that separates goal *achievers* from goal *setters*. One page, four quadrants: Intention for the Week, Forecast for the Month, Status toward OKRs (confidence), Health Metrics.

**How to apply:** put it on the calendar for the whole quarter. Same time every Monday. 30-60 min. Missing Monday is the failure mode.

### Run Friday Celebrations — separately

Different tone. Demos, wins, cross-team bragging. **No hard conversations** — those belong Monday.

**How to apply:** if a hard topic surfaces Friday, park it on Monday's agenda. Preserve the Friday vibe.

**Author's words:**
> "The Friday meeting is for fun and celebration. Tough discussions belong Monday."

### Write outcomes, not outputs, as Key Results

*"Shipped feature X"* is an output. *"Users who used feature X returned 40% more"* is an outcome. This is the single most common first-time mistake.

**How to apply:** for every candidate KR, ask *"if we hit this number, did the world actually change?"* If the answer is "we shipped a thing," rewrite as the change the thing was supposed to produce.

### Ground the Objective in the team's own language

If they say "pwn it" or "kill it," use it. If they say "delight," use "delight." The Objective is the pull; it has to feel like the team's, not the CEO's.

### Move from cascading to alignment

Wodtke's biggest post-book position shift.

**How to apply:** set ONE company (or BU) OKR. Then let teams write their own OKRs that show *how* they'll contribute. Leadership recommends, doesn't approve.

**Author's words:**
> "Cascading creates bottlenecks. The best OKRs don't come from the top — they're co-created."

**Source:** [What I've Learned from 15 Years of Doing OKRs](https://eleganthack.com/what-ive-learned-from-15-years-of-doing-okrs/)

### Grade quarterly, but focus on learning

Score the KRs 0.0–1.0. But don't get stuck on the number.

**How to apply:** for each KR, run three questions: *what worked, what didn't, what will we do differently?* The score is context; the learning is the point.

## Don'ts

### Don't do individual OKRs

The single most vocal deletion. Individual OKRs collapse into performance-review theater, kill team accountability, and turn OKRs into an HR instrument.

**Author's words:**
> "The best OKRs don't come from the top."

Also on cascading (the near-cousin):
> "Cascading is you take your boss's Key Result and make it your Objective. I really hate this one."

**Source:** [You Can't Handle OKRs](https://cwodtke.medium.com/you-cant-handle-okrs-5465cf161e81)

### Don't do quarterly-only OKRs (no weekly cadence)

Setting OKRs quarterly and not talking about them again = a goal fantasy. The cadence IS the method.

**How to redirect:** if the user says "we set OKRs but they don't move," diagnose the cadence first. 9 times out of 10 that's the problem. The OKR wording is fine.

### Don't confuse OKRs with KPIs

KPIs = dashboard (maintain the thing you do). OKRs = GPS (transform to somewhere new). Not interchangeable.

**Test:** *"What change do you want to see this quarter?"* If the answer is "maintain X," it's a KPI or a Health Metric — not an OKR.

**Author's words:**
> "OKRs aren't about maintaining anything. They're about transformation."

**Source:** [KPIs Are Your Dashboard. OKRs Are Your GPS to Somewhere New](https://eleganthack.com/kpis-are-your-dashboard-okrs-are-your-gps-to-somewhere-new/)

### Don't write Objectives that are baseline states

*"Be a high-performing team"* is not an Objective — it's baseline. Same for *"be more agile,"* *"be more innovative,"* *"delight customers."*

**Author's words:**
> "Being a high-performing team isn't a destination you arrive at. It's baseline."

### Don't write output KRs disguised as outcomes

*"Ship feature X."* *"Complete the migration."* *"Publish 10 blog posts."* These are tasks. They belong on a roadmap or a project plan, not a KR.

**How to redirect:** ask *"what does the ship / complete / publish actually produce for the user or business? Measure that."*

### Don't roll out OKRs company-wide simultaneously

Big-bang rollouts break trust. Pilot with one team, learn, then expand.

### Don't do OKRs when prerequisites are missing

If your team is remote-only-and-siloed, has no authority over the product, is dependent on other teams for every decision, or has no psychological safety — **fix that first.** OKRs won't paper over these.

**Author's words:**
> "If you've read about OKRs and the first thing you think is 'we can't do that because we're remote / no one has control over their product / everyone is dependent on other teams / stakeholders need roadmaps' — STOP and work on those issues first."

**Source:** [You Can't Handle OKRs](https://cwodtke.medium.com/you-cant-handle-okrs-5465cf161e81)

### Don't require approval to set team OKRs

Wodtke killed this in 2024. Trust the teams; recommend, don't approve.

**Author's words:**
> "Waiting for approvals isn't just frustrating — it's a sign of something deeper. It's a trust issue."

**Source:** [Decoupling OKRs: It's Time to Let Go](https://eleganthack.com/decoupling-okrs-its-time-to-let-go/)

### Don't add "individual development OKRs" alongside team OKRs

That's the individual-OKR pathology sneaking in via a friendlier name. Development goals belong in 1:1s and coaching plans, not OKRs.

### Don't treat OKRs as strategy

OKRs *translate* strategy into team work. They don't set the strategic direction. If your OKRs feel arbitrary, the missing piece is upstream — reach for [Playing to Win](../../playing-to-win/) or [Good Strategy Bad Strategy](../../good-strategy-bad-strategy/).

## Gotchas (things that go wrong even when you think you're doing it right)

### Goal-of-the-week whiplash

Multiple OKR sets per team, changing every couple of weeks. Feels like agility; is actually indecision. Fix: force one Objective per quarter and hold the line.

### The Objective that's really a KPI

*"Maintain 99.9% uptime."* Sounds like an Objective. Isn't. That's a Health Metric — protect it, don't push it.

### The KR that's a project

*"Implement the new billing system."* Not a KR. The KR is what changes because you implemented the billing system — reduced billing errors, faster invoicing, higher revenue capture.

### Confidence theater

Everyone reports "8/10 confidence" every Monday. Signal: nobody's being honest. Fix: ask *"what would have to change for you to drop to 4/10?"* to surface real risks.

### Grading as HR event

The end-of-quarter score becomes a report card that lands in performance reviews. This is how individual OKRs sneak back in the back door. Keep grading in the team's hands and about learning.

### Weekly-status-report meeting masquerading as Monday Commitments

If Monday is people reading off task lists, you don't have a Monday Commitments meeting — you have a status update. Fix: force the 4-quadrant format, put OKR confidence and Health Metric colors on the wall.

### The "we do OKRs" that's actually "we have OKR software"

Filling in Perdoo / Ally / Betterworks weekly is not the practice. The practice is the conversation. Software helps track; it doesn't replace the meeting.

## Pro tips (accelerators)

### Start with one team, not the company

Pick a team with autonomy and a clear objective. Run one quarter. Learn. Then expand. Big-bang rollouts fail.

### Write the Objective, then walk away for a day

If it still gets you jumping out of bed 24 hours later, keep it. If not, rewrite. Inspiration isn't decoration — it's what makes the OKR pull.

### Draft KRs in pairs

Each KR should have a "yes, but" counterweight. *"40% of users return 2× — yes, but the recommendation score must stay ≥8."* The pair prevents gaming.

### Use the Team Health Monitor alongside OKR confidence

From *The Team That Managed Itself*. Beyond the Health Metrics (business protection), track team health (burnout, trust, clarity). Teams that hit OKRs but rate low on team health are a leading indicator of collapse next quarter.

### Give the team veto over their own Objective

If leadership hands down an Objective the team doesn't believe in, cadence won't save it. Wodtke's model: leadership sets the *company* Objective and constraints; teams write their own OKRs to serve it.

### Retire an OKR mid-quarter — if WWHTBT proves wrong

If the world changed and the OKR no longer serves the Objective, kill it. But don't kill it because *"things aren't working yet"* — that's impatience. Kill it because the assumption was wrong.

## Anti-patterns (the "bad OKRs" Wodtke explicitly names)

### Individual OKRs

**What it looks like:** every person on the team has their own OKRs, often tied to their performance review.
**Why it fails:** people sandbag, cross-team collaboration dies, the OKR becomes an HR instrument.
**How to redirect:** team OKRs, always. Individual development goes in 1:1s.

### Cascading OKRs

**What it looks like:** the CEO's KRs become the VP's Objectives; the VP's KRs become the director's Objectives; and so on.
**Why it fails:** creates approval bottlenecks, kills team ownership, collapses into org-chart theater.
**How to redirect:** alignment model — one company Objective, teams write their own.

**Author's words:**
> "Cascading is you take your boss's Key Result and make it your Objective. I really hate this one."

### OKRs as KPIs (or KPIs as OKRs)

**What it looks like:** "OKR: keep NPS above 50." That's a KPI or a Health Metric — it's a maintenance goal, not a transformation.
**Why it fails:** conflates push with protect. Nothing actually changes.
**How to redirect:** KPI/Health Metric dashboard alongside OKRs.

### Quarterly-only OKRs with no cadence

**What it looks like:** OKRs set at quarterly kickoff, filed, forgotten, dug up at end of quarter for grading.
**Why it fails:** without weekly ritual, nothing moves. The score at end of quarter is a fossil, not a signal.
**How to redirect:** put Monday Commitments and Friday Celebrations on the calendar first, then set the OKRs.

### Output OKRs disguised as outcomes

**What it looks like:** *"Ship the redesign. Launch feature X. Publish 10 blog posts."*
**Why it fails:** you can hit all three and change nothing about the business. Outputs are means; outcomes are ends.
**How to redirect:** for every candidate KR, ask *"if we hit this, did the world change?"* If no, rewrite as the change.

### 5+ Objectives per team

**What it looks like:** the team is "focused on" churn, growth, quality, revenue, and platform migration.
**Why it fails:** with 5 priorities, they have zero. Focus is elimination, not accumulation.
**How to redirect:** force to one. The rest is business-as-usual + support.

### Rolling OKRs company-wide simultaneously

**What it looks like:** CEO announces "we're doing OKRs starting next quarter, everyone."
**Why it fails:** without pilots and learning, the anti-patterns propagate before anyone knows how to spot them.
**How to redirect:** pilot with one team, learn, then expand.

## Language and vocabulary — say this, not that

Small phrasing shifts that Wodtke has made explicit across her essays:

| Instead of | Use | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Individual OKRs | Team OKRs | Individual OKRs collapse into performance reviews |
| Cascading | Alignment | Cascading creates bottlenecks and kills ownership |
| Approving OKRs | Recommending on OKRs | Approval is a trust issue in disguise |
| Quarterly planning | Quarterly reset + weekly cadence | Planning without cadence is a fantasy |
| KPIs and OKRs | KPIs (dashboard) + OKRs (GPS) + Health Metrics (protect) | Different tools for different jobs |
| Ship feature X (as KR) | Users who use feature X return N% more (as KR) | Outcomes, not outputs |
| Status update (Monday) | Commitments meeting (Monday) | The point is intent + confidence, not reporting |
| Be more innovative (as Objective) | Ship the first live-conversation product for X segment | Baseline states aren't Objectives |
| Rollout OKRs company-wide | Pilot with one team, learn, expand | Big-bang fails |
| Perfect score = success | Learn from the score | 0.7 is stretch working; 1.0 is stretch broken |
