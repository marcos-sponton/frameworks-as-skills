# The Lean Startup — Material after the 2011 book

> **This is one of two differentials of this skill** (the other is `heuristics.md`). *The Lean Startup* (2011) laid down the 5 principles, Build-Measure-Learn, MVP, and Innovation Accounting. Since then, Eric Ries has published:
> - **A companion book:** *The Leader's Guide* (Kickstarter-only, 2016) — the executive-facing supplement.
> - **A second commercial book:** *The Startup Way* (Currency, 2017) — the enterprise extension. GE FastWorks, Growth Board governance, entrepreneur-as-org-chart-role.
> - **A third commercial book:** *Incorruptible* (Authors Equity, May 2026 — NYT bestseller) — the governance frame around long-term mission-controlled companies. Financial gravity. Spiritual holding company. Governance-as-product-design.
> - **An active podcast since 2024** — *The Eric Ries Show* — long-form guest interviews on growth with purpose.
> - **An active newsletter since ~2024** — news.theleanstartup.com — cross-posts key material.
> - **A 15-year retrospective on the movement** — the June 2024 Lenny's Podcast episode "Reflections on a movement."
> - **LTSE — Long-Term Stock Exchange** — the operator project that proves the Incorruptible thesis. SEC-approved 2019; first listings 2021.
>
> Most Claude/Codex responses about "Lean Startup" pull from the 2011 book alone. This file captures the 15 years of subsequent refinement, extension, and reframing.

## 1. The Leader's Guide (2016, Kickstarter-only)

A companion to *The Lean Startup* funded via Kickstarter, distributed only to backers. **Not commercially reissued.** Focuses on how to lead a Lean Startup transformation from an executive perch. Referenced in *The Startup Way* as its intellectual bridge. Rarely cited today but historically the bridge between the 2011 book (which addressed entrepreneurs directly) and the 2017 book (which addressed executives running enterprises that need entrepreneurial units).

Practically: if a user asks about The Leader's Guide, tell them the book is out of print and Ries has since folded most of its content into *The Startup Way*.

## 2. The Startup Way (Currency, 2017) — the enterprise extension

**Full title:** *The Startup Way: How Modern Companies Use Entrepreneurial Management to Transform Culture and Drive Long-Term Growth.* Amazon: [1101903201](https://www.amazon.com/Startup-Way-Companies-Entrepreneurial-Management/dp/1101903201).

### What The Startup Way adds to The Lean Startup

- **Applies the method to established enterprises.** GE, Toyota, Amazon, Facebook, Airbnb, Twilio, and Intuit are all treated as case studies of "modern companies" doing entrepreneurial management. Ries's central proof: the method wasn't just for VC-backed tech startups; it can rewire how a Fortune 500 innovates.
- **Introduces a phased playbook:** Awareness → Foundation → Scaling → Deep Systems. A staged transformation model for spreading entrepreneurial method inside a large company.
- **Names the enterprise-specific obstacles:** quarterly-earnings pressure, misaligned incentive structures, defensive middle management, procurement/finance/legal treating startup teams as anomalies to be normalized. Each obstacle gets a chapter with countermeasures.
- **The "Growth Board"** — Ries's proposed governance body inside the enterprise, modeled on a VC investment committee. Funds internal startups tranche-by-tranche and holds pivot-or-persevere meetings at the corporate level. Members: exec sponsor, finance, at least one operator-turned-investor perspective, and the startup lead. Tranches: cheap discovery money for the search phase; graduated investment once actionable metrics stabilize.
- **The "Entrepreneur" as a role in the org chart.** A first-class career path inside enterprises, not a euphemism for "innovation team leader who gets fired in the next reorg." Ries argues this must be a promotable, evaluated, compensated role — otherwise the enterprise reverts to BAU.
- **The dual operating system:** BAU (existing business, executed on classical management) + Entrepreneurial (new products under uncertainty, executed on Lean Startup) run in parallel with distinct governance. The Startup Way explicitly rejects the "innovation lab as separate skunkworks" model — the entrepreneurial function must be integrated but protected.

### The GE FastWorks case (Ries's canonical enterprise proof)

Origin: 2012. CEO Jeff Immelt tasked GE with faster new-product development. **Viv Goldstein and Janice Semper** attended Ries's book signing for *The Lean Startup*, were converted, and initiated pilot lean methodologies at GE.

Program design:
- **Trained the top 5,000 leaders** in Lean Startup principles via a roadshow with Ries + co-founder David Kidder.
- **Eight pilot projects** to test the method inside GE.
- **Growth Board governance** installed at multiple business units.
- Later expanded to **FastWorks Everyday** across HR, legal, and finance functions using four core concepts: Discover, Develop, Learn, Act.

Documented results:
- **One gas turbine developed 2 years faster and ~40% cheaper** than GE's traditional process.
- **300+ initiatives influenced by 2015.**
- **Neonatal incubator for Indian clinics** — deep ethnographic research led to an innovative low-cost solution for the local context.

Documented failure modes (Ries has been honest about these):
- **Projects operated independently without coaching** — managers dismissed the method: *"We can't do minimum viable engines here."*
- **Tools alone were insufficient** — behavioral and cultural change required sustained commitment.
- **The transformation partially unwound after Immelt's departure** and GE's broader strategic crisis. Which validates his 2026 *Incorruptible* thesis: without governance structures that survive an executive transition, the method is at the mercy of whoever's in charge this year.

**Source (with Ries + FastWorks co-founders reflecting):** [FastWorks: Reflecting on its Origin and Evolution — Lean Startup Co.](https://leanstartup.co/resources/articles/fastworks-reflecting-origin-evolution/)

### Other enterprise cases from The Startup Way

- **Toyota (in-dash electronics system)** — Toyota itself asked Ries to help apply lean-startup principles to a new in-car system. The framework Toyota gave the world (Toyota Production System) returned to Toyota through Ries.
- **Intuit** — chairman Brad Smith publicly endorsed *The Startup Way*; Intuit uses variants of the method for internal innovation.
- **Pitney Bowes** — one of the four year-long transformation clients Ries names in book publicity.
- **Amazon, Facebook, Airbnb, Twilio** — cited more lightly as tech-native examples of the pattern.

### When to invoke The Startup Way in a session

Any time the user is:
- Applying Lean Startup **inside an enterprise or business unit within one.**
- Standing up an **internal innovation team** and worried about it getting killed by BAU.
- Designing **governance for an entrepreneurial function inside a large org** — this is where the Growth Board applies.
- Reporting an "innovation team failed" story — usually the missing scaffolding is executive sponsorship, protected budget, or Growth Board governance. Diagnose the scaffolding gap, don't blame the team.

## 3. Incorruptible (Authors Equity, May 26, 2026) — the governance frame

**Full title:** *Incorruptible: Why Good Companies Go Bad… and How Great Companies Stay Great.* Amazon: [B0FWZZBPZB](https://www.amazon.com/Incorruptible-Good-Companies-Great-Stay/dp/B0FWZZBPZB). **Instant NYT bestseller.**

### Central argument

The failure mode of "companies founded with strong ideals drifting toward short-term thinking, extractive behavior, and mission abandonment" is **structural, not moral**. Well-intentioned people inside those companies can't stop it because the incentive structure and governance design will produce the drift regardless of who's inside.

This is the *scaffolding* argument, one level up from *The Startup Way*. Where *The Startup Way* asked "how do we install entrepreneurial method inside an existing company?", *Incorruptible* asks "how do we design governance so the entrepreneurial method — and the mission it served — survive the founder, survive the IPO, survive the succession, survive the acquisition?"

### Key devices from the book

**"Financial gravity"** — the accumulated pressure of quarterly earnings, dispersed shareholders, and short-tenured executives that pulls every company toward extraction. Named as a **force** that must be actively counteracted, not an ambient atmosphere companies just happen to breathe.

**"Spiritual holding company"** — Ries's proposed governance blueprint. A parent structure that owns the mission and holds the operating entity accountable to it, insulated from short-term shareholder pressure. Modeled loosely on Patagonia's 2022 restructuring and on LTSE-listed companies' governance commitments.

**"Governance as product design."** Ries's most-quoted 2026 line:
> "Governance is not bureaucracy — it is the most important product a founder will ever design." — Ries, [Thought Economics interview, 2026](https://thoughteconomics.com/eric-ries/).

Governance is not the legal boilerplate you sign at Series A. It is the mechanism that protects the mission when the founder is gone. Founders design products; they should design governance with the same rigor.

**"Corruption is structural, not moral."** The load-bearing thesis. Reframes the corporate-scandal narrative away from villain-CEOs toward incentive-and-governance failure. Ries's motivating story:
> "I watched with mounting horror as many of the companies I had helped build were 'surgically deboned.'" — Ries, Thought Economics interview, 2026.

**The 6× survival claim** — mission-controlled companies are ~6× more likely to survive to their 50th year (Ries's data, per Incorruptible; press citations vary).

### Case categories (from the 2026 press cycle)

Specific companies named are dispersed across the book; the podcast interviews from May–July 2026 name several by category:
- **Startups that "surgically deboned" their founding mission post-IPO or post-acquisition.**
- **Established corporations that resisted the pull** — Patagonia (via the 2022 restructuring) is the most-cited.
- **Public institutions retooled for long-term commitment.**
- **LTSE listings themselves** — Twilio, Asana (early dual listings from 2021) as examples of companies making the long-term commitment public.

### When to invoke Incorruptible in a session

Any time the user is:
- **Post-Series-C or public** and asking how to protect the mission from short-term pressure.
- **Founder considering succession or exit** and worried about mission drift after they leave.
- **Board member designing compensation, voting rights, or IPO structure** for a mission-driven company.
- **Watching a peer company drift** and asking what governance would have prevented it.
- Debating **founder ousting patterns** (OpenAI/Altman, older cases like Jobs/Apple) — the frame reads these as governance-design outcomes, not personal drama.
- **Working at LTSE / considering an LTSE listing.**

### The bridge from Lean Startup to Incorruptible

Ries's own framing (2024 Lenny episode + 2026 interviews): the three books form an arc.

- **The Lean Startup (2011):** how to find the business — via validated learning under extreme uncertainty.
- **The Startup Way (2017):** how to install the method inside an established company — with scaffolding that protects it from BAU.
- **Incorruptible (2026):** how to design governance so the mission the business served survives the founder, the IPO, the succession, the acquisition.

Each book addresses a failure mode the previous book didn't anticipate. Lean Startup helped founders find the business but didn't teach them how to protect the mission at scale. The Startup Way helped enterprises install entrepreneurial units but didn't specify the governance that keeps those units alive across CEO transitions (GE FastWorks partially unwound because of this). Incorruptible addresses the governance layer.

## 4. LTSE — Long-Term Stock Exchange (2015 → present)

The operator project that proves the *Incorruptible* thesis. https://ltse.com.

**Timeline:**
- **2015** — Ries founds LTSE.
- **2019** — SEC approval as a national securities exchange.
- **August 2021** — first listings: Twilio and Asana dual-list.
- **2024** — Ries transitions day-to-day CEO role to **Bill Harts**; Ries remains **Founder & Executive Chairman**.
- **2024** — LTSE revenue $23.2M.
- **2025** — $69.6M valuation in a M&A Offer round.
- **2026** — ~67 employees.

**The idea:** a US stock exchange that **requires listed companies to adopt long-term governance commitments** — long-term voting power for long-term shareholders, restrictions on short-term-oriented compensation, quarterly reporting reforms, mandatory disclosure of long-term operating strategy.

**Current state (2026):** operating, but has struggled to attract listing volume. The operating hypothesis (long-term governance draws premium companies) is still being validated. Ries treats LTSE as his own long-arc bet on the *Incorruptible* argument — the operator experiment that either proves or falsifies the thesis over decades, not years.

**Adjacent LTSE property:** *The Eric Ries Show* podcast, hosted on ltse.com — see next section.

Ries's primary live channel since 2024. https://ltse.com/the-eric-ries-show.

**Format:** long-form (60–120 min) conversations with technologists, operators, and thinkers on **growth with purpose, long-term company building, mission-controlled organizations.**

**Cadence:** bi-weekly-ish; irregular but active through 2024–2026. No fixed weekly schedule.

**Distribution:** [Apple](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-eric-ries-show/id1744818044) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/1PA861kDcuviHDqTi2AmuC) · YouTube.

- **Dustin Moskovitz (Asana co-founder)** — scaling without sacrificing values. Asana is LTSE-listed.
- **Sahil Lavingia (Gumroad)** — bootstrapped alternatives; small-team operating model.

## 7. Recurring 2024–2026 themes on LinkedIn and the newsletter

- **Corruption as structural.** The load-bearing frame that ties *Lean Startup* → *Startup Way* → *Incorruptible*: the method matters, but the governance around the method determines whether the method survives.
- **"Great companies stay great" as an active discipline.** Ries's argument against the "companies inevitably rot" fatalism. Rot is the default; staying great is a designed outcome.
- **Founder ousting and mission drift** — Sam Altman / OpenAI, Steve Jobs / Apple (as historical), and unnamed contemporary cases used as examples of governance-design failure.
- **"Financial gravity"** as a named force. This phrase became a Ries signature during the 2026 Incorruptible cycle.
- **The AI question.** No standalone AI-and-Lean-Startup thesis yet. Ries's implicit position: BML applies unchanged to AI-native startups; AI accelerates the loop but doesn't rewrite the discipline.

## 8. Position on the "movement" — how Ries talks about Lean Startup 15 years later

Since 2018 Ries has been publicly comfortable saying **the method has been widely misapplied**, and he says it without disowning the underlying work. Load `voice-and-tone.md` for how this shows up in his register. Two patterns worth naming:

**The definitional-reset move (constant).** Every time Ries uses "MVP," "pivot," "innovation accounting," "startup," "fail fast," he re-anchors the definition first, because the industry has degraded each term. This is a load-bearing pattern; the skill should mirror it.

**The graceful correction, not the retraction.** Ries doesn't say "I was wrong in 2011"; he says "here's what I actually meant, and here's how the term got degraded." The 2024 Lenny episode is the fullest expression of this posture.

## Adjacent frameworks Ries has commented on since 2011

Compatibility mapping he's discussed in podcasts and essays. See `applications.md` for the deeper table.

- **Steve Blank / Customer Development** — the direct intellectual parent. Ries always credits.
- **Toyota Production System / Taiichi Ohno / Deming** — the manufacturing lineage. Ries meticulous about attribution.
- **Marty Cagan / Inspired** — complementary; Cagan writes for PMs, Ries writes for founders. See [[inspired]] (queued).
- **Teresa Torres / Continuous Discovery Habits** — highly compatible; Torres updates the tactical practices for 2020s teams and her Opportunity Solution Tree fits inside Ries's BML loop. See [[continuous-discovery-habits]] (queued).
- **Melissa Perri / Escaping the Build Trap** — Perri credits Ries as foundational influence and *explicitly redefines MVP using Ries's original meaning* to break the industry's degraded association. See [[escaping-the-build-trap]].
- **Colin Bryar & Bill Carr / Working Backwards** — **honest disagreement**. Amazon rejects MVP at commitment altitude; uses PR/FAQ instead. Ries's method thrives when experiments are cheap; Working Backwards thrives when launch quality dominates. See [[working-backwards]] and `applications.md`.
- **JTBD (Christensen / Moesta / Kalbach)** — complementary demand-side lens. JTBD tells you which hypothesis is worth testing; Lean Startup tells you how to test it.
- **Design Sprint (Knapp)** — compressed 5-day Learn phase; composable.
- **Shape Up (Basecamp / Ryan Singer)** — a delivery cadence for shipped products; complementary to Lean Startup, addresses a different phase.
- **DORA / Accelerate** — engineering delivery metrics; overlaps with Ries's continuous-deployment lineage but at the delivery-throughput layer.
- **Playing to Win (Martin), Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Rumelt), 7 Powers (Helmer)** — corporate-strategy altitude; above where Lean Startup operates. Use those first if the user is deciding what business to build; then bring Lean Startup for tactical hypothesis testing.
