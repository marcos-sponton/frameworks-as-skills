# Sprint — Invocation Templates

> Prompt templates for common Sprint situations. Copy the shape, fill in the specifics. The templates prime the assistant to apply the method with fidelity — checking whether Sprint is the right tool, insisting on the Decider, and following the day-by-day structure.

## Decide whether to sprint

> "We're facing [PROBLEM / QUESTION]. We've been debating it for [WEEKS/MONTHS]. I'm considering running a sprint to test our assumptions. Help me think through whether a Sprint (Jake Knapp's 5-day method) is the right tool here. Check: Is this a big enough question? Do we have a testable hypothesis? Can we get 5 target customers for Friday? Do we have a Decider with real authority? If Sprint isn't the right fit, suggest what is."

## Plan a sprint from scratch

> "I want to run a sprint on [CHALLENGE / QUESTION]. Walk me through what I need to set up before Monday starts. Team composition (who should be in the room?), who should be the Decider, logistics (room, supplies, schedule), and how to recruit 5 customers for Friday. Use Jake Knapp's Sprint method exactly as described in the book."

## Run Monday (Map and Target)

> "It's Monday of our sprint. Our challenge is [CHALLENGE]. Walk me through the day — long-term goal, Sprint Questions, Map, expert interviews, How Might We notes, voting, and picking the target. Help me write Sprint Questions that are specific enough to test on Friday. Push back if my Sprint Questions are vague or untestable."

## Run Tuesday (Sketch)

> "It's Tuesday of our sprint. Yesterday we mapped the problem and picked [TARGET]. Walk me through today — Lightning Demos, Notes, Ideas, Crazy 8s, and the Solution Sketch. Help me brief the team on the 4-step sketch process, especially Crazy 8s (most people resist it). Remind them: work alone, no group brainstorming."

## Run Wednesday (Decide)

> "It's Wednesday of our sprint. We have [N] Solution Sketches on the wall. Walk me through the decision process — Art Museum, Heat Map, Speed Critique, Straw Poll, Supervote, Rumble vs. All-in-One, and the Storyboard. Our Decider is [ROLE]. Help me facilitate so the Decider can make a real decision, not a compromise."

## Build the Thursday prototype strategy

> "It's Thursday of our sprint. We have a Storyboard from yesterday. Help me plan the prototype. What's the fastest way to build something that looks real enough to test? What tools should we use (Keynote, Figma, something else)? How do we divide the team (Maker, Stitcher, Writer, Asset Collector, Interviewer)? What does 'Goldilocks quality' mean for our specific case?"

## Structure Friday's test

> "It's Friday of our sprint. We have a prototype ready. Walk me through the 5-act interview structure. Help me write the interview script — Act 1 (welcome), Act 2 (context questions about [CUSTOMER TYPE]), Act 3 (introduction to the prototype), Act 4 (tasks to observe), Act 5 (debrief). Remind me what to watch for and how to take notes."

## Analyze Friday's results

> "We just finished our 5 customer interviews. Help me find patterns. Here's what we observed: [OBSERVATIONS]. Map the observations back to our Sprint Questions: [SPRINT QUESTIONS]. What did we learn? What should we do next — build it, iterate, pivot, or kill it?"

## Adapt Sprint for remote/virtual

> "I want to run a remote sprint with a distributed team. What changes from the in-person format? Help me plan the remote adaptation — tools (Miro, Zoom, etc.), schedule adjustments, how to handle the 'no devices' rule when everyone is on a device, and tips for keeping energy up over video. Reference Knapp's remote sprint cheat sheet."

## Write Sprint Questions

> "We're sprinting on [CHALLENGE]. Help me write Sprint Questions. I want questions that are: (a) specific enough to test with a prototype, (b) important enough that the answer changes what we build, and (c) answerable from 5 customer interviews. Push back if my questions are too vague ('Will they like it?') or too broad ('Will this increase retention over 6 months?')."

## Brief the Decider

> "I need to brief [PERSON / ROLE] on their role as Decider in next week's sprint. Help me explain: what a sprint is (one paragraph), what the Decider does (picks the target Monday, casts the Supervote Wednesday, breaks ties anytime), why the role exists (prevents consensus paralysis), and what days they absolutely must attend (Monday and Wednesday minimum, ideally all 5)."

## Decide whether to use Design Sprint or Foundation Sprint

> "I'm trying to decide between running a Design Sprint (5-day, from the 2016 Sprint book) and a Foundation Sprint (2-day, from the 2025 Click book). My situation is [SITUATION]. Help me choose. Design Sprint = 'does this solution work for customers?' Foundation Sprint = 'is this idea differentiated enough to build?' Which question do I need to answer first?"

## Run a 3-Hour Brand Sprint

> "I want to run Jake Knapp's 3-Hour Brand Sprint with my team to align on our brand positioning. Walk me through the exercises, how to facilitate Note-and-Vote for brand attributes, and how to get to a shared positioning in 3 hours."

## Post-sprint: decide what to do next

> "We finished our sprint. Here's what we learned: [RESULTS]. Help me decide the next step. Options: (a) build it — results were strong enough to commit, (b) iterate — run another sprint on the weak areas, (c) pivot — the results showed we're solving the wrong problem, (d) kill it — the idea doesn't work. Use Sprint's framework for interpreting results, not gut feeling."
