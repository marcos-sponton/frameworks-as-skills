# Sprint — Post-Book Material (2016 → 2026)

> Everything Jake Knapp has published or developed *after* the 2016 Sprint book. This is the differential of this skill — the density the book alone doesn't capture. The 2016 mechanisms are all current; what's changed is (a) remote/virtual sprint adaptations, (b) the 4-day sprint format, (c) *Make Time* (2018), (d) *Click* and the Foundation Sprint (2025), and (e) Knapp's shift from Google Ventures to Character Capital. Nothing in the original method has been formally deprecated.

## Make Time (2018) — Knapp and Zeratsky's second book

**Source:** *Make Time: How to Focus on What Matters Every Day* (Currency / Penguin Random House, 2018). Co-authored with John Zeratsky.

**What it is:** A personal productivity framework. Four steps daily:

1. **Highlight** — choose one priority for the day. Not a to-do list — one thing that, if accomplished, makes the day feel successful.
2. **Laser** — create focus to work on the Highlight. Tactics: distraction-free phone (remove social media, email, news), time-blocking, visible countdown timer.
3. **Energize** — sustain energy through the day. Tactics: exercise, real food, caffeine timing, face-to-face interaction.
4. **Reflect** — end of day, quick review. What worked? What didn't? Adjust tomorrow.

**Relationship to Sprint:** Make Time is about daily personal productivity. Sprint is about team problem-solving in a week. They share a philosophy — structure creates freedom, defaults are the enemy, you design your time or it gets designed for you — but operate at different scales.

**The "distraction-free iPhone":** Knapp's most viral post — removing social media, email, and news apps from his phone. Six years of practice documented on Medium and jakeknapp.com. This became a signature Knapp teaching.

**The Burner List:** A 2-column to-do list. Left column = the one big project you're working on right now. Right column = everything else, which you acknowledge exists but aren't working on today. From a jakeknapp.com blog post, popularized through the Make Time community.

## Remote Design Sprint adaptations (2020)

When COVID-19 shut down in-person sprints in March 2020, Knapp, Zeratsky, and Jackie Colburn developed **remote sprint adaptations.**

**Key source:** *The Remote Design Sprint Cheat Sheet* — published by Knapp on Medium: https://medium.com/@jakek/remote-design-sprint-cheat-sheet-6d0bfdf011db

**Official Remote Design Sprint Template** — created by Steph Cruchon, Jake Knapp, John Zeratsky, and Jackie Colburn. Includes exercises not in the Sprint book but developed through trainings. Available at https://design-sprint.com/remote-design-sprint-template/

**Miro template (updated 2024):** https://miro.com/templates/the-2024-design-sprint/

**Key adaptations for remote:**
- **Miro (or similar) replaces whiteboards.** Sticky notes, voting, maps, storyboards all move to the digital whiteboard.
- **Video on, cameras on.** The "no devices" rule becomes "one device, camera on, notifications off."
- **Shorter days.** Remote sprint fatigue hits faster. Sessions run 4-5 hours instead of 7.
- **More breaks.** 10-minute break every 60-90 minutes.
- **Pre-work replaces some Monday activities.** Lightning Demos can be done asynchronously before Tuesday.
- **Prototype tools stay the same.** Keynote, Figma, Slides — remote doesn't change the prototype approach.
- **Friday testing works well remote.** Video interviews are natural in the remote context.

**What doesn't translate well remote:**
- The energy of a shared room. Remote sprints tend to be more controlled, less spontaneous.
- Whiteboard sketching in real-time. Digital whiteboarding is slower and less fluid.
- Cross-pollination during breaks. In-person breaks produce serendipitous conversations; remote breaks are silence.

## The 4-Day Design Sprint format

Developed collaboratively between Knapp, AJ&Smart (a Berlin-based innovation studio), and others. Condenses the 5-day sprint into 4 days.

**Key changes from the 5-day format:**
- **Day 1:** Monday and Tuesday compressed — Map + Sketch in one day.
- **Day 2:** Wednesday's Decide exercises.
- **Day 3:** Thursday's Prototype.
- **Day 4:** Friday's Test.
- **Full team needed only 2 days** (Days 1-2) instead of all 5.

**When to use 4-day over 5-day:**
- Team can't commit to a full week.
- The problem is well-scoped enough that Monday's mapping is fast.
- The team is experienced with sprints and can move faster.

**When to stick with 5 days:**
- First sprint for the team. The extra day helps the team learn the process.
- Complex, multi-stakeholder problem that needs a full Monday of mapping.
- The Decider needs time to process before deciding.

## Click: How to Make What People Want (2025) — the Foundation Sprint

**Source:** *Click: How to Make What People Want* (Avid Reader Press / Simon & Schuster, April 2025). Co-authored with John Zeratsky.

**What it is:** A new book introducing the **Foundation Sprint** — a 2-day process for validating a startup idea's differentiation before committing to build. This is Knapp's answer to the question he kept encountering: "We ran a Design Sprint and built the right product — but nobody cared because we weren't different enough."

**The Foundation Sprint's premise:** The Design Sprint (2016) tests whether a *solution* works. The Foundation Sprint (2025) tests whether the *idea is differentiated enough to matter.* It sits upstream of the Design Sprint.

**The Foundation Sprint — 3 phases in 2 days:**

1. **The Basics** — What are you building? For whom? What's the problem? (Compressed framing — similar to Sprint Monday but focused on positioning.)
2. **Differentiation** — What makes this genuinely different from alternatives? The "magic lenses" activity forces the team to stress-test differentiation from multiple angles. This is the load-bearing exercise.
3. **Approach** — Given the differentiation, what's the fastest way to validate it with real customers?

**How Foundation Sprint relates to Design Sprint:**
- **Foundation Sprint** = "Is this the right thing to build?" (upstream — differentiation, positioning)
- **Design Sprint** = "Can we build it in a way that works for customers?" (downstream — solution, usability)

Most teams need the Foundation Sprint *before* the Design Sprint. The Design Sprint assumes you're building the right thing and tests how to build it. The Foundation Sprint tests whether the thing is right.

**Stories from *Click*:** Nike, Google, Slack, and "the frontiers of artificial intelligence research" — plus Character Capital portfolio companies.

**Free resources:** 16-page preview, Foundation Sprint Worksheets (29 pages), Miro templates, bonus pack — available at https://www.character.vc/click

**Jake & JZ on Lenny's Podcast (July 2025):** The freshest long-form interview. Discusses the Foundation Sprint, what makes it different from the Design Sprint, and examples from Character Capital. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-foundation-sprint-jake-knapp-and-john-zeratsky

**Knapp's own framing (Design Better Podcast, January 2025):**
The Foundation Sprint is "the sprint we use most often with our portfolio companies at Character Capital." The Design Sprint remains valid for testing solutions, but most early-stage companies fail not because the solution is wrong but because the differentiation is weak.

## "Is the Design Sprint still relevant?" (2025)

Knapp and Zeratsky addressed this question directly in a January 2025 video and in multiple 2025 podcast appearances.

**Their answer:** Yes, but it's one tool in a toolkit now, not the only one. The Design Sprint is for testing solutions with customers. The Foundation Sprint is for validating differentiation upstream. And ongoing product development needs its own methodology (they point to Lean Startup, continuous discovery, or whatever fits the team).

**Key evolution in Knapp's thinking (2016 → 2025):**
- **2016:** "Sprint is the answer to 'how do we decide what to build?'"
- **2025:** "Sprint is the answer to 'how do we test whether this solution works?' — but first, use the Foundation Sprint to test whether you're solving the right problem with real differentiation."

This parallels Ryan Singer's evolution with Shape Up — Singer added Framing (2022) as an upstream step to Shaping, recognizing that teams were shaping solutions to problems not worth solving. Knapp added the Foundation Sprint as an upstream step to the Design Sprint, recognizing that teams were prototyping solutions that weren't differentiated.
