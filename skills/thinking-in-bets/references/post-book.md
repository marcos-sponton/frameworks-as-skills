## Refinements across the three books

### From "wanna bet?" (2018) → 6-step framework (2020) → kill criteria (2022)

The trajectory is meaningful: each book fills a gap in the prior.

- **2018 (*Thinking in Bets*)** gave the frame — decisions are bets, separate DQ from OQ, form a truthseeking pod — but was thin on the *process* of running the decision.
- **2020 (*How to Decide*)** filled that gap with the 6-step framework and the speed-triage tests. But it was thin on the *committed-execution* problem: how do you keep from riding a losing bet into the ground?
- **2022 (*Quit*)** filled that gap with kill criteria (state + date), monkeys and pedestals, quitting coach. Kill criteria are premortem + precommitment, formalized.

**Practitioner implication:** if a user only knows *Thinking in Bets*, they have the frame but not the tools. If they only know *Quit*, they have the counter-force but not the initial structure. The integrated system is what makes the toolkit complete.

### From "mental time travel" (2018) → 10-10-10 as speed test (2020)

10-10-10 first appears in *Thinking in Bets* as one of several mental-time-travel devices. In *How to Decide*, it becomes a first-class speed-triage test alongside the happiness test and the two-way door.

### From premortem (Klein, borrowed in 2018) → premortem + precommitment (mature 2022)

The premortem alone is journaling. Duke's contribution — most fully developed in *Quit* — is that the premortem must be paired with a **precommitted action** for each failure signal. Otherwise you'll observe the signal, rationalize it, and continue.

## First Round Capital investment protocol

Duke is a **Special Partner at First Round Capital**. She helped design their explicit decision-recording system.

**What the protocol includes** (per Lenny 2024):
- **1–7 scale on multiple dimensions per deal.** Not a single yes/no.
- **Decision journal per partner.** Each partner writes their reasoning before the collective discussion.
- **Calibration tracked over time.** Partners see whether their 5s are outperforming their 3s over years.
- **Individual partner strengths mapped.** Some partners are better on certain dimensions; the protocol makes this legible.

**Why this matters:** it's the most fully instrumented real-world implementation of Duke's method. When a user asks "how would this actually work at scale?" — First Round is the answer.

## "Nevertheless" — the disagreement move (post-2022 refinement)

Popularized on Lenny 2024 and in the "How to Disagree Better" Substack essay. When you disagree with someone, don't say "you're wrong" — say "nevertheless." Hold both positions.

The move accomplishes three things:
1. **Validates** the other person's reasoning (you don't dismiss it).
2. **Names your disagreement** clearly (you don't hide it).
3. **Preserves the working relationship** (you don't force them into defensive posture).

**Companion move:** "I don't understand" instead of "you're wrong." Reframes the disagreement as a question about your comprehension, invites elaboration, and often surfaces the gap in their reasoning without confrontation.

## Refinement on grit (post-*Quit*)

*Quit* was widely read as an anti-grit book. Duke has spent 2022–2026 re-messaging that it isn't:

> "Quitting and grit are two sides of the same decision."
> — Duke, *Quit* (**quote confidence: moderate**)

**Grit is not the enemy.** Unconditional grit is the enemy. Grit is good *conditional on the goal being worth it*. This nuance is more prominent in her 2023–2026 podcast appearances than in the book itself, where the counter-force framing dominates.

**Practitioner implication:** don't quote Duke as "quit more." Quote her as "quit the wrong things so you can grit on the right ones."

## Refinement on identity (post-*Quit*)

*Quit* named identity as the hardest thing to quit. Since 2022, Duke has expanded on this — especially in the Alliance podcast — around **organizational identity** (Sears retail, Nokia phones, incumbent car makers vs. EVs).

Framework she uses in later material:
1. What is our identity anchored to? (Product? Category? Founding story? Founder personality?)
2. What would a market change look like that made this identity unhelpful?
3. What's the earliest observable signal of that change?
4. What would we pre-commit to do when we see it?

This is kill criteria applied at the *identity* layer — the deepest layer — where sunk cost is thickest.

## Refinement on premortem (post-*How to Decide*)

Duke's public teaching on premortems has evolved. Original version (from Klein, popularized in *Thinking in Bets*): imagine failure, ask why. Later version (via Lenny 2024, First Round Review): pair each failure mode with a precommitted signal AND action. Without the precommitment, teams observe the failure signal, rationalize it, and keep going.

**Best current formulation:**
1. Imagine you failed 12 months from now.
2. List the top 5 reasons why.
3. For each reason: what's the earliest observable signal?
4. For each signal: what will you do when you see it?
5. Write it down. Share it with the truthseeking pod / quitting coach.

## Adjacent frameworks Duke has commented on since 2018

Compatibility mapping she's discussed across various essays and podcasts:

- **Kahneman (System 1/2)** — she is his translator/popularizer. Accepts System 1/2 wholesale, then extends: "System 2 isn't always superior — the deer-in-headlights driver overthinks and dies." She reframes probability estimation for lay users ("how promising?" instead of point probabilities). She interviewed Kahneman for the Alliance and for a widely shared Medium piece.
- **Klein (naturalistic decision making, RPD)** — sourced the premortem. She defends Klein's disciplined-intuition school when she pushes back on rejection of System 1.
- **Tetlock (Superforecasting)** — calibration exercises, state+date forecasts, Student Forecasting Tournaments at Alliance are modeled on the Good Judgment Project. She interviewed Tetlock on the Alliance podcast.
- **Duckworth (Grit)** — positioned as counter-force, not rejection. *"Quitting and grit are two sides of the same decision."*
- **Rumelt (Good Strategy Bad Strategy)** — no direct citation surfaced, but methodological cousin: both attack post-hoc narrative substitutes for real analysis. Rumelt's crux + Duke's resulting map to the same underlying insight.
- **Munger / Farnam Street / Shane Parrish** — natural allies. Parrish has hosted her twice on The Knowledge Project.
- **Astro Teller (X, Alphabet's moonshot factory)** — she's a *user* of his frameworks (monkeys and pedestals), not the other way around. Duke consulted at X and popularized the metaphor beyond it.
