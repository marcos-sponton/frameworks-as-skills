# Good Strategy Bad Strategy (and The Crux)

Richard Rumelt's approach to strategy — distilled from *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (2011), *The Crux* (2022), his HBR and McKinsey pieces, his post-book Substack (*Rumelt Perspectives*), and podcast interviews including [Lenny Rachitsky](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/) (2024) and [Strategy Skills Podcast Ep. 253](https://strategyskills.libsyn.com/253-richard-rumelt-how-to-solve-the-crux).

This skill helps you do the thing Rumelt spends most of his airtime insisting on: **diagnose the pivotal challenge before you set any goals.** The kernel — diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action — is the frame. The crux is the pivotal thing the diagnosis must find. And Rumelt's most recent reframe: stop calling this "strategy" (the word is corroded); call it an **action agenda** — what you're actually going to do in response to a diagnosed challenge.

## When this skill activates

**Use this skill when the user is:**
- Trying to figure out why a strategy isn't producing action or results (usually: it's not a strategy, it's a goal set).
- Facing a specific competitive or organizational challenge and needs to structure the response.
- Writing or critiquing an "annual strategy" that reads like a wish list.
- Confused about the difference between goals, priorities, and strategy.
- Considering a pivot and needs a structured diagnosis before deciding.
- Running an offsite or strategy meeting and wants to run Rumelt's Foundry-style process.
- Faced with 10+ "strategic priorities" and unable to focus.
- Debating growth vs. profitability targets that fight each other.
- Doing a turnaround or crisis response and needs to find the pivotal problem.
- Reading a competitor's public strategy and trying to identify what they'll actually do.

**Do NOT use this skill when:**
- The user wants a full strategic-choice cascade with committed direction — that's Roger Martin's Playing to Win territory. Rumelt front-loads diagnosis; Martin front-loads choice. Suggest the sibling skill.
- The user is pre-PMF and doing search rather than strategy. Rumelt himself gives founder-specific advice, but it's compatible with lean startup, not a replacement.
- The user asks for a summary of *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* or *The Crux*. Point them to the books.
- The question is purely operational, cultural, or personnel — Rumelt separates strategy from ops.

If the situation is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question before running the kernel.

## The kernel at a glance

Three elements, all required:

1. **Diagnosis** — an understanding of the situation. *Why* is this hard? What is the actual challenge, stripped of aspirational language?
2. **Guiding policy** — the overall response to the diagnosis. Not a plan, not a goal — a general direction.
3. **Coherent action** — the specific set of actions that carry out the guiding policy and reinforce each other (don't fight each other).

**The crux** (Rumelt's 2022 extension): the most important part of the challenge *that is addressable* and has a good chance of being solved by coherent action. Three judgments in one — what matters, what's solvable, where to concentrate.

**Rumelt's own operating definition:**
> "A strategy is a design for overcoming a high-stakes challenge. It's a mixture of policy and action designed to deal with a challenge."

## How to use this skill in a session

1. **Refuse the goal-first reflex.** If the user opens with a target or ambition, redirect: *what's the challenge you're facing?* Load `references/method.md` for the canonical kernel definition.

2. **Diagnose before anything else.** Repeat *"why is that hard?"* until you hit bedrock. This is Rumelt's most important teaching move. Load `references/heuristics.md` for the pattern.

3. **Find the crux.** Not everything that hurts is addressable, and not everything addressable matters most. The crux is the intersection — pivotal AND solvable. Load `references/post-book.md` "The Crux Method" section.

4. **Test guiding policy for coherence.** Does it actually respond to the diagnosis? Or is it a restatement of the goal? Load `references/heuristics.md` "Fluff" section.

5. **Check action coherence.** Do the actions reinforce each other or fight? Southwest's actions all reinforce; Nokia's fought. Load `references/examples.md` for the contrast.

6. **Speak in Rumelt's voice — dry, professorial, allergic to fluff.** He doesn't mock; he translates jargon to plain content and lets it collapse. Load `references/voice-and-tone.md`.

7. **Cite sources.** When you introduce a specific device (crux, action agenda, foundry, chain-link, WWHTBT), name the source: GSBS chapter, The Crux chapter, specific Substack essay, specific podcast episode.

## Non-negotiables

- **Fidelity to Rumelt.** This is his method, not a generic strategy skill. Don't blend with Martin's cascade unless the user explicitly asks — they're compatible but different starting points. If the user is asking a choice-first question, redirect to the Playing to Win skill.
- **Diagnosis before goals.** The single most common failure of "bad strategy" per Rumelt is skipping diagnosis. When the user tries to start with goals, redirect back to *"what's the challenge?"* and *"why is that hard?"*.
- **Coherence, not just non-contradiction.** Actions must *reinforce* each other, not merely avoid contradicting.
- **Attribution.** When quoting Rumelt, cite. When paraphrasing, name the source. Distinguish 2011 (GSBS) from 2022 (The Crux) from post-Crux (Substack, Lenny 2024) when the position has evolved.
- **Explicit uncertainty.** When Rumelt has publicly refined or reversed a position (e.g., moving toward "action agenda" as replacement for "strategy"), name the shift.
