# Good Strategy Bad Strategy — Applications

> When Rumelt's kernel fits, when it doesn't, and what to reach for instead.

## Situations where the kernel + crux fits well

- **You're facing a specific challenge and need to structure the response.** The kernel was designed for this exact case.
- **A "strategy" document is producing no action.** The kernel diagnoses why (usually: no diagnosis, or fluff-diagnosis; guiding policy is a goal in disguise; actions don't cohere).
- **You're stuck between multiple competing priorities.** Crux-finding forces the importance × addressability filter.
- **You're doing a turnaround, crisis response, or competitive-threat response.** Diagnosis-first is exactly right.
- **You're reading a competitor's public strategy and trying to identify what they'll actually do.** The kernel gives you the analytic frame.
- **You're running an offsite and want to avoid producing another "vision, mission, values" deck.** The Foundry process is Rumelt's operational alternative.
- **A leader is describing 12 "priorities" and can't focus.** The kernel is the argument for focusing to one crux.
- **Growth-AND-profit is producing paralysis.** Rumelt's diagnosis of conflicting objectives is directly applicable.
- **You need to critique someone else's strategy document.** The kernel gives you three diagnostic lenses (diagnosis quality, guiding policy vs. goals, action coherence).

## Situations where it does NOT fit

- **You have a diagnosed challenge and need to commit to a market/customer/HTW choice.** That's Roger Martin's Playing to Win territory. Rumelt front-loads diagnosis; Martin front-loads choice. Use the sibling `playing-to-win/` skill.

- **You're pre-PMF as a startup founder doing search.** Rumelt himself acknowledges this exception: *"When you're a founder in a startup, you're making a bet... there's a search like a truffle hound."* Pre-PMF is search, not choice. Use Lean Startup / Continuous Discovery methods. Rumelt's founder advice (find an important solvable problem, solve for many) is compatible with lean methods, not a replacement.

- **Purely operational or execution questions.** *"How do we ship faster?"* isn't a Rumelt question. Use TOC / Lean / SRE / EOS depending on flavor.

- **Culture, hiring, org-design questions without a competitive-choice angle.** Rumelt separates strategy from these. Bring in the kernel only where the org problem *is* the strategic crux (Nokia-style).

- **When the user just wants a summary of Good Strategy Bad Strategy or The Crux.** Give the book links; don't run the kernel at them.

- **Innovation portfolio prioritization (N bets out of 100).** The kernel is meant for one integrated response to one crux; not for portfolio ranking. Use ODI / JTBD prioritization / RICE.

- **Financial modeling and forecasting.** Rumelt is explicit: *"He who forecasts the future lies, even if he tells the truth."* Prediction is not strategy. Use financial-planning tools for the modeling; use the kernel to diagnose whether the underlying strategy the model represents is real.

## Adjacent frameworks — when to reach for a different one

| If the user's situation is... | Reach for... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Committing to a market/customer/HTW after diagnosis | Roger Martin's **Playing to Win** cascade | Martin's WTP–HTW matched pair is exactly the choice frame Rumelt's diagnosis leads into |
| Pre-PMF search | Lean Startup, Continuous Discovery | Search, not choice |
| Understanding industry structure | Porter's **Five Forces** | Rumelt endorses as prompt to think; not as fill-in template |
| Mapping evolutionary landscape over time | **Wardley Mapping** | Rumelt's kernel is static-snapshot; Wardley is dynamic |
| Portfolio prioritization across many bets | **ODI (Ulwick)**, RICE, JTBD prioritization | Kernel is single-response, not multi-bet ranking |
| Business model design | **Osterwalder Canvas** | Sits alongside; useful artifact, not substitute |
| Positioning (marketing) | **April Dunford's** *Obviously Awesome* | Positioning is downstream of a chosen strategy |
| Narrative for the market | **Andy Raskin's** Strategic Narrative | Narrative is downstream artifact |
| Change management once strategy is set | Kotter, ADKAR, EOS Traction | Kernel tells you what to change to; not how to change the org |
| System-level constraint identification | **Theory of Constraints (Goldratt)** | Highly compatible with crux + chain-link; can be composed |
| Discovering hidden customer needs | **JTBD** (Christensen, Moesta) | Rumelt doesn't cover discovery mechanics; JTBD does |

## How the kernel composes with other frameworks

Rumelt has commented publicly on many:

- **Playing to Win (Martin) — pairs well.** Rumelt's kernel maps to Martin's problem → HTW → integrated activities. **Use Rumelt to find the crux + build the response; use Martin to commit to a where-to-play / how-to-win pair.** The two are complementary in sequence: diagnose (Rumelt) → choose (Martin). Note: Martin has publicly disagreed with Rumelt on specific cases (P&G in Lenny 2024). Both methods survive the disagreement.

- **Porter's Five Forces — pairs well as a prompt for diagnosis.** Rumelt's 1991 paper *"How much does industry matter?"* actually showed firm-specific factors matter more than industry — but he still uses Porter as a diagnostic aid, not as a mechanical exercise.

- **Blue Ocean Strategy — bounded compatibility.** Blue Ocean without a diagnosed challenge is decorative. But its search moves (reduce/raise/eliminate/create) are compatible with the "audacious leap" move in the crux method.

- **OKRs — pairs well as an alignment tool downstream of the kernel.** *"OKRs are useful for aligning execution once a strategy exists. Without the underlying strategic logic, OKRs are just goals with key results, and goals without diagnosis are wishes."*

- **Balanced Scorecard — measurement tool, not diagnosis.** Belongs after the kernel is set.

- **Lean Startup — compatible with sequential-crux thinking.** Each pivot = next crux. Rumelt is more sympathetic to lean/discovery methods in founder contexts than to corporate strategic planning.

- **Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) — highly compatible.** TOC's identification of the system constraint maps closely to Rumelt's crux-finding. Where TOC is dominant (manufacturing, operations), it does the crux-finding job.

- **7 Powers (Helmer) — compatible with the "sources of power" list in GSBS.** Helmer's 7 powers are types of asymmetric advantage; Rumelt's kernel says find the one you have.

- **Jobs to Be Done (Christensen, Moesta) — complementary.** JTBD gives you discovery mechanics that GSBS lacks. Once you know the customer's job, the kernel structures the response.

## Frameworks Rumelt has explicitly critiqued (see `heuristics.md` for details)

- **Vision-Mission-Values as strategy substitute** — one of three root causes of bad strategy.
- **Priority lists** — the word "priority" means first; lists violate the word.
- **Growth AND profit as parallel targets** — they fight each other.
- **OKRs used as strategy** — goals without diagnosis are wishes.
- **Strategic planning as substitute for strategy** — usually a budget process wearing a strategy costume.
- **Framework performance (SWOT, 5F, BCG matrix filled in mechanically)** — cargo-cult strategy.
- **M&A "synergies" narratives** — usually accounting-driven; real synergies rarely quantified.
- **Culture-as-strategy** — culture is a resource, not a plan.
- **Bright-shiny-object management fashions** — adopting whatever's trendy without diagnosis.

If the user invokes any of these expecting them to work, redirect gently but clearly.
