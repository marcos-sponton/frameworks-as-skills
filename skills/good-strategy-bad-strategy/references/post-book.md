## The Crux — Rumelt's central 2022 extension

The Crux (2022) added a filter *before* diagnosis: **find the crux**. It didn't replace the kernel — it deepened it.

**Definition:**
> "The crux is the most important part of a set of challenges that is addressable and has a good chance of being solved by coherent action."

Three judgments in one — what matters, what's solvable, where to concentrate. See `references/method.md` for full detail. The pieces below extend the crux method operationally.

### How to find the crux — Rumelt's heuristics

1. **WWHTBT test.** Run through every condition the strategy relies on. The crux is the pivotal condition you are *least* confident in.
2. **The elimination question:** *"If we solved everything else but not this, would the strategy still fail?"* If yes — you found it.
3. **The "why is that hard?" question, repeated.** Repeat until you hit bedrock.
4. **Structural, not emotional.** The crux is not what people complain about most; it's the condition on which the logic actually turns.
5. **Listen for the paradox.** *"Like a designer, the strategist feels a sense of blockage or constraint when examining the crux."* Executives will circle the central paradox without stating it.
6. **Addressable AND important.** Neither alone is sufficient.

### Rock-climbing metaphor

The crux is the pivotal pitch of a climb — the one hard move on which the entire climb turns. Miss it and the summit is unreachable no matter how well you climb the rest. Rumelt uses this metaphor to teach that not all challenges deserve equal weight.

### The three sub-skills of the strategist

Per Rumelt on the Strategy Skills Podcast Ep. 253:

> "Figuring out what is critical; constraining what is critical vs. what is controllable; focusing energy on that."

Notice: three distinct skills, not one. Each is trainable independently.

**Source:** *The Crux* (2022), Strategy Skills Podcast Ep. 253, BCG Henderson Institute interview (Sept 2022).

## The "action agenda" reframe — Rumelt's most notable recent shift

Introduced heavily on Lenny's Podcast (Jan 2024) and echoed in Substack.

**The core reframe:**
> "Replace the term strategy altogether and just call it an 'action agenda.'"

**Why:** the word "strategy" has been so corroded by managerial usage (financial forecasts, aspirational goals, values statements) that it now confuses more than it clarifies. Call it an action agenda and the ambiguity dies.

> "Goals, ambitions, visions, missions, values — none of these things are a strategy. An action agenda forces the question: what are we actually going to *do*, in what order, in response to which specific challenge?"

**Practical implication:** organizations can maintain a **Standard Narrative** (public strategy document with values and long-term aspirations) for stakeholders while leadership works from a **separate, focused Action Agenda** that drives the actual work. Don't try to make them the same document.

**Test:** if what you wrote could be read aloud at an all-hands and no one could tell what you'd do differently on Monday, it's not an action agenda.

**Sources:** [Lenny's Podcast: Good Strategy Bad Strategy | Richard Rumelt](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/good-strategy-bad-strategy-richard) · [Rumelt Substack: The Standard Narrative](https://rumelt.substack.com/p/the-standard-narrative)

## The Strategy Foundry — Rumelt's operational group process

The Foundry is Rumelt's actual client offering. Detailed enough now to run yourself if you want the shape.

### Structure

- 2 to 4 days, intensive
- Small group, less than 10, ideally under 8
- **Senior executive officer must attend** — not delegated
- Off-site, no phones, separated from budgeting cycle
- Confidential pre-interviews and written questions prior
- Leader cannot dominate discussion (this is a rule, not a preference)

### Sequence

1. Ground in context: *"What's changing? Where will you be in 3-5 years?"*
2. Past successes and failures (builds psychological safety)
3. **Generate 8-10+ challenges openly.** Deferred judgment. No filtering yet.
4. Cluster challenges. Fuzzy edges are OK.
5. Screen and winnow using group consensus or voting
6. Filter by importance × addressability
7. Reframe using: analogies, comparisons (*"what would competitor X do?"*), explicit assumptions
8. **"Why is that hard?"** — break down each remaining candidate
9. Isolate the crux
10. Design guiding policy + coherent actions
11. **"Instant strategy"** — force one-sentence concrete recommendations
12. **Red teaming** — role-play competitors
13. **"Swearing in"** — participants make verbal commitment to implement. Symbolic but load-bearing.

### Design principles

- *"Most organizations do not need further deep analysis to do strategy work. What they needed was a focus on challenge and action."* This is *why* the Foundry exists as a distinct alternative to "strategic planning."
- *"You put smart people in a room and hope for the best."* Strategy is human judgment, not a formula.
- The power of the Foundry is enabling *"a group of executives to pierce the veil of each others' expectations and reach what might be a fairly private consensus."*

**Sources:** [richardrumelt.com/strategy-foundry](https://www.richardrumelt.com/strategy-foundry) · BCG Henderson Institute interview (Sept 2022).

## Chain-link logic — an operational principle expanded post-book

Weakest link = performance ceiling. Rumelt's principle:

- Adding better components to an all-weak system usually *hurts* profit (the pin-factory example: adding zero-defect workers to a shop with 40% defect workers means high-quality output gets discarded downstream).
- **Don't invest asymmetrically in strong links** when the weak link is unfixed. It wastes capital.
- **Applies to:** manufacturing, economic development, org design, any system where output requires the coordination of multiple parts.
- **Cases:** Space Shuttle Challenger O-ring, 1970s GM (quality transmissions in cars with rattling dashboards).
- **The fix:** identify the weak link, absorb the cost of running while you fix it, then improve.

**Source:** [Rumelt Substack: The Logic of the Chain](https://rumelt.substack.com/p/the-logic-of-the-chain).

## What Rumelt says he'd change about The Crux

From Lenny 2024:

> "If I could rewrite The Crux book, I would, maybe, write it a little differently today, a whole couple of things I would change."

The changes he's leaning into (from the accumulated post-Crux material):

1. **Replace "strategy" with "action agenda" more forcefully.** In The Crux it's implied; post-Crux it's his lead recommendation.
2. **Add explicit "beliefs" step** between diagnosis and guiding policy. Structure becomes: *Given diagnosis X, and beliefs Y, we choose guiding policy Z.* (This is Kellblog's refinement but Rumelt has echoed the direction in Substack pieces about "Perspectives.")
3. **More emphasis on the "Perspective" (framing) as an act.** Substack piece "The Shade of Strategy" argues a Perspective is a distinct way of seeing that *"triggers the realization that the problem you thought you had is not the problem you actually have."*
4. **More on organizational dysfunction as the frequent locus of the crux** — not just external market. Post-Crux he leans harder on internal capability/politics as the true blocker of most failed strategies.

**Sources:** [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/good-strategy-bad-strategy-richard) · [Substack: The Shade of Strategy](https://rumelt.substack.com/p/the-shade-of-strategy).

## Frameworks Rumelt has commented on since 2011

### Porter's Five Forces

Complementary, but Rumelt pushes back on mechanical use. His 1991 paper *"How much does industry matter?"* showed industry structure explains less variance than firm-specific factors. **Being good at what you do matters more than the industry you're in.** Use Porter as a prompt to think, not as a form to fill in.

### Resource-Based View (RBV)

Rumelt is one of the founders of RBV (uncertain imitability). Post-book he frames it as *"where success comes from — hard-to-replicate, usually intangible resources."* He accepts it descriptively but doesn't use it as a prescriptive tool.

### Blue Ocean

Not attacked explicitly, but the general critique of template strategy applies. Blue Ocean without a diagnosed challenge is decorative.

### OKRs

Not a substitute for strategy. Only useful *after* strategy exists — as an alignment tool for coherent action.

### Balanced Scorecard

Measurement tool, not diagnosis. Belongs downstream of the kernel.

### Lean Startup

Compatible with sequential-crux thinking. Each pivot = next crux. Rumelt is more sympathetic to lean/discovery methods in founder contexts than to corporate strategic planning.

### BCG / Bruce Henderson

Positive citation. Henderson's "Perspectives" paper stock is why Rumelt's Substack uses Rusty Green. Henderson framed strategy as *comprehending forces at work* — Rumelt's diagnosis-first stance.

### Roger Martin's Playing to Win

Broadly compatible. Rumelt's kernel maps to Martin's problem → HTW → integrated activities. Rumelt front-loads diagnosis; Martin front-loads choice. Note: Martin has publicly disagreed with Rumelt on the P&G case (Lenny 2024): *"What he said about Procter and Gamble had zero to do with reality."* Rumelt has not returned fire publicly — consistent with his general disinclination to perform contrarianism.

## Founder-specific advice (from Lenny 2024)

> "The job of a founder is to find an important and solvable problem and then solve it for as many people as possible."

- **Problem-solution fit > grandiose vision.**
- **Prioritize agility and willingness to pivot** in early-stage. Startups that survive and prosper are the ones that switch when their initial theory doesn't hold.
- **Sequence cruxes** — Salesforce model (developers first, then adoption).
- **Don't confuse investor traction with customer traction** (WeWork warning). Growth engineered with investor money isn't sustainable.
- **Startup exception to the general theory:** *"When you're a founder in a startup, you're making a bet... The research that we have done on the startups is that generally it doesn't work. But the ones that survive and prosper switch... there's a search like a truffle hound."* Pre-PMF is search, not choice.

**Source:** Lenny 2024.
